# Morris Park Aerodrome

**Morris Park Aerodrome** was a pioneering airfield located in the Bronx, New York City, that operated from 1908 to 1909. Established by the Aeronautical Society of the City of New York on the grounds of the defunct Morris Park Racecourse, it is recognized as the first official flying field in the United States. The site served as a testing ground for early aviation experiments, including the construction of aircraft, wind wagon races, and the first public air meets in the country. It was the location of the first airplane flight over New York City, performed by Glenn Curtiss in June 1909.[1][2][3]

# History

### Origins
The airfield occupied the site of the Morris Park Racecourse, a thoroughbred horse racing track that had operated since 1889. Following the track's closure, the Aeronautical Society of the City of New York, an organization formed in June 1908 to support aviation experimentation, sought a location for its members to build and test flying machines. In August 1908, the Society leased the 372-acre property, converting the former stables into hangars and workshops.[4][2]

The Society's members, described as "enthusiastic amateurs," utilized the facility to construct gliders, ornithopters, and powered aircraft. The grounds included a mile-long straightaway cleared of fences to facilitate takeoffs and landings. Lacking a natural hill for glider launches, the Society constructed a catapult system and a wooden launch tower; the latter was subsequently condemned by city officials for being a residential structure without proper plumbing or fire escapes.[2]

### Aviation Activities
Morris Park Aerodrome hosted several significant events during its brief existence. The Society organized its first public exhibition on Election Day, November 3, 1908. The event drew an estimated crowd of 20,000 spectators, many of whom entered without paying admission due to the dilapidated state of the perimeter fences. The exhibition featured wind wagon races—wheeled vehicles powered by aircraft propellers—and glider demonstrations. A notable incident during this meet involved Laurence Lesh, a 16-year-old aviation pioneer, who was injured when his glider crashed during a towed flight.[5][2]

In June 1909, the Society hosted its second major exhibition, which featured the debut of the *Golden Flyer* (also known as the *Gold Bug*), a biplane designed and piloted by Glenn Curtiss. The Aeronautical Society had commissioned the aircraft for $5,000, marking the first purchase of an airplane by a civilian organization in the United States. On June 26, 1909, Curtiss flew the *Golden Flyer* at the aerodrome, completing a flight that is recorded as the first airplane flight over the territory of New York City.[3][6][7][1][2]

Other aviation activities at the site included the first passenger-carrying flight in New York, conducted by Dr. William H. Greene in a biplane of his own design. The field also hosted model airplane competitions, including an event where Percy Pierce exhibited a rubber-powered model in 1908.[8][9]

# Closure and Legacy
The aerodrome's operation was short-lived due to the rapid urbanization of the Bronx. The property was slated for residential development, and the Aeronautical Society's lease expired at the end of 1909. The airfield officially closed, and the land was subdivided into lots for housing and streets.[2]

Following the closure, the Aeronautical Society moved its operations to a new site in Mineola, Long Island, which later contributed to the development of Roosevelt Field. Despite its brief operational period, Morris Park Aerodrome established a precedent for dedicated aviation facilities in the United States and played a role in the early career of Glenn Curtiss and the promotion of heavier-than-air flight to the American public.[10][11][1][2]

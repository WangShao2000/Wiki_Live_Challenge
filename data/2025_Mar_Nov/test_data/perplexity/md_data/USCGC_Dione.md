# USCGC Dione

**USCGC *Dione* (WPC-107)** was a *Thetis*-class patrol boat operated by the United States Coast Guard from 1934 to 1963. Built by the Manitowoc Shipbuilding Corporation in Manitowoc, Wisconsin, *Dione* was one of eighteen steel-hulled cutters constructed between 1931 and 1934 for Prohibition enforcement and coastal patrol duties. The cutter served throughout World War II as a convoy escort and anti-submarine vessel before transitioning to search and rescue operations in the postwar period. She gained historical significance as the first Coast Guard cutter stationed in Freeport, Texas, and notably participated in the escort of surrendered German U-boats to American ports in 1945.

# History

### Construction and Launch

*Dione* was laid down in November 1933 at Manitowoc Shipbuilding Corporation in Manitowoc, Wisconsin, and launched on 30 June 1934. The cutter was commissioned into active service on 5 October 1934, following successful sea trials. Her construction cost $258,000. *Dione* was named after Dione, a Titaness in Greek mythology who was the mother of the goddess Aphrodite. The cutter's hull designation was WPC-107, and her signal letters were NRGV. The vessel's building number was P-13.[1]

### Design and Technical Specifications

*Dione* was a *Thetis*-class (also called the *Argo*-class) patrol boat, one of eighteen cutters built between 1931 and 1934. The class was designed to improve upon the earlier 125-foot *Active*-class cutters, which had proven inadequate for Prohibition enforcement. The *Thetis*-class vessels were purpose-built to intercept large, seagoing smuggling vessels known as "mother ships" while balancing considerations of speed, seaworthiness, range, radio equipment, and armament.[2]

*Dione* had a length overall of 165 feet (50 m) and a length between perpendiculars of 160 feet (49 m). Her maximum beam measured 25 feet 3 inches (7.70 m), with a beam at waterline of 23 feet 9 inches (7.24 m). When constructed, the cutter's draft was 7 feet 8 inches (2 m), later increased to 10 feet (3 m) during World War II modifications. Her displacement was 337 long tons (342 tonnes) when fully loaded in 1933, increasing to 350 long tons (360 tonnes) by 1945.[1]

The cutter was powered by two Winton Model 158 six-cylinder diesel engines producing 1,340 horsepower total, driving twin three-bladed propellers. *Dione* could achieve a maximum speed of 16 knots, with a maximum sustained speed of 14 knots for 1,750 statute miles, a cruising speed of 11 knots for 3,000 statute miles, and an economic speed of 6 knots for 6,417 statute miles.[1]

Initially, her complement consisted of 5 officers and 39 men, increasing to 7 officers and 68 men following World War II modifications. *Dione* was constructed with a steel hull and was equipped with extensive deck features including a pilot house, auxiliary diesel generator, three 18-inch (46 cm) searchlights, and two single-mount 1-pound (0.45 kg) guns positioned on either side of the pilot house. A 3-inch/23-caliber gun was mounted on the bow with a magazine located in the keel. Behind the superstructure were two funnels, and on both sides of the aft funnel were the cutter's boats, consisting of 19-foot (5.8 m) dories.[1]

### Prewar Service (1934–1941)

*Dione* departed Manitowoc on 5 October 1934, the same day of her commissioning. The cutter navigated through the Great Lakes and docked in Ogdensburg, New York on 15 October for repairs to her oil purifier. She subsequently traveled through the Saint Lawrence River and the gulf of the same name before stopping in Philadelphia on 30 October to be outfitted with her weaponry. *Dione* arrived at the Coast Guard base on the Elizabeth River in Norfolk, Virginia on 1 November 1934, where she took on fuel and provisions and would establish her homeport.[1]

Based in Norfolk, *Dione* primarily operated in a search and rescue capacity throughout the prewar period. The cutter responded to vessels in distress, transported injured seamen to Norfolk, and located wreckage from downed aircraft. She performed miscellaneous duties including breaking through ice in February 1936 to allow ship access to the Smith and Tangier Islands and accompanying vessels in a Hampton One-Design race in August 1941. *Dione* also delivered and received mail at lightships operating off the Outer Banks.[1]

On 1 November 1941, President Franklin D. Roosevelt signed Executive Order 8929, transferring control of the Coast Guard from the Department of the Treasury to the Navy for the duration of the emergency. Sometime in 1941, *Dione*'s two 1-pound (0.45 kg) guns were removed.[1]

### World War II Service (1941–1945)

Following the United States entry into World War II, *Dione* was assigned to the Eastern Sea Frontier as a convoy escort unit. Between January and June 1942, the cutter conducted escort duties in waters off the eastern coast of the United States, particularly in the region known as "Torpedo Alley" along the North Carolina and Virginia coasts, where numerous Allied merchant vessels were torpedoed by German U-boats.[3][1]

During her convoy escort missions, *Dione* conducted multiple depth charge attacks on suspected U-boat contacts. On 20 March 1942, a plane from Elizabeth City Air Station dropped two depth charges on a contact made by *Dione* with unknown results. On 5 April 1942, *Dione* picked up a survivor from the torpedoed oil tanker Byron D. Benson. On 18 April 1942, a plane observed *Dione* dropping depth charges following a submarine attack on a tanker, though with no visible results. On 24 June 1942, while escorting a convoy, *Dione* made an underwater contact and dropped depth charges with negative results. Later that night, two ships in the convoy, the SS Nordland and the SS Manuela, were torpedoed. *Dione* conducted a sweep search for the submarine but was unsuccessful in locating it. On 25 June 1942, *Dione* made another underwater contact and expended five depth charges, resulting in quantities of oil rising to the surface. On 27 June 1942, *Dione* made another contact and dropped four charges, though the contact was uncertain.[1]

*Dione* was based out of Tompkinsville on Staten Island from December 1943 to December 1944, where she continued escort duties with HK convoys traveling from New York to Key West and KN convoys heading from Key West to New York. During this period, her wireless system was replaced with an SF radar system and a QCN-1 sonar system. Her armament was significantly upgraded, receiving two 3-inch/50-caliber single-mount guns, two 20-millimeter/80-caliber single-mount guns, two depth charge tracks, two Y-guns, and two Mousetraps.[1]

In 1945, *Dione* was selected as one of six patrol vessels to serve as the Navy's "Surrender Group" for the First Naval District off New England. This group was tasked with escorting surrendered Axis vessels to American ports for acquisition by the United States government. In May 1945, *Dione* escorted the German submarine U-1228 to Portsmouth, New Hampshire, where it surrendered to U.S. forces on 17 May 1945.[1]

In June 1945, *Dione* was assigned to air-sea rescue service with the Fifth Coast Guard District.[1]

### Postwar Service and Decommissioning (1945–1963)

From 1945 through 1947, *Dione* served out of Norfolk conducting law enforcement and search and rescue operations. On 8 February 1947, due to a lack of personnel, *Dione* was decommissioned. The cutter remained in inactive reserve for approximately four years before being recommissioned on 8 February 1951.[1]

Following recommissioning, *Dione* was reassigned to the Eighth Coast Guard District and transferred to Freeport, Texas. Originally scheduled to arrive on 13 March 1952, the vessel's arrival was delayed until 19 March due to rough weather during her shakedown cruise. *Dione* became the first Coast Guard cutter to be permanently stationed in Freeport, a distinction that both Freeport and the nearby city of Corpus Christi had competed to obtain. Operating from Freeport, *Dione* served in a search and rescue capacity throughout the Gulf of Mexico. In January 1957, *Dione* towed the disabled buoy tender USCGC Iris to New Orleans from 2–5 January 1957.[1]

*Dione* remained based in Freeport until her final decommissioning on 8 February 1963. The cutter was placed in reserve for approximately one year before being sold on 24 February 1964. Following her sale, *Dione* was converted to a supply ship and given the name *Al Rashid*. In the 1978–1979 edition of Lloyd's Register of Shipping, *Al Rashid* was listed as a supply ship with a length of 154.3 feet (47 m), a beam of 25.3 feet (7.7 m), and a draft of 11.3 feet (3.4 m). She carried a gross register tonnage of 256 and a net register tonnage of 174, with an IMO number of 7515080.[1]

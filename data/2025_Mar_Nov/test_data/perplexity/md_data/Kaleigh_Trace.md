# Kaleigh Trace

**Kaleigh Trace** (born 1986) is a Canadian writer, sex educator, and therapist based in Toronto, Ontario. Trace holds a Master of Science in Couple and Family Therapy from the University of Guelph and is a Registered Psychotherapist. Her 2014 memoir *Hot, Wet, and Shaking: How I Learned to Talk About Sex*, which chronicles her experiences as a disabled, queer, feminist sex educator, won the 2015 Evelyn Richardson Memorial Non-Fiction Award at the Atlantic Book Awards. She republished an updated edition of the memoir in 2024, which includes new essays on grief and medicine.[1][2][3]

### Early life and personal history

Trace was born in 1986. In 1995, she and her family experienced a car accident that left her with a severe spinal cord injury. As a result of the accident, she spent part of her childhood in a wheelchair. She graduated from Dalhousie University with a Bachelor of Arts in 2009.[1]

### Career

#### Sex education and Venus Envy

Following her graduation, Trace became employed at Venus Envy, a Canadian sex shop and book retailer based in Halifax, Nova Scotia, where her work included sex education. Trace credited her time at Venus Envy with expanding her knowledge of human sexuality. While working there, she began writing a blog called *The Fucking Facts*, in which she discussed deeply personal topics such as an abortion she had two years prior to starting the blog. Her blog later served as inspiration for her memoir *Hot, Wet, and Shaking*.[1]

In 2013, Trace received internet attention for her parody song of Robin Thicke's "Blurred Lines" entitled "Ask First," which she co-wrote with Mary Burnet, a fellow Venus Envy staff member. The parody song featured alternative lyrics emphasizing the importance of sexual consent, with the message that sex should be based on mutual agreement rather than coercion. At the time, Trace also conducted workshops on sexuality and consent at university campuses.[4][5][1]

#### Memoir and award

Trace's book *Hot, Wet, and Shaking: How I Learned to Talk About Sex* was released in 2014 by Invisible Publishing. The memoir details her experience as a disabled, queer, feminist sex educator, exploring her personal journey with sexuality through the lens of her spinal cord injury and her work at Venus Envy. Through honest and humorous anecdotes, the book addresses topics including struggles with orgasm, personal relationships, abortion, and ableism. The memoir won the Evelyn Richardson Memorial Non-Fiction Award in 2015.[3][6][1]

In 2024, Trace published a revised and expanded tenth anniversary edition of the book, which includes new essays on grief and medicine, as well as a foreword by Trace contemplating her continued pursuit of pleasure while living with a terminal cancer diagnosis.[3]

#### Therapy practice

Trace remained employed at Venus Envy until moving to Toronto, Ontario. She later completed a Master of Science in Couple and Family Therapy from the University of Guelph. As a Registered Psychotherapist, she works as a couples and family therapist in Toronto. Her work has appeared in publications including *The Coast*, *Shameless Magazine*, and on CBC Radio's *Tapestry*.[2][3][1]

# 2025 Tour Championship

The **2025 Tour Championship** was a professional golf tournament held from August 21–24, 2025, at East Lake Golf Club in Atlanta, Georgia. The event served as the culminating tournament of the PGA Tour Playoffs for the FedEx Cup, the PGA Tour's season-long points competition. Tommy Fleetwood of England won the tournament by three strokes, claiming his first PGA Tour victory and the $10 million first-place prize, along with the FedEx Cup title. Fleetwood's victory came after 164 career PGA Tour starts, making him the player with the most starts by any golfer before recording a first win on the circuit.[1][2][3][4]

The 2025 edition featured a significant format change implemented by the PGA Tour. The starting strokes system, which had been in place since 2019 and gave the FedEx Cup leader a head start at even par, was eliminated. All 30 players in the field began the tournament at even par and competed in a traditional 72-hole stroke-play format. This change was designed to provide greater competitive balance and increase drama in the tournament's closing rounds.[5][6]

## Tournament Format and Field

The 2025 Tour Championship featured the top 30 players in the FedEx Cup standings after the BMW Championship, the second playoff event. World No. 1 Scottie Scheffler entered as the favorite after winning five tournaments during the 2025 season, the first time since Tiger Woods in 2006–07 that a golfer had won five or more times in back-to-back seasons. Rory McIlroy of Northern Ireland, seeking a fourth FedEx Cup title that would be unprecedented in PGA Tour history, appeared in his ninth consecutive Tour Championship with nine top-10 finishes in eleven starts at East Lake.[7][8][9]

The tournament featured seven players making their Tour Championship debut, including J.J. Spaun (ranked third in the FedEx Cup standings), Maverick McNealy, Ben Griffin, Andrew Novak, Harry Hall, Jacob Bridgeman, and Chris Gotterup. Other notable competitors included former FedEx Cup winners Justin Thomas, Patrick Cantlay, Viktor Hovland, and Justin Rose, who earned his spot after winning the FedEx St. Jude Championship earlier in the month.[9]

## Course Setup and Renovation

The 2025 Tour Championship marked the first tournament played at East Lake following a comprehensive $30 million restoration completed in 2024. Architect Andrew Green oversaw the restoration, drawing inspiration from a 1949 aerial photograph to rediscover the original design elements of legendary course architect Donald Ross. The renovation included the rebuilding of every tee, green, fairway, and bunker. Fairways were converted from Meyer Zoysia to Zorro Zoysia grass, and putting surfaces were reshaped and converted from Mini Verde Bermudagrass to TifEagle grass to create firmer, faster playing conditions.[10][11][12]

The course was lengthened by less than 100 yards and set as a par 71, up from par 70 in previous years. The 18th hole underwent notable modifications, and the course setup was designed to emphasize risk-reward moments rather than pure difficulty, encouraging more aggressive play by the world's top golfers.[6][10]

East Lake has served as the permanent home of the Tour Championship since 2005, after first hosting the event in 1998. All proceeds from the tournament benefit the East Lake Foundation, which has raised over $40 million since the inaugural 1998 event for community reinvestment in the surrounding neighborhood.[2][13][14][10]

## Tournament Progression

### Final Round and Results

Tommy Fleetwood emerged as the winner on Sunday, August 24, finishing at 18 under par (262) with a final-round 68. He won by three strokes over Patrick Cantlay and Russell Henley, both of whom finished at 15 under par (265). Fleetwood shared the lead entering the final round with Cantlay but benefited from his playing partner's early struggles with a bogey and double bogey at the opening holes, taking a two-shot advantage.[15][16][17][18]

Throughout the final 18 holes, Fleetwood demonstrated composure on the course's signature holes. Most notably, he navigated the par-3 15th hole, which features an island green surrounded by water. After hitting his opening shot into the water on Saturday for a double bogey, Fleetwood safely found the collection area on Sunday, recording a bogey while avoiding disaster. At the par-5 18th, Fleetwood hit a driver down the left side of the fairway and recorded pars on the final three holes to secure victory.[17][18]

Patrick Cantlay finished his final round with a 71, while Russell Henley carded a 69. Scottie Scheffler, playing as the world No. 1, faced difficulty on the opening hole when his tee shot went out of bounds, recovering to finish tied for fourth at 14 under par (266) along with Corey Conners and Cameron Young. The third-round lead did not hold as expected when Scheffler's second-round position was complicated by water trouble at the 15th hole on Sunday. Rory McIlroy, the three-time FedEx Cup champion, finished tied for 23rd at 6 under par (274).[15][17]

## Prize Money

The 2025 Tour Championship offered a total purse of $40 million, distributed among all 30 competitors. Tommy Fleetwood's first-place prize of $10 million represented a reduction from the previous year's $25 million winner's share, though the total purse had been reallocated across the entire FedEx Cup Playoffs system.[16][19][20]

Prize money distribution for selected finishers included: second place shared by Patrick Cantlay and Russell Henley at $4,352,500 each; T4 finishes (Corey Conners, Cameron Young, and Scottie Scheffler) at $2,616,667 each; and T7 finishes (Justin Thomas, Sam Burns, and Keegan Bradley) at $1,121,667 each.[16][15]

## Significance

Fleetwood's victory held particular significance as his first PGA Tour win after 164 starts, making him the player with the longest career start count before achieving a tour victory. His three-shot margin at 18 under par represented a commanding performance in a tournament featuring the world's top 30 golfers. The win concluded a challenging season for Fleetwood, marked by near-misses at the Travelers Championship in June, where he finished second, and at the FedEx St. Jude Championship two weeks prior, where he lost a two-shot lead with three holes remaining.[4][21][17]

The tournament's format changes, eliminating starting strokes in favor of traditional stroke play, were part of broader efforts by the PGA Tour to increase competitiveness and fan engagement by ensuring that season-long performance determined seeding but not direct competitive advantage.[6]

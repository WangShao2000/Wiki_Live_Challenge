# Skiddaw Group SSSI

**Skiddaw Group SSSI** is a Site of Special Scientific Interest (SSSI) located in the Lake District High Fells in Cumbria, England. Designated for both its geological importance and its biological diversity, the site covers an extensive area of the Northern Fells, including the summits of Skiddaw, Blencathra, and Great Calva. The SSSI is named after the Skiddaw Group, a sequence of Ordovician sedimentary rocks that dominate the geology of the region.

# Location and Extent
The site is situated within the Lake District National Park and encompasses a large portion of the Northern Fells. It acts as a major watershed, with drainage patterns flowing north via the River Caldew towards Carlisle, and west via the Ellen and Derwent catchments towards the Irish Sea. The SSSI boundaries include high mountainous terrain, with several peaks exceeding 2,000 feet (610 m), such as Skiddaw, Little Man, Long Side, Carl Side, Lonscale Fell, and Blencathra.

# Geology
The primary geological interest of the site lies in the exposures of the **Skiddaw Group**, which are among the oldest rocks in the Lake District.

### Stratigraphy and Lithology
The Skiddaw Group consists of a thick sequence of turbidite mudstones and sandstones deposited during the Lower Ordovician period (Tremadoc to Llanvirn epochs, approximately 480–460 million years ago). These sedimentary rocks were formed in a deep marine environment on the margin of the microcontinent Avalonia, which was then located at high southern latitudes. The depositional environment is interpreted as extensional, associated with the waning phase of a continental margin volcanic arc.[1][2][3][4]

The lithology is dominated by grey mudstones, siltstones, and wacke-type sandstones. Notable formations within the group include the **Bitter Beck Formation**, **Watch Hill Formation**, **Hope Beck Formation**, **Loweswater Formation**, and **Kirk Stile Formation** in the Northern Fells.[5]

### Biostratigraphy and Fossils
Although macrofossils are generally sparse, the Skiddaw Group yields important assemblages of graptolites and acritarchs. These fossils have been crucial for biostratigraphy, allowing geologists to date the rocks and correlate them with similar sequences in the Isle of Man and eastern Ireland. The preservation of these fossils assists in understanding the complex stratigraphical sequence which has been tectonically disrupted.[6][2]

### Structural Geology and Igneous Activity
The area exhibits complex deformation resulting from the convergence of Avalonia with Laurentia during the closure of the Iapetus Ocean. The rocks were folded and faulted before being unconformably overlain by the volcanic rocks of the **Borrowdale Volcanic Group**.[3]

The SSSI also contains features associated with the **Skiddaw Granite**, a Devonian intrusion. The contact metamorphism around this granite has created a thermal aureole, altering the surrounding slate into hornfels. Mineralization associated with the granite and the nearby **Caldbeck Fells** mining area is also of significant scientific interest, particularly at localities like Grainsgill.[7]

# Ecology and Habitat
The biological designation of the SSSI recognizes the diversity of upland habitats and the species they support.

### Flora
The site supports extensive areas of dwarf shrub heath and acid grassland. The heath communities are dominated by heather (*Calluna vulgaris*), bilberry (*Vaccinium myrtillus*), and various species of *Sphagnum* moss. High-altitude areas exhibit montane characteristics, providing habitats for alpine plant communities that are rare in England. Species such as *Racomitrium* moss heath and lichens are found on the exposed summits and ridges. The area is also noted for specific rare bryophytes, including *Rhytidiadelphus loreus*.[8][9]

### Fauna
The fell sides and heathlands support breeding populations of upland birds. Notable species include the ring ouzel (*Turdus torquatus*), merlin (*Falco columbarius*), and curlew (*Numenius arquata*). The crags and steep rock faces provide nesting sites for raven (*Corvus corax*) and peregrine falcon (*Falco peregrinus*).[10]

# History and Management
The SSSI is divided into multiple management units to monitor the condition of specific habitats. Assessments by Natural England classify the condition of these units, with some areas historically categorized as "unfavourable - recovering" due to pressures such as grazing.[11]

### Skiddaw Forest
A significant portion of the site includes **Skiddaw Forest**, a treeless area of high moorland despite its name. In 2024, the Cumbria Wildlife Trust purchased a 3,000-acre section of Skiddaw Forest, including the summit of Skiddaw itself. This acquisition aims to restore temperate rainforest (Atlantic rainforest) and montane scrub habitats to the lower slopes, establishing what is described as England's highest nature reserve.[12][13]

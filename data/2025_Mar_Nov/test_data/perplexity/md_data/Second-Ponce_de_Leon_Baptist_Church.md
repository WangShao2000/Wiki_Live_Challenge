# Second-Ponce de Leon Baptist Church

**Second-Ponce de Leon Baptist Church** is a Baptist church located in the Buckhead district of Atlanta, Georgia, United States. The church was established in November 1932 through the merger of two historic Atlanta Baptist congregations: Second Baptist Church, which had been founded in 1854, and Ponce de Leon Baptist Church, which had been founded in 1904. Both predecessor churches had been established as missions of First Baptist Church, which was founded in 1848. The merger occurred during the Great Depression, as both churches experienced declining congregations and financial difficulties. The current church building was completed and dedicated on October 10, 1937. Designed by architect George Harwell Bond in the Georgian Colonial and neocolonial style, the church has maintained a significant role within Baptist denominations since its founding. The congregation has been counted among the largest churches in the Southern Baptist Convention since the mid-20th century.

The church is affiliated with both the Cooperative Baptist Fellowship and the Southern Baptist Convention through its state affiliate, the Georgia Baptist Convention. Second-Ponce de Leon Baptist Church is located at 2715 Peachtree Road NE in Atlanta and maintains an active ministry program, including an ecumenical preschool partnership with the neighboring Cathedral of Christ the King.

# History

### Predecessor Churches

Second-Ponce de Leon Baptist Church was formed through the combination of two Atlanta-based Baptist churches that had developed separately over the preceding decades.

#### Second Baptist Church

Second Baptist Church was organized on September 1, 1854, by nineteen members of First Baptist Church. The Reverend Charles M. Irwin served as the church's first pastor. During its early months following establishment, Second Baptist operated as a house church while its own church building was under construction. The congregation eventually settled at the intersection of Mitchell Street and Washington Street in downtown Atlanta, directly across from the Atlanta City Hall. The permanent church building was completed in 1858.

#### Ponce de Leon Baptist Church

Ponce de Leon Baptist Church was established in 1904 as another mission of First Baptist Church. Like Second Baptist, the church developed a significant congregation and became an established presence in Atlanta's religious community during the early twentieth century.

### The 1932 Merger and New Church Building

By the early 1930s, both Second Baptist Church and Ponce de Leon Baptist Church had experienced decline in their membership rolls. The financial pressures of the Great Depression exacerbated these difficulties for both congregations. In response to these challenges, the two churches made the decision to merge and combine their resources. The merger was formalized in November 1932, and the combined congregation adopted the name "Second-Ponce de Leon Baptist Church," creating a unified entity from the two historic congregations.

For the newly merged church, a new building was commissioned. Architect George Harwell Bond was selected to design the structure. Bond drew his inspiration from colonial meeting houses and churches of the colonial era, designing the building in the Georgian Colonial and neocolonial style. Construction proceeded through the mid-1930s, and the completed sanctuary was officially dedicated on October 10, 1937. The dedication of this new building marked the beginning of a new chapter for the consolidated congregation and provided the physical home that would support its growth through the subsequent decades.

The church bell that had been installed in the original Second Baptist Church building was transferred to the new Second-Ponce de Leon building and installed on December 12, 1939. Through the late 1930s and 1940s, the church campus was expanded with the construction of several additional buildings, including a chapel building, a recreation building, and a pastorium to house the senior pastor.

### Growth and Development

Following the dedication of the new church building in 1937, Second-Ponce de Leon Baptist Church experienced significant growth. By 1954, the congregation had expanded to approximately 3,600 members, with Sunday school enrollment reaching approximately 3,500. This substantial membership base positioned the church as one of the largest congregations within both the Georgia Baptist Convention and the Southern Baptist Convention more broadly. The church's growth during the post-World War II era was part of a broader trend of Baptist church expansion throughout the South, coinciding with increased church attendance and urban development in the Atlanta area.

During this period of growth and expansion, Second-Ponce de Leon Baptist Church developed ambitions to extend its ministry into developing areas of Atlanta. In 1946, the congregation expressed a vision to plant a new church in the North Buckhead area, which it perceived as a growing mission field. The church purchased property on Wieuca Road in 1947 for this purpose. The new congregation, known as Wieuca Road Baptist Church, held its first service on May 9, 1954. Wieuca Road Baptist Church developed into one of the largest Baptist churches in Atlanta, with rapid growth during its early years and subsequent prominence within the Southern Baptist Convention.

# Architecture and Building

The Second-Ponce de Leon Baptist Church building, completed in 1937, represents the work of architect George Harwell Bond, a prominent Atlanta architect of the early twentieth century. Bond designed the structure to evoke the architectural traditions of American colonial religious buildings, incorporating elements characteristic of the Georgian Colonial and neocolonial styles.

According to a 1941 survey conducted by the Works Progress Administration, the building was classified in the Georgian Colonial architectural style. Later architectural analysis offered a slightly different designation, with a 1993 publication from the American Institute of Architects describing the structure as neocolonial, noting that later additions to the structure incorporated postmodern design elements.

The campus has undergone multiple phases of renovation and expansion since its initial construction. Beginning in 2009, comprehensive renovation and expansion projects were undertaken, including the renovation of the sanctuary and associated spaces, the addition of new facilities for children's education, and the renovation of the chapel building. These improvements expanded the campus to approximately 80,000 square feet of space and enhanced the church's capacity to serve its congregation and surrounding community.

# Denominational Affiliations and Community Engagement

Second-Ponce de Leon Baptist Church maintains dual affiliation with both the Cooperative Baptist Fellowship and the Southern Baptist Convention. Its connection to the Southern Baptist Convention is formalized through the Georgia Baptist Convention, the state-level organization affiliated with the national convention. The church's membership in the Cooperative Baptist Fellowship reflects its alignment with the moderately progressive Baptist tradition that emerged in the late twentieth century.

In addition to its primary religious mission, the church has engaged in community partnerships and ecumenical initiatives. Most notably, in 2022, the church formalized a partnership with the Cathedral of Christ the King, the neighboring Roman Catholic parish located at the same intersection of Peachtree and East Wesley roads. Through this partnership, the Catholic preschool Christ the King Preschool is hosted on the Second-Ponce campus. The arrangement grew out of a longstanding relationship between the two congregations, which had previously coordinated Good Friday services and other ecumenical activities. The preschool began operations in August 2022, serving students from infancy through kindergarten age and maintaining continuity with the preschool program that Second-Ponce had previously operated since 1968.

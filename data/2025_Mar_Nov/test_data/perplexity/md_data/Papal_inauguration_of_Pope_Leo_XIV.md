# Papal Inauguration of Pope Leo XIV

The **Papal Inauguration of Pope Leo XIV** was the formal liturgical ceremony marking the beginning of Pope Leo XIV's pontificate as Bishop of Rome and Supreme Pontiff of the Roman Catholic Church. Held on May 18, 2025, in St. Peter's Square at the Vatican, the inauguration Mass formally installed Cardinal Robert Francis Prevost, who became the 267th pope and the first American to assume the papacy in the Church's 2,000-year history. The ceremony, officially titled the "Mass for the Beginning of the Petrine Ministry of Bishop Leo XIV of Rome," was celebrated with traditional liturgical elements and attended by an estimated 150,000 to 200,000 pilgrims, approximately 200 cardinals, 750 bishops and priests, and some 30 heads of state and other international dignitaries.[1][2][3]

# Background

Robert Francis Prevost was born on September 14, 1955, in Chicago, Illinois, and professed vows with the Order of Saint Augustine in 1981. Before his election as pope, Prevost spent significant portions of his ministry in missionary work in Peru, including service in Chulucanas and Trujillo from 1985 to 1998, and later as Bishop of Chiclayo from 2015 to 2023. He was elected Prior General of the entire Augustinian order from 2001 to 2013, serving two terms, and was appointed Prefect of the Dicastery for Bishops by Pope Francis in January 2023.[4][5][6][7]

The papal conclave convened on May 7 and 8, 2025, following the death of Pope Francis on April 21, 2025. Prevost was elected on the fourth ballot on May 8, 2025, receiving more than 100 votes—exceeding the two-thirds majority of 89 votes required. White smoke emerged from the Sistine Chapel at 18:07 CEST (UTC+2), signifying the election of a new pope. After accepting his election, Prevost chose the papal name Leo XIV, at least in part to honor Pope Leo XIII and his legacy of Catholic social teaching.[8][9][1]

# First Mass as Pope

On May 9, 2025, the day after his election, Pope Leo XIV celebrated his first Mass as Pope in the Sistine Chapel before the assembled College of Cardinals. In his homily, he preached against the lack of faith in the world and spoke of a church that would act as a "beacon that illuminates the dark nights of this world." He introduced his episcopal motto, *In illo Uno unum* ("In the One, we are one"), and requested that the Church's "first great desire be for a united Church, a sign of unity and communion, which becomes a leaven for a reconciled world."[10][1]

# Inauguration Ceremony

### Date and Venue

The Mass for the inauguration of Leo XIV's pontificate took place on Sunday, May 18, 2025, beginning at 10:00 a.m. Rome time in St. Peter's Square. The choice of St. Peter's Square, designed by Gian Lorenzo Bernini between 1656 and 1667, allowed for the attendance of large numbers of the faithful, as the plaza spans 320 meters long and 240 meters wide, capable of accommodating up to 300,000 people.[11][12]

### Liturgical Elements

The liturgy formally began at the tomb of Saint Peter underneath the baldachin of the high altar of St. Peter's Basilica. Surrounded by the patriarchs of the Eastern Catholic Churches, Leo incensed the tomb while the choir sang *Tu es Petrus*, a motet composed by Giovanni Pierluigi da Palestrina in the sixteenth century. The Gospel was proclaimed in both Greek and Latin, expressing the unity of the Church and emphasizing that the pope serves both Latin Catholics and Eastern Catholics.[13][14]

Pope Leo XIV delivered his homily in Italian, addressing themes of unity, love, and pastoral service. Drawing on the teachings of Saint Augustine and Pope Leo XIII, he emphasized that the Petrine ministry consists of "loving as Jesus did" and that Peter must "shepherd the flock without ever yielding to the temptation to be an autocrat, lording it over those entrusted to him." He stressed that Peter is called to serve his brothers and sisters and to walk alongside them in "fraternal communion, in the harmony of the Spirit, in the coexistence of diversity."[3]

### Reception of Papal Insignia

During the Mass, Pope Leo XIV received two significant symbols of papal authority: the pallium and the Ring of the Fisherman. Cardinal Mario Zenari, representing the cardinal deacons and the order of deacons, imposed the pallium on Leo's shoulders. The pallium, a white woolen vestment made from lamb's wool blessed on the feast of Saint Agnes, symbolizes the Pope's role as the Good Shepherd and represents how he carries the Church upon his shoulders as the successor of Saint Peter and visible head of the Catholic Church.[15][16][13]

Cardinal Luis Antonio Tagle, Pro-Prefect of the Dicastery for Evangelization of Peoples and representing the order of bishops and the Asian continent, bestowed the Ring of the Fisherman upon Pope Leo XIV. The ring, engraved with an image of Saint Peter the Apostle and the pope's name, serves as the pope's official seal and symbolizes his role as successor to Peter, the apostle called to be a "fisher of men." Leo appeared visibly moved during the moment of receiving the ring.[13][15]

Cardinal Fridolin Ambongo Besungu, representing the cardinal priests and the African continent, recited a prayer for the pope before the imposition of the insignia. Cardinal Dominique Mamberti, the Protodeacon who had announced Leo's election from the balcony of St. Peter's Basilica on May 8, was scheduled to impose the pallium but became ill during the Mass and was replaced by Cardinal Zenari.[15]

### Rite of Obedience

Following the investiture of the papal insignia, twelve delegates representing the Catholic Church from around the world pledged obedience to the new pope. This addition represented a novelty compared to Pope Francis's inauguration Mass in 2013, which did not include representatives of the lay faithful in the rite of obedience. The delegates included clerics, vowed religious, a married couple, and young adults representing the three orders of the clergy, consecrated life, and the laity. Among them were Frank Leo, Archbishop of Toronto, representing North America; Jaime Spengler, Archbishop of Porto Alegre, representing South America; and John Ribat, Archbishop of Port Moresby, representing Oceania, each representing cardinal orders and continents.[15]

### Conclusion of Mass

Before the conclusion of the Mass, Pope Leo XIV offered remarks in which he prayed for peace, specifically mentioning conflicts in Gaza, Myanmar, and Ukraine. The *Regina cæli* was then sung, and the pope imparted his final blessing. Throughout the ceremony, Leo used the papal ferula (crosier) commonly used by Pope John Paul II.[1]

# Attendance

The inauguration Mass was attended by approximately 30 heads of state, 200 cardinals, and 750 bishops and priests, along with an estimated 150,000 to 200,000 pilgrims gathered in and around St. Peter's Square. Notably, U.S. Vice President JD Vance and Secretary of State Marco Rubio attended on behalf of the United States government. Other notable attendees included Spanish King Felipe VI and Queen Letizia, and Italian President Sergio Matarella. The presence of international leaders underscored the global significance of Leo's election as the first American pope.[17][3][1]

# Papal Residence

Prior to his inauguration, it was reported that Pope Leo XIV would take up permanent residence in the papal apartments in the Apostolic Palace rather than in the Domus Sanctae Marthae, where his predecessor Pope Francis had chosen to live. This marked a return to the traditional papal residence used by predecessors before Francis.[1]

## Subsequent Ceremonies

Following his inauguration Mass, Pope Leo XIV took canonical possession of the other major basilicas of Rome in a series of ceremonies. On May 20, 2025, he took possession of the Basilica of Saint Paul Outside the Walls, where he venerated the tomb of the Apostle Paul. On May 25, 2025, he celebrated his second Regina cæli and took formal possession of the Archbasilica of Saint John Lateran, the cathedral of the Bishop of Rome, and the Basilica of Santa Maria Maggiore, where he venerated the icon of the *Salus Populi Romani* (Protection of the Roman People). These ceremonies formally completed the installation of Leo XIV as the Bishop of Rome, as the taking of possession of these four major papal basilicas is a traditional element that emphasizes the pope's role as Bishop of Rome and head of the universal Church.[18][1]

## Theological and Pastoral Themes

Pope Leo XIV's inauguration Mass homily emphasized several key themes that would characterize his early pontificate. He called for the Church to be a "sign of unity and communion, which becomes a leaven for a reconciled world," addressing the discord and division present in contemporary society caused by "hatred, violence, prejudice, the fear of difference, and an economic paradigm that exploits the Earth's resources and marginalizes the poorest."[1]

Leo repeatedly stressed the inseparability of love and unity as the two dimensions of the Petrine ministry, drawing on Scriptural and patristic sources to articulate a vision of papal authority rooted in service and charity rather than power. He spoke of the Church's missionary vocation as requiring openness to the world and dialogue with other Christian denominations, those of other faiths, and all people of goodwill, seeking to "build a new world where peace reigns."[3][10]

The pope's emphasis on ecumenism and interfaith dialogue reflected the legacy of Pope Francis while presenting Leo's own distinctive pastoral approach. In his remarks after Mass, he prayed specifically for the resolution of conflicts in Gaza, Myanmar, and Ukraine, demonstrating his commitment to the Church's advocacy for peace in troubled regions.[1]

## Historical Significance

The inauguration of Pope Leo XIV represents a historic moment in the Roman Catholic Church. Prevost's election marks the first time in the Church's 2,000-year history that an American-born cardinal has been elevated to the papacy. His choice of the papal name Leo XIV, connecting him to the legacy of Pope Leo XIII and that pontiff's pioneering work in Catholic social teaching, signaled his intention to engage with contemporary social and economic issues through the lens of Catholic social doctrine.[2][19]

The prominence of lay participation in the rite of obedience and the international representation among the delegates reflected contemporary developments in how the Church understands the participation of the entire People of God in the life of the Church. Leo's explicit commitment to unity, dialogue, and missionary engagement announced during his inauguration would frame the initial direction of his pontificate.

# Joy Laking

**Joy Snihur Wyatt Laking** (born 1950) is a Canadian visual artist and author based in Nova Scotia, known for her watercolour depictions of Nova Scotian landscapes and her contributions to representational realism. Laking has had national and international exhibitions, and has received multiple awards recognising her artistic achievements. In addition to her paintings, she has written theatrical works and published illustrated books celebrating Nova Scotia's visual character.

### Early life and education

Laking was born in 1950 in Owen Sound, Ontario. She grew up in an artistic family; her mother was a professional painter, while her father crafted marionettes and toy trains. As a child, Laking frequently accompanied her mother on excursions to Georgian Bay to paint the scenery. She attended Owen Sound Collegiate and Vocational Institute, graduating in 1969. In a Grade 8 writing assignment, she predicted she would be making a living as an artist within ten years, a prediction that would prove accurate.[1]

Laking earned a Bachelor of Arts with a major in Fine Art from the University of Guelph in 1972. That same year, she married and relocated to Nova Scotia with her husband. Shortly after her graduation, her mother died of breast cancer, an event that profoundly influenced Laking's artistic direction and career trajectory.[2]

### Artistic career

#### Early years in Nova Scotia

After relocating to Nova Scotia in 1972, Laking began her career as a professional painter. In the early 1980s, she illustrated children's books for author Gary Saunders. Although she valued the illustration experience, she found the compensation insufficient and pursued other artistic opportunities. She subsequently created and released a series of calendars featuring her paintings.[3]

Laking held her first solo exhibition, titled "Flowers of Nova Scotia," at Mount Saint Vincent Art Gallery in 1982. This exhibition marked the beginning of her growing national reputation. In 1984, her work was selected to be featured on the cover of the MTT provincial phone book. The following year, 50 of her watercolour paintings were displayed at the Royal Botanical Gardens in Burlington, Ontario, and she held her first New Brunswick solo exhibition at the Owens Art Gallery at Mount Allison University.[1]

Between 1982 and 1992, Laking participated in six group exhibitions and held nine solo exhibitions. In 1989, she exhibited a 40-piece watercolour collection at the Art Gallery of Nova Scotia, followed by a year-long regional tour that ended in May 1990.[1]

In the late 1980s, Laking and her husband built the Joy Laking Gallery at their Portapique home, opening the space to the public in 1988. The gallery displays her work and operates by appointment. Her paintings have also been sold through private galleries in Halifax and Hamilton, Ontario.[4]

#### Expanding artistic practice

In 2006, one of Laking's pink grapefruit paintings was selected to be featured on a Canadian postage stamp, as part of a fundraising campaign for the Jewish General Hospital in Montreal. This honour reflected the broader recognition of her artistic work.[1]

Following a serious health issue in 2007, Laking experienced what she described as an "explosion of creativity." She began expanding beyond her primary focus on watercolour painting to experiment with other mediums, including photography, rug hooking, wool dyeing, and oil stick painting. In January 2007, she held a dual exhibition with photographer Laurie Gunn in Truro, Nova Scotia, consisting entirely of photographs.[1]

By this time, Laking's artistic style had matured, and she had developed a deep connection to Nova Scotia's landscapes. While self-taught in watercolour painting, she developed a distinctive approach that contrasts with traditional watercolour techniques, often working from dark to light rather than following conventional methods. Her work is characterised by representational realism and a focus on *plein air* paintings, particularly landscapes of the Bay of Fundy region near her home.[1]

#### Later career and recognition

In 2013, Laking served as community artist at the White Rabbit Arts Festival in Upper Economy. That year also marked 25 years of annual open houses at the Joy Laking Gallery, a testament to her sustained artistic practice and community engagement.[1]

In 2016, Laking was named artist-in-residence at the Port Bickerton Lighthouse, continuing her practice of drawing inspiration from Nova Scotia's maritime heritage. In 2017, she travelled throughout Europe and was granted the opportunity to paint in the gardens of Claude Monet.[5][1]

In 2021, Laking's work was featured in two exhibitions at the Fraser Art Gallery in Tatamagouche. Her paintings have been exhibited across Nova Scotia, New Brunswick, Ontario, and Quebec, including at the Art Gallery of Nova Scotia and the Royal Botanical Gardens in Burlington.[5]

### Artistic style and influences

Laking's work is characterised by representational realism, with emphasis on capturing the visual qualities of landscapes and natural phenomena. She is primarily known as a watercolourist, though she also works extensively in oils and acrylics. Her style was influenced by her childhood in Grey County, Ontario, an area shaped by the aesthetics of the Group of Seven landscape painters. She names Emily Carr as an important artistic influence.[1]

Her practice of painting on location (*en plein air*) during warmer months has been central to her artistic method. International travel has enriched her work, introducing her to new facets of light and colour. She has painted in numerous countries, including Italy, Ghana, India, Greece, Spain, France, England, Iceland, Japan, Thailand, Brazil, Argentina, Bolivia, Peru, Chile, Colombia, Ecuador, and New Zealand.[1]

Laking's work also demonstrates a particular fascination with light and reflection, particularly reflected light captured through watercolour. She has created numerous paintings of views through windows, often incorporating glass bottles on windowsills to explore how light refracts and reflects.[6]

### Literary works

In addition to her paintings, Laking has pursued writing. She wrote *Invisible Prisons*, a play consisting of thirteen monologues about domestic abuse. The play was performed in Truro, Nova Scotia in March 2013, directed by Bryden MacDonald and produced by Lenore Zann, with twelve members of the Truro Theatre Society performing the various monologues. The work was also performed in New York.[1]

Laking published *Colours in Winter* in 2019 and *The Painted Province* in 2020. *The Painted Province* is an illustrated book organised into forty regions of Nova Scotia, each with Laking's personal commentary and selected paintings. The book includes GPS coordinates for several locations depicted in her work, inviting readers to visit the sites and photograph them. She has also written children's books, contributed articles about rural life, and produced work for the Canadian Broadcasting Corporation.[1]

In 2025, Laking released *Capturing Beauty*, a 160-page book in full colour featuring more than 100 paintings spanning her career and demonstrating her work across different artistic styles and mediums.[7]

### Community involvement and affiliations

Laking has been an active member of Nova Scotia's arts community. She is an elected member of the Society of Canadian Artists and a founding member of Professional Living Artists of Nova Scotia (PLANS). She has served on the boards of numerous cultural and community organisations, including the Ship's Company Theatre, the Elizabeth Bishop Society, the Art Gallery of Nova Scotia, the Third Place Transition House, the Robert Pope Foundation, and the Ivar Mendez International Foundation.[5]

### Awards and honours

Laking received the Halifax Progress Club Woman of Excellence Award in 2009. In 2012, she was awarded the Queen Elizabeth II Diamond Jubilee Medal in recognition of her contributions to the arts. She also received the Lifetime Achievement Award from the Cobequid Arts Council.[5][1]

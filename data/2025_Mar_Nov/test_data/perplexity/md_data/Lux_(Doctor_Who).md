# Lux (Doctor Who)

**Lux** is the second episode of the fifteenth series of the British science-fiction television series *Doctor Who*. It was written by Russell T Davies, the *Doctor Who* showrunner, from a concept he first developed over two decades prior, and directed by Australian director Amanda Brotchie. In the episode, the Fifteenth Doctor (Ncuti Gatwa) and his companion, Belinda Chandra (Varada Sethu), land in 1952 Miami, Florida, while trying to return to 2025. After doing so, they discover a mysterious cinema where fifteen people have disappeared. The pair investigate and ultimately confront an animated god, Lux Imperator (voiced by Alan Cumming), who traps them in film. The episode aired on BBC iPlayer, BBC One, and Disney+ on 19 April 2025 and features a mixed live-action and animated format unusual for the programme.

# Plot

The TARDIS, unable to return to 24 May 2025, lands in 1952 Miami. The Doctor and Belinda discover a cinema with its doors chained shut. At a nearby diner, they speak to the mother of one of fifteen people who disappeared while attending the cinema, which continues to play movies at night. Although racial segregation is still in effect in 1952, a diner worker named Logan allows them to remain, bending the segregation laws to permit conversation with the missing person's mother.

Inside the cinema, the Doctor and Belinda discover a living rubber hose cartoon character named Mr. Ring-a-Ding, who is revealed to be the embodiment of Lux Imperator, the God of Light and a member of the Pantheon of Discord. The projectionist, Reginald Pye, plays films for Lux to sustain him. In exchange, Lux has recreated Pye's deceased wife from a film projection.

Lux has imprisoned the missing fifteen people within a film reel. When the Doctor and Belinda are similarly trapped and turned into animated cartoon characters, they gradually recover their three-dimensional form by exploring deeper emotional dimensions of their characters. They flee into another false reality where a police officer, conjured by Lux's power and falsely presented as an NYPD officer, accuses them of violating segregation laws.

The pair escape through a television watched by three *Doctor Who* fans named Lizzie, Hassan, and Robyn. These fans, delighted to meet the Doctor, discuss how to defeat Lux and suggest burning the film reels. However, the fans reveal they are not real and will cease to exist once the Doctor and Belinda escape to the real world, though they encourage the pair to proceed regardless.

Back in the cinema, the Doctor injures his hand escaping but heals it using residual regeneration energy remaining from a previous regeneration. Lux steals this energy to create a solid body for himself and attempt to leave the theatre. Belinda attempts to burn the film reels to cause an explosion. Encouraged by his wife's presence, Reginald Pye sacrifices himself in the explosion. The explosion exposes the cinema to sunlight, causing Lux to expand infinitely until he evaporates into the universe, releasing the missing people from the theatre.

As the Doctor and Belinda depart, Mrs. Flood appears and encourages bystanders to watch the TARDIS dematerialise, claiming the "show" is a "limited run" that ends on 24 May. The three fans are discovered to still exist during the end credits, critiquing the episode that viewers have just watched.

# Production

### Development and concept

Russell T Davies developed the concept for "Lux" over two decades before it was produced, making it an unusually long-gestated project within *Doctor Who*. Davies described animation as expensive but justified as a genuine art form requiring tremendous effort and dedication.[15] He acknowledged that venturing into animation was a learning curve, requiring him and the production team to approve every detail from eye movements to hand gestures of the animated character.[15]

### Filming and animation

Filming took place in Penarth, Cardiff and at Wolf Studios Wales in January 2024.[1] Voice-over work was recorded internationally in New York City in June 2025.[1] Animation and post-production work continued throughout the year, with some tasks being completed as late as September 2025.[1]

For sequences where the Doctor and Belinda transform into cartoon characters, Ncuti Gatwa and Varada Sethu filmed their performances in character, allowing animators to match the animated figures closely to the actors' movements.[15] Gatwa described the process as "a unique approach to our work that we haven't experienced before," while Sethu noted they initially expected to provide voice-overs separately but instead participated in actual filming.[15]

The animated villain Mr. Ring-a-Ding was created in the style of 1930s rubber hose animation, reminiscent of characters in the video game *Cuphead*.[4]

### Casting

Alan Cumming voices the antagonist, Mr. Ring-a-Ding/Lux Imperator. This marks Cumming's second appearance in the show after his role as King James VI and I in the 2018 episode "The Witchfinders."[7] Cumming remarked on his experience portraying the character, noting that misunderstood villains are his favourite type to perform, and he appreciated that while Mr. Ring-a-Ding does not possess redeeming qualities, he begins as a fun and entertaining presence.[4]

Linus Roache stars as Reginald Pye, the theatre's projectionist, and Lewis Cornay plays a diner worker who helps the Doctor and Belinda investigate the disappearances.[7] Anita Dobson makes a brief appearance as recurring character Mrs. Flood.[7] The trio of fans were portrayed by Samir Arrian, Bronte Barbe, and Steph Lacey.[7]

### Themes and references

The episode addresses racism and segregation within its 1952 Miami setting. The Doctor explains to Belinda that in 1952, they are breaking the law by sitting in a segregated diner as people of colour.[43] While the show includes these themes, showrunner Russell T Davies emphasized that he did not want them to be the dominant focus of the episode.[7] A notable scene features a police officer (who exists only in the animated reality created by Lux) stating the cinema is a space "reserved for white folk" and making disparaging assumptions about Belinda based on her appearance.[7]

The episode contains references to popular culture, including mentions of Rock Hudson and the *Scooby-Doo* character Velma.[7] It features multiple fourth wall breaks, with Mrs. Flood addressing the camera and the Doctor and Belinda literally breaking through a television screen to escape the animated world.[34]

The concept of *Doctor Who* existing within its own universe had been briefly explored in the 1988 serial *Remembrance of the Daleks* and other expanded media.[7] Following the instance in "Lux," this idea was considered quasi-canon by reviewers.[7] The episode features an unusual mid-credits scene, which is rare for the series.[7]

# Reception

### Critical response

"Lux" received generally positive reviews from critics. *The Guardian*'s Martin Belam responded positively to the episode, praising Cumming's performance and the fourth-wall-breaking scene.[7] Will Salmon, writing for *GamesRadar+*, drew attention to Mr. Ring-a-Ding and Gatwa's performance, though felt Murray Gold's musical score "drowned out" some scenes.[7] Adi Tantimedh, writing for *Bleeding Cool*, found the episode to be the strongest episode headed by Davies in his second tenure as showrunner.[7]

*Collider* described the episode as "one of the best episodes of *Doctor Who* to date," praising the blend of animation and live-action and highlighting the chemistry between Gatwa and Sethu.[31] *Polygon* noted that the animation process represented a long-held creative dream for Davies that budget constraints had previously prevented.[15]

Despite positive critical reception, fan opinions varied. Some praised the meta-commentary on fandom and the episode's creative ambition. Others found the rapid tonal shifts jarring and felt the resolution involving the introduction of yet another Pantheon god seemed repetitive.[12]

### Ratings

"Lux" recorded the lowest overnight broadcast ratings in *Doctor Who* history, with only 1.58 million viewers on BBC One.[44] This marked the first time overnight viewers dropped below 2 million.[44] The episode was the fourth most-watched programme on BBC One that day, behind *News at Ten*, *Casualty*, and *Blankety Blank*.[44]

However, when consolidated ratings were released, accounting for viewers who watched on BBC iPlayer and other platforms, the episode achieved 3 million viewers.[7] Despite the lower live broadcast figures, the feedback from those who watched was predominantly positive, with many expressing admiration for the animation and Mr. Ring-a-Ding's character design.[12]

# Pantheon connection

Lux Imperator is identified as the God of Light and the fourth member of the Pantheon of Discord to appear in the Fifteenth Doctor's era, following the Toymaker, Maestro, and Sutekh from the previous season.[24] The Doctor identifies himself to Lux as the "destroyer of the pantheon."[4] The episode suggests the Pantheon's influence extends beyond what was defeated in the previous season's finale.

# Novelisation

A novelisation of the episode written by James Goss was released on 10 July 2025 by BBC Books as part of the Target Collection.[27][30] Goss previously adapted other recent *Doctor Who* episodes including *City of Death*, *The Pirate Planet*, and *The Giggle*.[27] The novelisation synopsis describes fifteen missing people, a light that hungers, and a trip outside reality, elaborating that the Doctor and Belinda must contend with Mr. Ring-a-Ding, who wants something only the Doctor can provide.[30]

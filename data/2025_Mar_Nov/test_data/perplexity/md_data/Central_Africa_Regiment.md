# Central Africa Regiment

The **Central Africa Regiment** (CAR) was a British colonial military unit raised in the British Central Africa Protectorate (present-day Malawi). Formed in 1896 as the **British Central African Rifles**, the regiment played a central role in maintaining colonial authority in the region and participated in British military expeditions across the African continent, including campaigns in the Gold Coast (Ghana), The Gambia, and Somaliland. In 1902, the regiment was amalgamated into the King's African Rifles (KAR), forming the unit's 1st and 2nd Battalions.

# History

### Formation
Following the establishment of the British Central Africa Protectorate in 1891, British administrators relied initially on a small force of Sikh soldiers from the Indian Army and local police to suppress slave trading and assert control over the territory. In 1896, the colonial administration formalized these forces into a distinct military unit known as the **British Central African Rifles**.[1][2]

The unit was initially created to ensure internal security and counter resistance from local groups, such as the Yao and Ngoni people. In 1898, the unit was renamed the **Central African Rifles**, dropping the "British" prefix. In 1899, following the decision to raise a second battalion for service outside the protectorate, the force was re-designated as the **Central Africa Regiment**.[3][2][1]

### Composition and Structure
The regiment was commanded by British officers seconded from the British Army or Indian Army. A contingent of Sikh non-commissioned officers (NCOs) provided the backbone of the unit's discipline and training structure, while the rank and file were recruited from local ethnic groups, predominantly the Yao, Tonga (Atonga), and Ngoni.[1]

The regiment was eventually organized into two battalions:
*   **1st Battalion:** Primarily responsible for garrison duties within the British Central Africa Protectorate.
*   **2nd Battalion:** Established in 1899 specifically for "foreign" service in other British colonies.[4][3]

### Uniform
The regiment's parade uniform consisted of a blue jersey, shorts, and a red fez. On active service, soldiers wore khaki drill uniforms. A distinguishing feature of the Nyasaland (Malawi) units was the use of a black fez during field operations, a tradition that continued after the amalgamation into the King's African Rifles. The regimental band was distinct for wearing bright yellow trousers, earning them the nickname "canary birds".[5][6][1]

# Campaigns

The Central Africa Regiment saw active service both within its home territory and in other parts of the British Empire.

### Internal Security
In 1898, elements of the regiment, including six companies of African troops and a Sikh contingent under the command of Lieutenant Colonel H.E.J. Brake, conducted an expedition against the Ngoni chief Mpezeni. The operation employed 7-pounder field guns and Maxim guns, resulting in the defeat of the Ngoni forces.[2]

### War of the Golden Stool (1900)
During the War of the Golden Stool (also known as the Yaa Asantewaa War) in the Gold Coast (modern Ghana), the British colonial government requested reinforcements to relieve the besieged garrison at Kumasi. The 2nd Battalion of the Central Africa Regiment, under the command of Lieutenant Colonel Brake, was deployed to the region. Soldiers from the regiment were involved in the relief operations and the subsequent suppression of the Ashanti uprising. For their service, members of the regiment were awarded the Ashanti Medal.[7][8][9]

### The Gambia Expedition (1901)
In 1901, the 2nd Battalion was diverted from its return journey to the protectorate to assist in suppressing an uprising in The Gambia. Operating alongside the West India Regiment and the newly forming Gambia Company, the Central Africa Regiment contingent formed part of the "Gambia Field Force" commanded by Lieutenant Colonel Brake.[10][4]

### Somaliland Campaign
Elements of the regiment were also deployed to British Somaliland to participate in operations against the Dervish state led by Sayyid Mohammed Abdullah Hassan (referred to by the British as the "Mad Mullah"). These operations were part of a series of campaigns that involved troops from across the British Empire, including India and East Africa.[11][12]

# Amalgamation
On January 1, 1902, the British colonial military forces in East and Central Africa were reorganized to create a unified command structure. The Central Africa Regiment was amalgamated with the Uganda Rifles and the East Africa Rifles to form the **King's African Rifles** (KAR).[11][5]

Upon this merger:
*   The **1st Battalion, Central Africa Regiment** became the **1st (Nyasaland) Battalion, King's African Rifles**.
*   The **2nd Battalion, Central Africa Regiment** became the **2nd (Nyasaland) Battalion, King's African Rifles**.[13][3]

These battalions continued to serve throughout the colonial period, seeing action in both World War I and World War II, until the independence of Malawi in 1964.[3][5]

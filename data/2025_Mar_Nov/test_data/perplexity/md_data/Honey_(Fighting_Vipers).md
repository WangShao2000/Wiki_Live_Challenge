# Honey (Fighting Vipers)

**Honey** (Japanese: ハニー, Hepburn: *Hanī*) is a character introduced in the 1995 fighting game *Fighting Vipers*, developed by Sega's AM2 development team. She was created by character designer Masahiro Sugiyama to be a rich young girl and a "cute" counterpart to the game's other female characters.[1] In localized versions of the game released outside Japan, her name was changed to **Candy**.[2] A variant of the character, **Honey the Cat**, was later discovered as unused code in Sega's *Sonic the Fighters* game and was officially restored as a playable character in the 2012 digital re-release for Xbox Live Arcade and PlayStation Network.

Honey is regarded as the most well-known character from the *Fighting Vipers* series and is frequently cited as the game's ensemble dark horse.[3] The character has been noted for her appeal to players and has become a subject of fan works, particularly cosplay, with merchandise and costume replicas being sold commercially.[4] While praised for her design and fighting style, Honey has also been the subject of criticism regarding her sexualized depiction and her portrayal in game advertising, particularly in relation to her young character age.

# Conception and development

When developing the arcade game *Fighting Vipers*, the Sega AM2 development team sought to create a more visually "intense" fighting game than their previous work in the *Virtua Fighter* series.[1] The developers wanted a less bloody presentation than titles such as Midway Games' *Mortal Kombat* series, and instead implemented a distinctive armor-breaking system where characters wore segmented armor that could be damaged and removed during fights.[1] After establishing this core mechanic, the development team considered additional thematic elements such as skateboarding and guitars, features they believed would appeal to younger players.[1]

Honey was created by Masahiro Sugiyama, one of the game's two character designers.[1] Director Hiroshi Kataoka acknowledged that while Honey's design represented a departure from the character designs typically produced by AM2, the character was intentionally conceived to provide visual variety within the game's roster.[1] In the original Japanese version, she was named Honey (ハニー), though the character's age, appearance, and design made her a distinctive presence in the game's cast. Her costume, a self-made rubber dress that serves as her character armor, was designed with a focus on visual appeal and distinctive silhouette.[2]

For localized releases outside Japan, the character underwent a significant redesign. Her hair was dyed blonde, and her name was changed to Candy, creating what amounts to a regional variant of the character.[2] This localization approach was common during this era of arcade game distribution and reflected regional preferences for character presentation.

# Character profile

According to her official in-game profile, Honey is a young fashion designer and aspiring apparel designer studying to establish her own fashion line.[2] She enters the *Fighting Vipers* tournament not primarily to compete as a fighter, but rather to showcase her self-designed clothing and promote her fashion work.[2] Her character is described as typically shy and quiet in personality, but when she puts on her rubber dress armor, she adopts a more confident and assertive demeanor suited for combat.[2]

In *Fighting Vipers 2*, she is established as a trainee stylist and apparel designer, and her motivation for entering the tournament remains centered on promoting her self-made dress designs.[2] The game's profile suggests that she uses the fighting tournament as a platform to demonstrate both her combat abilities and her fashion creations simultaneously.[2] Rumors within the game's narrative suggest a possible romantic connection between Honey and another character named Sanman, though this element is presented with ambiguity.[2]

Her official statistics list her as 16 years old in the original *Fighting Vipers*, standing 158 centimeters tall and weighing 41 kilograms.[2] In *Fighting Vipers 2*, released three years later in the game's timeline, she is 18 years old and has grown to 160 centimeters in height.[2] Her personal preferences include strawberry cheesecake ice cream, and she dislikes carrots.[2] Her specialty in combat is jumping attacks, and her hobby is making clothing.[2]

# Gameplay and mechanics

In *Fighting Vipers*, Honey is characterized by a fighting style emphasizing agility and acrobatic techniques. Her moveset contains numerous jump attacks and aerial maneuvers, reflecting her character specialty.[2] Many of her moves contain "cat" in their names and exhibit cat-like characteristics, including her fighting stance and the manner in which she executes her punches.[3] Several of her techniques utilize her posterior as a weapon, a design choice that distinguished her from other characters in the roster.[3]

Like all characters in *Fighting Vipers*, Honey wears breakable armor consisting of two pieces—a chest piece and leg protection.[2] When her armor sustains enough damage from opponent attacks or environmental interaction, it breaks away, leaving her more vulnerable to damage but also allowing her to perform special high-speed attacks in an unarmored state.[3] A distinctive feature of the game mechanic involves the complete destruction of her lower armor piece under specific conditions; after winning one hundred matches with Honey in versus mode, her destroyed armor changes to reveal additional undergarments rather than armor plating.[3]

Her fighting style remains largely consistent across both *Fighting Vipers* and *Fighting Vipers 2*, though the sequel introduced additional techniques and expanded move variations available to all characters.[2]

# Appearances

### Fighting Vipers

Honey appeared as a selectable playable character in the original arcade release of *Fighting Vipers* in November 1995.[1] She was included in the 1996 Sega Saturn port, which was released as part of the system's 1996 holiday lineup and received positive critical reception.[1] The Saturn version provided her with multiple alternate costumes that were not available in earlier arcade versions, including a swimsuit outfit and a schoolgirl costume, expanding her visual options beyond the original arcade release.[3]

### Fighting Vipers 2

Honey returned as a playable character in *Fighting Vipers 2*, released in 1998 for the Sega Model 3 arcade hardware and ported to the Sega Dreamcast in 2001.[2] The Dreamcast port provided her with an additional alternate outfit styled as a nurse uniform.[3] Her character remained one of the core roster members across both arcade and home console versions of the game.[2]

### Fighters Megamix

Honey appeared as a playable character in *Fighters Megamix*, a 1996 crossover fighting game for the Sega Saturn that combined the rosters of both *Fighting Vipers* and *Virtua Fighter 2*.[4] The game included promotional materials prominently featuring her, including the image known colloquially as the "Taste of Honey" wallpaper, which showcased her in a sexualized pose.[3] This wallpaper became one of the most recognizable pieces of promotional artwork associated with the character.[3]

### Sonic the Fighters

Honey's presence in *Sonic the Fighters* originated from unused character data left in the arcade game's code by character designer Masahiro Sugiyama.[5] The inclusion occurred because *Sonic the Fighters* was initially designed by placing Sonic franchise characters into the *Fighting Vipers* engine, and Sugiyama appears to have inserted Honey's data as a reference to the original game.[5] The character model in *Sonic the Fighters* featured modifications that gave her feline characteristics, including cat ears, establishing her as "Honey the Cat."[5]

Honey the Cat's data remained unused and undiscovered in the arcade version until summer 2005, when Sonic CulT forum user sonicblur unearthed the character's code.[5] Subsequent research and reverse engineering by the emulation community revealed her complete moveset and character model.[5] By 2007, it became publicly known that the model rendering in early emulated versions had omitted her distinctive pigtails due to timing issues in the Model 2 emulator, a fact that had become established in fan artwork by that time.[5]

The character became recognized as a cult favorite within the Sonic fan community, with fan art appearing on community sites such as DeviantArt and eventually spreading across the internet.[5] This unexpected popularity prompted Sega's porting team to investigate the character when preparing the 2012 digital re-release of *Sonic the Fighters* for Xbox Live Arcade and PlayStation Network.[5] After discovering substantial fan art and community interest in the character, Sega officially restored Honey the Cat as a fully playable character with proper graphics, animations, and VS. screen artwork in the 2012 re-release.[5]

# Reception and legacy

Honey was well received upon the original release of *Fighting Vipers* and has maintained her status as one of the most recognizable characters from the series in subsequent years.[1] The character is consistently identified as the ensemble dark horse of the *Fighting Vipers* franchise, achieving greater recognition among players and in popular culture than the game's protagonist, Bahn.[3]

The character's sex appeal was explicitly recognized as a factor in the arcade game's commercial performance and popularity, particularly in attracting players to arcade cabinets during the mid-1990s.[4] Her visual design and promotional imagery were noted as relatively bold for a 1990s Sega arcade game, preceding and potentially influencing the later emphasis on sexualized female characters in fighting games such as the *Dead or Alive* series.[4]

Honey has been a frequent subject of fan works and cosplay across multiple decades. Commercial retailers have sold costume replicas of her Fighting Vipers outfit, and fan artists have created extensive artwork featuring the character.[4] Her appearance in *Sonic the Fighters* code and the subsequent fan discovery and restoration of that version significantly expanded her recognition beyond fighting game communities into broader gaming fandom.

# Criticism

The character's young age—16 years old in the original game—combined with her sexualized appearance and portrayal in game advertising generated criticism from some observers regarding the appropriateness of the character's design.[1] These concerns contributed to broader discussions about the sexualization of female characters in video games, particularly those depicted as minors or young teenagers.[1] The character's design choices, including the breakable armor mechanic that leaves her in minimal clothing, became part of conversations about fanservice in 1990s arcade games.[3]

Despite these criticisms, Honey has remained a popular character in fan communities and gaming history discussions, with her role in the development of fighting game character design being recognized by gaming historians and critics.[4]

# Diego García Miravete

**Diego Leonardo García Miravete** is a Mexican former gridiron football coach and player. He is widely recognized as one of the most successful Mexican college football coaches, best known for his 18-year tenure as head coach of the Cóndores UNAM from 1976 to 1993. During this period, he led the team to nine national championships, tying him with Roberto "Tapatío" Méndez for the most national titles in Mexican American football history. García Miravete also coached the Auténticos Tigres UANL, the Borregos Salvajes Toluca, and the Borregos Salvajes Santa Fe in Mexico, and held coaching positions with the Cardinals Palermo and Corsari Palermo in Italy as well as the Badalona Drags in Spain. He served as a coach for the Mexico junior national team in international competitions and was inducted into the Mexican Hall of Fame of American Football in 2017. García Miravete played college football as a linebacker for the Pumas UNAM and Cóndores UNAM from 1966 to 1971, winning three national championships during his time as a player.[1]

## Early life and playing career

Diego Leonardo García Miravete was born in Mexico City. He was introduced to American football from an early age through his father, who played the sport as a guard. As a seven-year-old in 1952, García Miravete was taken by his father to the inauguration of the Estadio Olímpico Universitario, where he watched the traditional rivalry game between the Pumas UNAM and the IPN team that inspired his passion for the sport.[1]

He attended Escuela Nacional Preparatoria 5 for his high school education, where he first played organized American football at the age of 15. During his initial training session, he suffered a broken clavicle and missed the entire season. García Miravete experienced significant physical growth before the next season, increasing in height from 1.68 meters (5 feet 6 inches) to 1.82 meters (6 feet 0 inches), which allowed him to eventually become a starter on his high school team.[1]

García Miravete enrolled at the National Autonomous University of Mexico (UNAM) in the School of Engineering and joined the Escorpiones Rojos, the university's intermediate category team (for players 20 years old and under). He later transferred to the School of Accounting at UNAM. He played college football for both the Pumas UNAM and Cóndores UNAM from 1966 to 1971, accumulating three national championships during the height of American football's popularity in Mexico.[1]

## Coaching career

### Early coaching and appointment

García Miravete began his coaching career while still finishing his college playing career. Coach Manuel Neri Fernández invited him to assist with coaching in 1973 while still pursuing his studies. Following the completion of his degree, García Miravete was considered for the head coaching position at Cóndores UNAM. In 1975, he was officially named head coach, becoming the youngest coach in the ONEFA circuit at age 26.[1]

### Cóndores UNAM era (1976–1993)

Under García Miravete's leadership, the Cóndores UNAM became one of the most dominant teams in Mexican college football history. The team won nine national championships during his 18-year tenure: in 1978, 1979, 1980, 1983, 1984, 1985, 1986, 1990, and 1991. Notably, the team achieved four consecutive championships from 1983 to 1986, making them the first team in the history of the Organización Nacional Estudiantil de Fútbol Americano (ONEFA) to accomplish this feat. García Miravete's approach to coaching was distinctive; he trained his teams using business management principles, treating the organization and development of his players as he would manage a company.[2][3][1]

The championship victories included dominant performances against traditional rivals. In the 1984 championship game, Cóndores UNAM defeated Águilas Blancas IPN 40–29 with a perfect 8–0 regular season record. In 1985, they shut out Pieles Rojas IPN 31–0, also with an undefeated 8–0 regular season record. The 1991 championship marked one of his most impressive victories, with Cóndores UNAM finishing 11–0 and defeating Borregos Salvajes ITESM 41–16.[2][1]

When García Miravete was replaced as head coach in 1994, he left behind a legacy of consistent excellence. The nine national championships he won with Cóndores UNAM tied him with Roberto "Tapatío" Méndez for the most national titles by any coach in Mexican American football history.[1]

### Later coaching positions

Following his departure from Cóndores UNAM, García Miravete coached the Auténticos Tigres UANL of the Universidad Autónoma de Nuevo León. He later became the head coach of the Borregos Salvajes Toluca from 2000 to 2005. Despite having considerable success with the team and consistently reaching the semifinals, Borregos Toluca was unable to capture a national championship during his tenure. He also served as head coach of the Borregos Salvajes Santa Fe.[1]

García Miravete's international coaching experience included stints with Italian teams. He coached the Cardinals Palermo and Corsari Palermo, professional or semi-professional American football teams based in Palermo, Italy. He also coached the Badalona Drags in Spain during a period when the team was competing in international American football leagues.[1]

Beyond club-level coaching, García Miravete served as a coach for the Mexico junior national team in international American football competitions.[1]

## Legacy and honors

García Miravete was inducted into the Mexican Hall of Fame of American Football in 2017, recognizing his exceptional contributions to the sport in Mexico both as a player and coach. His nine national championships with Cóndores UNAM remain tied for the most won by any single coach in Mexican college American football history. The team's four consecutive championships from 1983 to 1986 established a standard of excellence in the ONEFA that stood until 2007, when Borregos Salvajes Monterrey became the second team to achieve four consecutive titles.[1]

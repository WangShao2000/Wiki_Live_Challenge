# Grupo Frontera Political Controversy

The **Grupo Frontera political controversy** was a dispute that emerged in early 2025 involving the American regional Mexican band Grupo Frontera, centered on allegations that the group or its associates supported the 2024 U.S. presidential campaign of Donald Trump. The controversy was triggered by a viral video featuring a family member of one of the band's members dancing to "Y.M.C.A." by the Village People, a song frequently used at Trump political events. Despite the band's repeated statements denying any political endorsement, fans on social media interpreted the video and related content as evidence of Trump support, leading to widespread calls for boycotts and cancellation. The controversy highlighted tensions within the Latino music community regarding political expression and prompted debates about the responsibility of cultural figures on social issues affecting immigrant communities.[1][2][3]

# Background and Initial Interview

On January 5, 2025, the Spanish newspaper *El País* published an interview with Grupo Frontera conducted by Raúl Novoa in Madrid. During the discussion about the band's professional achievements and experiences, interviewer Novoa asked about Donald Trump's re-election as U.S. president. Juan Javier Cantú responded, stating that the group "rarely gets involved in politics. It's very controversial for us and we just want to make music." Both Cantú and bass player Alberto "Beto" Acosta highlighted the band's donation of toys to children at a community center for immigrants who had been deported due to border enforcement policies. Regarding the Mexico–United States barrier, Acosta expressed the band's disagreement with the wall's construction, while member Julián Peña Jr. commented on its incomplete status.[3][1]

# The Viral Video and Initial Backlash

In late January 2025, a video began circulating on social media showing "La Abuela Frontera," the grandmother of vocalist Adelaido "Payo" Solís, performing a dance resembling the choreography of "Y.M.C.A." that appeared celebratory of Trump's victory in the 2024 presidential election. Although the grandmother did not explicitly state her political preference, the video sparked significant criticism online. Mexican and Latino fans interpreted the video as evidence of the band's support for Trump, and social media users began searching for additional content allegedly supporting this interpretation.[2][1][3]

Simultaneously, fans reported discovering a previously deleted TikTok video in which band members themselves performed the same dance. Additional screenshots and social media activity allegedly showing band members following Trump or liking his Instagram posts circulated online. Considering this material as confirmation of their accusations, social media users initiated boycott campaigns, labeling the band members as "traitors" and demanding their removal from scheduled festival performances.[4][1][2][3]

# Festival Announcement and Petition

On February 4, 2025, Grupo Frontera was announced as a headliner for the Sueños music festival in Chicago. The announcement immediately prompted the creation of a Change.org petition titled "REMOVE 'GRUPO FRONTERA' FROM SUEÑOS LINEUP!" The petition accumulated over 10,400 signatures from fans seeking the band's removal from the festival lineup. The individual who initiated the petition claimed to have spent more than $800 on tickets and requested a refund, arguing that the band's perceived political shift justified his action. While acknowledging that the video itself did not explicitly mention Trump or display overt political views, the petitioner expressed concern about what he perceived as the timing's significance.[5][6]

# Band's First Response

On February 7, 2025, Grupo Frontera posted a statement on their Instagram Story attempting to address the mounting criticism. The band stated in Spanish, "We want to clarify that Grupo Frontera has no affiliation or alliance with any political party that is against immigrants and the Latino community." The statement continued: "Like many of you, our families and members have fought and struggled for a better future, and we will always take our people's side, defending our roots and values." The band also emphasized, "It's crucial for you to understand that the views of our friends and family do not reflect Grupo Frontera. We are immigrants, from the border, and Grupo Frontera will always stand with and for the people."[7][4]

Public response to the first statement was predominantly negative, with critics arguing that it was merely an attempt to preserve the band's reputation among fans rather than a genuine clarification. The response did not substantially reduce online criticism or boycott calls.[2][3]

# Escalation and Premio Lo Nuestro Incident

In mid-February 2025, during the Premio Lo Nuestro red carpet event, host Raúl de Molina from *El Gordo y la Flaca* asked the band about their position on the border crisis, emphasizing the significance of their name, which translates to "Border Group" in Spanish. Julián Peña Jr. provided a vague response that many interpreted as evasive: "We focus on music. We cherish everyone and desire peace and love. Take care of yourselves." His bandmates appeared visibly uneasy during the exchange, and social media users interpreted the evasive answer as further evidence of the band's alleged Trump support.[8][4][5]

Following this exchange, Carlos Eduardo Espina, a social media personality and immigrant rights advocate with 12.3 million TikTok followers, posted a critical TikTok video on February 21, condemning Peña Jr.'s response. Espina stated, "For me, this was one of the worst responses that I have ever seen to a question in my whole life. Just like the saying goes, there are times when not saying anything says a lot... what an embarrassment."[6][4][5]

# Second Response and Clarifications

On February 22, 2025, Grupo Frontera released a video on Instagram directly addressing the accumulating controversy and attempting more detailed explanations. Peña Jr. stated at the beginning of the video: "We want to set the record straight on many things that have been said about us. To be clear: We are migrants, and our parents are migrants. We want to express that we do not endorse any political party that is against immigrants."[4][5][8]

Alberto Acosta, the band's bajo quinto player, addressed what he characterized as "fake news" circulating online. He explained that the band had not previously addressed the rumors due to the constant nature of online speculation. Acosta stated, "We are clarifying this because people are pitting us against our raza," using the Spanish term for one's own people or community.[5][8][4]

Accordionist Juan Javier Cantú provided context for the "Y.M.C.A." dancing footage, explaining that the group had maintained a routine during their previous tour where they danced to various songs during performances. He stated, "We had a routine during our last tour where we danced huapangos or various songs. We danced to that song in elementary school, and it was unrelated to any political party at that time."[6][4][5]

Vocalist Payo Solís specifically addressed the TikTok videos posted by his grandmother, stating: "The only voices that matter from Grupo Frontera are those that come from the group itself. Anyone who is not part of us cannot speak for us or convey an opinion on our behalf." Solís emphasized the distinction between his grandmother's personal social media activity and the band's official positions.[8][4][5]

The video concluded with Peña Jr. restating the band's stance: "We are migrants. We stand with migrants. We always have. You all know what we have done and how we have supported our raza in both the U.S. and Mexico. That is what this group signifies, not the narratives that have been spread about us." However, the video did not directly address the screenshots allegedly showing band members following Trump or liking his social media posts, which continued to circulate online.[4][8]

## Other Public Responses

The controversy received attention from Emiliano Aguilar and Leonardo Aguilar, sons of Mexican-American musician Pepe Aguilar, who responded to the situation through social media. Their reactions contributed to the broader public discussion about the band's alleged political affiliations and the expectations placed on Latino cultural figures regarding social and political issues.[1][3][2]

The social media response to the band's statements remained largely critical, with users continuing to post negative comments on the band's social media accounts. Typical comments included "Make music in English now," "In Mexico we don't forgive," and "Let's stop supporting a group that is anti-Mexican." Some social media users disputed the band's explanations, arguing that the deletion of the original TikTok video suggested awareness of controversy, or that the band should have been more forthright in their political advocacy on behalf of immigrant communities.[5][6]

## Sueños Festival Performance and Outcome

Despite the ongoing controversy and the Change.org petition with over 10,400 signatures, Grupo Frontera proceeded to perform at the Sueños music festival in Chicago on May 25, 2025, as the headliner for day two of the event. According to reporting on the festival, while anticipation regarding the band's performance was high due to the prior controversy, the actual event was described as having been "blown out of proportion" by online commentary, with the performance proceeding without significant incident.[9][10][11]

## Band Membership Changes

In July 2025, bassist Brian Ortega departed from the band. Ortega, who had joined Grupo Frontera in mid-2021 and played a significant role during the band's rise to international prominence, announced on social media that he had been let go without prior notice or involvement in the decision-making process. In his statement, Ortega expressed gratitude for his time with the band while noting his surprise at the abrupt nature of his departure. He had been with the group for approximately three and a half years. The timing and circumstances of Ortega's departure were subject to speculation on social media regarding potential connections to the political controversy, though no official statement from the band addressed such speculation.[12][13]

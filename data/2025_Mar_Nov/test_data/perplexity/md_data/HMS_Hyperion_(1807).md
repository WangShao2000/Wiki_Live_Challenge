# HMS Hyperion (1807)

**HMS *Hyperion*** was a 32-gun fifth-rate frigate of the Royal Navy, launched in 1807. The ship served throughout the Napoleonic Wars and the subsequent period of peace, participating in operations ranging from Mediterranean patrols to anti-piracy duties in the Caribbean. Her career ended in 1833 when she was broken up after serving as a base for anti-smuggling operations in southern England.

### Design and construction

*Hyperion* was designed as an 18-pounder fifth-rate frigate. Frigates were three-masted, full-rigged ships that carried their main battery on a single, continuous gun deck. They were smaller and faster than ships of the line and primarily intended for raiding, reconnaissance, and messaging. During the Napoleonic Wars, the Royal Navy mostly produced frigates from three well-developed designs. The 32-gun frigate design represented an economy version that could be built by smaller shipyards.[1][2]

*Hyperion* was copied from the design of the French frigate *Magicienne*, which had been captured by the British in 1781. The ship was ordered on 13 June 1805 and had her keel laid on 2 January 1806. She was built by William Gibson at Kingston upon Hull and designed by Sir John Henslow, the Surveyor of the Navy. *Hyperion* was launched on 3 November 1807.[3][1]

### Early service and Mediterranean operations

*Hyperion* was commissioned in 1808 under the command of Captain Thomas Brodie. After fitting out at Sheerness, she departed for the Mediterranean on 30 June 1808. Brodie's command proved controversial. In one notable incident, he attempted to attack what he believed to be an enemy vessel, which turned out to be a rock, causing such concern among the ship's passengers that some abandoned the vessel over fears of his capabilities.[4][5]

In February 1811, while stationed in the West Indies, an incident at Gonaïves in Haiti resulted in Haitians killing three members of *Hyperion*'s crew. Brodie himself died of an illness probably contracted at Gonaïves in 1811.[6][7]

### Under Cumby's command

*Hyperion* was placed under the command of Captain William Pryce Cumby, who had gained fame for his actions at the Battle of Trafalgar. Under Cumby's command from 1811 to 1815, the frigate served on the Newfoundland Station on fishery protection duties in 1812. In 1813, she was engaged in Atlantic convoy duties, during which her charge of 37 merchant vessels was dispersed in severe Atlantic storms.[8][9]

In the English Channel and Bay of Biscay in 1814, *Hyperion* captured the American privateer *Rattlesnake* on 3 June 1814, after that vessel had burned several British merchant ships.[10]

### Post-war service

Following the conclusion of the Napoleonic Wars, *Hyperion* was recommissioned and briefly served off Portsmouth as an escort to George, Prince Regent. She was then transferred to the South America Station in 1820 to protect British trade interests during the Chilean War of Independence and the related conflicts affecting the region. *Hyperion* was present at Callao in Peru on 5 November 1820, when the Spanish frigate *Esmeralda* was captured by Chilean forces under Lord Cochrane in a celebrated cutting-out operation.[11][12]

*Hyperion*'s captain, Thomas Searle, developed such poor relations with Cochrane and the Chilean naval command that the frigate was soon ordered back to Britain. Searle's ship had returned from South America in 1821 carrying specie valued at approximately £500,000 sterling.[13][14]

Under the command of Captain James Lillicrap, *Hyperion* joined the Cape of Good Hope Station in 1821, subsequently moving to combat piracy off Cuba in 1822. The ship returned to Britain in 1824.[15]

### Final years and decommissioning

From 1825 to 1831, *Hyperion* served as a base for anti-smuggling operations at Newhaven in Sussex, operating as part of the Coast Blockade—a Royal Navy initiative to suppress smuggling along the British coastline. The ship was broken up in June 1833.[16][1]

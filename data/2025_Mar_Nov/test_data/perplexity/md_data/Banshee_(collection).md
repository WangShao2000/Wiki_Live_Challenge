# Banshee (collection)

**Banshee** (Irish: *Bheansidhe*) is the fourth collection by British fashion designer Alexander McQueen, released for the Autumn/Winter 1994 season of his eponymous fashion house. McQueen was inspired by Irish folklore concerning banshees—female spirits whose mournful wailing is said to herald death—and sought to present women as resilient survivors of tragic circumstances. The collection combined this concept with historical references to shipwrecks and maintained the dark romanticism of McQueen's aesthetic. Like his preceding collection, *Nihilism*, *Banshee* featured provocative styling, experimental techniques, and innovative silhouettes that would become hallmarks of McQueen's early work.[1]

### Design and Construction

Although McQueen had been trained in traditional Savile Row tailoring and the collection featured classically tailored garments, he deliberately departed from convention with unconventional shapes and silhouettes. The collection emphasized sexuality through sheer fabrics and body-exposing garments, including the return of McQueen's signature bumster trousers, whose extremely low waist exposed the top of the intergluteal cleft. Even knitwear was rendered provocative, with the insertion of sheer panels that deliberately exposed the breasts.[1]

The collection incorporated historical references including frock coats, naval dress jackets, and Elizabethan necklines. Many garments were deliberately distressed for aesthetic effect. One of the collection's most notable pieces was a moulded bodice constructed from plaster of Paris on a chicken wire frame, which served as a showpiece look and later attracted scholarly analysis regarding its conceptual implications.[1]

### Runway Show

The runway show was staged on 26 February 1994 at the Café de Paris nightclub during London Fashion Week. As with his previous collection, the show's production was deliberately unconventional and amateurish; all contributors worked without payment, and no pre-show fittings took place before the presentation. Models were styled aggressively, with the McQueen brand logo stencilled onto their hair, and they were instructed to act provocatively on the runway. The musical accompaniment began with Celtic pipe music, which transitioned into heavy electronic dance music as the show progressed.[1]

### Production and Collaborators

Due to limited financial resources, production of the collection was funded through unconventional means. McQueen financed the collection using his welfare cheques supplemented by cash provided by Isabella Blow's husband, Detmar Blow. McQueen was assisted by his then-boyfriend Andrew Groves and his roommate Simon Ungless, a friend from Central Saint Martins College of Art and Design (CSM) who had collaborated with McQueen on previous collections. Ungless developed the prints for the collection, while stylist Katy England contributed to the overall visual presentation.[1]

One notable model, Rebecca Lowthorpe, wore the plaster bodice; due to its weight and rigid construction, she was forced to lie down backstage under a rail of clothes to prevent being accidentally stepped on by other people moving through the backstage area.[1]

### Reception

Although few mainstream journalists attended the show, the collection received positive reviews from industry guests and fashion insiders. Reviewers highlighted the novelty of McQueen's design ideas and his exceptional tailoring skills. The collection was subsequently re-staged for New York Fashion Week, where it was received warmly by the fashion community.[1]

### Legacy and Exhibition

Two pieces from the *Banshee* collection appeared in the retrospective exhibition *Alexander McQueen: Savage Beauty*, which originally opened in 2011 at the Metropolitan Museum of Art in New York City and was later presented at the Victoria and Albert Museum in London. The first piece, loaned by friend and muse Daphne Guinness, was a jacket made of black wool and silver silk with gold military braid embroidery. The second piece, loaned by early collaborator Ruti Danan, was a gray wool jacket, also embroidered with gold military braid. The collection has attracted scholarly analysis regarding both the novelty of McQueen's conceptual approach and the symbolic significance of the distinctive plaster bodice.[1]

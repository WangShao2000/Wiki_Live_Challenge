# 2025 World Seniors Championship

The **2025 World Seniors Championship** (officially the **2025 JenningsBet World Seniors Championship**) was a snooker tournament held from 7 to 11 May 2025 at the Crucible Theatre in Sheffield, England. It marked the sixteenth edition of the World Seniors Championship, which was first held in 1991. The event was the seventh consecutive year the tournament was hosted at the Crucible. Organized by the World Seniors Tour, the championship was broadcast domestically on Channel 5 in the United Kingdom and internationally through other broadcasters.[1]

The tournament was open to amateur players aged 40 and over and professional players aged 45 and above who were ranked outside the top 64 in the snooker world rankings. Alfie Burden won his first World Seniors title, defeating Aaron Canavan 8–4 in the final to claim the championship. Igor Figueiredo, the defending champion, was eliminated in the first round, losing 2–4 to Wayne Townsend. The tournament featured several notable retirements from professional snooker, including the final professional appearance of Joe Perry and Dominic Dale's transition from professional to seniors competition. Michaela Tabb's final appearance as a traditional snooker referee occurred at the championship final, concluding her 24-year refereeing career.[2][3][4][5][6][1]

## Overview

The 2025 World Seniors Championship maintained its status as an invitational event at snooker's most iconic venue, the Crucible Theatre in Sheffield. Sponsored by bookmaker JenningsBet, it was the championship's tenth edition with sponsorship. The tournament featured sixteen players competing in a single-elimination format with modified frame structures.[7][1]

## Format

In contrast to previous years, the 2025 edition introduced extended match formats. The first and second rounds were contested as best-of-seven frames, while the quarter-finals remained best-of-seven frames. Semi-finals were expanded to best-of-eleven frames, and the final was contested over best-of-fifteen frames, up from the previous best-of-nine format.[7]

## Participants

The field included several former World Snooker Champions and decorated professionals. Defending champion Igor Figueiredo from Brazil was seeded as the top seed and began proceedings on the opening Wednesday afternoon. Jimmy White, a record four-time World Seniors Champion, was among the invited players and entered on Thursday afternoon. Ken Doherty, a two-time runner-up at the championship, also competed.[8]

Three continental champions qualified through their respective regional championships: Craig Steadman (European Seniors Champion), Hassan Kerde (Asia-Pacific Seniors Champion), Charl Jonck (African Seniors Champion), and Fabio Anderson Luersen (Pan-American Seniors Champion).[8]

The championship saw the debuts of several notable players. Joe Perry and Dominic Dale, both of whom announced retirement from professional snooker, participated as debutants at the World Seniors level. Wayne Townsend also made his World Seniors Championship debut as a qualifier through the Race to the Crucible series. The tournament did not feature any female competitors, unlike some previous editions of the championship.[4][5][7][8]

Aaron Canavan qualified through the Race to the Crucible series top seeding and sought to add a second world seniors title to his 2018 championship victory. Tony Knowles and Tony Drago completed the field as invited legendary players.[8]

## Tournament Progress

### First Round

The opening round produced several notable upsets. Defending champion Igor Figueiredo lost his first-round match to Wayne Townsend, exiting 2–4. Alfie Burden defeated four-time World Seniors Champion Jimmy White 4–1 in what was a rematch of the 2023 World Seniors final, which White had won. This victory demonstrated Burden's determination, as he had lost that previous final and sought redemption at the championship.[9][1]

Dominic Dale, in his World Seniors debut, defeated Craig Steadman 4–2. Hassan Kerde blanked Tony Knowles 4–0 in commanding fashion, earning praise as the first Australian player to reach the World Seniors Championship semi-finals. Aaron Canavan defeated Andrew Norman 4–1. Charl Jonck defeated Ken Doherty 4–2, and Tony Drago overcame Gerard Greene 4–3. Joe Perry dispatched Fabio Anderson Luersen 4–2.[10][1]

### Quarter-Finals

The quarter-final stage on 9 May produced dramatic matches. Dominic Dale advanced with a 4–1 victory over Joe Perry. Alfie Burden recovered from a 3–0 deficit against Tony Drago to win 4–3 in a match featuring two respotted blacks, including one that Drago won to move 3–0 ahead. Hassan Kerde defeated Charl Jonck 4–2, with Kerde recording a break of 85. Aaron Canavan advanced past Wayne Townsend 4–2.[1][10]

### Semi-Finals

The semi-final stage on 10 May saw the field narrowed to two finalists. Alfie Burden faced Hassan Kerde in a match that Burden dominated 6–2, with Burden posting breaks of 101 and 65. In the other semi-final, Aaron Canavan narrowly defeated Dominic Dale 6–5 in a closely contested match. Dominic Dale recorded a break of 51 during this semi-final, but Canavan ultimately prevailed.[2][10]

### Final

The championship final took place on 11 May 2025. Alfie Burden claimed his first World Seniors title with an 8–4 victory over Aaron Canavan. After losing the opening frame, Burden surged ahead with four consecutive frames to lead 4–1 at the mid-session interval. Burden extended his advantage to 7–2 with breaks of 109 and 81, demonstrating commanding control. Canavan mounted a brief comeback, winning two frames to reach 7–4, but Burden held firm to secure the title with a final break of 76. Despite acknowledging nervousness at 7–3 ahead, Burden recovered to win the final frame decisively.[11][1][2]

## Notable Performances

The tournament produced five century breaks, four of which were compiled by Dominic Dale. In his semi-final match against Canavan, Dale recorded the tournament's highest break of 131, marking a significant achievement in his final appearances before retiring from professional snooker. The breaks by Dale included efforts of 128, 99, and 97 across the tournament.[12][1]

Alfie Burden recorded several notable breaks during his championship run, including contributions of 109, 81, 75, and 76 in the final. Hassan Kerde posted a break of 85 during his quarter-final victory over Jonck. Aaron Canavan contributed a break of 75 during the final. Joe Perry recorded the highest break among non-finalists with 128 during his quarter-final loss to Dale.[10][11][12]

## Refereeing

The final marked the conclusion of Michaela Tabb's 24-year career as a traditional snooker referee. After refereeing the first session of the 2025 World Seniors Championship final, Tabb retired from standard snooker events while continuing with other refereeing duties. Tabb had previously refereed two World Championship finals during her professional career.[6][1]

## History and Context

The World Seniors Championship began in 1991 as an invitational event at Trentham Gardens in Stoke-on-Trent, won by Cliff Wilson. The tournament was revived in 2010 and relocated to various venues before becoming a permanent fixture at the Crucible Theatre from 2019 onwards. The 2025 edition represented the seventh consecutive year the championship was held at the Sheffield venue.[13][1]

The World Seniors Tour, which organizes the championship, was founded in 2017 and began formal operations in 2018, providing competitive opportunities for senior snooker players. The tournament holds significance within the snooker calendar as winners automatically qualify for the Champion of Champions event.[14]

## Prize Fund and Winner

At 48 years old, Alfie Burden became the first World Seniors Champion since Igor Figueiredo to claim the title. Burden had previously finished runner-up in 2023, losing to Jimmy White 3–5 in that final. His 2025 victory marked his first triumph at the championship despite competing for years at the professional level. Burden received recognition from fellow competitors, with both Jimmy White and Ken Doherty sending him congratulatory messages following his victory.[15][9][11]

## Gameplay


In Kururin Squash!, players control Kururin as he pilots the Helirin, a stick-shaped helicopter with a constantly spinning rotor. The core mechanic involves navigating through maze-like stages while avoiding obstacles and walls. Players can adjust both the rotation speed of the Helirin and its movement speed by holding buttons on the GameCube controller. Colliding with obstacles causes Kururin to lose a heart from his default supply of three. Each collision also adds three seconds to the in-game timer, with the goal being to complete stages quickly for higher scores. Checkpoints throughout levels allow heart regeneration. Players can select either a larger or more compact Helirin design to adjust difficulty levels.\[1]


The game introduces a currency system based on coins scattered throughout each stage. Collecting all coins in a level without taking damage awards a perfect bonus and increases the player's total coin count. Coins can be spent in shops run by members of the Magic Group, characters from the previous game Kururin Paradise, to purchase helpful items and accessories including extra hearts, respawning options, and minimaps. Shops also offer instructional movies demonstrating how to complete specific levels.\[1]


Kururin Squash! features five distinct helicopter variants, each with unique mechanics suited to different stage types. The Helibokaan functions as a standard helicopter with extendable gloves for punching obstacles and enemies. The Helibashan can dive underwater to navigate aquatic sections and avoid surface-level hazards. The Heliboboh shoots fire in multiple directions for destroying obstacles and enemies. The Heliburuun can generate whirlwinds to move objects and protect against hazards. The Helibibyuun fires projectiles in rapid succession.\[1]


The single-player campaign spans multiple worlds with distinct visual themes and progressively challenging stages. Boss encounters appear at the end of stage groupings and incorporate mechanics specific to each helicopter variant. After completing the Adventure mode, players unlock Challenge mode, where they race to complete stages against a time limit. Completing Challenge mode unlocks a third mode featuring the Helirin's core sphere without rotating blades, which must constantly move. Completing this mode unlocks Extra mode, featuring rotated versions of previously completed stages.\[1]


Two multiplayer modes support up to four players. Race Mode features over forty obstacle courses where players compete to reach the goal first while attempting to hit opponents into walls or block their progress. Battle Mode takes place on ten unique stages where players fight to collect the most coins using power-ups and weapons similar to those in Super Monkey Ball. By connecting a GameCube to a Game Boy Advance using a link cable, up to four GBA systems can play a spot-the-difference quiz game purchasable from the shop.\[1]


## Plot


The story begins when Kururin's family wins a lottery and embarks on a cruise across four continents. Upon returning home to Kururin Village, only Kururin's parents arrive; the other family members have gone missing. Guided by Teacher Hare, Kururin travels across the four continents piloting specialized Helirins to rescue his siblings. Throughout his journey, Kururin faces off against Professor Isogashima, a mad inventor bird, and his mechanical creations called Isogashimachines. After rescuing his siblings, Kururin discovers his parents have also been kidnapped by Professor Isogashima, prompting him to venture into space for the fifth world. Upon Professor Isogashima's final defeat, he crash-lands on the home planet and dedicates himself to repairing the damage caused by his machines, allowing the Kururin family to reunite.\[1]


## Development


Eighting developed Kururin Squash! as the first game in the Kururin series to incorporate 3D computer graphics instead of the 2D sprites used in previous entries. The directors and producers from Kururin Paradise returned to oversee development of this final series installment. Yōichi Kotabe served as the game's character designer, continuing his role from earlier Kururin titles. This was Kotabe's final contribution to the Kururin series and marked his last character design work at Nintendo before his departure from the company in 2007.\[1]


The game was officially revealed in an issue of Famitsu magazine on August 5, 2004, and released on October 14, 2004. Nintendo employed a modest marketing campaign that included publication in Nintendo Dream magazine and television commercials featuring the Helirin as a prominent prop. The relatively low price of 3,800 yen was intended to encourage a broad audience to purchase the game.\[1]


## Reception


Kururin Squash! received generally positive reviews from critics. The game earned praise for its 3D visuals, music, improved gameplay mechanics, and control scheme compared to its predecessors. Critics highlighted the colorful 3D environments and distinctive art style featuring stylized cutscenes made with cardboard puppets on sticks as visually refreshing and charming.\[1]


Ben Kosmina of Nintendo World Report described the overall experience as stellar, commending the game's low language barrier despite its Japan-exclusive release. Nadia Grace Mullings of Game Rant stated the game was worthy of being numbered among the best GameCube titles despite never releasing in North America. Anoop Gantayat of IGN expressed interest in seeing an international release due to the addictive gameplay, noting that no plans existed for a North American launch by Nintendo of America. Edge magazine praised the game's low price point as enticing.\[1]


The primary criticism concerned the game's difficulty and length. In comparison to the more challenging Game Boy Advance titles, Kururin Squash! featured generally easier levels that could be completed more quickly due to improved movement accuracy resulting from the transition from a directional pad to the GameCube's control stick.\[1]


## Sales


Kururin Squash! reached the eighteenth position on Japan's sales charts during its launch week, selling 5,745 copies. The game appeared on the top thirty weekly video game sales list only once more in the following week. Between its October 14 release and December 20, 2004, Kururin Squash! sold a total of 18,562 copies in Japan. This made it the lowest-selling game in the Kururin series and Nintendo's second worst-performing GameCube game in the Japanese market.\[1]


## Legacy


Kururin Squash! is considered one of the best Japanese-exclusive GameCube games despite its poor commercial performance. The game's failure to reach a wider international audience was attributed to Nintendo's decision to limit the release to Japan, continuing the pattern of the two Game Boy Advance predecessors remaining region-exclusive. The Helirin appeared as an Assist Trophy in the fighting game Super Smash Bros. Brawl, representing the series in the Super Smash Bros. franchise.\[1]

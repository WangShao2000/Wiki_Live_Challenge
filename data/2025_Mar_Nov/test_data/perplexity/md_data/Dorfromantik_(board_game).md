# Dorfromantik (board game)

**Dorfromantik: The Board Game** (German: *Dorfromantik: Das Brettspiel*) is a cooperative board game released in February 2023 by Pegasus Spiele, designed by Lukas Zach and Michael Palm. The game is based on the 2022 video game of the same name developed by Toukana Interactive. Dorfromantik is a tile-placement game in which up to six players work together to build a rural landscape composed of hexagonal tiles representing forests, villages, wheat fields, rivers, and railroads.[1] Players complete tasks assigned by virtual residents to earn points, with the goal of achieving the highest possible score through cooperation.[2] The game features a campaign mode that progressively unlocks sealed boxes containing new tiles and objectives as players accumulate points across multiple games.[1]

Dorfromantik received widespread critical acclaim and won the Spiel des Jahres award in 2023, one of the most prestigious board game awards in the world.[3] The game has been praised for its peaceful gameplay and design that removes stress from everyday life.[3] Since its initial release, Dorfromantik has expanded into a series of standalone titles, including a competitive two-player variant called *Dorfromantik: The Duel* (2024) and a portable travel edition called *Dorfromantik: Light Luggage* (2025), demonstrating the game's popularity and sustained commercial success.[1][4]

# Background and development

The original *Dorfromantik* video game was developed by Toukana Interactive, a German indie game studio founded in 2020 as part of a master's degree program at the Berlin University of Applied Sciences (HTW Berlin).[1] The video game was designed by four German and Swiss students—Timo Falcke, Sandro Heuberger, Luca Langenberg, and Zwi Zausch—who created the game as their final project with the intention of developing a peaceful, minimalist experience.[5] The video game entered early access on March 25, 2021, and received substantial positive attention, winning Best Game Design and Best Debut awards at the Deutscher Computerspielpreis (German Computer Games Awards) in 2021, as well as a nomination for Best Student Game at the Independent Games Festival Awards.[5]

The board game adaptation originated when designer Lukas Zach, who worked at Pegasus Spiele since at least 2012, noticed the video game's success at the Deutsche Computerspielepreis.[1] Zach and his longtime collaborator Michael Palm, with whom he had published their first game together titled *Die Kutschfahrt zur Teufelsburg* in the early 2000s, recognized the video game as similar to a hexagonal tile-placement concept they had been developing for several years.[1] Following this discovery, Zach and Palm initiated communication with Toukana Interactive to establish cooperation for a board game version.[1] The two design teams began developing a prototype through collaborative playtesting and first met at Essen SPIEL, Europe's largest board game convention.[1] The finished board game was released by Pegasus Spiele in February 2023 at Essen SPIEL, with illustrations by Paul Riebe.[1][2]

# Gameplay

Dorfromantik is played by placing hexagonal tiles to expand a growing landscape. On a player's turn, they reveal either one landscape tile or one task tile and place it according to specific rules.[2] Landscape tiles feature combinations of five terrain types: forests, villages, wheat fields, rivers, and railroads.[1] When a task tile is drawn, it must be accompanied by a corresponding task marker and placed in the play area, with the game maintaining exactly three active task tiles at all times.[1]

Placement rules require that each new tile must connect to at least one edge of an existing tile, with two primary restrictions: tiles cannot cause rivers or railroads to terminate abruptly at a tile's edge; instead, water paths and rail lines must continue uninterrupted across adjacent tiles.[2] This means rivers must connect to other river edges, and railroads must connect to other railroad edges, though players may begin multiple separate, non-contiguous rivers or railroads within their landscape.[1]

When players complete a task—by creating a contiguous region of the required terrain type with the number of tiles specified—they score points and draw a replacement task tile to maintain three active tasks.[1] At game's end, which occurs when the task tile pile is exhausted, players calculate their total score from completed tasks, the length of the longest river, the length of the longest railroad, and bonus points from flags placed in closed areas.[1] The game emphasizes collective cooperation, as players work together with no competitive element or losing condition in the basic game; all players succeed or fail as a unified group.[1][3]

### Campaign system

A distinguishing feature of Dorfromantik is its campaign mode, which encourages repeated plays across multiple sessions. The campaign utilizes a scoring track where players record their cumulative points from each game played.[1] As players surpass designated score thresholds on this track, they unlock five sealed boxes hidden within the base game, each containing new landscape tiles with additional terrain types, new task types, and alternative objectives that increase gameplay complexity and challenge.[1] This progression system provides players with ongoing motivation to improve their performance across subsequent games while gradually introducing fresh strategic elements and increased scoring possibilities.[1]

The campaign's design philosophy eliminates frustration by maintaining that players cannot "lose" in any meaningful way; even lower-scoring games advance the campaign and unlock new content, ensuring forward progress regardless of performance.[3] This approach creates what reviewers have described as a "feel-good" gaming experience where relaxation and gradual improvement coexist rather than competition and failure.[3]

# Gameplay variants and expansions

### The Duel

Following the success of the original game, Pegasus Spiele released *Dorfromantik: The Duel* in February 2024.[1] This variant transforms the game into a competitive experience for two players or two teams, where each player maintains their own landscape and competes to achieve the highest score.[2] While retaining the core tile-placement mechanics of the original, *The Duel* introduces a tile-matching system: one player draws a tile, and the other player then finds a matching tile from the tile pile, after which both players place their respective tiles in their own play areas.[1] This modification eliminates most aspects of luck inherent in drawing random tiles and creates a perfectly symmetrical game state for both competitors.[1]

The Duel also introduces two new task types not present in the base game: double assignments and all-around assignments, which can be combined with the original game for added variety.[2] Additionally, two independently playable modules are included to increase strategic depth and player interaction, and four special tiles can be integrated into the original cooperative *Dorfromantik: The Board Game* for those who own both versions.[2]

### Sakura Edition

In addition to The Duel, Pegasus Spiele released the *Dorfromantik: Sakura Edition*, a themed variant that maintains the same core gameplay mechanics while featuring visual reskins and new tiles inspired by Japanese aesthetics. The Sakura Edition replaces wheat fields with rice fields displayed in green rather than the traditional yellow and includes additional thematic changes while remaining mechanically comparable to the original game.[5] The rulebook explicitly indicates that gameplay and mechanics are identical between the standard and Sakura versions, making it a reskin with modest new content additions rather than a traditional expansion.[5]

### Light Luggage

In November 2025, Pegasus Spiele released *Dorfromantik: Light Luggage*, a standalone portable edition designed for travel and compact gaming spaces.[4] This edition features a magnetic box roughly one-quarter the size of the original game's package, with smaller tiles requiring minimal table space, making it suitable for journeys, picnics, and quick gaming sessions during breaks.[4] Rather than progressing through a traditional campaign map, Light Luggage players track high scores, with each score threshold unlocking new components and challenges for subsequent games.[4] The compact edition includes a distinct standalone campaign with fresh gameplay elements and a secret envelope containing hidden content to be revealed as the campaign progresses.[4]

# Releases and reception

### Awards

*Dorfromantik: The Board Game* won the Spiel des Jahres in 2023, one of the most prestigious board game awards internationally.[3] The Spiel des Jahres jury selected the game as the year's best, with jury chairman Harald Schrapers explaining that the game "takes the pressure out of everyday life" as a cooperative experience where players "can never lose" while facing progressively more challenging objectives with each playthrough.[3] The award beat competing nominations including *Fun Facts* by Kasper Lapp (Repos Productions) and *Next Station London* by Matthew Dunstan (HCM Kinzel).[3]

### Critical response

The game has received positive reviews from board game critics and publications. Reviewers have highlighted the game's accessible ruleset, peaceful aesthetic, and strong replay value driven by the campaign progression system.[2][6] Critics noted that gameplay sessions typically last between 20 to 60 minutes depending on player count and deliberation style.[2] The game's cooperative nature without loss conditions has been described as creating a uniquely relaxing experience compared to other tile-placement games with competitive elements, with some reviewers comparing its design favorably to cooperative variants of games like Carcassonne while emphasizing Dorfromantik's stress-free atmosphere.[6]

# Game components and specifications

The base game of Dorfromantik accommodates one to six players aged eight and older.[1][2] Standard gameplay sessions typically last between 30 and 60 minutes, though games played with more deliberate strategic planning may extend longer, and solo games often complete in 20 to 25 minutes.[6] The game's complexity has been rated as very low (approximately 1.6 on a 5-point scale), making it accessible to players of all experience levels.[2]

The game features five locked boxes within the base package that progressively unlock as players advance through the campaign, each containing additional tiles, task types, and objectives that expand gameplay possibilities.[1] The visual design and illustrations were created by Paul Riebe, contributing to the game's appealing aesthetic presentation.[1][4]

# The Reality War

**The Reality War** is the eighth and final episode of the fifteenth series of the British science-fiction television series *Doctor Who*. Written by Russell T Davies and directed by Alex Sanjiv Pillai, it is the second of a two-part story alongside the preceding episode, "Wish World". The episode was released simultaneously on BBC iPlayer, BBC One, and Disney+ on 31 May 2025. It was also given a limited theatrical release across the United Kingdom and Ireland.

The episode marks the end of Ncuti Gatwa's tenure as the Fifteenth Doctor. The story concludes with the Doctor sacrificing himself to save Poppy, a child who exists in an altered timeline, resulting in the Doctor's regeneration into a new incarnation played by Billie Piper. The episode also features cameo appearances from previous cast members and the return of the villain Omega, a renegade Time Lord and one of the originators of time travel.

# Production

The episode was written by Russell T Davies, the showrunner for the fifteenth series (known as Season Two following Disney's international acquisition of broadcasting rights). It was directed by Alex Sanjiv Pillai, who has also directed other episodes of the current series. The episode has a runtime of one hour and six minutes, making it the longest *Doctor Who* episode since "The Power of the Doctor" in 2022. Production filming took place in 2024, with some re-shoots reportedly extending into 2025, which caused delays to the episode's editing.

The two-part finale required a simultaneous release strategy. "Wish World" premiered on 24 May 2025 on BBC iPlayer, while "The Reality War" aired on 31 May 2025. To maintain secrecy surrounding the episode's ending, the BBC opted not to provide critics with advance screenings. The episode was broadcast at 6:50 PM GMT on BBC One, earlier than the standard time slot. For theatrical release, cinemas across the UK and Ireland presented a double feature of both episodes beginning at 6:00 PM.

# Plot

The episode begins with the Fifteenth Doctor being rescued from a collapsing Earth by Anita Benn, a character from the preceding Christmas special "Merry Christmas, Everyone", who brings him to the Time Hotel. The Doctor learns that the Rani has deliberately put Earth into a time loop, using a wish granted by Conrad Clark through Desiderium, an infant god with the power to actualise wishes.

The antagonists of the story form what the narrative refers to as the "Unholy Trinity": the Rani, Omega, and Desiderium. The Rani, previously revealed to have been disguised as Mrs. Flood, aims to bring Omega back to the universe to recreate their shared home planet Gallifrey under her rule. Omega, the first Time Lord and creator of time travel technology, has been trapped in an antimatter dimension for millennia after being presumed dead in a supernova. The Rani plans to use this scenario to restore herself and Omega to power.

At the heart of the Doctor's internal conflict is Poppy, an infant child who exists only in the alternate reality called Wish World. In this reality, the Doctor and his companion Belinda Chandra are in a romantic relationship and have a daughter together. As the Doctor attempts to undo the Rani's manipulation and restore reality, Poppy begins to disappear because she was created as a consequence of the wish. Ruby Sunday, the Doctor's other companion, brings Poppy back to Belinda and the Doctor's attention, forcing the Doctor to make a choice between restoring the original timeline or preserving Poppy's existence.

The climax takes place at the Bone Palace, a new addition to London's skyline that was created by Wish World. Facing impossible odds, the Doctor decides to use his regeneration energy to alter the fabric of reality itself, making Poppy a permanent part of the timeline so she will not be erased when Wish World is undone. This sacrifice causes the Doctor's regeneration into a new incarnation. The episode ends with Billie Piper, who previously played Rose Tyler, appearing as the newly regenerated Doctor.

# Cast

Ncuti Gatwa stars in his final regular appearance as the Fifteenth Doctor. Varada Sethu and Millie Gibson return as his companions, Belinda Chandra and Ruby Sunday, respectively. Archie Panjabi portrays the Rani, a renegade Time Lord villain first introduced in 1985 who had not appeared on screen since 1993. Anita Dobson appears as the Rani's previous incarnation, Mrs. Flood, a mysterious elderly woman who has appeared throughout the series.

Additional cast members include Jodie Whittaker as the Thirteenth Doctor in a guest cameo, and Jonah Hauer-King as Conrad Clark, a podcast conspiracy theorist whose wish inadvertently created Wish World. Billie Piper returns to the *Doctor Who* universe, playing the newly regenerated Sixteenth Doctor in the episode's closing moments. The casting of Piper was kept secret from critics and was not revealed until the episode broadcast. The character of the Doctor played by Piper was not credited as "The Doctor" in the closing credits, leaving open questions about the nature of the character's identity.

Other cast members include Anita Benn, portrayed by Steph de Whalley, and various UNIT personnel. Jemma Redgrave reprises her role as Kate Lethbridge-Stewart from the military organization UNIT.

# Audience reception

"The Reality War" was viewed by a total of 3.44 million viewers in the UK through consolidated ratings (which include time-shifted viewing up to seven days after broadcast). The overnight viewership was 2.17 million, representing a 58.5% increase through time-shifting. This made the episode the second-highest rated episode of the fifteenth series based on overnight statistics. The theatrical releases across cinemas in the UK and Ireland were not included in these figures.

# Critical reception

The episode received mixed reviews from critics. Many praised the episode's conclusion and certain dramatic moments, particularly the resolution of Poppy's storyline and the Doctor's ultimate sacrifice. Jodie Whittaker's cameo appearance as the Thirteenth Doctor was noted by some as a treat for viewers.

However, critics were divided on other aspects. The handling of Belinda Chandra's character arc and her role in the finale drew criticism, with several reviewers feeling the character was underutilised or "wasted". The usage of the Rani and Omega as the main antagonists divided opinion, with some praising the return of these classic villains while others felt their deployment was unsatisfying. The overall plot structure and pacing of the episode were also points of contention among reviewers.

The regeneration of the Doctor into Billie Piper's character generated particular discussion. Some viewers and critics questioned whether Piper's character was the Doctor, Rose Tyler, or an entirely new incarnation, given that the character was not credited as "The Doctor" in the credits. Theories emerged about connections to Rose's previous interaction with the TARDIS's time vortex and her separation in a parallel universe, though the episode provided no definitive explanation.

Despite the mixed critical response, the episode was noted as providing emotional weight and attempting to maintain Doctor Who's tradition of endings that serve as springboards for future narratives, leaving significant questions unresolved for future seasons.

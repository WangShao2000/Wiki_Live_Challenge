# Arne Furumark

**Josef Arne Gerdt Furumark** (26 September 1903 – 8 October 1982) was a Swedish archaeologist specialising in the pottery of Mycenaean Greece and the Bronze Age Mediterranean. His three-volume work *The Mycenaean Pottery*, published between 1940 and 1941, established a systematic classification and chronology of Mycenaean ceramics that remains the standard reference in the field. A 1943 scholarly review compared his contribution to that of Humfry Payne for Corinthian pottery and John Beazley for Athenian wares. Furumark's pottery typology system, comprising types numbered 1 through 333, continues to be used by archaeologists worldwide for identifying and dating Bronze Age ceramics.

# Early Life and Education

Furumark was born in Kristiania (present-day Oslo), Norway. After an unhappy childhood, during which his family expected him to enter the paper business, he enrolled at Uppsala University in Sweden in 1925 to study the humanities. There, he became a student of the prominent archaeologist Axel W. Persson, who was professor of classical archaeology at Uppsala and had conducted important excavations at sites including Asine and Dendra in Greece.

Under Persson's guidance, Furumark focused his studies on Aegean Bronze Age ceramics. He completed his doctorate in 1939 with a dissertation titled *Studies in Aegean Decorative Art: Antecedents and Sources of the Mycenaean Ceramic Decoration*. That same year, he was appointed as an associate professor at Uppsala University, where he would establish himself as a leading expert on prehistoric Mediterranean pottery.

# Academic Career and Scholarly Work

### Mycenaean Pottery Studies

Furumark's major scholarly contribution came with his three-volume work on Mycenaean pottery published in the early 1940s. The first volume, *The Mycenaean Pottery: Analysis and Classification* (1940), presented a detailed morphological and decorative analysis of Mycenaean vessels. The second volume, *The Chronology of Mycenaean Pottery* (1941), established a chronological framework for dating the ceramics based on stylistic changes. A third publication, *The Mycenaean III C Pottery and its Relations to Cypriote Fabrics* (1944), examined the latest phases of Mycenaean pottery production and its connections to Cyprus.

The systematic organization and detailed typology presented in these works became immediately influential in Mediterranean archaeology. Furumark identified distinctive characteristics in mainland Greek (Helladic) ceramics that distinguished them from Cretan (Minoan) pottery traditions, clarifying the ceramic distinctions between these two major Bronze Age cultural spheres. His work established a chronological framework that helped scholars date archaeological contexts across the Mediterranean and Near East.

### Linear B and Writing Systems

Although initially sceptical of the proposal that the Linear B script represented Greek, Furumark later became an advocate of this revolutionary idea. When Michael Ventris deciphered Linear B between 1948 and 1952, demonstrating that the script encoded an archaic form of Greek dating to approximately 1400 to 1200 BCE, Furumark was among the first archaeologists to recognize the significance of this discovery. He corresponded with Ventris and subsequently integrated the decipherment's conclusions into his own research, using Linear B evidence to better understand Mycenaean society and material culture.

Beyond Linear B, Furumark also pursued scholarly interests in Linear A, the undeciphered syllabic script of Minoan Crete. His correspondence on Linear A research, now preserved in archival collections, reflects his broader engagement with Bronze Age writing systems and their relationship to the development of Mediterranean civilizations.

### Later Scholarly Interests

In the post-World War II period, Furumark expanded his scholarly range beyond pottery studies. He authored *Det Äldsta Italien* (The Earliest Italy), examining the archaeology and history of the Italian peninsula in antiquity. He also wrote popular works on ancient Greek history intended for general audiences, including *Redan de Gamla Grekerna* (The Ancient Greeks, 1961) and *Hellener och Barbarer* (Greeks and Barbarians, 1962), which introduced complex historical and cultural topics to Swedish readers.

# Excavations and Field Archaeology

### Sinda, Cyprus

In 1947 and 1948, Furumark conducted excavations at Sinda on Cyprus, marking the resumption of Swedish archaeological work on the island following World War II. These excavations were undertaken while Furumark was preparing a comprehensive volume on the Late Bronze Age for the Swedish Cyprus Expedition, the pioneering systematic survey conducted between 1927 and 1931. Upon recognising that published excavation reports from contemporaneous French and Cypriot investigations were unavailable, Furumark decided to conduct his own excavations to clarify unresolved problems of the latest Bronze Age phases on Cyprus.

At Sinda, Furumark identified a Late Bronze Age fortified settlement with three distinct occupation phases. The site, likely positioned along ancient copper trade routes, showed evidence of major destructions separating the three periods. The settlement's material culture included both local Cypriot pottery and imported Mycenaean wares, particularly Late Helladic IIIB and IIIC ceramics. Furumark's excavations and analysis helped establish the chronological sequence and settlement patterns of the Late Bronze Age in Cyprus, contributing significantly to understanding the interactions between the Aegean and the island during this period. The final publication of the Sinda excavations, prepared by Furumark and Charles M. Adelman, appeared in 2003.

### San Giovenale, Italy

Between 1962 and 1963, Furumark participated in excavations at San Giovenale in Etruria, Italy, conducted in collaboration with the Swedish Institute of Classical Studies at Rome. These excavations contributed to understanding the early Iron Age settlement patterns and architecture in central Italy. Furumark's involvement extended the geographic scope of his fieldwork beyond the traditional Aegean sphere, reflecting his broad interest in Bronze and Iron Age Mediterranean archaeology.

### Rhodes

Furumark also published significant research on the Mycenaean settlement at Ialysos (Trianda) on the island of Rhodes. His 1950 publication in *Opuscula Archaeologica*, titled "The Settlement at Ialysos and Aegean History c. 1550–1400 B.C.," established the chronological framework for understanding the site's three major occupation phases spanning from the Late Minoan IA period through Late Helladic IIIA. This work remains foundational for understanding Late Bronze Age settlement and cultural development in the eastern Aegean.

# Academic Positions and Administration

Furumark was promoted to professor of classical archaeology at Uppsala University in 1952, a position he held until his retirement in 1970. During this period, he remained a central figure in Scandinavian archaeology and maintained an active publication record. From 1956 to 1957, he served as director of the Swedish Institute at Athens, a research centre for classical archaeology that coordinates Swedish archaeological work in Greece. His tenure as director occurred during an important period of expansion and reorganization for the institute.

# Scholarly Legacy and Influence

Furumark's *Mycenaean Pottery* continues to serve as a standard reference work in Bronze Age Mediterranean archaeology more than eighty years after its initial publication. Subsequent scholars have praised his methodological rigor and systematic approach to ceramic classification, while some have critiqued his methodology for adopting an overly rigid typological system and for privileging stylistic analysis at the expense of contextual and depositional information about the objects themselves.

The typology system established by Furumark—with vessel shapes designated as "FS" (Furumark Shape) types and decorative motifs as "FM" (Furumark Motif) types—has become embedded in the standard vocabulary of Aegean Bronze Age archaeology. Archaeologists continue to reference Furumark's typology when cataloguing and dating Mycenaean and related pottery from excavations throughout the Mediterranean, the Near East, and Egypt. His work fundamentally shaped how scholars understand the development, distribution, and chronology of Mycenaean material culture.

Beyond his direct contributions to pottery studies, Furumark's career exemplified the international scope of mid-twentieth-century Mediterranean archaeology. His excavations in Cyprus and Italy, his correspondence with leading scholars of the era, and his willingness to engage with new decipherments and methodologies demonstrated a commitment to understanding Bronze Age Mediterranean societies in their full complexity.

# Death and Burial

Furumark died on 8 October 1982 in Uppsala, Sweden, at the age of 79. He was buried in Uppsala gamla kyrkogård (Uppsala Old Cemetery), the traditional burial ground for Uppsala's academic and cultural figures. Following his death, his scholarly papers and correspondence, including materials related to his Linear A research, have been preserved in archival collections that remain accessible to scholars.

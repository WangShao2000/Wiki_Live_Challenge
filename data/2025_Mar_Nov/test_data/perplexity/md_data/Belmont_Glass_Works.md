# Belmont Glass Works

**Belmont Glass Works**, also known as the **Belmont Glass Company**, was one of Ohio's early glassmaking companies. It was located in Bellaire, Belmont County, Ohio, and operated from 1866 to 1890. The company became notable for its pressed and blown glassware products, particularly pattern glass with intricate designs that remain collectible today. Belmont Glass Works played a foundational role in establishing Bellaire as a major glass manufacturing hub in the nineteenth century, known as the "Glass City."

# History

### Establishment and Early Operations

Belmont Glass Works was established in 1866 in Bellaire, a riverfront village on the east side of Belmont County, Ohio. The company was originally organized as Barnes, Faupel & Co. in 1865 before being incorporated in 1868 as Belmont Glass Works. The founders included experienced glassmakers from the Hobbs, Brockunier and Company glassworks, a major glass manufacturer based in Wheeling, West Virginia. Among the founders were George Barnes and Henry Faupel, both of whom brought substantial expertise in glassmaking to the new venture.[1][2][3][4]

Bellaire's location proved ideal for glass manufacturing. The town sat at the intersection of the Ohio River, the National Road, and two railroad lines, providing excellent transportation infrastructure for shipping products and receiving raw materials. The region also benefited from abundant fuel—Belmont County was part of the Pittsburgh coal seam, which supplied the significant quantities of coal necessary for heating glass furnaces. Additionally, Bellaire had access to an experienced workforce with glassmaking skills, as glass production had been conducted in nearby Wheeling, West Virginia, since the 1820s.[5][6][7]

### Products and Manufacturing

The company's initial focus was on producing lamp chimneys—the glass sleeves that surround the wicks of lanterns. However, Belmont Glass Works soon expanded its product line to include lamps and bar goods, such as drinking glasses and decanters. An 1877 catalog document listed the company's products as "table ware, lamps, [and] stationers glass ware."[8][9][10]

Initially, the company produced blown glassware, manufactured by hand using traditional glassblowing techniques. However, as production methods evolved in the late nineteenth century, Belmont Glass Works transitioned to producing pressed glassware, which was manufactured using molds and presses—a more efficient production method. The company developed several distinctive pressed glass patterns that became valued by collectors. Among these was the "Zipper" pattern, which featured intricate geometric designs. The company also produced variations of the popular "Daisy and Button" pattern, including the "Daisy and Button, Slender" (Pattern No. 82) design.[11][12][13][14]

### Management and Workforce

The company employed skilled glassmakers, including Henry Crimmel, who served as a manager at Belmont Glass Works. Crimmel was a notable figure in the glass industry who would later become involved with other glass manufacturers in the Ohio and Indiana regions. Other important personnel included John Robinson and Charles Henry Over, both former employees of the Hobbs works, who held management positions at the factory. These experienced glassmakers helped establish and maintain the quality standards for which Belmont Glass Works became known.[15][16]

### Closure and Regional Impact

Belmont Glass Works ceased operations in 1890. Multiple factors contributed to the closure. The American economy proved difficult for many manufacturers during this period, affected by financial instability. More significantly, many glassmakers began relocating to northwest Ohio in the late 1880s, attracted by promises of free land, reduced-cost fuel, and cash incentives. This migration was prompted by the discovery of abundant natural gas deposits near Findlay, Ohio, in 1886, which offered glassmakers an inexpensive fuel source compared to coal. The migration of manufacturing capacity to northwest Ohio weakened the competitive position of eastern Ohio glass producers, contributing to the closure of operations at Belmont Glass Works.[17][18][19][20]

### Legacy

Despite its relatively brief period of operation, Belmont Glass Works made a significant contribution to American glass manufacturing. The company was Bellaire's first glassworks and the second glass factory established in Belmont County. Following the establishment of Belmont Glass Works, Bellaire quickly attracted additional glass manufacturers and became known as the "Glass City" due to the concentration of glass production facilities in the town. By 1880, Belmont County had become the sixth-largest glass manufacturing region in the nation.[21][22][23]

Several key personnel from Belmont Glass Works went on to establish or lead other major glass companies. In 1876, just a decade after Belmont Glass Works began operations, John Robinson, Charles Henry Over, and other former Belmont and Hobbs employees founded the Bellaire Goblet Company, which became one of the largest manufacturers of goblets in the United States. This company would eventually move to Findlay, Ohio, to take advantage of the gas boom before joining the United States Glass Company trust in 1891.[24][25]

Products manufactured by Belmont Glass Works are valued by collectors of American pattern glass today. The pressed glass patterns produced by the company, particularly the Zipper and Daisy and Button variations, remain sought-after examples of nineteenth-century American glassware craftsmanship and design innovation.

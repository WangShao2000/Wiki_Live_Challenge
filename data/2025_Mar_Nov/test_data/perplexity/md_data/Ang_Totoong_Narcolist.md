# Ang Totoong Narcolist

**Ang Totoong Narcolist** (Filipino: The True Narcolist) is a YouTube video series created in April 2019 by Peter Joemel Advincula, a former member of a drug syndicate operating in the Philippines. The series featured a hooded whistleblower using the alias "Bikoy" who made allegations that members of President Rodrigo Duterte's family and close associates were involved in illegal drug trafficking. The videos generated significant national controversy and triggered numerous legal investigations, with various opposition politicians and civil society figures charged with sedition, though most charges were later dismissed or discontinued.

# Background

Peter Joemel Advincula was born in 1989 in Donsol, Sorsogon, Philippines.[1] On May 6, 2010, he was introduced to a drug syndicate operating in Misibis Bay, a resort destination in Albay province.[1] Within the organization, he was tasked with heading closed-circuit television operations in underground facilities and later assigned to prepare monthly documents called "TARA," which listed the collections and payoffs made that month.[1] During his involvement with the syndicate, Advincula alleged that he witnessed the killing of one syndicate member and the rape of the member's family.[1]

Fearing for his life after witnessing these crimes, Advincula fled the organization.[1] He was subsequently apprehended by National Bureau of Investigation agents and, after attempting to flee, was arrested and convicted of fraud in 2012.[1] He received a six-year prison sentence, spending his first three years at Naga City District Jail before being transferred to New Bilibid Prison due to good behavior.[1] While imprisoned, Advincula engaged in religious activities and allegedly attempted to contact Senator Tito Sotto's office to expose the businessmen connected to the drug syndicate, though Sotto's office dismissed his claims as "suspicious."[1] He was released in 2017 after serving five years, benefiting from a one-year reduction for good character.[1]

# The Video Series

On April 2, 2019, Advincula created a YouTube channel titled "Ang Totoong Narcolist."[1] The channel centered around a hooded figure claiming to be a former drug syndicate member who used the pseudonym "Bikoy." Over the course of one month, five videos were uploaded between April 2 and May 6, 2019.[1] According to media analysis, the videos appeared professionally produced with quality annotation.[1]

### Episode 1 (April 2)

The first episode displayed documents that "Bikoy" claimed were TARA records related to drug syndicate operations.[1] These documents allegedly included dates, code names, bank accounts of high-ranking officials, and deposit records, with signatures purporting to verify the information's accuracy.[1] "Bikoy" presented two code names, noting their similarity to actual criminal names. One was allegedly associated with Waldo Carpio, the brother of Sara Duterte's husband Mans Carpio, while another was claimed to belong to Paolo Duterte, with the accusation that Duterte bore a back tattoo displaying the code name.[1]

### Episode 2 (April 7)

Released through Bombo Radyo Iloilo on April 7, the second episode presented additional code names allegedly associated with three bank accounts, including one belonging to Elijah Manuel Pepito, partner lawyer for Mans Carpio's law firm, and another to "Veronica Salvador," which "Bikoy" constructed by merging the names of Duterte's domestic partner Honeylet Avanceña and their daughter Kitty (born Veronica).[1]

### Episode 3 (April 8)

The third episode, released on YouTube on April 8, focused on allegations against Senator Bong Go, claiming that drug proceeds were being funneled to his bank accounts and that Go bore a dragon tattoo with his alleged code name.[1]

### Episode 4 (April 12)

In the fourth episode, "Bikoy" reiterated claims about Go's tattoo and displayed a photograph of Go playing basketball, pointing out a portion of what he claimed was a dragon tattoo visible on Go's back.[1] He also reaffirmed Carpio's alleged involvement in drug operations.[1]

### Episode 5 (April 22)

The final episode, released on April 22, identified organizations allegedly connected to the Misibis Bay syndicate and presented the "Quadrangle Group," described as an alleged organization of wealthy businessmen engaged in drug operations.[1] Among those named were businessman Elizaldy Co, Albay Representative Fernando Gonzalez, Camarines Norte Governor Edgardo Tallado, former congressman and governor Luis Villafuerte, Catanduanes Governor Joseph Cua, Sorsogon provincial board member Krunimar Escudero, businessman Victor Lorenzo Rosales, Naga businessman Thomas Enrile, and businesswoman Tess Rañola.[1]

# Government Response and Investigation

### Initial Reaction

The Duterte administration quickly rejected the allegations. Presidential Spokesperson Salvador Panelo characterized the claims as "black propaganda" and dismissed "Bikoy" as a "pawn."[1] President Duterte blamed the political opposition and suggested that Senator Antonio Trillanes was likely behind the videos, making derogatory comments about Trillanes.[1] The Liberal Party publicly denied involvement in creating the videos.[1] The Department of Justice questioned "Bikoy's" credibility, citing his anonymity as a significant concern.[1]

The President challenged Paolo Duterte to display the alleged tattoo, which Duterte refused to do.[1] Communist Party of the Philippines founder Jose Maria Sison called for "Bikoy" to be granted protection to tell his story, though this voice stood alone among government officials.[1]

### Rodel Jayme Arrest

Rodel Jayme, who owned a website that distributed the videos, was apprehended on April 30, 2019.[1] Jayme subsequently faced charges of inciting sedition in connection with the distribution of the materials.

### The Oust Duterte Matrix

On April 22, 2019, the same day the fifth video was released, Presidential Spokesperson Salvador Panelo announced the release of the "Oust Duterte matrix," a diagram purporting to show links between journalists, civil society organizations, and alleged plots to overthrow President Duterte.[1] Panelo claimed the matrix had undergone "proper validation." The diagram named journalists affiliated with prominent news organizations including the Philippine Center for Investigative Journalism and Rappler.[1] Many individuals included in the matrix subsequently denied involvement in any ouster plot, while one affected group sought government protection from harassment.

# Advincula's Disclosure and Recantation

On May 6, 2019, Advincula surfaced publicly at the headquarters of the Integrated Bar of the Philippines, identifying himself as "Bikoy" and requesting legal assistance.[1] He stated that he had decided to reveal his identity because of threats to his safety and his conscience regarding the destruction caused by drug trafficking, in which he had once participated.[1] He emphasized that the documents shown in the videos were authentic and contained details about drug syndicate operations dating back to 2010.[1]

On May 22, 2019, just 16 days after publicly identifying himself, Advincula surrendered to police.[1] During a televised press conference, he recanted his previous allegations, stating that he had played a role in a script created by the Liberal Party.[1] Following this recantation, he requested protective custody, claiming that his life was in danger.[1] On June 3, 2019, Advincula turned himself over to police for protection, and he was released from protective custody on June 25, 2019.[1]

# Legal Proceedings

### Against Opposition Figures

On May 17, 2019, the Philippine National Police Criminal Investigation and Detection Group filed multiple criminal complaints against various opposition politicians and civil society figures, including Vice President Leonor Robredo, Senator Antonio Trillanes IV, and numerous others.[1] The charges included sedition, inciting to sedition, cyber libel, libel, estafa, harboring a criminal, and obstruction of justice.[1]

Following investigation, the Department of Justice initially pursued the case against Trillanes and eleven others on a charge of conspiracy to commit sedition, while clearing Vice President Robredo on February 10, 2020, citing lack of probable cause.[1] The sedition charges against most other defendants were also dismissed, though the DOJ continued prosecuting the conspiracy to commit sedition case against Trillanes and a smaller group of co-accused.[1]

### Against Advincula

In July 2019, Advincula filed a sworn affidavit alleging that three lawyers—Jose Manuel I. Diokno, Theodore O. Te, and Lorenzo R. Tañada III—were part of a group called "Project Sodoma" that plotted to commit sedition, cyber libel, and libel, and that they met with him at Ateneo de Manila University.[1] The three lawyers denied the allegations, stating that they were present at the location for a senatorial candidates forum, not to meet with Advincula.[1] Charges of sedition were subsequently filed against opposition members based on Advincula's affidavit, though these charges were later dismissed as the Department of Justice determined that Advincula himself was probably guilty of conspiracy to commit sedition.[1]

On May 22, 2023, the Manila Metropolitan Trial Court Branch 17 found Advincula guilty beyond reasonable doubt of perjury in connection with the false statements he made in his July 2019 affidavit regarding the three lawyers.[1] The court sentenced him to an indeterminate penalty of three months and one day to one year and one day of imprisonment.[1]

### Against Private Citizens and Platforms

Elizaldy Co, the prominent Bicolano businessman named in the fifth video as the alleged leader of the Quadrangle Group, and Misibis Bay Resort jointly filed a one billion Philippine peso cyber libel suit against Facebook, YouTube, Advincula, and others in May 2019.[1] Co claimed that the allegations damaged his business reputation and resulted in a bank suspending the processing of a significant loan application.[1] Co characterized the video's content as false and stated that Misibis Bay Resort had never been used for illegal drug operations or as a drug laboratory.[1] This marked the first cyber libel suit filed in the Philippines against major social media platforms.[1]

# Legacy and Aftermath

The "Ang Totoong Narcolist" videos occurred during a period of intense political tension in the Philippines, coinciding with the 2019 midterm elections scheduled for May 13, 2019, just days after the first video's release.[1] The controversy highlighted broader divisions over President Duterte's drug war policy, with critics alleging that the campaign was being used to eliminate rivals while protecting allies allegedly involved in the drug trade, while administration supporters viewed the videos as part of a political attack on the presidency.

The series demonstrated the potential for viral online content to trigger political upheaval, prompt government investigations, and result in subsequent legal proceedings affecting multiple individuals across the political spectrum. The recantation by Advincula, the subsequent perjury conviction, and the dismissal of charges against most opposition figures complicated the narrative surrounding the videos and raised questions about the reliability of the allegations presented.

# Everest, North Dakota

**Everest** is an unincorporated community in Everest Township, Cass County, North Dakota, United States. Located south of Casselton, it was platted in 1881 and served as a notable railroad hub and agricultural center in the late 19th century. The community experienced substantial prosperity during its early years but declined following the removal of a major rail line and a series of destructive fires. As of 2020, Everest Township had a population of 99, though Everest itself has been removed from official county maps.[1][2]

# History

### Founding and early development

Everest was platted in 1881 during the period of intensive railroad development and settlement expansion across Dakota Territory. The post office was established on May 24, 1882, with Carl Knuppel serving as the first postmaster. The community benefited from its strategic location at the junction of two major rail lines: the Great Northern Railway and the Northern Pacific Railway, making it an important shipping point for agricultural products from the surrounding region.[3][1]

During the late 1880s and 1890s, Everest became a prosperous community with a diverse economy. At its height, the town boasted over 200 residents and included a railroad depot, post office, two hotels, a school, a church, two general stores, two saloons, a butcher shop, a blacksmith shop, a lumber yard, and four grain elevators, along with various other businesses including a harness maker.[1]

### The Langer family connection

Everest gained prominence in North Dakota history as the birthplace of William Langer, who would become the 17th and later 21st governor of North Dakota and a United States senator. Born on September 30, 1886, near Everest to German-American parents Frank and Mary (Weber) Langer, William Langer spent his early childhood in the community before eventually relocating to pursue his political career. Frank Langer, William's father, was a member of the first North Dakota state legislature, making the Langer family prominent residents of the Everest area during the late 1800s. Joseph Langer, William's uncle, also became a prominent member of the community.[4][1]

### Economic importance and decline

The community's fortunes changed following significant structural changes in regional railroad infrastructure. Around 1896, the westernmost of the two rail lines—which had connected the Great Northern Railway and the Northern Pacific Railway—was removed when it was acquired by the Great Northern Railway. This loss of rail competition and connectivity substantially reduced Everest's economic importance.[1]

Compounding the effects of railroad decline, a series of disastrous fires devastated the community's commercial infrastructure. The combination of these fires and the closure of the post office in 1908 accelerated Everest's decline. By 1960, most of the community's establishments had permanently closed, leaving Everest with a population of just 10 residents. The community has since been removed from official county maps.[1]

# Notable residents

Beyond the Langer family, Everest was home to several individuals who achieved prominence in North Dakota. James Trammell, Edward Meilicke, Edward Redmon, Edward Weber, Edward Arnold, William MacFadden, and August Hilke each served as Everest Township or Cass County officials.[1]

**Herbert Nilles**, a resident of the Everest area, was elected to the presidency of the North Dakota State Bar. **Morgan Ford**, born in Wheatland, North Dakota, was appointed as a judge of the United States Customs Court (later known as the Court of International Trade) by President Harry S. Truman in 1949. Although Ford was born in Wheatland, his family connections to the Everest area were significant enough that he is noted among the community's prominent figures.[5][1]

# Geography

Everest is located 20 miles (32 km) from Fargo, the county seat of Cass County, and 2 miles (3.2 km) south of Casselton. The community lies at the junction of North Dakota Highway 18 and 155th Avenue SE, in close proximity to the Casselton Robert Miller Regional Airport.[1]

Everest Township, which contains the unincorporated community, has a total area of 35.08 square miles (90.9 km²). As of the 2020 census, the township had a population of 99, and as of 2023, there were an estimated 26 households in the township.[2]

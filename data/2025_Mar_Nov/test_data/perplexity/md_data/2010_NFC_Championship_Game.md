# 2010 NFC Championship Game

The **2010 National Football Conference (NFC) Championship Game** was a playoff game played between the Green Bay Packers and the Chicago Bears on January 23, 2011, at Soldier Field in Chicago, Illinois. The Packers defeated the Bears 21–14 to advance to Super Bowl XLV. This was the first playoff meeting between the two teams since 1941, marking a rare postseason encounter in a rivalry dating to the founding era of professional football.[1][2]

The sixth-seeded Packers, who had entered the playoffs as a wild card, won the NFC Championship despite being lower-seeded than the second-seeded Bears. The Packers built a 14–0 halftime lead before the Bears mounted a comeback in the second half. The Bears' starting quarterback Jay Cutler sustained a Grade II medial collateral ligament (MCL) sprain and left the game. Backup Todd Collins replaced him but struggled, followed by third-string quarterback Caleb Hanie. Hanie led two touchdown drives that narrowed the gap to 21–14, but threw two interceptions that prevented a Bears victory. Green Bay's defense, led by interceptions from B.J. Raji and Sam Shields, secured the win. The victory sent the Packers to Super Bowl XLV on February 6, 2011, where they defeated the Pittsburgh Steelers 31–25.[3][1]

# Background

### Green Bay Packers

The Green Bay Packers had made the playoffs in the 2009 season but were eliminated by the Arizona Cardinals in the wild card round. In the 2010 regular season, the Packers started with a 4–4 record. However, they won nine of their remaining twelve games to finish 10–6 and secure a wild card playoff berth, entering the postseason as the sixth seed in the NFC. Quarterback Aaron Rodgers, the team's starter since the prior season, led the late-season turnaround.[1][3]

### Chicago Bears

The Chicago Bears finished the 2010 regular season with an 11–5 record, winning the NFC North division and earning the second seed in the playoffs. Quarterback Jay Cutler directed the Bears' offense, and the team won seven of their final eight regular-season games.[4][1]

# Road to the Championship Game

### Packers' Path

The Packers' playoff journey began as a wild card team facing the Philadelphia Eagles in the wild card round. Green Bay defeated Philadelphia 21–16 to advance to the divisional round. In the divisional playoff game, the Packers faced the top-seeded Atlanta Falcons and won decisively, 48–21, to reach the NFC Championship Game. With two road playoff victories already secured, the Packers had to win one more away game to reach the Super Bowl.[5][3][1]

### Bears' Path

The Bears received a bye week as the second seed and began their playoff campaign in the divisional round against the Seattle Seahawks. Chicago defeated Seattle 35–24 to advance to the NFC Championship Game, earning a home game at Soldier Field.[4][1]

# Game Summary

### First Half

The game took place on a sunny afternoon with temperatures around 20 degrees Fahrenheit at Soldier Field. The Packers scored early when Aaron Rodgers led a touchdown drive and ran for a 1-yard score on a bootleg play, giving Green Bay a 7–0 lead.[3][1]

The Packers scored again in the second quarter when rookie running back James Starks rushed for a 1-yard touchdown, extending the lead to 14–0. The Bears struggled to move the offense during the first half, with the Packers' defense limiting Chicago's effectiveness.[1][3]

Late in the first half, Cutler suffered a left knee injury. Medical evaluation revealed a Grade II MCL sprain. The injury ended Cutler's participation in the game. Although Cutler wanted to return, team medical staff determined he could not continue, and he did not play in the second half. The Packers led 14–0 at the end of the first half.[6][3]

### Second Half

Backup quarterback Todd Collins replaced Cutler at the start of the second half but was ineffective in running the Bears' offense. The Bears subsequently inserted third-string quarterback Caleb Hanie into the game. Hanie led a touchdown drive that culminated in a 9-yard touchdown run by Chester Taylor, reducing Green Bay's advantage to 14–7.[3][1]

On the Bears' next possession, Packers defensive tackle B.J. Raji intercepted a Hanie pass and returned it for an 18-yard touchdown, increasing the Packers' lead to 21–7.[1][3]

Late in the fourth quarter, Hanie led another touchdown drive that produced a 5-yard touchdown pass to bring the Bears within 21–14 with over two minutes remaining. Chicago's final drive did not result in a score.[3]

### Final Moments

On the Bears' final drive, Hanie was intercepted for a second time by Packers cornerback Sam Shields on fourth down with fewer than 45 seconds remaining. Shields' interception ended the Bears' opportunity to score. Shields recorded two interceptions in the game.[1][3]

# Statistics

Aaron Rodgers completed 20 of 35 passes for 246 yards and threw three touchdown passes and one interception. He also scored one rushing touchdown. Running back James Starks rushed for 74 yards on 22 carries.[3][1]

For the Bears, Todd Collins had limited playing time as the backup quarterback before being replaced by Hanie. Caleb Hanie led two scoring drives but threw two interceptions.[3]

The Packers' defense recorded multiple interceptions. Defensive tackle B.J. Raji intercepted a pass and returned it for a touchdown. Cornerback Sam Shields recorded two interceptions, including the game-ending interception.[1][3]

# Aftermath

The Packers advanced to Super Bowl XLV on February 6, 2011, in Arlington, Texas, where they defeated the Pittsburgh Steelers 31–25.[2][1]

The loss eliminated the Bears from postseason contention. The 2010 NFC Championship was the last playoff game the Bears won until 2018.[4]

The game marked the first playoff meeting between the Packers and Bears since the 1941 NFL Playoff Game, despite the teams having met more than 180 times in regular-season competition.[1]

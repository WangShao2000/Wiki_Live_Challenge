# Champions of the Future

**Champions of the Future** (**COTF**) is a kart racing competition organized by the RGMMC Group and sanctioned by FIA Karting (CIK-FIA). Founded in 2020, COTF is one of 11 competitions on the international kart racing calendar in CIK-FIA classes. Initially a two-round series in OK and OK-Junior (OK-J)—with single-round finals in KZ2 and 60 Mini—the championship has expanded to encompass all eight CIK-FIA classes across multiple series formats, including the Euro Series, Shifters, and Academy Program. The series has become a major platform for developing young karting talent, with numerous former champions progressing to higher levels of motorsport, including Formula 4 and other international single-seater championships.[1][2]

# History

### Founding and early years

Champions of the Future was established in 2020 by the RGMMC Group, the official FIA Karting (CIK-FIA) promoter and operator of Italian American Motor Engineering (IAME) international events. The series was created to promote and develop the primary CIK-FIA karting classes, including OK (the senior direct-drive class), OK-Junior (the under-15 direct-drive class), KZ2 (the secondary gearbox class), and 60 Mini (the under-12 international class).[3][2][4]

The inaugural season in 2020 faced delays due to the COVID-19 pandemic in Europe. The scheduled opening round at Wackersdorf (7–10 May) and the second round at Kristianstad (25–28 June) were both cancelled. The series made its debut on 16–19 July at Zuera in Spain, with over 170 drivers entered in preparation for the opening round of the Karting European Championship that same month.[2]

### Expansion and development (2023–2024)

As the series matured, Champions of the Future expanded its scope beyond its original two-round format. By 2023, the Euro Series had grown to five rounds across different European circuits, including Valencia, Triñec, Rødby, Cremona, and Franciacorta. The championship further diversified with the introduction of the Shifters series, featuring KZ, KZ2, and KZ2-M classes, which ran on parallel schedules with major championship rounds.[5][2]

In 2022, COTF hosted a one-off **Winter Series** at Franciacorta in Italy, marking the only edition of this format to date.[2][5]

### Academy Program and international expansion

Building on its success with the Euro and Shifters series, Champions of the Future launched the **Academy Program** in the mid-2020s as an "Arrive and Drive" series designed to make international karting more accessible to young drivers. Supported by F1 Academy as part of the "Discover Your Drive" initiative, the Academy Program features three mixed-gender categories: Mini 60 (ages 8–11), OK-N Junior (ages 11–14), and OK-N Senior (ages 14–17). The program incorporates cost control measures, including entry fee limits and a chassis and engine lottery system to ensure equal equipment and level competition.[6][7][1]

F1 Academy identifies and supports three female drivers in each age category with financial assistance and branded equipment as part of its commitment to increasing female participation in karting. As of 2025, female drivers comprise over 30% of the Champions of the Future program.[8][6]

The Academy Program has expanded to multiple continents, with events held in the United Arab Emirates, Europe, and scheduled events in Asia and Macau.[9][1][6]

### National series and future expansion (2026)

Champions of the Future is set to expand to national series in 2026, with RGMMC partnering with regional motorsport authorities to create localized championships. The **Motorsport UK British Champions of the Future Academy Programme** will launch in 2026, featuring four double-header race weekends at UK locations with drivers competing in Mini 60, OK-N Junior, and OK-N Senior classes. Additionally, COTF will introduce an Emirati national championship and a Macanese national championship in 2026.[10][8][9][2]

# Format and structure

### Euro Series

The Euro Series represents the flagship international competition of Champions of the Future, featuring the OK and OK-Junior classes. Typically held over five rounds annually between March and September, the Euro Series takes place at major European circuits in the weeks prior to World or European Championship rounds at the same venues. This proximity to world championship events allows drivers to compete at elite levels while preparing for higher-profile races.[11][2]

### Shifters series

The Shifters series encompasses the gearbox classes (KZ, KZ2, and KZ2-M), showcasing higher-powered karts. This series typically features multi-round schedules running parallel to the Euro Series, with events held at prestigious circuits such as Valencia and Franciacorta.[5][2]

### Academy Program

The Academy Program operates as a global "Arrive and Drive" series with supplied equipment and cost-controlled entry fees. Drivers in the Mini 60, OK-N Junior, and OK-N Senior classes compete across six double-header race weekends, with events held across multiple continents including Europe, the Middle East, and Asia.[7][1][6]

The weekend format typically includes free practice on Friday, qualifying and heat races on Saturday, and a second round of qualifying, heats, and finals on Sunday.[9]

# Notable champions

Champions of the Future has produced numerous drivers who have progressed to international racing success. The series serves as a stepping stone to higher categories, with past champions moving on to compete in Formula 4, Formula 3, and single-seater championships.

**2020 champions** Nikita Bedrin (OK) and Arvid Lindblad (OK-Junior) became early indicators of the series' ability to identify top talent. Bedrin claimed the inaugural title, while Lindblad's victory marked the beginning of his progression through the karting ranks.[12][13]

**2021 champions** Rafael Câmara (OK) and Freddie Slater (OK-Junior) added to the series' track record.[14][15]

**2023** saw Alex Powell capture the OK title. Powell, a Mercedes junior driver, went on to compete in multiple F4 championships and finished runner-up in the 2025 F4 Middle East Championship.[16][17][2]

**2024 champions** included Dries Van Langendonck in OK-Junior, who followed his victory with the 2023 World Championship and 2024 FIA Karting European Championship in the same category. Luna Fluxá (also known as Luna Fluxa Cross) became the only female driver to win a COTF title, claiming the 2024 OK-N Senior championship. Fluxá subsequently received the Rising Star Award and continued her progression to Formula 4 racing.[18][19][20][21][22][23][2]

# Regulatory framework

Champions of the Future operates under FIA Karting (CIK-FIA) sporting standards and technical regulations. All supplied equipment used in the Academy Program is CIK-FIA homologated, meeting international specifications for chassis and engines. The Euro Series and Shifters competitions utilize standardized equipment determined by CIK-FIA class specifications.[6][2][9]

# Partnership with F1 Academy

The Academy Program works closely with F1 Academy, the female-only Formula 4–level single-seater championship founded by the Formula One Group. Through the "Discover Your Drive" initiative, F1 Academy provides talent identification, sponsorship support, and career guidance to promising young drivers in the COTF program, creating a pipeline of developed talent for progression into single-seater racing.[24][7][6]

## Organizer and promotion

RGMMC is a Switzerland-based motorsport event management company founded in 2004. Beyond Champions of the Future, RGMMC is responsible for organizing the FIA Karting European and World Championships, IAME International events, and various national karting championships. The company provides comprehensive event services including race control, technical assistance, broadcast production, and hospitality for major international competitions.[4][3]

# Muscatine Mall

**Muscatine Mall**, originally known as **Muscatine Plaza**, is a shopping mall located in Muscatine, Iowa, on Park Avenue. Constructed in 1971, the mall represents a significant commercial development in the city and serves as a retail destination in eastern Iowa. Originally developed by General Management (later known as General Growth Properties), the mall initially featured an enclosed shopping center with major department stores as anchor tenants. Since its opening, the mall has undergone numerous changes in ownership and tenant composition, reflecting broader trends in American retail development and consumer shopping patterns.

# History

### Planning and Construction

In March 1970, General Management, a shopping mall developer based in Des Moines, Iowa, announced plans for a large enclosed shopping center to be constructed on Park Avenue in Muscatine.[1] The proposed mall was designed to be approximately 287,000 square feet in size, with two major anchor department stores serving as the primary tenants.[1] At a meeting held at the city's Rotary International club, General Management's leasing agent Jon E. Batesole outlined the company's vision for an enclosed shopping center that would feature Montgomery Ward and Woolworth as anchor stores.[1] Other confirmed tenants at the time of the announcement included Walgreens, a Hy-Vee supermarket, and a movie theater.[1] Groundbreaking for the project began on May 17, 1970.[1]

Montgomery Ward became the first anchor store to open on November 27, 1970, ahead of the main mall's completion.[1] The store occupied 55,000 square feet and included automotive repair bays and a restaurant, replacing a catalog store that had previously been located in downtown Muscatine.[1] Woolworth opened on the north side of the mall in June 1971 in a 72,000-square-foot space that was notable for being the retailer's largest store that did not feature multiple levels.[1] The main shopping mall, which connected the two anchor stores, consisted of a 36-foot-wide and 663-foot-long enclosed hallway.[1]

### Opening and Early Years

The official opening day for Muscatine Plaza occurred on August 5, 1971.[1] Opening-day ceremonies included a ribbon-cutting event hosted by the city's mayor, as well as entertainment activities such as pony rides and a vintage car show.[1] Among the first tenants to open within the mall were Baskin-Robbins, Kinney Shoes, and Karmelkorn, with Pizza Hut, Maurices, and Musicland opening shortly thereafter.[1]

In September 1972, the mall held a first-anniversary celebration that included drawings with over $600 in prizes distributed by mall merchants.[1] At that time, the mall contained 27 stores.[1] In 1974, Salkin & Linoff, a Minnesota-based clothing company, opened two stores at the mall: Morrey A and Bostwick's for Men, while also remodeling their existing Stevensons clothing store.[1] In 1975, JCPenney joined the mall as a third anchor store, occupying approximately 48,000 square feet on the mall's east side.[1]

### Mid-to-Late 1970s and 1980s Transitions

Montgomery Ward announced its closure in September 1978, citing the store's small size as a limitation in offering a full merchandise line.[1] In August 1979, the former Montgomery Ward space was occupied by Petersen Harned Von Maur, marking the eighth location for the Iowa-based department store chain.[1] 

In mid-1981, Woolworth converted its store to Woolco, a discount division of the company.[1] Following financial difficulties, Woolco announced the closure of all its stores by early 1983 due to the division's lack of profitability.[1] The Woolco location at Muscatine Mall underwent conversion to a Walmart store, which opened on July 1, 1983.[1] This store marked Walmart's first location in Iowa and was also the company's northernmost location at the time, leading the company to refer to it internally as their "Yankee" store.[1] The decision to locate in Muscatine was influenced by the city's lower unemployment rate compared to nearby Quad Cities communities.[1]

Concurrent with Walmart's opening, the former Hy-Vee location within the mall was subdivided in August 1983 to create a new northwestern entrance and spaces for additional tenants.[1] Among the new tenants was Kimberly Smorgasbord, a buffet restaurant that also operated locations in the Quad Cities.[1] During the 1980s, other retailers opened within the mall, including County Seat and regional clothing chain Seifert's, while Maurices returned to the center.[1] Subway and Diamond Dave's restaurants also opened during this period, and both Walgreens and JCPenney undertook store renovations.[1] Additionally, in 1989, Aetna sold its interests in Muscatine Mall and five other Iowa malls to Rouse Properties, which became the sole owner of these properties.[1]

By 1990, the mall had expanded to 345,000 square feet with a capacity of approximately 50 stores.[1] In July 1991, Menards opened in the former Walmart location, with mall merchants anticipating this opening would contribute to improved tenancy at the center.[1]

### Late 1990s and Early 2000s Redevelopment Efforts

In mid-1998, Rouse Properties sold its Iowa mall holdings to Landau & Heyman (L&H) Real Estate, a Chicago-based firm, for an undisclosed amount.[1] In August 1999, Staples announced plans to open a store at Muscatine Mall, occupying approximately 24,000 square feet of the former Von Maur location.[1] 

The announcement of Staples coincided with a major renovation project initiated by Landau & Heyman in October 1999.[1] The company's plans intended to convert most of the mall property into a strip mall configuration by removing a substantial portion of the enclosed concourses and constructing new storefronts facing Park Avenue.[1] This approach was conceived as a strategy to increase customer traffic, with the company believing the center would maintain greater competitiveness as an open-air rather than enclosed mall.[1] Prior to the renovation commencement, several stores had already closed, including B. Dalton, Payless ShoeSource, and a second Maurices location.[1]

Staples opened its location in April 2000.[1] By the end of that year, Walgreens announced its relocation to a newer store at the site of a former second Hy-Vee location, a decision reflecting the chain's strategic shift toward standalone stores with drive-through pharmacies.[1] Among the stores remaining at that time were JCPenney, Menards, the mall's movie theater, Regis Corporation (hair salon services), GNC, and various local retailers.[1]

### 2003 Onward: Elder-Beerman and Operational Changes

Despite the closure of numerous inline stores, Elder-Beerman opened at Muscatine Mall in November 2003.[1] This store operated in the combined space of the former Staples location and the remainder of the former Von Maur area, and it marked the retailer's first location west of the Mississippi River.[1]

In July 2006, local developers Thad Denhartog, Scott Anderson, Kevin Koellner, and Rodney Blackwell purchased the mall property through the limited liability corporation Muscatine Mall Management.[1] A new Menards location opened on November 13, 2007, relocating from its previous mall space.[1] In 2008, the parent company Bon-Ton consolidated its store names, renaming the Elder-Beerman location at Muscatine Mall to Younkers, a brand it also owned at the time.[1]

By 2010, Slumberland Furniture opened at the former Menards location within the mall, and additional retailers including Jimmy John's (sandwich restaurant) and Shoe Sensation (footwear store) also began operations.[1] Younkers closed in 2018 as part of a larger series of store closures executed by The Bon-Ton company, raising concerns among remaining tenants regarding the mall's long-term viability.[1]

# Architecture and Layout

The original mall design consisted of an enclosed hallway measuring 36 feet in width and 663 feet in length, connecting the Montgomery Ward and Woolworth anchor stores.[1] The facility expanded beyond its initial 287,000 square feet during the 1980s and 1990s, eventually reaching 345,000 square feet by 1990.[1] During the late 1990s and early 2000s renovation period, portions of the interior concourses were removed as the property transitioned toward an open-air retail configuration, with some tenants converting to exterior-only storefronts facing Park Avenue.[1]

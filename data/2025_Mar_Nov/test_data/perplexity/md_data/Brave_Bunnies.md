# Brave Bunnies

**Brave Bunnies** (Ukrainian: Хоробрі зайці, romanized: *Khorobri zaitsi*) is a Ukrainian animated television series for preschool children aged 2 to 5. The series follows the adventures of two rabbit siblings, Bop and Boo, who travel with their family on the Bunny Bus and befriend animals from diverse environments. Created by Olga Cherepanova and produced by the Ukrainian company Glowberry in partnership with Spanish animation studio Ánima Kitchent, the series has been broadcast in more than 80 countries. The show emphasizes themes of diversity, acceptance, and healthy childhood development through its narrative of cross-cultural friendships and inclusive play. [1]

The first season premiered in December 2020 in Ukraine and subsequently achieved prominence as the first Ukrainian children's animated series to achieve significant international distribution. Production was interrupted by the Russian invasion of Ukraine in February 2022, but resumed later that year following a partnership with Canadian entertainment company WildBrain, which became the majority stakeholder of the intellectual property in December 2022. The second season was released in 2023 and has continued the franchise's expansion across international markets. [2]

## Development and production

### Conception and early development

Brave Bunnies originated from a children's picture book created by Glowberry, a Ukrainian creative production company founded in 2009 that specialized in children's literature and illustration. Olga Cherepanova, the company's co-founder and creative producer, developed an initial concept featuring a family of rabbits embarking on a journey and meeting unusual animals. Cherepanova, who studied psychology, sought to create content that would encourage young children to respect and accept differences in others while supporting healthy childhood development. [3]

The project evolved from the picture book into a television series intended for children aged 2 to 5. Over the course of several years, Cherepanova and Glowberry worked to develop the concept into a fully animated series, recruiting international partners to ensure adequate funding and market access. Illustrator Anna Sarvira developed the visual style for the series, serving as art director, while Cherepanova served as creative producer. [4]

### International partnership

In 2019, production entered development as an international co-production involving Glowberry, Ánima Kitchent (the Spanish subsidiary of Mexican animation studio Ánima), and the Ukrainian State Film Agency and Ministry of Youth and Sports, which provided support. The production team brought together creative personnel from multiple countries, including British director Tim Fehrenbach and Canadian lead writer John Van Bruggen, both of whom had previous experience in children's animation. The production involved coordination among over 100 people working across different countries. [5]

The series was presented at industry events including MIPCOM in 2019, and by early 2020, the UK-based animation studio Aardman Animations had partnered with the production to serve as international distributor. Aardman secured additional sales at the 2020 Kidscreen Summit in Miami, positioning the series for international broadcast. At the subsequent MIPJunior market, the series became the second most-watched project, demonstrating strong industry interest. [6]

The first season consisted of 52 episodes, each seven minutes in length. [7]

### Production process and approach

The writing team for Brave Bunnies consulted with child psychologists throughout the development process to ensure that episodes would maintain the interest of young viewers, present stories supporting early socialization, and create characters representing diverse human personality types. Music celebrating the characters' achievements was composed in Ukraine. [8]

The production sought to present stories that would help introduce children to concepts of diversity and teach them to accept and communicate with people of different appearances and behaviors. Rather than simply portraying different characters, the show focused on depicting real-life scenarios demonstrating how to approach each individual uniquely, without pressure or expectation of conformity. [9]

### Impact of Russian invasion

Production of Brave Bunnies in Kyiv was suspended when the full-scale Russian invasion of Ukraine began in February 2022. Many members of the Glowberry team were forced to evacuate from Kyiv due to security concerns. Notably, production coordinator Natalia Yermak left her position at Glowberry to become a war correspondent and journalist for the New York Times, covering the conflict in Ukraine. [10]

Production remained halted for several months. However, by August 2022, production resumed following expressions of support from the production's international partners. In December 2022, Canadian children's entertainment company WildBrain became a majority stakeholder in Brave Bunnies, acquiring the rights to continue reliable production and distribution of the series. This partnership ensured that the second season could proceed without disruption. WildBrain's Vancouver animation studio joined Glowberry and Ánima Kitchent to produce the second season of 52 episodes. [11]

## Narrative and themes

The 2D animated series follows a family of rabbits consisting of two children—Bop and Boo—along with their parents (Ma and Pa) and four younger siblings known as the Bunny Babies. The family lives on the Bunny Bus, a vehicle they use to travel through the world they inhabit. Each episode begins with a cheerful song as the family travels to a new location. [12]

At each stop on their journey, brother Bop and sister Boo meet and befriend different animal characters, creating new games and activities with them. These interactions form the core narrative of each episode, allowing the show to introduce viewers to various animals and their distinct characteristics while modeling friendship-building and cooperative play. [13]

The series attempts to show preschool viewers the diversity of the world and to encourage acceptance of differences. Through the example of the Brave Bunnies characters, who approach each new friend with openness and curiosity, the show demonstrates how to communicate with and befriend people with different appearances and behaviors. Beginning in the second season, some episodes feature the bunny family revisiting friends at their homes, meeting their families, and learning about different lifestyles. [14]

## Release and broadcast

### Initial premiere and distribution

The series premiered on December 5, 2020, on PLUSPLUS, a Ukrainian television channel. Following the Ukrainian premiere, the series entered international broadcast, launching on major networks including Nick Jr. (United Kingdom), Super RTL (Germany), ABC (Australia), Yle (Finland), and HOP! (Israel). By 2022, the first season had been broadcast in more than 80 countries. [15]

Aardman Animations distributed the first season internationally, leveraging its established relationships with broadcasters and distribution partners worldwide. The series appeared on prominent children's programming blocks, including Milkshake! on Channel 5 in the United Kingdom (beginning February 6, 2021) and ABC Kids in Australia. In Canada, the series was broadcast on WildBrain's Family Jr. channel, and in the United States, episodes became available on the WildBrain+ SVOD platform on Comcast. [16]

### Second season and expanded distribution

The second season was released in Canada in June 2023, followed by other territories later that year. Following WildBrain's acquisition of majority control, the company expanded the series' international distribution network. As of 2023, distribution deals were in place with platforms across Latin America, Canada, the United Kingdom, Europe, the Nordic countries, the Middle East, Asia, and Australia. Notable distribution partnerships included TeleTOON+ (Poland), Rai Yoyo (Italy), and renewed partnerships with existing broadcasters including Milkshake! in the United Kingdom. [17]

In Southeast Asia and across the Asia-Pacific region, the series was made available through Warner Media's Cartoon Network and streaming platform HBO GO. The series also became available on YouTube, including through the Sunflower TV channel, which launched in July 2022 to provide ad-free Ukrainian-language children's content for Ukrainian refugees across Europe and the United Kingdom. [18]

## Voice cast

The original Ukrainian voice cast included prominent performers. Actress Olena Kravets provided the voice for Bunny Ma, while musician and songwriter Potap voiced Bunny Pa. The casting of such high-profile Ukrainian performers was noted as significant, as it marked the first time a Ukrainian children's animated series had attracted voice talent of this caliber, according to reports by 1+1 Media. [19]

The English-language dub featured different voice actors: Wilbur Conabeare as Bop, Margot Powell as Boo, Harriet Kershaw as Bunny Ma, Jensen Adeyanju as Bunny Pa, and Chetna Pandya as Narrator. [20]

## Reception

Brave Bunnies achieved strong viewership ratings in multiple international markets. The series was among the top-rated children's programs in Ukraine, the United Kingdom, Israel, and Australia. In the United Kingdom, the series ranked as the number one program in its broadcast slots at its premiere on the Milkshake! platform and subsequently reached an audience of 4.4 million viewers. [21]

In 2020, the British research agency Childwise conducted a focus group study of children aged 3 to 5 who watched the series. The study found that 79 percent of children reported feeling happier after watching episodes, and 90 percent indicated they liked the characters. The publication The New Voice of Ukraine praised the series for its message of diversity and acceptance. [22]

The series received recognition at international animation festivals and industry events. It became a finalist at Animation Production Day (Germany), Cartoon Forum (France), Cartoon 360 (Spain), and the International Animated Film Festival MIFA (France). The series also received consideration at the Financing Forum for Kids Content (Sweden) and the Ottawa International Animation Festival (Canada). [23]

## Merchandise and publishing

Merchandise based on the series was developed through partnerships with major consumer products companies. In April 2022, Spin Master, a global toy manufacturer, launched a line of Brave Bunnies plush toys and other merchandise in Germany and Italy. [24]

Publishing deals expanded the brand into books. De Agostini's Planeta Libre imprint published books in Italy, Five Mile Publishing released titles in Australia, and Ladybird (an imprint of Penguin Random House) launched a series of preschool storybooks in the United Kingdom beginning in early 2022. WildBrain CPLG, the consumer products division of WildBrain, manages global licensing and merchandising for the brand (excluding Ukraine and Italy). [25]

# Colten Ellis

**Colten Ellis** (born October 5, 2000) is a Canadian professional ice hockey player who serves as a goaltender for the Buffalo Sabres of the National Hockey League (NHL). Originally drafted by the St. Louis Blues in the third round (93rd overall) of the 2019 NHL Entry Draft, Ellis began his professional career with the Blues' minor league affiliates before being claimed by the Buffalo Sabres on waivers in October 2025. He made his NHL debut that month, establishing himself as one of the team's goaltenders.[1]

Ellis earned recognition as one of Atlantic Canada's most promising young goaltenders while competing in regional hockey and major junior competitions. During his junior career, he posted some of the best statistical performances in recent Quebec Major Junior Hockey League (QMJHL) history, including numerous single-season records and awards.

# Early Life and Youth Hockey

Ellis was born in River Denys, Nova Scotia, and grew up in the Cape Breton region. He began his hockey career with local youth programs before progressing to competitive levels. Playing for the Cape Breton West Islanders, a major midget team, Ellis gained provincial recognition and led his team to the Telus Cup national championship in 2017. The victory marked the first time an Atlantic Canada team had won the national midget title. During the tournament, Ellis was named player of the game in an opening match, stopping 34 shots in a 2-1 victory.[2][3][4][1]

# Junior Hockey Career

### Cape Breton Screaming Eagles

Ellis was drafted by the Cape Breton Screaming Eagles in the fourth round (59th overall) of the 2016 QMJHL Entry Draft. He appeared in three games for the Eagles during the 2016-17 season before being traded to the Rimouski Océanic.[1][2]

### Rimouski Océanic

After his trade to Rimouski, Ellis established himself as a starting goaltender for the Océanic. During the 2017-18 season, his first full year with the team, Ellis posted a 33-8-1 record with 2.35 goals-against average, a .913 save percentage, and six shutouts in 51 games. The following season, he recorded 27 wins and three shutouts across 46 games. In his final season with Rimouski (2019-20), Ellis compiled 18 wins and two shutouts in 29 games while maintaining a 2.41 goals-against average and .904 save percentage.[1]

### Charlottetown Islanders

Ellis completed his junior career with the Charlottetown Islanders during the 2020-21 season, which took place under COVID-19 restrictions. In a remarkably successful season, Ellis compiled a 23-1-0 record across 24 games, posting a 1.78 goals-against average, a .926 save percentage, and seven shutouts. His performance led the QMJHL in wins and established a new career QMJHL shutout record. Ellis also earned the award for best goaltender of the year and received a spot on the first all-star team. Following this exceptional final junior season, he signed a three-year entry-level contract with the St. Louis Blues.[5][1]

# Professional Career

### St. Louis Blues Organization

Ellis signed with the St. Louis Blues in February 2021 and began his professional career in their minor league system. During the 2021-22 season, his rookie year, Ellis split time between the Worcester Railers of the ECHL and the Springfield Thunderbirds of the American Hockey League (AHL). With Worcester, he appeared in 34 games with a 15-13-5 record, 3.21 goals-against average, and .905 save percentage. He earned six combined AHL appearances with Springfield.[1]

The 2022-23 season proved challenging for Ellis. With limited opportunities in Springfield due to competition from other prospects, he was assigned to the ECHL's Tulsa Oilers, where he appeared in 27 games. Midway through the season, Ellis underwent hip surgery in early 2023, which ended his season early. During his rehabilitation at his home in Nova Scotia, Ellis underwent a significant perspective shift regarding his career. He later credited this time away from hockey with helping him rediscover his passion for the sport and improving his mental approach to competition.[6]

Ellis returned to action with the Orlando Solar Bears of the ECHL during the 2023-24 season, appearing in 21 games with improved statistics including a 2.45 goals-against average and .923 save percentage. He was subsequently called up to Springfield in January 2024 and did not return to the ECHL. During the 2024-25 season, Ellis established himself as a primary goaltender for Springfield, recording a 22-14-3 record, a 2.63 goals-against average, a .922 save percentage, and three shutouts across 42 games.[1]

### Buffalo Sabres

In October 2025, the St. Louis Blues placed Ellis on waivers, and the Buffalo Sabres claimed him. Ellis made his NHL debut on October 22, 2025, against the Detroit Red Wings, earning a 4-2 victory and stopping 31 of 33 shots for a .939 save percentage. With this appearance, Ellis became the 770th player in ECHL history to reach the NHL and the first Cape Breton-born goaltender to earn a victory in the league.[7][8][9][1]

Following his debut, Ellis saw increased playing time with the Sabres. By mid-November 2025, he had compiled a 3-1-0 record across early appearances, including a notable performance against the Edmonton Oilers on November 17, where he recorded a .970 save percentage in a 5-1 victory. As of December 2025, Ellis had appeared in multiple games for Buffalo and was competing for time in the team's goaltending rotation alongside other netminders.[10][1]

# Playing Style

Ellis is described as an athletic goaltender with strong positioning and quick reflexes. He demonstrates good mechanical form and maintains composure during games. His development as a goaltender has emphasized consistency and mental resilience, particularly following his return from hip surgery.[2]

# Career Statistics

Ellis holds a career QMJHL shutout record and previously set the best goals-against average single-season record in the league. His 2020-21 season with Charlottetown ranks among the most successful single seasons by a goaltender in recent QMJHL history.[1]

## Personal Life

Ellis is from River Denys, Nova Scotia. He has a brother named Matthew Ellis and a cousin named Chase Ellis, also in hockey.[11]

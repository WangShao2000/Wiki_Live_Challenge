# Brave Bunnies
**Brave Bunnies** (Ukrainian: *Хоробрі зайці*, romanized: *Khorobri zaytsi*) is a Ukrainian-origin animated television series aimed at preschool children aged 2 to 5\. Created by Olga Cherepanova and originally developed by the Kyiv-based production company Glowberry, the series has gained international prominence not only for its distinct visual style and social-emotional curriculum but also for its complex production history marked by the 2022 Russian invasion of Ukraine.

The series follows a family of "Brave Bunnies"—siblings Bop and Boo, along with their parents Ma and Pa and the "Wee Bunny Babies"—as they embark on a perpetual road trip around the world in their Bunny Bus. The core narrative engine of the franchise is the exploration of diversity; in each episode, the protagonists meet animals of different species, temperaments, and physical abilities, necessitating the invention of new games to bridge these differences[1] .

As of December 2025, the series comprises two seasons totaling 104 episodes. It has been broadcast in over 80 territories worldwide, including major markets such as the United Kingdom, Italy, Australia, Germany, and Canada[1] . The property is currently managed through a multinational partnership led by the Canadian media conglomerate WildBrain, which acquired a majority stake in the intellectual property (IP) in 2022 to rescue the production following the displacement of the original Ukrainian team[3] .

## 1\. Concept and Origins
### 1.1 Genesis and Vision
The conceptual foundation of *Brave Bunnies* was laid by Olga Cherepanova, a creative producer and author at Glowberry. The genesis of the project was driven by a desire to create a "Great Journey" narrative that could serve as a metaphor for a child’s early socialization experiences. Cherepanova identified a gap in the preschool market for content that specifically addressed the nuances of "first contact"—the moment a child steps out of their family circle and encounters peers who are fundamentally different from themselves[2] .

The series was explicitly designed to function as a tool for social-emotional learning (SEL). The "Brave" in the title refers not to physical acts of heroism, but to the social courage required to engage with the unknown. The show’s motto, "We might seem different at first, but we’ll find a way to be friends and play together," encapsulates its didactic goal: to teach tolerance, flexibility, and empathy[7] .

### 1.2 Psychological Foundation
From its inception, the development of *Brave Bunnies* was distinguished by a rigorous reliance on psychological consultation. The scriptwriting process involved active collaboration with child psychologists to ensure that the scenarios depicted were developmentally appropriate for the 2–5 age demographic[2] .

**Natalia Pashko**, a practical psychologist at the Municipal Center for Children in Ukraine, served as a key consultant, particularly for the accompanying literary adaptations. The creative team focused on identifying "sensitive periods" in early childhood development—phases where children are particularly receptive to learning social cues. The narrative structure was crafted to model "joint ritual play," a psychological concept where shared structured activities (games) serve as a bridge between isolated individuals[8] .

This psychological underpinning is evident in the show's conflict resolution mechanism. Unlike many adventure series where the antagonist is an external villain, the "antagonist" in *Brave Bunnies* is often a barrier to communication or interaction—such as a size difference (a bear is too big for a bunny game) or a temperament clash (a mouse is too shy to pop bubbles). The resolution requires the characters to pause, reflect, and devise a "Bunny Brilliant Idea"—a cognitive reframing of the situation that modifies the game to include everyone[2] .

### 1.3 Visual Identity and Art Direction
The visual aesthetic of *Brave Bunnies* was established by **Anna Sarvira**, a celebrated Ukrainian illustrator and co-founder of the Pictoric Illustrators Club[10] . Sarvira served as the Art Director from 2019 to 2023, creating a style that diverged significantly from the 3D CGI animation that dominated the preschool landscape in the late 2010s[10] .

Sarvira's direction resulted in a 2D digital animation style characterized by:

* **Vector-Based Flat Design:** A "cut-out" aesthetic that emphasizes silhouette and shape over depth and texture.  
* **Non-Standard Color Palettes:** The use of sophisticated color combinations (teals, ochres, soft purples) rather than the primary-heavy palettes typical of commercial preschool TV.  
* **Expressive Minimalism:** Character designs that convey emotion through posture and simple facial cues, making the animation readable across cultural boundaries[10] .

The art style was not merely decorative but functional; the clarity of the design was intended to make the diversity of the characters (e.g., the difference between a tall llama and a small bunny) immediately visually legible to toddlers[12] .

## 2\. Production History
The production of *Brave Bunnies* is a bifurcated history, split by the geopolitical tragedy of the 2022 invasion of Ukraine. It represents a rare instance in the animation industry where a production pipeline was dismantled by war and reconstructed on a different continent to ensure the survival of the IP.

### 2.1 Phase I: The Pan-European Co-Production (2019–2021)
Production of the first season began in 2019 following a co-production agreement between **Glowberry** (Ukraine) and **Ánima Kitchent** (Spain)[6] . This partnership was strategic, combining Glowberry's creative origination with Ánima's extensive technical pipeline and experience in the global animation market.

* **Creative Hub:** Kyiv, Ukraine (Scripts, Design, Direction).  
* **Animation Hub:** Madrid/Canary Islands, Spain (Animation, Compositing).

In January 2020, the project received a massive boost when **Aardman Animations**—the legendary British studio behind *Wallace & Gromit*—signed on as the global distribution partner for Season 1[14] . Aardman’s involvement acted as a seal of quality, facilitating pre-sales to major broadcasters like Nick Jr. and Super RTL before the series was even completed[7] .

The first season, consisting of 52 episodes of 7 minutes each, was delivered successfully despite the logistical challenges posed by the COVID-19 pandemic in 2020\. The series premiered in Ukraine in December 2020 and rolled out globally throughout 2021[1] .

### 2.2 The Disruption: Russian Invasion of Ukraine (2022)
The trajectory of the franchise was violently interrupted on February 24, 2022, with the full-scale Russian invasion of Ukraine. The Glowberry studio in Kyiv was forced to suspend operations as the city came under bombardment.

The impact on the team was profound:

* **Displacement:** A significant portion of the Glowberry staff, including creator Olga Cherepanova and key creatives, were forced to flee their homes, becoming refugees in various European countries[1] .  
* **Career Pivots:** The war altered the life trajectories of the staff. Notably, production coordinator **Natalia Yermak** left the animation industry to become a war correspondent for *The New York Times*, documenting the atrocities of the conflict rather than managing production schedules[1] .  
* **Artistic Shift:** Art Director Anna Sarvira spoke publicly about the psychological trauma of the invasion, noting that the concept of "safety"—a core theme of the show—had been stripped from their reality. Her personal work shifted during this period to illustrating Ukrainian resilience[15] .

Production on Season 2, which was already in development, was indefinitely halted. The assets and intellectual property were at risk of becoming "frozen" due to the inability of the Ukrainian entity to fulfill contractual delivery obligations[1] .

### 2.3 Phase II: The WildBrain Acquisition and Rescue (2022–Present)
Recognizing the value of the IP and the perilous situation of the Ukrainian partners, a rescue deal was facilitated by **Big Picture Licensing** involving **WildBrain**, a global leader in kids' and family entertainment based in Canada[13] .

On December 1, 2022, a restructuring agreement was announced:

* **Majority Acquisition:** WildBrain acquired a majority ownership stake in the *Brave Bunnies* IP[3] .  
* **Stakeholder Roles:** Glowberry and Ánima Kitchent retained minority equity stakes.  
* **Production Relocation:** The production of Season 2 was moved to WildBrain’s animation studio in **Vancouver, Canada**. This ensured a stable infrastructure for the show while maintaining the creative input of Cherepanova and the original creators[3] .  
* **Global Management:** WildBrain assumed responsibility for all global distribution and consumer products (licensing), leveraging its "360-degree" infrastructure[3] .

This deal was explicitly framed by WildBrain CEO Eric Ellenbogen as a necessary intervention to "ensure the future of this wonderful property" following the "terrible events in Ukraine"[17] . It allowed for the resumption of production in late 2022, with Season 2 delivery commencing in June 2023[3] .

## 3\. Narrative Architecture and Characters
The narrative of *Brave Bunnies* is structured around a "Road Trip" format. This device allows for infinite scalability, as the bus can travel to any biome (Jungle, Savanna, Forest, Mountains) to facilitate new character interactions without altering the core cast.

### 3.1 The Core Family
The series centers on the Bunny family, who live a nomadic lifestyle aboard the Bunny Bus.

* **Bop:** The older brother. He is characterized by high energy, adventurousness, and a propensity for physical play. He is voiced in the English dub by **Wilbur Conabeare**.18  
* **Boo:** The younger sister. She is portrayed as observant, empathetic, and often the character who notices the emotional subtleties of the new friends they meet. She is voiced by **Margot Powell**.18  
* **Ma:** The matriarch of the family. She is the driver of the Bunny Bus, subverting the traditional trope of the father as the driver. She is voiced by **Harriet Kershaw**.18  
* **Pa:** The patriarch. He is supportive, engaged in childcare, and often participates in the logistics of setting up camp. He is voiced by **Jensen Adeyanju**.18  
* **The Bunny Babies:** Four infant siblings who serve as a "Greek Chorus" of cuteness. They accompany the family but rarely participate in the central adventure, serving to emphasize Bop and Boo's role as the "big kids"[1] .

### 3.2 The Bunny Bus
The Bunny Bus is treated almost as a sentient character. It is a stylized, rabbit-shaped vehicle that serves as the mobile home for the family. Its arrival and departure bookend every episode, providing a ritualistic "start" and "end" to the adventure that offers security to the preschool viewer[9] .

### 3.3 The "Friends" and Diversity
The supporting cast consists of the "Best Bunny Friends" (BBFs) met along the way. Each friend represents a specific attribute of diversity.

* **Physical Diversity:** Characters like **Jeff the Giraffe** or **Barry the Bear** introduce size disparities that must be navigated during play[12] .  
* **Temperamental Diversity:** In the episode "Bubble Trouble," the bunnies meet **Midge the Mouse**. Midge is terrified of the popping bubbles that Bop and Boo find delightful. The episode's narrative arc focuses on validating Midge's fear ("It's okay to be scared") and then gently desensitizing her to it through explanation and controlled exposure, allowing her to eventually join the game[9] .  
* **Cultural/Lifestyle Diversity:** In Season 2, the narrative expands to visiting these friends *inside* their homes, exploring their unique family structures and lifestyles, thereby deepening the curriculum on cultural literacy[17] .

## 4\. Broadcast and Global Distribution
The distribution strategy for *Brave Bunnies* has been aggressive and highly successful, achieving penetration in over 80 territories within two years of its launch[1] .

### 4.1 Domestic Significance (Ukraine)
The world premiere on **PlusPlus** (Ukraine) on **December 5, 2020**, was a landmark event for the Ukrainian animation industry[2] *Brave Bunnies* was marketed as a national achievement—a high-quality, Western-style animated product that originated entirely in Kyiv. The show quickly became one of the top-rated children's programs in the country[1] .

### 4.2 The "Big Five" Western Markets
The series secured placement on public service broadcasters and major commercial networks in the most lucrative Western markets, a rarity for Eastern European animation.

* **United Kingdom:** The series premiered on **February 6, 2021**, on **Milkshake\!** (Channel 5's preschool block). It also secured a slot on **Nick Jr. UK**, giving it dual exposure on free-to-air and pay-TV platforms. The UK market was critical for establishing the brand's credibility globally[21] .  
* **Italy:** Premiered on **Rai Yoyo** (the national broadcaster's children's channel) on **April 19, 2021**, and on the pay-TV channel **DeA Junior**.22 Italy has been one of the strongest markets for the property, leading to early publishing deals[13] .  
* **Australia:** The series was acquired by the Australian Broadcasting Corporation (ABC) and premiered on **ABC Kids** in **January 2021**.21  
* **Germany:** Broadcast on **Super RTL** (within the Toggolino block), the dominant kids' network in the region[2] .  
* **Spain:** Airs on **Clan TV** (RTVE), leveraging the co-production status with Ánima Kitchent to secure a prime slot[23] .

### 4.3 Expansion and Streaming
By late 2023, following the WildBrain acquisition, distribution expanded further:

* **Canada:** **Family Jr.** (a WildBrain network) acquired exclusive linear rights for Season 1[16] .  
* **Poland:** Acquired by **TeleTOON+** (Canal+ Poland)[4] .  
* **South Korea:** Acquired by **CNC Media International** and **Daekyo NoriQ**.4  
* **Streaming:** The series is available on **HBO Max** (Latin America, EMEA), **Amazon Prime Video**, **RaiPlay**, **My5**, and the **WildBrain+** SVOD channel on Comcast (USA)[4] .

## 5\. Educational Curriculum and Social Impact
The educational value of *Brave Bunnies* has been a central pillar of its marketing and reception. The show operates within the framework of Social-Emotional Learning (SEL), specifically targeting the "Self-Community-World" approach often cited by educational experts[24] .

### 5.1 The "First Socialization" Model
The show addresses the developmental milestone where children move from "parallel play" (playing next to each other) to "cooperative play" (playing with each other toward a common goal). The scripts are engineered to show this transition in real-time.

* **The Conflict:** Usually stems from a failure of parallel play strategies when applied to a diverse peer.  
* **The Solution:** The characters verbalize the problem ("The bubble popped and scared Midge") and negotiate a solution ("We can blow smaller bubbles," or "We can explain what bubbles are").  
* **The Outcome:** Cooperative play is established.

### 5.2 Research and Validation
The effectiveness of this curriculum has been validated by third-party research. A focus group study conducted by the British research agency **Childwise** in 2020 yielded significant results:

* **79%** of children aged 3–5 reported feeling "happier" after watching the series.  
* 90% of the children expressed a strong liking for the characters[1] .  
  These metrics suggest that the show succeeds in creating a "safe container" for children to explore complex emotions like fear of the unknown.

### 5.3 Industry Recognition
The show’s commitment to diversity has been recognized by **Common Sense Media**, the leading US-based advocacy group for families. The organization awarded the series a "Selection for Families" seal, praising its positive messages and "gentle storytelling"[25] .

## 6\. Commercial Ecosystem: Licensing and Merchandising
As a "360-degree" franchise, *Brave Bunnies* was designed from the outset to extend beyond the screen into physical play, reinforcing the show's curriculum through toys and books.

### 6.1 Toy Licensing
**Spin Master**, the Canadian toy giant responsible for *PAW Patrol*, was appointed as the **Global Master Toy Partner**.13 This was a significant endorsement, as Spin Master rarely commits to new IP without substantial evidence of potential.

* **Product Lines:** The toy line, launched in 2022, includes plush toys of Bop and Boo, articulated figurines of the diverse animal friends, and playsets featuring the Bunny Bus[12] .  
* **Integration:** The toys are designed to replicate the "play mechanics" of the show, encouraging children to enact their own "Bunny Brilliant Ideas."

### 6.2 Publishing
**Penguin Random House** serves as the multi-territory publishing partner[13] .

* **Italian Market:** A specific partnership with **De Agostini (DeA Libri)** resulted in the first collection of books being published in Italy in September 2021\.  
* **Ukrainian Market:** Glowberry published domestic titles such as *Brave Bunnies in the Jungle* (2018) early in the brand's life to seed the market. These books were co-developed with psychologist Natalia Pashko to ensure they functioned as bibliotherapy tools for parents[8] .

### 6.3 Licensing Structure
Following the 2022 restructuring, the licensing rights are managed as follows:

* **Global (excluding Ukraine):** Managed by **WildBrain CPLG**.  
* **Ukraine:** Retained by **Glowberry**.  
* **Italy/Spain:** Managed by **KidsMe** and **Ánima Kitchent**.4

## 7\. Critical Reception and Awards
The series has garnered critical acclaim for its aesthetics and its ethical stance.

* **Animation Magazine** highlighted the show as a "breakthrough for the Ukrainian animation world," noting that it looked "like nothing else" on the slate due to Anna Sarvira's art direction[14] .  
* **The New Voice of Ukraine** praised the show for its message of diversity, interpreting it as a reflection of Ukraine's integration into the broader European cultural context[1] .  
* **Awards:**  
  * **MIPJunior 2020:** Most viewed program in the screening library[27] .  
  * **Quirino Awards:** Nominated for Best Animation Series (recognizing the Ibero-American co-production connection)[28] .  
  * **Cartoon Forum:** Finalist and highly acclaimed pitch[6] .

## 8\. Future Outlook
As of late 2025, *Brave Bunnies* stands as a resilient franchise. Season 2 has completed its rollout across global partners, and the show remains a staple on major networks like Milkshake\! and Rai Yoyo. The partnership with WildBrain has stabilized the brand, allowing Glowberry to continue its creative contributions despite the ongoing challenges in Ukraine.

The series is widely regarded not just as a successful children's entertainment product, but as a symbol of the resilience of the Ukrainian creative sector. It serves as a case study in how international industry solidarity (exemplified by Aardman, Spin Master, and WildBrain) can preserve cultural capital during times of war.

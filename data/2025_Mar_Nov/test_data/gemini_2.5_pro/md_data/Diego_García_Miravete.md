# Diego García Miravete
**Diego García Miravete** is a Mexican American football coach, former player, and industrial engineer, widely recognized as one of the most successful and influential figures in the history of the sport in Mexico[1] . He is best known for establishing and leading the **Cóndores UNAM** dynasty during the 1970s and 1980s, a period in which the team representing the National Autonomous University of Mexico (UNAM) achieved unprecedented dominance in the Organización Nacional Estudiantil de Fútbol Americano (ONEFA)[1] .

His career, which spans more than four decades, includes winning nine national championships with UNAM—three as a player and six as a head coach—cementing his status as a pivotal figure in the university's athletic history[1] . Beyond the collegiate level, García Miravete has coached professionally in Europe, leading teams in Italy and Spain, and has directed the Mexican national team to victories in international competitions such as the Aztec Bowl and the NFL Global Junior Championship. In 2017, he was inducted into the **Mexican Hall of Fame of American Football** (*Salón de la Fama del Fútbol Americano de México*).

## Early Life and Education
Diego García Miravete was born in Mexico City, Mexico. He enrolled at the National Autonomous University of Mexico (UNAM) in the mid-1960s, a time when the university was not only the academic heart of the nation but also a focal point for social and cultural transformation. He pursued and completed a degree in **Industrial Engineering**. During his time as a student, he balanced the rigorous demands of an engineering curriculum with the intense physical and time commitments of varsity athletics.

### The 1968 Student Movement Context
García Miravete’s university years coincided with the Mexican student movement of 1968, a seminal event in the country’s modern history. As a student-athlete at UNAM, he was present on campus during the period leading up to the Tlatelolco massacre. In interviews, he has described the atmosphere on campus as initially vibrant and "amazing," noting that "there were no big movements before, then suddenly everything was happening at UNAM".

He recalled the privilege of being part of the university community during such a politically charged era, although the environment shifted rapidly from student activism to tragedy. He witnessed the confusion and trauma that engulfed the student body following the violent crackdowns, noting the pervasive silence and lack of answers in the immediate aftermath: "Everybody was expecting questions like, 'What happened?... And yet, nothing'". This period of civil unrest shaped the generation of students and athletes at UNAM, embedding a sense of resilience and institutional identity that would later characterize the spirit of the football programs García Miravete played for and coached.

## Playing Career (1966–1971)
García Miravete enjoyed a highly successful playing career as a defensive end and linebacker, positions that required a blend of physical imposition and tactical intelligence. His tenure as a player spanned from 1966 to 1971, a transition period for American football at UNAM.

### Pumas UNAM (1966–1969)
He began his collegiate career with the **Pumas UNAM** (Pumas Dorados), the varsity team that represented the entire university institution. During the late 1960s, the Pumas were a powerhouse in Mexican college football, engaged in a fierce and legendary rivalry with the Águilas Blancas of the Instituto Politécnico Nacional (IPN). As a member of the Pumas, García Miravete contributed to a squad that secured three national championships. These victories were significant in establishing UNAM's athletic superiority during a decade of intense collegiate competition.

### Cóndores UNAM (1970–1971)
In 1970, the structure of football at UNAM underwent a significant reorganization. The single representative team (Pumas Dorados) was disbanded in favor of creating multiple teams representing different faculties or clusters of campuses to foster internal competition and increase student participation. García Miravete was assigned to the newly formed **Cóndores UNAM**, a team that would become synonymous with his name. He played his final two seasons (1970–1971) with the Cóndores, helping to lay the foundation for the program's future success.

## Coaching Career
Following his graduation and the conclusion of his playing days, García Miravete transitioned into coaching, applying the analytical skills from his engineering background to the gridiron. His coaching philosophy emphasized discipline, physical conditioning, and perfect execution, traits that would define his teams for decades.

### The Cóndores Dynasty (1976–1986)
García Miravete is most celebrated for his tenure as the head coach of the Cóndores UNAM. Under his leadership, the team transformed into the dominant force of the ONEFA, creating a dynasty that is often cited as the "Team of the Decade" for the 1980s.

#### The Rise to Power (Late 1970s)
Taking the helm of the Cóndores, García Miravete sought to break the parity of the league. In **1979**, he led the team to the ONEFA National Championship, marking the beginning of a historic era. This victory was not an isolated event but the start of a sustained period of excellence.

#### The Golden Era (1980s)
The 1980s saw the Cóndores, under García Miravete, engage in a fierce struggle for supremacy with the Águilas Blancas of the IPN, coached by the legendary Jacinto Licea. The rivalry between García Miravete and Licea became the central narrative of Mexican college football.

* **1980 Championship:** The Cóndores successfully defended their title, winning the 1980 championship.  
* **1983–1986 Dominance:** After a brief period where the Águilas Blancas claimed titles (1981, 1982), García Miravete’s squad roared back to win consecutive championships in **1983, 1984, 1985, and 1986**.3  
  * The 1983 title reclaimed the trophy for UNAM.  
  * The 1985 championship was particularly notable as it secured a "Tricampeonato" (three-peat), a feat that solidified the team's legendary status.  
  * In 1986, the Cóndores continued their reign, further widening the gap between them and the rest of the league.

During this period, García Miravete also served as a full-time professor at UNAM, demonstrating his commitment to the "student-athlete" model. He supervised youth football leagues year-round, ensuring a continuous pipeline of talent that was well-versed in his system before they even reached the varsity level.

### Professional Coaching in Europe
Seeking new challenges, García Miravete expanded his career to Europe, where the sport of American football was growing in popularity.

#### Italy (Cardinals and Corsari Palermo)
García Miravete coached in the Italian football leagues, taking charge of the **Cardinals Palermo** and later the **Corsari Palermo**. His tenure with the Corsari was marked by a remarkable coaching achievement: despite fielding a roster composed largely of rookies and inexperienced players, he guided the team to the league semifinals. The Corsari eventually lost in overtime to the Skorpions Varese, but the season is remembered for García Miravete's ability to maximize limited resources.

#### Spain (Badalona Drags)
He also served as the head coach of the **Badalona Drags** (now Dracs) in Spain. The Badalona franchise is one of the most historic and successful in Spain, and García Miravete's presence helped professionalize the coaching standards within the Spanish league (LNFA).

### Return to Mexico: The ITESM Era
Upon his return to Mexico, García Miravete joined the coaching ranks of the Instituto Tecnológico de Estudios Superiores de Monterrey (ITESM), the private university system that began to challenge the traditional dominance of UNAM and IPN in the 1990s and 2000s.

* **Borregos Salvajes Toluca:** He served on the coaching staff for the Toluca campus.  
* **Borregos Salvajes Santa Fe (2010–2014):** In May 2010, he was appointed head coach of the Borregos Salvajes Campus Santa Fe. He led the program for five seasons.  
  * **2011–2012:** Under his guidance, the team reached the CONADEIP playoffs in consecutive years, establishing the Santa Fe campus as a competitive program within the Premier Conference.  
  * **2013–2014:** The team struggled in his final two seasons, recording losing records before his departure in 2014\.

### UVM and Recent Years
Between his stints in Europe and at Borregos Santa Fe, García Miravete worked as an assistant coach for the **Linces UVM** (Universidad del Valle de México) from 2007 to 2009, serving under head coach Rafael Duk.

Most recently, he has been involved with the **Sharks de Aguascalientes** (sometimes transcribed as "Charts"), a football organization focused on youth and intermediate development. As part of the staff, he continues to mentor young players and coaches, leveraging his vast experience to build football culture in the region[2] .

## National Team Career
García Miravete is one of the most decorated national team coaches in Mexican history, having led various selections to international victories.

### The Aztec Bowl (*Tazón Azteca*)
He was selected as the head coach for the Mexican All-Star team in the **Aztec Bowl**, an annual postseason classic against collegiate all-stars from the United States (typically NCAA Division II/III).

* **1984:** Led Mexico to a victory over **Tarleton State University**.  
* **1986:** Head Coach of the Mexican selection.  
* **1990:** Head Coach of the Mexican selection.  
* **1991:** Led Mexico to a victory over **Southwestern Oklahoma State University**.

### NFL Global Junior Championship
In the late 1990s, the NFL organized the Global Junior Championship to promote the sport internationally during Super Bowl festivities. García Miravete was entrusted with the Mexican Junior National Team, leading them to immediate success.

* **1997:** **Champion**. Mexico won the inaugural tournament under his command.  
* **1998:** **Champion**. Successfully defended the title.  
* **1999:** **Runner-up**. Reached the final for the third consecutive year.

In 2014, he was named head coach of the Mexico national university team for the World University Championship in Sweden but ultimately declined the position due to prior commitments.

## Legacy and Impact
Diego García Miravete is revered as a foundational figure in Mexican American football. His induction into the **Mexican Hall of Fame of American Football** in 2017 was a formal recognition of a career that influenced generations of players and coaches.

* **Tactical Innovation:** He is credited with modernizing the tactical approach of UNAM football, moving away from pure athleticism to a more disciplined, schematic approach.  
* **Institutional Pride:** During the 1970s and 80s, his Cóndores teams served as a rallying point for the university community, restoring pride to UNAM athletics during a period of intense rivalry with IPN.  
* **International Respect:** His success in the NFL Global Junior Championships proved that Mexican players could compete with and defeat international counterparts, paving the way for the increased recruitment of Mexican players by foreign leagues.

## Personal Life
While a student at UNAM, García Miravete worked at the **Cuauhtémoc Moctezuma Brewery**, where he was responsible for designing packaging. He married in 1968, a pivotal year in his life both personally and historically. He has multiple sons, two of whom, **Diego** and **César**, followed in his footsteps to play American football, and whom he coached during his career.

Known affectionately by the nicknames "**Lapizote**" (Big Pencil) due to his height and build, and "**Flaco**" (Skinny One), García Miravete is also known for his fandom of the **Dallas Cowboys**.

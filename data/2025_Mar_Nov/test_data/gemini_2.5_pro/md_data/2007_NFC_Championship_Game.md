# 2007 NFC Championship Game
The **2007 NFC Championship Game** was a National Football League (NFL) postseason game played on January 20, 2008, to determine the National Football Conference (NFC) champion for the 2007 NFL season. The contest featured the visiting New York Giants, the NFC's fifth seed, facing the second-seeded Green Bay Packers at Lambeau Field in Green Bay, Wisconsin[1] . In a game characterized by sub-zero temperatures and dramatic momentum shifts, the Giants defeated the Packers 23–20 in overtime[1] . The victory secured the Giants' berth in Super Bowl XLII, where they would go on to defeat the undefeated New England Patriots in one of the most significant upsets in league history[2] .

The game is historically notable for several reasons, primarily the extreme weather conditions. With a kickoff temperature of −1 °F (−18 °C) and a wind chill of −23 °F (−31 °C), it stands as the second-coldest game in the history of Lambeau Field, trailing only the 1967 "Ice Bowl"[1] . The harsh elements profoundly influenced the gameplay, affecting ball security, kicking mechanics, and player endurance. The image of Giants head coach Tom Coughlin, his face flushed red from the freezing wind, became an enduring symbol of the match, which NFL Films and media outlets have subsequently dubbed "The Chilling Championship"[4] .

Beyond the weather, the game marked a pivotal transition in NFL history involving Green Bay quarterback Brett Favre. It served as Favre's final appearance for the Packers, the franchise with which he had spent 16 seasons, won three MVP awards, and captured Super Bowl XXXI[1] . His final pass attempt as a Packer was an interception in overtime, which set up the Giants' winning drive[1] . Conversely, the game is viewed as a defining moment for Giants quarterback Eli Manning, who orchestrated his third consecutive road playoff victory, dispelling criticisms regarding his consistency and leadership[6] .

The game was broadcast in the United States by the Fox network and was watched by approximately 53.9 million viewers, making it one of the most-watched television programs in American history at the time, surpassing viewership for major cultural events and previous playoff contests[7] .

## Background
The 2007 NFL season was defined by the New England Patriots' pursuit of a perfect season, but in the NFC, the narrative centered on the resurgence of the Green Bay Packers and the improbable, resilience-testing journey of the New York Giants. The two teams took divergent paths to the title game, with Green Bay dominating their division and New York battling through a volatile regular season to qualify as a Wild Card.

### Green Bay Packers Season
The Green Bay Packers finished the 2007 regular season with a record of 13–3, earning the NFC North division title and the number two seed in the conference playoffs[1] . Under the direction of head coach Mike McCarthy, the team rebounded from an 8–8 campaign in 2006\. The offense was led by 38-year-old quarterback Brett Favre, who experienced a renaissance season. Favre was selected to the Pro Bowl and named a second-team All-Pro, revitalizing an offense that featured a potent passing attack with wide receivers Donald Driver and Greg Jennings[8] .

Defensively, the Packers were anchored by defensive end Aaron Kampman and cornerback Al Harris, both of whom received Pro Bowl or All-Pro recognition[8] . The team secured a first-round bye in the playoffs, allowing them to rest before hosting the Seattle Seahawks in the Divisional Round.

In the Divisional Round, played on January 12, 2008, at Lambeau Field, the Packers faced snowy conditions in a matchup often referred to as the "Snow Globe Game." The Packers fell behind early, 14–0, but rallied emphatically to defeat Seattle 42–20.1 Running back Ryan Grant, despite fumbling twice early in the game, set a franchise playoff record with 201 rushing yards and three touchdowns. This dominant victory set the stage for Green Bay to host the NFC Championship, with the team and its fanbase anticipating a Super Bowl return for Favre.

### New York Giants Season
The New York Giants entered the 2007 season facing significant scrutiny. Head coach Tom Coughlin was widely considered to be on the "hot seat" following an 8–8 finish in 2006 and a Wild Card round exit[3] . The team started the 2007 season poorly, losing their first two games, including a 35–13 defeat to the Green Bay Packers in Week 2[9] . However, the Giants rallied to win six consecutive games, eventually finishing the regular season with a 10–6 record[3] .

Finishing second in the NFC East behind the Dallas Cowboys, the Giants entered the playoffs as the number five seed[3] . This seeding necessitated that they play all their postseason games on the road. The team's identity was forged in Week 17 of the regular season when, despite having already clinched a playoff spot, they played their starters against the undefeated New England Patriots. Although the Giants lost 38–35, the competitive nature of the game provided the team with a confidence boost heading into the postseason[9] .

The Giants' road to the NFC Championship was characterized by upsets over higher-seeded opponents, earning them the moniker "Road Warriors"[6] .

* **Wild Card Round:** On January 6, 2008, the Giants traveled to Raymond James Stadium and defeated the NFC South champion Tampa Bay Buccaneers, 24–14.3  
* **Divisional Round:** On January 13, 2008, the Giants faced their division rivals and the NFC's number one seed, the Dallas Cowboys, at Texas Stadium. The Giants won 21–17, marking the first time in NFL history that an NFC number four seed or lower defeated a number one seed in the divisional round[9] .

By defeating Dallas, the Giants extended their road winning streak to nine games and set up a rematch with the Packers[6] .

### Series History and Matchup
The 2007 NFC Championship Game was the seventh postseason meeting between the Giants and the Packers[9] . The rivalry between the two franchises dates back to the league's early years, featuring five NFL Championship Games played between 1938 and 1962[1] .

* **1938 NFL Championship:** Giants defeated Packers 23–17.  
* **1939 NFL Championship:** Packers defeated Giants 27–0.  
* **1944 NFL Championship:** Packers defeated Giants 14–7.  
* **1961 NFL Championship:** Packers defeated Giants 37–0.  
* **1962 NFL Championship:** Packers defeated Giants 16–7.

In more recent history, the Giants had defeated the Packers in the 2002 Wild Card playoffs, but the Packers had not lost a playoff game at Lambeau Field prior to the 2002 season (when they lost to the Atlanta Falcons)[5] .

Entering the 2007 NFC Championship, the Packers were considered the favorites. Oddsmakers established Green Bay as a 7.5-point favorite[1] . The Packers' 35–13 victory over the Giants in Week 2 of the regular season was cited as a primary reason for the confidence in Green Bay, although observers noted that the Giants had evolved significantly as a team since September[9] .

## Pre-Game Conditions
### Weather
The weather forecast for Green Bay, Wisconsin, dominated the pre-game narrative. The National Weather Service issued a wind chill advisory for the region, predicting dangerous cold[1] . The conditions at kickoff confirmed these fears, creating one of the most hostile environments in NFL playoff history.

* **Temperature:** −1 °F (−18 °C)[1] .  
* **Wind Chill:** −23 °F (−31 °C)[1] .  
* **Wind Speed:** 12 miles per hour[10] .  
* **Conditions:** Clear skies, frozen turf[1] .

These conditions made the game the second-coldest in the history of Lambeau Field, a stadium renowned for its "frozen tundra" lore[4] . Only the 1967 "Ice Bowl" against the Dallas Cowboys, which featured a temperature of −13 °F (−25 °C) and a wind chill of −48 °F, was colder[4] .

The extreme cold had tangible effects on the game and the stadium experience. Reports indicated that beer sold in the stands froze inside the bottles before it could be consumed[4] . Hand warmers were distributed to the 72,740 fans in attendance[4] . On the sidelines, massive heaters were used to keep players warm, but the exposure to the elements was unavoidable. Giants head coach Tom Coughlin opted not to wear a face mask or scarf, and television cameras frequently captured his face turning a deep, frostbitten red, a visual that became emblematic of the game's intensity[4] . Giants center Shaun O'Hara later remarked, "I've never felt pain like that in my fingertips before"[4] .

### Venue
The game was held at **Lambeau Field**, the historic home of the Green Bay Packers. The stadium's natural grass surface was frozen hard, impacting footing and the behavior of the ball when it hit the ground. The attendance was a sellout of 72,740.1

### Officiating Crew
The game was officiated by referee **Terry McAulay** and his crew[1] .

## Game Summary
The game was a physical contest of attrition, with both teams relying heavily on field position and managing the difficult conditions. While Green Bay relied on big plays in the passing game, the Giants focused on a punishing ground attack to wear down the Packers' defense.

### First Quarter
The game began with the Packers winning the coin toss and electing to receive, looking to establish early dominance at home. However, the first quarter was defined by defensive stalemates as both teams adjusted to the frozen ball and footing.

The Giants managed to construct the first scoring drive of the game late in the quarter. Utilizing the running back tandem of Brandon Jacobs and Ahmad Bradshaw, New York moved into scoring position. The drive stalled in the red zone, leading to a field goal attempt. Kicker Lawrence Tynes successfully converted a 29-yard field goal with 4:50 remaining in the first quarter, giving the Giants a 3–0 lead[12] .

**Score at end of First Quarter:** New York Giants 3, Green Bay Packers 0[1] .

### Second Quarter
The second quarter saw an explosion of scoring as both offenses found a rhythm despite the cold.

The Giants extended their lead early in the period. After a drive spanning 7 plays and 38 yards, the offense was halted again by the Packers' defense. Tynes entered the game and kicked a 37-yard field goal at the 11:41 mark, extending the lead to 6–0.1

The Packers responded immediately and explosively. On the very first play of their ensuing possession, Brett Favre dropped back and launched a deep pass down the sideline to wide receiver Donald Driver. Driver caught the ball and outran the Giants' secondary for a 90-yard touchdown reception[1] . This play stands as the longest pass play in Green Bay Packers postseason history[1] . Mason Crosby’s extra point gave Green Bay their first lead of the game at 7–6 with 11:18 remaining in the half[12] .

Following the touchdown, the game settled back into a defensive rhythm until the final minutes of the half. The Packers mounted another scoring drive, covering 29 yards in 8 plays. The short field, set up by field position, allowed Green Bay to add to their lead. With 1:30 remaining in the second quarter, Mason Crosby converted a 36-yard field goal, making the score 10–6.1

The Giants attempted a two-minute drill to narrow the gap before halftime. They drove 40 yards downfield, but the drive ended when the Packers' defense recorded a sack on fourth down, resulting in a turnover on downs[1] . The Packers ran one play to exhaust the clock.

**Halftime Score:** Green Bay Packers 10, New York Giants 6[1] .

### Third Quarter
The Giants emerged from the halftime break with a renewed commitment to the running game. The cold weather began to fatigue the defense, and the Giants' physical offensive line began to open lanes for Jacobs and Bradshaw.

New York regained the lead on their opening possession of the second half. The drive culminated in a 1-yard touchdown run by the 264-pound running back Brandon Jacobs with 7:56 remaining in the quarter[12] . Lawrence Tynes added the extra point, putting the Giants ahead 13–10.

The lead changed hands again quickly. Favre, displaying the improvisation that defined his career, led the Packers downfield. He capped the drive with a 12-yard touchdown pass to tight end Donald Lee with 5:00 remaining in the quarter. Crosby's extra point restored the Packers' lead at 17–13.12

Refusing to fold, the Giants responded with another touchdown drive. This time, it was the agile rookie running back Ahmad Bradshaw who finished the drive with a 4-yard touchdown run at the 2:12 mark[12] . The successful conversion gave the Giants a 20–17 lead heading into the final quarter, setting up a dramatic finish.

**Score at end of Third Quarter:** New York Giants 20, Green Bay Packers 17[1] .

### Fourth Quarter
The fourth quarter was a tense affair filled with missed opportunities and special teams drama.

Early in the quarter, the Packers capitalized on a scoring opportunity to tie the game. Mason Crosby kicked a 37-yard field goal with 11:46 remaining, leveling the score at 20–20.12

For the remainder of regulation, the Giants had multiple chances to secure the victory, but special teams execution faltered in the freezing conditions.

1. **First Miss:** With 6:49 remaining in regulation, the Giants drove into field goal range. Lawrence Tynes attempted a 43-yard field goal. Tynes later admitted he misplayed the wind, which was gusting at 15 mph, and the kick hooked wide left[6] .  
2. **Second Miss:** In the final minute of the game, Eli Manning connected with rookie wide receiver Steve Smith on passes of 15, 14, and 11 yards, moving the Giants deep into Packers territory[13] . With just 4 seconds remaining on the clock, the Giants lined up for a game-winning 36-yard field goal. The snap from rookie long snapper Jay Alford was high, disrupting the timing of holder Jeff Feagles. Tynes managed to kick the ball, but the disruption resulted in a low "knuckleball" trajectory that hooked wide left as time expired[6] .

Tynes was visibly distraught, having missed two potential game-winning kicks in the fourth quarter of a conference championship game. The game headed to overtime tied at 20\.

**Score at end of Regulation:** New York Giants 20, Green Bay Packers 20[1] .

### Overtime
The game utilized the sudden-death overtime rules in effect at the time, meaning the first team to score any points would be declared the winner. The Packers won the coin toss and elected to receive, giving Brett Favre and the offense the first opportunity to win the game[1] .

The Packers began their drive at their own 26-yard line. On the first play, Ryan Grant rushed for a minimal gain. On the second play of overtime, Favre dropped back and looked for Donald Driver. He threw a pass intended for the receiver, but Giants cornerback Corey Webster read the play, jumped the route, and intercepted the ball[1] . Webster returned the interception to the Green Bay 34-yard line, placing the Giants immediately in scoring range.

This interception would historically stand as Brett Favre's final pass attempt as a member of the Green Bay Packers[1] .

The Giants took over with excellent field position. Unlike regulation, where they moved the ball aggressively, they ran three conservative plays that netted only five yards, setting up a fourth down[1] . Despite Tynes' two previous misses, head coach Tom Coughlin sent the kicking unit onto the field for a 47-yard attempt—longer than the 43-yarder Tynes had missed earlier.

With 2:35 elapsed in overtime, Tynes approached the ball. The snap and hold were clean. Tynes struck the ball firmly, and it sailed through the uprights, good for a 47-yard field goal[6] . Tynes immediately sprinted toward the locker room tunnel in celebration as his teammates mobbed him on the field. The Giants had won 23–20.

## Statistical Analysis
### Team Statistics
The statistical profile of the game highlights the Giants' dominance in the running game and time of possession, which proved critical in the arctic conditions.

* **Rushing Yards:** The Giants rushed for **134 yards**, while the Packers were held to just **28 yards** on the ground[1] . This disparity allowed New York to control the tempo and keep the Packers' offense off the field.  
* **Total Plays:** The Giants ran significantly more offensive plays than Green Bay, wearing down the Packers' defense in the extreme cold.  
* **Turnovers:** The Giants played a clean game with zero turnovers. The Packers committed two turnovers, including the fatal interception in overtime[1] .

### Individual Performances
**New York Giants:**

* **Eli Manning:** Manning managed the game efficiently in difficult conditions, avoiding turnovers. He utilized his wide receivers, particularly Plaxico Burress, effectively to move the chains.  
* **Plaxico Burress:** Burress was a dominant force, recording **11 receptions**, which proved vital for the Giants' ball-control strategy[14] .  
* **Brandon Jacobs & Ahmad Bradshaw:** The running back duo combined for the bulk of the Giants' 134 rushing yards and scored both of the team's touchdowns[12] .  
* **Lawrence Tynes:** Despite missing two field goals in the fourth quarter, Tynes finished 3-for-5 on field goal attempts, including the game-winner[13] .

**Green Bay Packers:**

* **Brett Favre:** Favre finished with a mixed performance. While he threw a 90-yard touchdown pass, he struggled to sustain drives consistently and threw the game-ending interception.  
* **Donald Driver:** Driver was the standout performer for Green Bay, catching 5 passes for **141 yards** and a touchdown[1] .  
* **Ryan Grant:** After a record-setting performance the previous week, Grant was neutralized by the Giants' defense, gaining negligible yardage on the ground.

## Broadcasting and Media
The game was broadcast nationally in the United States on the **Fox** network. The commentary team consisted of Joe Buck providing play-by-play, Troy Aikman providing color commentary, and Pam Oliver and Chris Myers serving as sideline reporters[1] .

### Television Ratings
The 2007 NFC Championship Game drew massive television ratings, reflecting the popularity of the two franchises and the dramatic nature of the contest.

* **Viewership:** The broadcast averaged **53.9 million viewers**.7 Other reports cite the figure at 48.6 million viewers 15, but regardless of the specific metric used, it was the most-watched television program since the series finale of the sitcom *Seinfeld* in 1998[7] .  
* **Ratings:** The game earned a 31.7 overnight rating and a 46 share, meaning nearly half of all televisions in use at the time were tuned to the game[15] .  
* **Comparison:** The viewership represented a significant increase over the previous year's NFC Championship Game (43.2 million) and the AFC Championship Game played earlier the same day (Patriots vs. Chargers)[7] .

### Cultural Impact
The game is frequently cited in NFL media as one of the most memorable playoff games of the 2000s. The visuals of the frozen breath of the players, the red face of Tom Coughlin, and the snow-hardened Lambeau Field turf have been used extensively in NFL Films productions. The game's nickname, "The Chilling Championship," reflects both the temperature and the dramatic tension of the overtime finish[4] .

## Aftermath and Legacy
### New York Giants: Super Bowl Champions
The victory advanced the Giants to **Super Bowl XLII**, played on February 3, 2008, in Glendale, Arizona. They entered the game as heavy underdogs against the New England Patriots, who had completed a perfect 16–0 regular season and were 18–0 entering the Super Bowl.

The Giants' win over Green Bay was their 10th consecutive road victory of the season, a streak that spanned both the regular season and the playoffs[6] . In the Super Bowl, the Giants defeated the Patriots 17–14, executing one of the biggest upsets in sports history and denying New England the first 19–0 season in NFL history[2] . The victory over the Packers is viewed as the toughest test the Giants faced during their playoff run, proving their ability to win in hostile environments.

### Brett Favre's Departure
The aftermath of the game for the Green Bay Packers was dominated by the saga of Brett Favre.

* **Retirement:** On March 6, 2008, less than two months after the loss, Favre held an emotional press conference announcing his retirement from professional football[17] . He cited mental fatigue and a desire to leave the game on his own terms.  
* **Un-retirement and Trade:** In the summer of 2008, Favre expressed a desire to return to the team. However, the Packers management, led by General Manager Ted Thompson and Head Coach Mike McCarthy, had committed to moving forward with quarterback Aaron Rodgers. This led to a tense standoff and Favre's eventual trade to the New York Jets in August 2008[1] .  
* **Legacy of the Game:** The interception thrown by Favre in overtime to Corey Webster remains the final play of his storied career in Green Bay[1] .

### Tom Coughlin's Job Security
For Giants head coach Tom Coughlin, the victory was a vindication. Entering the season on the "hot seat," Coughlin's job status was a constant subject of media speculation. The playoff run, characterized by discipline and physical play in adverse conditions, secured his tenure with the team. He would go on to coach the Giants to another Super Bowl victory (Super Bowl XLVI) four years later, again defeating the Packers at Lambeau Field in the playoffs along the way[9] .

### Lawrence Tynes' Redemption
Kicker Lawrence Tynes' performance in the game—missing two fourth-quarter field goals before making the winner in overtime—became a defining moment of his career. He is the only kicker in NFL history to have two postseason game-winning field goals in overtime (he kicked another one in the 2011 NFC Championship Game against the San Francisco 49ers)[19] . His resilience in the 2007 title game is often cited by coaches and players as an example of mental toughness.

# Everyone Hates Elon: A Comprehensive Analysis of the 2025 Anti-Billionaire Resistance Movement
## Executive Summary
The emergence of the activist collective **Everyone Hates Elon (EHE)** in early 2025 marks a significant pivot in the landscape of 21st-century protest movements. Originating in London, United Kingdom, as a direct response to the perceived political overreach of the billionaire class—specifically Tesla CEO Elon Musk—the group rapidly evolved from a localized "guerrilla marketing" operation into an internationally recognized brand of resistance.

This report provides an exhaustive examination of the group's genesis, operational structure, campaign strategies, and socio-economic impact. By synthesizing data from financial reports, Freedom of Information (FOI) requests, social media analytics, and press coverage, we analyze how a small, anonymous collective utilized the mechanisms of the attention economy to challenge the world's wealthiest individual. The report further explores the group's expansion beyond Musk to target figures such as Jeff Bezos, Donald Trump, and J.D. Vance, situating their activities within the broader "tech-lash" and anti-oligarchy sentiments of the mid-2020s.

## 1\. Socio-Political Genesis
### 1.1 The Context of 2025
To understand the formation of Everyone Hates Elon, one must first analyze the volatile socio-political climate of January 2025\. The global political landscape was characterized by a resurgence of right-wing populism, underscored by the second inauguration of Donald Trump as President of the United States. This period saw an unprecedented convergence of technology, capital, and state power, exemplified by the appointment of Elon Musk to the Department of Government Efficiency (DOGE)[1] .

In the United Kingdom, where the group was founded, public sentiment was increasingly critical of the influence of American tech oligarchs on domestic affairs. Elon Musk’s utilization of his social media platform, X (formerly Twitter), to comment on British riots and support figures like Tommy Robinson had already primed a segment of the British public for opposition[3] .

### 1.2 The Inciting Incident: The Inauguration "Salute"
The singular catalyst for the group's transition from private grievance to public action was the inauguration of Donald Trump in January 2025\. During the televised ceremony, Elon Musk was captured on video and in photographs performing a gesture—a raised arm with a flat palm—that was widely interpreted by critics as resembling a Nazi "Sieg Heil" salute[4] .

While supporters and some commentators argued the gesture was a harmless wave or a "Roman salute," the ambiguity of the action, combined with Musk's previous engagement with far-right memes and rhetoric, served as a flashpoint. For the founders of EHE, this image was not merely a gaffe but a semiotic declaration of alignment with authoritarianism. As noted in later interviews, the founders viewed this moment as the "point of no return," where the distinction between the eccentric billionaire and the political ideologue collapsed[4] .

### 1.3 Formation and Identity
Everyone Hates Elon was formally established in the weeks following the inauguration. The group emerged from what was described as a "ranty group chat" among a small circle of friends in London. Unlike traditional NGOs with boards and formal memberships, EHE adopted a decentralized, anonymous structure typical of Gen Z and Millennial digital activism[4] .

* **The Core Team:** The group is steered by a "handful of people," specifically a core operational team of three individuals. These members maintain strict anonymity to avoid legal retaliation from the litigious billionaire class and harassment from Musk’s fervent online fanbase[4] .  
* **The Mission:** The collective's stated purpose is to "hold Elon Musk to account" and prevent him from "poisoning our politics." They position themselves as a bulwark against the normalization of far-right ideology funded by tech wealth[8] .

## 2\. Organizational Architecture and Financial Model
A defining characteristic of Everyone Hates Elon is its sophisticated approach to resource mobilization. Despite its grassroots origins, the group developed a transparent and highly "gamified" funding model that leveraged the very behaviors they sought to critique.

### 2.1 The "People vs Elon" Fund
The group’s primary financial vehicle is a GoFundMe campaign titled "People vs Elon." By January 2025, the campaign had established a clear operational budget, seeking to raise £50,000 to sustain their activities through the spring of 2025[8] .

This transparency regarding "wages" is notable. Unlike many activist groups that rely on volunteer labor, EHE explicitly argued that professionalizing their resistance—allowing organizers to quit their day jobs—was necessary to combat a billionaire with unlimited resources. They framed this as "the power of people" versus "billionaire donors"[8] .

### 2.2 Gamification: The Pledge-Per-Tweet Model
Perhaps the group’s most innovative engagement strategy was the "Pledge Per Tweet" system. Recognizing that Elon Musk posts on X with high frequency (estimated by the group at roughly 2,000 times per month), EHE created a platform where supporters could pledge a micro-donation (e.g., 1p, 2p, 5p) for every tweet Musk published[10] .

* **Mechanism:** A donor pledging 1p per tweet would donate approximately £20 per month.  
* **Psychological Impact:** This model inverted the power dynamic of Musk’s social media addiction. Instead of his tweets generating revenue solely for X (via engagement), they now generated revenue for the group dedicated to his downfall. The group marketed this as "turning every single Elon Musk tweet into a weapon against him"[10] .  
* **Fund Distribution:** Proceeds from this specific stream were designated not just for EHE's operations, but also for donations to causes Musk "hates," such as refugee charities (e.g., Women for Refugee Women) and anti-racist organizations (e.g., Hope not Hate)[4] .

## 3\. The "Swasticar" Campaign: A Semiotic Offensive
In February 2025, Everyone Hates Elon launched its inaugural and most notorious public campaign: The "Swasticar" initiative. This campaign utilized guerrilla marketing tactics to permanently associate the Tesla brand with the imagery of the Nazi regime, capitalizing on the inauguration controversy.

### 3.1 The Neologism and Imagery
The campaign hinged on the creation of the portmanteau **"Swasticar"** (Swastika \+ Car). This linguistic device served as a sticky meme, transforming every Tesla vehicle into a mobile billboard for the group's critique.

* **Visual Design:** The primary poster featured a high-contrast black-and-white image of Elon Musk at the inauguration, arm raised. The typography mimicked the sleek, sans-serif aesthetic of modern tech advertising, creating a dissonance between the corporate style and the fascist content[1] .  
* **The Slogan:** "Goes from 0 to 1939 in 3 seconds." This tag line cleverly subverted the standard automotive performance metric (0 to 60 mph) to reference the year Nazi Germany invaded Poland, marking the start of World War II. The joke operated on two levels: criticizing the speed of the car and the speed of Musk’s political radicalization[1] .

### 3.2 Deployment Vectors
The campaign was multi-modal, targeting both fixed infrastructure and mobile assets.

#### 3.2.1 The Transit Infiltration (TfL)
Activists infiltrated the Transport for London (TfL) network, opening locked advertising cabinets at bus stops and on London Underground carriages to replace legitimate advertisements with EHE posters.

* **Locations:** High-visibility areas including Bethnal Green, Walthamstow, Clerkenwell, and the Victoria Line[1] .  
* **Variations:**  
  * *The Financial Critique:* A graph showing Tesla's plummeting stock with the text "Hate doesn't sell. Just ask Tesla"[7] .  
  * *The Insult:* A stark poster reading "Elon Musk is a bellend. Signed, the UK"[4] .  
  * *The Cinematic Spoof:* A mock movie poster for "The Fast and Führer," depicted a Doge-plated Tesla Model S with the caption "Heil Tesla"[13] .

#### 3.2.2 The Sticker Intifada
Simultaneously, the group released print-at-home templates for "Don't Buy a Swasticar" stickers via Instagram. This democratized the activism, allowing followers to tag Tesla vehicles in their own neighborhoods. The group claimed hundreds of Teslas in London were tagged, effectively turning the product against its owner[4] .

#### 3.2.3 Merchandise and Accessories
The group extended the campaign into satirical consumer goods, producing "Musk B-Gone" car air fresheners with the tagline "covers the stench of fascism." These were distributed to supporters and placed in shops, further embedding the narrative into everyday commerce[4] .

### 3.3 The Showroom Confrontation
In a direct confrontation, the core team carried a life-sized cardboard cutout of the "saluting" Musk into a flagship Tesla showroom in North London. The action was filmed and uploaded to TikTok, showing staff and customers reacting with hostility. The group reported that customers told them to "f\*ck off," a reaction EHE cited as evidence that they had successfully pierced the bubble of the brand's prestige[4] .

## 4\. Institutional Response and Legal Repercussions
The aggressive nature of the "Swasticar" campaign provoked significant responses from state authorities and corporate bodies, revealing the legal and ethical boundaries of the group's methodology.

### 4.1 Transport for London (TfL) and Police Action
TfL took a hardline stance against the campaign. In internal emails released via Freedom of Information (FOI) requests, TfL officials described the posters as "criminal damage" and "illegal flyposting."

* **Rapid Removal:** TfL coordinated with their advertising partner, JCDecaux, to "expeditiously remove" the posters. The agency also engaged with the British Transport Police regarding the security breaches that allowed activists to access locked ad spaces[4] .  
* **Bureaucratic Panic:** The FOI documents reveal a flurry of internal communication, with subject lines like "FW: Fake Posters \- Anti-Musk/Anti-Tesla," highlighting the urgency with which the transport authority viewed the reputational risk[12] .

### 4.2 The Antisemitism Controversy
A critical dimension of the institutional response came from Jewish advocacy groups. Internal TfL correspondence noted that a "Jewish security organisation" had contacted the authority to express concern.

* **The Critique:** The organization highlighted that "any reference to Nazi salutes or swastikas is understandably triggering for the Jewish community," regardless of the satirical intent. This raised a complex ethical question for EHE: in their zeal to label Musk a fascist, were they inadvertently utilizing hate speech imagery that harmed the very minorities they claimed to support?.12

## 5\. "London vs Musk": The Aesthetics of Destruction
Following the viral success of the poster campaign, EHE pivoted to performance art. On April 10, 2025, the group staged an event titled **"London vs Musk"** in South London, which introduced a visceral, physical element to their digital-first activism.

### 5.1 The Event Mechanics
The centerpiece of the event was a used Tesla Model S, which the group claimed was "donated anonymously" and was already destined for the scrapyard. This framing was crucial to deflect accusations of environmental waste or wanton vandalism[3] .

* **Participation:** Members of the public were invited to take turns smashing the vehicle with sledgehammers, crowbars, and other tools. The event was billed as a "public art piece" designed to allow citizens to "vent their rage" at the billionaire class safely and legally[17] .

### 5.2 Theoretical Framework: Destruction Art
The event drew clear parallels to the tradition of "Destruction Art" (e.g., Gustav Metzger), where the annihilation of an object serves as a critique of the system that produced it. By physically dismantling a Tesla—the ultimate status symbol of the eco-conscious elite—participants were symbolically dismantling the myth of ethical consumption under capitalism.

### 5.3 Philanthropic Component
To further legitimize the violence of the act, the group announced that the scrap metal and battery would be recycled, and the "remains" of the vehicle would be auctioned. All proceeds were pledged to food bank charities, reinforcing the group's narrative contrast between Musk’s $200 billion wealth and the hunger of British nurses[3] .

### 5.4 Safety and Legal Disclaimers
Cognizant of the potential for copycat crimes, EHE issued stern warnings alongside the event coverage. A spokesperson stated, "We absolutely do not condone damage to any other vehicles... you would face serious legal consequences." This disclaimer highlighted the tension between their incendiary rhetoric ("Don't buy a Swasticar") and the need to avoid liability for widespread criminal damage[17] .

## 6\. Strategic Expansion: Targeting the Oligarchy
As 2025 progressed, Everyone Hates Elon expanded its target list. The group recognized that Musk was merely the most visible symptom of a broader systemic issue: the unchecked power of the ultra-wealthy.

### 6.1 The Siege of Venice: Jeff Bezos (June 2025\)
In June 2025, the group targeted Amazon founder Jeff Bezos during his wedding to Lauren Sánchez in Venice, Italy.

* **Coalition Building:** EHE collaborated with Greenpeace Italy and a local collective called "No Space for Bezos." This marked a significant step in internationalizing their operations[19] .  
* **The Tactics:** Activists unfurled a massive banner in St. Mark's Square and threatened to disrupt the wedding procession on the canals using "whatever can float," including inflatable crocodiles. The protest focused on Amazon's labor practices and carbon footprint[19] .  
* **Outcome:** The threat of disruption was credible enough that Bezos and Sánchez reportedly altered their wedding reception plans to avoid the protesters, a victory EHE claimed as proof of their disruptive capacity[20] .

### 6.2 The State Visit: Donald Trump and Jeffrey Epstein (September 2025\)
Anticipating Donald Trump’s state visit to the UK in September 2025, EHE launched a campaign focused on the President's past association with convicted sex offender Jeffrey Epstein.

* **Windsor Castle:** Less than 24 hours before Trump’s arrival, the group unfurled a "supersized" photo of Trump and Epstein on the grounds outside Windsor Castle. The location was chosen to maximize embarrassment for the Royal Family and the visiting delegation[21] .  
* **Aberdeen Golf Course:** In Scotland, the group drove a van emblazoned with the Trump/Epstein image around the perimeter of Trump’s golf course while he met with UK Prime Minister Keir Starmer. They also installed a parody "memorial plaque" on a bench at the course[21] .  
* **Crowdfunding:** This specific sub-campaign raised over £32,000, demonstrating the public's willingness to fund anti-Trump actions specifically[21] .

### 6.3 Domestic Targets: Jim Ratcliffe and J.D. Vance
The group also targeted British billionaire Sir Jim Ratcliffe (CEO of INEOS) and U.S. Vice President J.D. Vance.

* **Ratcliffe:** Posters featuring Ratcliffe appeared alongside Musk, criticizing his tax practices and environmental record[24] .  
* **Vance:** During a UK visit, EHE utilized a billboard in Cheltenham to declare Vance "NOT welcome," citing his "far-right friends" and the starvation in Palestine as reasons he should not be afforded British hospitality[4] .

## 7\. Economic Impact Analysis: The "Tesla Takedown"
A central claim of the EHE campaign is that their actions materially damaged Tesla's financial standing. While establishing direct causality in stock markets is difficult, the correlation between the group's peak activity and Tesla's 2025 slump is notable.

### 7.1 Financial Performance in 2025
Data from early 2025 indicates a severe downturn for Tesla, Inc.

* **Stock Price:** Tesla stock plummeted from a high of $479.86 in late 2024 to a low of $281.95 in February 2025—the exact month of the "Swasticar" campaign launch[7] .  
* **Sales Volume:** In January 2025, Tesla sales in Europe dropped by nearly 50%. By March 2025, the European Automobile Manufacturers' Association reported a further 40% year-on-year decline[1] .

### 7.2 Attribution of Losses
Financial analysts have debated the drivers of this collapse.

* **The EHE Argument:** The group claims their "Swasticar" branding and the "London vs Musk" stunts made the car socially toxic. They argue that for the liberal, urban demographic that buys EVs, driving a "Swasticar" became a social liability[3] .  
* **The Analyst View:** Market analysts at firms like Wedbush and William Blair acknowledged that Musk’s "political toxicity" was a major factor. They noted that Musk’s involvement in the Trump administration (DOGE) and his "far-right politics" alienated the core customer base. While they did not always name EHE specifically, they cited the "brand damage" that EHE was actively manufacturing[2] .  
* **Market Context:** Other factors, such as the rise of Chinese EVs and the broader rotation out of "Magnificent Seven" tech stocks, also contributed to the decline[27] .

However, the specific collapse in *European* sales, where EHE was most active, suggests the group played a catalysing role in turning vague dissatisfaction into an active consumer boycott[1] .

## 8\. Global Ripple Effects and Copycat Movements
The success of Everyone Hates Elon inspired a wave of similar actions globally, creating a decentralized network of anti-Musk resistance.

* **Tesla Takedown:** A broader umbrella term used by activists in the US and Europe.  
* **The Cybertruck Hunters:** A TikTok-based group that projected anti-Musk videos onto the flat rear tailgates of Tesla Cybertrucks in major US cities[28] .  
* **San Francisco Protests:** In Musk’s home turf, protesters gathered at showrooms chanting "Elon Musk has got to go" and unfurled banners reading "No Swasticars," directly adopting the language coined by the London group[28] .  
* **Paris Effigies:** In April 2025, concurrent with the London sledgehammer event, protesters in Paris burned a cardboard effigy of a Tesla during a demonstration[29] .

## 9\. Conclusion
Everyone Hates Elon represents a new paradigm in corporate accountability activism. By fusing the irreverence of internet culture with the physical disruption of traditional protest, the group successfully punctured the aura of invincibility surrounding the world's richest man.

While their methods—particularly the use of Nazi imagery—remained controversial, their impact on Tesla's brand equity in the UK and Europe was palpable. The group demonstrated that in the age of the attention economy, a small, underfunded collective could inflict billions of dollars in reputational damage by simply refusing to let a billionaire control the narrative. As of late 2025, with Tesla's stock still struggling to recover its previous highs and Musk’s political entanglements deepening, the campaign shows no signs of abating.

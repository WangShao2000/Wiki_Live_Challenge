# Multo (song)
**"Multo"** (Filipino for 'Ghost') is a song by the Filipino alternative pop and pop rock band Cup of Joe. Released on September 14, 2024, by Viva Records 1, the track served as the debut single from the band’s first full-length album, *Silakbo*, which followed on January 17, 2025\. Musically, "Multo" is a melancholic synth-pop and pop rock ballad[1] .

The song achieved historic commercial success in the Philippines throughout 2025\. It reached number one on the *Billboard Philippines* Hot 100 chart and held the top position for an unprecedented 27 consecutive weeks, setting a new record for the chart[2] . It was also identified as the most streamed song on Spotify Philippines in 2025\. The Tagalog lyrics of "Multo" explore themes of persistent emotional anguish and unresolved grief, utilizing the titular "ghost" as a metaphor for lingering memories of past relationships or unfinished personal experiences[1] . Due to its widespread impact, the song was named Song of the Year at the 2025 Filipino Music Awards 5, and its success was the primary factor in *Billboard Philippines* crowning Cup of Joe as the No. 1 Artist of 2025\.

*Note: While other songs titled "Multo" were released in 2025, including tracks by Jaem (February 1, 2025\) and KHOBEE (October 6, 2025\) , this article focuses specifically on the record-breaking hit by Cup of Joe.*

## Background and Release
"Multo" was composed by brothers Raphaell Ridao and Redentor Immanuel Ridao. The track originated in 2020, with Raphaell Ridao describing the creation process as an emotional outlet. The two were confined and felt unable to process their experiences and feelings, leading them to use songwriting as a means to convey their "untold feelings and thoughts". The composition became an intense expression of "longing" and "sadness," and its completion reportedly provided a "sense of relief" and a feeling that a heavy emotional burden had been lifted.

The four-year interval between the song's composition in 2020 and its official release in September 2024 suggests that the track was initially a highly personal work that the band and label later recognized as having profound artistic merit and significant commercial potential[2] . The decision to release the track as the lead single for their debut album, *Silakbo*, signaled a pivotal shift in the band's public identity. Having previously been known for "upbeat pop" , positioning a distinctly melancholic track like "Multo" indicated the band's embrace of a more vulnerable and introspective artistic direction for their full-length debut.

The song was officially released by Viva Records. Production was handled by Jovel Rivera and Shadiel Chan[3] . The musical arrangement involved a collaborative effort between the Ridao brothers, Shadiel Chan, and Cup of Joe themselves. Technical credits include Shadiel Chan for mixing and recording, and Jan Aries Agadier Fuertez for mastering.

## Composition, Lyrics, and Thematic Analysis
"Multo" is classified as a synth-pop and pop rock ballad. Its sonic foundation is described as melancholic, utilizing a measured, slow tempo and a sorrowful melody[1] . This deliberate musical structure supports the lyrical narrative, aiming to articulate feelings of loneliness and confusion.

### Lyrical Interpretation
The Tagalog lyrics of "Multo" describe a pervasive sense of being unable to escape a past presence. The central metaphor of the *multo* (ghost) is invoked through the line, "Minumulto na 'ko ng damdamin ko" (I'm being haunted by my feelings)[1] . The song explores the difficulty of moving forward, acknowledging that feelings and memories persist and follow the individual, regardless of time elapsed. The lyrics express a desperate desire for peace and release, asking, "Di mo ba ako lilisanin, 'Di pa ba sapat pagpapahirop sa 'kin" (Will you not leave me, hasn't the suffering I've gone through been enough).

### Symbolism and Scholarly Review
The thematic depth of the song was significant enough to attract academic analysis. Research confirmed that the *multo* is not intended as a literal supernatural entity, but functions as a potent symbol. The analysis determined that the ghost represents memories, suppressed emotions, or "unfinished chapters in life" that continuously return. This symbolism highlights the emotional weight carried from unresolved grief. According to scholarly findings, the purpose of this symbolic ghost is not to frighten the subject, but to serve as a reminder that certain feelings must be fully acknowledged and confronted before true emotional liberation can occur.

By employing the theory of Symbolic Interactionism, researchers noted that although listeners derived varied interpretations—attributing the 'ghost' to lost loved ones, faded friendships, or even past versions of themselves —these interpretations all revolved around a core concept of personal connection. This thematic ambiguity allowed listeners to project their diverse, painful experiences onto the song, transforming the songwriters' personal reflection into a profound communal experience. This level of emotional connection facilitated the song's massive cultural spread and enduring appeal.

*Rolling Stone Philippines* observed that the song, described as a "sad banger," became a significant breakthrough, suggesting that its success "quietly rewrites their legacy". The publication interpreted the lyrics, particularly the call to "Pasindi na ng ilaw" (Turn on the lights), as a metaphor for the band's own maturation, reflecting a departure from their earlier, more lighthearted image towards a deeper, more introspective artistic exploration[1] .

## Commercial Performance
"Multo" experienced a unique trajectory, initially being overlooked upon its release in September 2024 before becoming a "slow-burning phenomenon" in 2025\. The track's ascent was primarily fueled by its consistent usage and virality on social media platforms like TikTok and Instagram.

The song debuted and sustained a long run on the charts, demonstrating extraordinary staying power in the highly competitive Philippine music market.

### Chart Records
"Multo" first secured the number one position on the *Billboard Philippines* Hot 100 chart in the week starting April 19, 2025\. It maintained this position for 27 consecutive weeks, an unprecedented period of dominance that established a historic longevity record for the chart. This sustained popularity indicated that the song transcended the temporary appeal of algorithmic trends, becoming a staple in daily consumer listening and solidifying its place within the national soundscape.

### Streaming Dominance and Industry Recognition
The song's widespread success was confirmed across digital platforms. For the year 2025, "Multo" was reported as the most streamed song on Spotify Philippines. This achievement, combined with the track's chart performance, resulted in *Billboard Philippines* naming Cup of Joe the No. 1 Artist of 2025, acknowledging that the band had "claimed the year as theirs".

## Music Video and Live Presentation
### Official Music Video
The official music video for "Multo" was directed by Kris Cazin. The video was noted for its "cinematic" quality and starred prominent Filipino actors Elijah Canlas and Miles Ocampo[3] .

The visual narrative was described as a powerful interpretation of the song's theme, offering an "artful depiction of love and loss". The video explores the concepts of the "ghost of what was" and the "echo of what could have been". The decision to feature established actors and invest in a high-quality visual production reflected the label's strategic understanding of the song's profound emotional resonance and its potential to connect deeply with the audience through a sophisticated visual narrative[3] .

### Cultural Impact in Live Performance
The song became a focal point for the band's concert appearances throughout 2025\. Cup of Joe performed "Multo" at major events, including the JBL Sound Fest and the Wonderful Moments Music Festival.

The song's cultural weight was particularly evident at the 2025 Filipino Music Awards, where the band's live performance of "Multo" was highlighted as a memorable moment characterized by "The Entire Arena Singing Along". This response transformed the track from an intimate emotional reflection (as noted by the songwriters ) into a unifying collective anthem, leading commentators to characterize the song as the Philippines' "secondary national anthem" for 2025\.

## Accolades and Legacy
The phenomenal reception of "Multo" resulted in significant industry recognition, solidifying the track’s status as a defining cultural milestone of the year.

"Multo" received the Song of the Year award at the 2025 Filipino Music Awards[5] . This validation provided industry confirmation that the band's shift toward introspective, melancholic synth-pop—a deliberate change from their earlier style—was critically successful and widely embraced by the public. The band also won Album of the Year for *Silakbo* at the same awards.

In assessing the band’s future, commentators debated the implications of the song’s overwhelming domestic success. The extent of the song's local dominance raised questions regarding the optimal strategy for the band's international growth. Some analysts suggested that Cup of Joe should wait until the domestic "Multo/Silakbo era's hype has slowly faded" before releasing a dedicated English single aimed at global markets. This discussion underscored the sheer magnitude of the track’s presence, indicating that local demand was so robust it necessitated a temporary deferral of global expansion plans.

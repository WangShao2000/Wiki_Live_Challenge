# Papal Inauguration of Pope Leo XIV

The papal inauguration of Pope Leo XIV took place on May 18, 2025, in St. Peter’s Square, Vatican City. This ceremony marked the formal beginning of his ministry as the successor of Peter and the bishop of Rome. Held amidst global attention, the event was attended by over 200,000 pilgrims and included leaders from around the world, along with representatives of various faith traditions. The inauguration followed established liturgical practices but adapted to modern ecclesial sensibilities, emphasizing themes of love, unity, humility, and service.

# Background

Pope Leo XIV, born Robert Francis Prevost in Chicago, Illinois, made history as the first American pope in the nearly 2,000-year history of the Roman Catholic Church. He was elected on May 8, 2025, the second day of the conclave following the death of Pope Francis. The passing of Pope Francis occurred on April 21, 2025, at his residence in Casa Santa Marta, ending his 12-year pontificate. His election to the papacy reflected both his decades of service within the Church and his broad missionary experience in Peru as a member of the Augustinian order.

Following his election, Pope Leo XIV chose the name Leo XIV, honoring earlier leaders with roots in justice and courage. He became the 267th occupant of the throne of Saint Peter.

# Ceremony Overview

The inauguration Mass, held at 10 a.m. local time, commenced inside St. Peter’s Basilica before proceeding to the outdoor altar in the square. The rite followed the “Order of Rites of the Beginning of the Petrine Ministry as Bishop of Rome,” established by Benedict XVI and reformed under Paul VI. The solemn celebration signaled a departure from the coronation tradition, with popes no longer crowned by the papal tiara since Paul VI’s time.

Central elements of the ceremony included:

- **Greeting and procession:** The pope arrived at St. Peter’s Altar of Confession, greeted by concelebrating cardinals and patriarchs of Eastern Catholic Churches.
- **Prayer at St. Peter’s tomb:** The pope offered silent prayer at the apostle’s tomb—a customary moment underscoring his role as successor.
- **Procession and Litany:** Afterward, a procession moved to St. Peter’s Square, accompanied by the Laudes Regiae, a historic litany originally associated with imperial ceremonies and renewed for the pope’s rite.
- **Presentation of symbols:** The pallium and fisherman’s ring were displayed and delivered to the pope, signifying his pastoral mission and connection to Saint Peter.
- **Gospel readings:** The service featured readings in both Greek and Latin, representing the universal nature of his leadership—both to Eastern and Latin Catholics.
- **Rite of obedience:** Representatives from every clerical order and lay state paid homage to the new pope, affirming their commitment and fidelity.

# Symbolic Elements

Three key symbols defined Pope Leo XIV’s inauguration:

1. **Pallium:** A white woolen stole adorned with five red crosses, woven by Benedictine nuns. It represents the pope as shepherd carrying the Church’s flock on his shoulders.
2. **Fisherman’s ring:** A gold ring engraved with an image of Saint Peter in his boat and the pope’s name. Traditionally used to seal documents, this symbol signifies the pope’s unique apostolic lineage.
3. **Papal tiara:** Though no longer used in modern inaugurations, its historical significance and symbolism are referenced as part of the broader papal tradition.

Other moments included the final acclamation, where the choir and assembly sang hymns, and the granting of the Urbi et Orbi blessing—an invocation for peace to Rome and the entire world.

## Themes and Homily

During his inaugural Mass, Pope Leo XIV addressed the gathered faithful and world leaders with an emphasis on universal love, unity, and selfless service. He expressed gratitude for prayers received after the passing of Pope Francis and called the Church to be a “small leaven of unity and communion.” The pope framed Peter’s mission as rooted in “self-sacrificing love,” urging believers to serve as shepherds rather than authorities wielding power.

He invoked Christ’s example as the model for leadership, stating that the Church’s true authority lies in charity—not force or propaganda. These messages reflected the enduring spirit of renewal fostered by his predecessor and set the tone for the future of the Catholic Church.

## Post-Inauguration Ceremonies

The full commencement of Leo XIV’s pontificate continued beyond St. Peter’s Square. Key events included:

| Date        | Event                                    | Location                         | Details                                                           |
|-------------|------------------------------------------|----------------------------------|-------------------------------------------------------------------|
| May 20      | Taking possession of St. Paul Outside the Walls | Vatican                          | Veneration of the tomb of Apostle Paul                            |
| May 25      | Regina Caeli + possession of basilicas   | St. John Lateran & St. Mary Major | Formal reception at Lateran Cathedral; Marian devotion at St. Mary Major |

At St. John Lateran Basilica—the cathedral of the bishop of Rome—Leo XIV was seated in the cathedra, receiving expressions of obedience and respect from representatives of the Diocese of Rome. This act symbolized his recognition as bishop of Rome and emphasized canonical tradition.

St. Paul Outside the Walls offered a focus on veneration of the apostle to the Gentiles, while St. Mary Major provided an opportunity for Marian devotion, including reverence for the Salus Populi Romani icon.

## Significance

Pope Leo XIV’s inauguration represented both continuity and change for the Catholic Church:

- **First American pope:** His election broke centuries of precedent, marking the United States as a focal point in Catholic leadership.
- **Augustinian legacy:** As the first Augustinian pope, Leo XIV brought a unique charism rooted in community, interreligious dialogue, and service to the marginalized.
- **Missionary vision:** With extensive experience in Peru, he carried forward the Church’s evangelizing mission, especially among the poor and remote communities.

These milestones extended beyond symbolic gestures, aiming to reshape the Church’s approach toward unity, social justice, and outreach in the 21st century.

## Conclusion

The papal inauguration of Pope Leo XIV stands out as a major event in Catholic history, reflecting both ancient traditions and contemporary values. Through liturgy, symbolism, and participation, the ceremony ushered in a new era marked by humility, inclusivity, and hope. As the leader of the Catholic Church and sovereign of Vatican City, Leo XIV’s ministry seeks to walk with the faithful and offer the world a message of reconciliation, compassion, and shared humanity.

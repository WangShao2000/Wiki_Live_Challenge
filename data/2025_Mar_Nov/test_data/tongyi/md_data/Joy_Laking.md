# Early Life and Education

Joy Laking was born in 1950 in Owen Sound, Ontario, into a family immersed in creativity. Her mother, Joyce Snihur, was a professional painter, while her father created marionettes and toy trains. Growing up surrounded by art and craft, Joy accompanied her mother on painting excursions along Georgian Bay during her childhood. This early environment fostered her deep appreciation for the arts and instilled the importance of diligence and commitment to craft.

Joy continued her artistic development through formal education. She graduated from the University of Guelph in 1972 with a Bachelor of Arts degree in Fine Art. During her university years, she observed that contemporary trends favored acrylics and abstract forms, but Joy gravitated toward watercolour for its environmental and technical qualities. After graduation, she married and relocated with her spouse to Bass River, Nova Scotia, a move that would shape much of her artistic focus for decades.

# Career and Exhibitions

Since settling in Nova Scotia in the early 1970s, Joy Laking established herself as a prominent figure in the Canadian art scene. She began building a public profile soon after moving to Nova Scotia, holding her first solo exhibition in 1982 at the Mount Saint Vincent Art Gallery. Notable milestones included the display of her floral works in “Flowers of Nova Scotia” (1982), artwork selected for the cover of Nova Scotia’s provincial phone book in 1985, and a substantial series of watercolour paintings shown at the Royal Botanical Gardens in Burlington, Ontario, first in 1984 and again in 1990.

Her most significant breakthrough came in 1989 when she held a major solo show at the Art Gallery of Nova Scotia. This exhibition then toured the province throughout 1990, earning critical acclaim and establishing her reputation beyond regional boundaries. Over the following years, she participated in numerous group exhibitions across Canada, including venues such as the MSVU Art Gallery in Halifax, Owens Art Gallery at Mount Allison University, and international galleries in Spain. These shows cemented her status as one of Nova Scotia’s leading painters.

To showcase her growing body of work, Joy constructed a dedicated gallery space adjacent to her home in Portapique in the late 1980s. The Joy Laking Gallery, open by appointment, became a destination for local and international visitors seeking to experience her art firsthand. Additionally, her paintings have been included in major collections, such as the Art Gallery of Nova Scotia and the Nova Scotia Art Bank.

# Diversification and Community Engagement

Beyond traditional painting, Joy Laking has actively explored other artistic mediums. In the early 1980s, she illustrated children’s books and produced calendars, further broadening her reach and engagement with the public. Following health challenges around 2007, she embraced new creative outlets, including photography, rug hooking, wool dyeing, and even beach glass work. Her photography exhibition “Here and Away – Seeing with Passionate Eyes,” staged jointly with fellow traveler and photographer Laurie Gunn, debuted in Truro, Nova Scotia.

Her involvement in the broader arts community is notable. Joy has served as a founding member of PLANS (Professional Living Artists of Nova Scotia), an elected member of the Society of Canadian Artists, and on the board of the Robert Pope Foundation. Her contributions extend to leadership roles and collaborative projects aimed at supporting emerging artists and cultural initiatives. She has also acted as artist-in-residence at various institutions, including Port Bickerton Lighthouse, and traveled abroad for inspiration, painting in India, Ghana, Italy, and Portugal.

## Personal Life

Joy Laking’s personal journey reflects a steadfast dedication to both family and artistic pursuit. She and her husband raised three children—Kelsey, Danica, and Yolande—between 1980 and 1985. Her close-knit family provided support and stability as she navigated periods of creative challenge and transition. Over the years, her studio practice became both her sanctuary and her method of coping with difficult times.

## The 2020 Nova Scotia Attacks and Artistic Response

In April 2020, Joy experienced profound loss and trauma due to the mass shooting tragedy in Portapique, Nova Scotia. Three of her friends were killed, including schoolteacher Lisa McCully and a couple, Joanne Thomas and John Zahl, whose lives were tragically cut short during the perpetrator’s 13-hour rampage. The event left Joy devastated and initially unable to paint, describing how “the brush suddenly felt too heavy to hold.” 

As part of her healing process, she returned to her studio and intensified her daily painting routine. Art served as both escape and therapy, allowing her to channel grief into creation. She expressed frustration regarding limited access to continuous mental health care in rural communities but credited regular artistic practice, alongside support from family and professionals, for sustaining her resilience. She also reached out to bereaved families, offering art classes as a means to help children cope with loss. Her reflections underscore the interconnection between her personal story and the wider community affected by the violence.

## Artistic Style and Influences

Joy’s approach is distinguished by a realistic sensibility and an immersive relationship with her surroundings. While she trained academically in fine art, she is largely self-taught in watercolour, developing a technique that diverges from academic conventions by working “light towards dark.” Her compositions are marked by strong tonal values, vibrant colours, and an acute attention to detail—all elements that make her landscapes sing visually.

Her choice of medium is influenced by environmental considerations; watercolour avoids harsh solvents, aligning with her preference for healthy, sustainable practices. She regularly employs on-location studies, using photographs and direct observation to create studio works that capture fleeting moments—whether atmospheric conditions or the mood of a landscape.

Among her artistic inspirations, Joy cites Emily Carr for her tenacity and self-belief in the face of doubt. The discovery of the Group of Seven affirmed Carr’s role as a kindred spirit, shaping Joy’s appreciation for bold vision and regional pride. Travel has played a key role in her growth, providing diverse visual experiences that inform her palette and perspective.

## Creative Writing

In addition to visual art, Joy Laking has contributed to literature and performance. Her play **Invisible Prisons**, first staged in Truro in March 2013, originated from interviews with survivors of domestic abuse. The play consists of thirteen monologues exploring themes of alienation, isolation, and power imbalances in relationships. Aimed at raising awareness and fostering dialogue, it is freely available for educational institutions and community groups.

Produced by Lenore Zann and directed by Bryden MacDonald, the play was supported by the Truro Theatre Society and funded by local organizations, including the Robert Pope Foundation. Its message seeks to break the silence surrounding abuse and promote societal change.

## Literary Works

Joy Laking is also an accomplished author. Her first children’s fiction book, **Colours in Winter** (2019), reflects her fascination with winter landscapes and uses playful narrative to introduce young readers to seasonal beauty. Many illustrations were painted years earlier, while the cover and some plates were created more recently.

Her next major project, **The Painted Province** (2020), pairs nearly 200 of her watercolours with descriptive “word pictures” about the places depicted. Designed as both an art collection and a travel companion, the book includes GPS coordinates for 39 locations, encouraging readers to visit and engage with Nova Scotia’s landscapes firsthand. Distributed via Nimbus Publishing, the volume has sold repeatedly and generated interest among tourists and locals alike.

## Summary and Legacy

Joy Snihur Wyatt Laking exemplifies the Canadian spirit of creativity, perseverance, and connection. Her watercolours convey the essence of Nova Scotia’s landscapes, capturing both their serene beauty and dynamic energy. Through writing, teaching, and community involvement, she continues to inspire others—not only artists but also those navigating hardship or seeking greater understanding.

Her legacy is evident in the countless works displayed throughout galleries, the books shared with readers, and the stories told on stage. By blending talent with compassion, Joy Laking remains a vital presence in the arts and a beacon for anyone committed to making a difference through creative expression.

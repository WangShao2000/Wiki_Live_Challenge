# 2025 Enderlin tornado

On the night of June 20, 2025, an extremely violent tornado struck the rural community of Enderlin in Ransom County, North Dakota, before crossing into Cass County. The National Weather Service conducted further damage surveys and forensic analyses months after the event, ultimately upgrading its rating to EF-5—an extremely rare classification indicating catastrophic wind speeds exceeding 200 mph. This tornado marked the first recorded EF-5 event in the United States since the 2013 Moore, Oklahoma tornado and remains the strongest tornado ever documented in North Dakota since the historical Fargo tornado of 1957.


# Meteorological Context

The 2025 Enderlin tornado developed within a particularly favorable environment for severe weather. Powerful supercell thunderstorms formed over southeast North Dakota during the late afternoon and evening of June 20, preceding a large-scale derecho—a widespread and destructive convective wind event—that would move across the region shortly afterward. These atmospheric conditions created sustained instability, moisture influx, and wind shear conducive to the formation of long-lived, intense tornadoes.

Multiple meteorological reports and storm surveys have indicated that at least two discrete supercells generated destructive vortices that night. One of these produced the Enderlin tornado, which exhibited unusual features for a North Dakota event, such as extreme wind speeds inferred from highly anomalous damage indicators. The presence of concurrent derecho activity complicated forecast efforts, as the outbreak included both tornado-producing storms and areas experiencing straight-line winds exceeding 100 mph.


# Path and Intensity

The tornado touched down just after 11:02 p.m. Central Daylight Time approximately 3 miles south-southeast of Enderlin Airport. Its last surface impact occurred around 11:21 p.m. near Alice, Cass County. Over the course of about 20 minutes, the tornado carved a total path spanning 12.1 miles, with 5.4 miles located within Ransom County and the remainder extending northward into Cass County.

At peak intensity, the vortex reached a maximum width of 1.05 miles (equivalent to 1850 yards). Forensic analysis of engineering evidence and radar observations allowed meteorologists to estimate sustained winds at more than 210 mph—well above the minimum EF-5 threshold. This figure was notably higher than earlier preliminary assessments, which had classified the event as an EF3 based on initial damage findings.


# Detailed Damage Assessment

The scale and nature of the destruction were exceptional even for the EF-5 category. Among the most distinctive damage indicators was the derailing of thirty-three railroad cars, including nineteen fully loaded grain hoppers weighing up to 286,000 pounds each, and fourteen empty tanker cars. Several of these were violently tipped or hurled considerable distances. Notably, one empty tanker car was projected nearly 475 feet away from the tracks—a distance and mass far exceeding any previously modeled under tornado-force winds. A cell phone tower also collapsed, while numerous farm structures suffered near-total destruction, with debris fields extending hundreds of meters from impacted sites.

Vegetation damage added to the gravity of the event. In the Maple River valley east of Enderlin, extensive tree debarking occurred, described as a “sandpapering” effect, and root balls of large trees were displaced, sometimes so severely that the original location of certain specimens could not be determined. Multiple farmsteads lost buildings entirely, and the foundations of some residences were swept clean except for remaining basements.

Farmers and residents described landscapes stripped of crops and trees, with entire silos and farm equipment missing from their original locations. Overall, the event demonstrated unprecedented destructive potential in both structural and environmental terms.


## Aftermath and Immediate Response

Emergency services launched swift recovery operations following the disaster. Local authorities, including the mayor of Enderlin, coordinated search-and-rescue missions and damage assessment teams to reach isolated areas affected by fallen power lines, blocked roads, and disrupted communications. Survivors described traumatic experiences due to the sudden onset and relentless strength of the tornado’s violence.

Despite claims of multiple fatalities in media and eyewitness accounts, official records released by the National Weather Service’s storm events database indicate zero direct casualties for this particular tornado. However, several reputable news sources independently report that three individuals lost their lives as a result of the event. The discrepancy between media coverage and government databases highlights ongoing challenges in post-event fatality accounting, especially in remote communities where identification of deceased persons can be delayed or complicated.

No injuries were formally documented in the storm events database, but some reports from local hospitals and medical facilities mentioned treating minor trauma cases among rescuers and property owners. Cleanup and restoration efforts continued throughout the subsequent weeks and months, focusing initially on restoring critical infrastructure and providing emergency shelter.


## Significance and Historical Context

The Enderlin tornado stands out historically for several reasons. It ended a twelve-year drought in EF-5 tornado occurrences in the United States, marking a return to the most extreme tornado rating since the infamous Moore, Oklahoma event in 2013. The event is also unique in North Dakota’s history, as no prior tornado in the state has achieved EF-5 status, making it the most violent tornado ever recorded in the region.

This tornado shattered previous expectations for midwestern and northern Plains tornado outbreaks. Prior to 2025, EF-5 intensities had been exceedingly rare in both geographic and temporal terms, typically associated only with the American South and Great Plains. Meteorologists emphasize that the severity of this event underscores the increasing unpredictability of severe weather patterns, possibly influenced by changing climate variables and shifting atmospheric dynamics.

Furthermore, the scientific community highlighted new methodologies in assessing tornado intensity, integrating advanced forensic analysis and multi-disciplinary research collaborations. The event prompted renewed discussion about tornado rating systems, damage survey protocols, and public warnings for similar threats in sparsely populated regions.


## Broader Weather Outbreak

The Enderlin tornado was part of a significant regional weather event lasting from June 19 to 22, 2025, known as the tornado outbreak and derecho of June 19–22. While the derecho itself brought widespread straight-line winds and additional tornadoes, the Enderlin vortex was exceptional in terms of both magnitude and rarity. Other tornadoes during this period ranged in intensity from EF1 to EF4, causing additional damage across parts of eastern North Dakota and western Minnesota.

## Conclusion

The 2025 Enderlin tornado represents a pivotal moment in modern American meteorology. Its exceptional intensity, destructive capacity, and historical significance have reshaped understanding of tornado risks in the Upper Midwest and challenged existing forecasting and damage assessment paradigms. Recovery efforts continue, while researchers anticipate valuable lessons for future preparedness in vulnerable communities facing increasingly erratic severe weather.

# 2009 NFC Wild Card Playoff Game (Green Bay–Arizona)

### Lead

The 2009 NFC Wild Card playoff game between the Green Bay Packers and the Arizona Cardinals was held on January 10, 2010, at University of Phoenix Stadium in Glendale, Arizona. This wild-card matchup resulted in a dramatic overtime victory for the Arizona Cardinals, who defeated the Packers by a score of 51–45. The game stands out in NFL history as one of the highest-scoring postseason contests ever played, featuring spectacular offensive performances from both quarterbacks—Aaron Rodgers, making his first playoff start, and Kurt Warner, the veteran leader of the Cardinals. The Cardinals’ win advanced them to the next round, while the loss ended the Packers’ season.


### Background

Heading into the postseason, the Green Bay Packers entered as an 11-5 team, buoyed by a seven-game winning streak and momentum built over the latter part of their regular season. Their opponent, the Arizona Cardinals, had finished the season with a 10-6 record as the NFC West champions and were seeking to replicate the playoff success of the previous year. The matchup pitted a rising young star in Aaron Rodgers against the seasoned experience of Kurt Warner, who was preparing for what would be his final NFL season.

The Packers were favored in the betting line, but both teams arrived well-prepared, with strong offenses at the forefront of their respective strategies. Offensively, Green Bay averaged over 35 points per game during the regular season, while Arizona’s offense ranked among the league leaders in passing and scoring. Both teams had faced each other twice during the regular season, with the Packers securing decisive victories. Nonetheless, the stakes of the wildcard round increased tension as both franchises sought advancement in their quest for a championship.


### Game Summary

The game began with the Cardinals striking quickly, establishing an early 17–0 lead through two rushing touchdowns and a field goal. Green Bay responded slowly, with Aaron Rodgers completing only two passes in the first quarter before connecting on his first touchdown pass in the second quarter, making the score 17–6 by halftime. The Cardinals extended their lead again in the third quarter, going ahead 31–10 at one point.

Despite trailing, the Packers staged a comeback throughout the second half. They successfully executed an onside kick in the third quarter and capitalized with multiple scoring drives led by Rodgers, who completed passes to ten different receivers and threw four touchdowns. By the fourth quarter, the Packers had tied the game at 45–45 late in regulation after Rodgers found James Jones on a 30-yard touchdown strike. A missed field goal attempt by Arizona in the final seconds sent the game to overtime.

Overtime proved decisive when, on Green Bay’s first drive, Rodgers was sacked by Michael Adams and fumbled the football. Cardinals linebacker Karlos Dansby scooped up the ball and returned it 17 yards for a touchdown, sealing a 51–45 victory for Arizona. Throughout the game, both teams showcased prolific passing performances: Rodgers finished with a Packers postseason-record 422 yards and four touchdowns, while Warner completed 29 of 33 passes for 379 yards and five touchdowns without an interception.

### Key Plays

- Early Cardinals dominance: Two rushing touchdowns and a field goal gave Arizona a 17–0 lead in the first quarter.
- Rodgers’ first playoff start: Rodgers was intercepted early but quickly established himself as a game-leader, showing poise and efficiency after the initial setbacks.
- Onside kick success: Green Bay recovered an onside kick in the third quarter, aiding their comeback momentum.
- Overtime turning point: Rodgers’ fumble after being sacked in overtime was recovered and returned for a touchdown by Karlos Dansby, marking the first time a defensive play decided a playoff game’s outcome in overtime since 1980.


### Aftermath

With the victory, the Cardinals advanced to the NFC Divisional Round, where they ultimately fell to the New Orleans Saints, who would go on to win Super Bowl XLIV. The Cardinals’ offense continued its high-octane performance, setting numerous records, including a league-leading 13 touchdowns in a single playoff game—a record at the time.

For Green Bay, the season came to an end, but the experience proved formative. Aaron Rodgers’ first playoff start catapulted him into superstardom and laid the foundation for future deep playoff runs, including a Super Bowl XLV victory in the following season.


### Legacy

The 2009 NFC Wild Card game between the Packers and Cardinals will be remembered as one of the greatest offensive showcases in NFL postseason history. The teams combined for 1,024 yards of offense and 13 touchdowns, both playoff records. Kurt Warner called it “the greatest offensive playoff game in the history of the NFL.” The dramatic overtime finish, defensive heroics, and record-breaking performances have made this game a staple of NFL lore, regularly referenced in discussions of the highest-scoring and most exciting postseason contests.

The clash between Rodgers and Warner exemplified contrasting styles: Rodgers brought youthful exuberance and skillful precision, while Warner offered veteran wisdom and consistent execution under pressure. Both players delivered historic performances, ensuring the game remains a benchmark for postseason excellence.

# 2010 NFC Championship Game

The 2010 National Football Conference (NFC) Championship Game was played on January 24, 2010 at the Caesars Superdome in New Orleans, Louisiana, marking a pivotal matchup between the defending NFC South champion New Orleans Saints and the NFC North champion Minnesota Vikings. This contest determined which team would advance to the Super Bowl and ultimately win the league's championship for the 2009 season.

### Background

Entering the game, the New Orleans Saints held a 13–3 regular season record and were seeking their first Super Bowl appearance in franchise history. The Minnesota Vikings, with a 12–4 record, featured veteran quarterback Brett Favre in what turned out to be his final playoff game. Both teams had advanced through earlier rounds of the playoffs to reach this high-stakes confrontation.

### Game Summary

The game saw both teams exchange scores throughout all four quarters, resulting in a tightly contested battle that required overtime to decide a victor.

Early in the contest, the Vikings established momentum with a 19-yard touchdown run by Adrian Peterson and a subsequent five-yard touchdown pass from Brett Favre to Sidney Rice, each followed by successful extra point kicks from Ryan Longwell. The Saints responded with a 38-yard touchdown pass from Drew Brees to Pierre Thomas, closing the first quarter trailing 14–7.

In the second quarter, Brees connected with Devery Henderson for a nine-yard touchdown to tie the game at 14–14 heading into halftime. Both teams continued to trade opportunities in the third quarter: Pierre Thomas scored on a nine-yard run for the Saints, while Peterson answered for the Vikings with a one-yard touchdown run, keeping the score level again at 21–21.

The fourth quarter saw Reggie Bush score on a five-yard run for the Saints, albeit after a challenge overturned an initial ruling, giving New Orleans a brief lead. Peterson then responded with a two-yard touchdown run to force regulation time to end at 28–28. Multiple turnovers and close plays characterized the latter stages, including interceptions and fumbles by both teams.

Overtime proved decisive. After winning the coin toss, the Saints drove downfield and secured victory when kicker Garrett Hartley made a 40-yard field goal, clinching a 31–28 win for the franchise’s first trip to the Super Bowl.

#### Key Plays and Turning Points

Several key moments defined the game:
- Adrian Peterson’s three rushing touchdowns (19-yard, 1-yard, and 2-yard runs) provided relentless offensive pressure for the Vikings.
- The Saints’ resilience in overcoming adversity included critical challenges for reviews and conversions, notably the review upheld Reggie Bush’s touchdown in the fourth quarter.
- Defensive players such as Jonathan Vilma and Tracy Porter intercepted Brett Favre late in regulation, directly contributing to the need for extra time.
- The Saints' ability to withstand numerous Viking possessions and repeatedly answer scoring drives was crucial in maintaining competitiveness throughout the game.
- Overtime possession and the successful 40-yard field goal by Hartley sealed the Saints’ victory after the Vikings failed to score following their own possession.

### Aftermath

Winning the NFC Championship sent the New Orleans Saints to Super Bowl XLIV, where they defeated the Indianapolis Colts 31–17, capturing their first-ever Super Bowl title. The victory was particularly significant for the city and franchise, providing inspiration and unity in the wake of Hurricane Katrina's devastation only five years prior.

For the Vikings, this game concluded Brett Favre’s storied NFL career in the postseason and marked their fifth consecutive NFC Championship Game loss, reinforcing the struggles they faced in reaching the ultimate stage.

### Significance and Legacy

The 2010 NFC Championship Game is remembered for its dramatic turns and high-level execution under pressure. It highlighted the impact that individual performances can have on the overall outcome, from Favre’s final playoff efforts and Peterson’s versatility to Brees’ leadership and timely challenges. The Saints’ triumph symbolized hope and renewal for a city still recovering from tragedy, and set a new benchmark for their future in the league. The overtime sequence and the coin toss further highlighted debates over the NFL’s playoff format, though it ensured one of the most thrilling games of the decade.

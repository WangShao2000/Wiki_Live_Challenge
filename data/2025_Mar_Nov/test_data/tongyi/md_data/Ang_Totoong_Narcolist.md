# Background and Development

Peter Joemel Advincula was born in Donsol, Sorsogon, in 1989. During his youth, he expressed aspirations to become a priest but was drawn into the world of organized crime in May 2010, allegedly becoming part of a drug syndicate operating in Misibis Bay. There, he managed surveillance systems in underground facilities and compiled monthly reports tracking drug collections and payments—a task central to the operation’s internal accountability mechanisms. While involved with the group, Advincula reportedly witnessed acts of violence and sexual assault, which contributed to his eventual decision to flee.

In August 2018, after leaving the drug syndicate, Advincula sought refuge and protection from law enforcement. However, upon being apprehended by agents from the National Bureau of Investigation (NBI), he was convicted of fraud in 2012 and sentenced to six years imprisonment. His incarceration at Naga City District Jail and later the New Bilibid Prison provided him time to reconnect with religious activities among fellow inmates. During this period, he attempted to contact Senator Tito Sotto and expose his own contacts in the syndicate, though these efforts did not yield meaningful results.

# Release and Structure of the Videos

Advincula launched the "Ang Totoong Narcolist" YouTube channel on April 2, 2019. Each episode centered around the hooded whistleblower character "Bikoy," who claimed firsthand knowledge of criminal operations involving influential Philippine politicians. Five episodes were produced between April 2 and Advincula’s public surrender on May 6, garnering rapid circulation on social media platforms.

# Political and Media Response

The release of the videos triggered immediate responses from both supporters and critics of the Duterte administration. President Rodrigo Duterte dismissed the allegations as “black propaganda,” accusing opposition figures and the Liberal Party of fabricating the claims. His presidential spokesperson, Salvador Panelo, characterized "Bikoy" as a pawn in a broader plot to destabilize the government, leading to heightened scrutiny and social media campaigns against those promoting the videos.

Senator Antonio Trillanes IV publicly praised the videos, expressing hope that the disclosures could foster political transparency. Nonetheless, some government-backed outlets and media personalities condemned the content as unfounded misinformation designed to undermine national stability. Notably, internet users engaged in extensive cross-referencing and analysis, attempting to verify the documents presented in the episodes.

## Manhunt and Legal Proceedings

The dissemination of the videos prompted authorities to launch aggressive investigations. Rodel Jayme, the administrator of Metrobalita.net—a site distributing the videos—was arrested on April 30 and subsequently charged with inciting sedition due to his role in facilitating their spread. While Jayme denied directly uploading the material, investigators suggested that supporters of the Liberal Party and members of the Magdalo movement may have influenced the platform’s operation. The National Bureau of Investigation (NBI) classified Jayme as a potential state witness.

Peter Joemel Advincula himself emerged during this period, requesting legal assistance from the Integrated Bar of the Philippines (IBP) to pursue lawsuits against key figures implicated in the videos. The IBP declined his offer, and Justice Secretary Menardo Guevarra issued a formal ultimatum on May 8, ordering Advincula to appear before the NBI with supporting evidence for his claims. Failure to comply would result in criminal charges for inciting sedition or other offenses.

Despite initial assertions, Advincula eventually retracted his claims and admitted collaboration with opposition groups, labeling his earlier statements as “flip-flops.” He publicly challenged Senator Antonio Trillanes and other lawmakers, suggesting coordinated efforts to influence the political landscape. Following his reversal, Advincula faced sedition charges, was investigated for credibility concerns, and ultimately received legal aid from Solicitor General Jose Calida. In subsequent court proceedings, he was convicted of perjury for making false sworn statements related to the videos’ origins and conspiracies referenced therein.

## Oust Duterte Matrix

Amid the controversy, President Duterte announced the existence of an “Oust Duterte matrix,” a document attributing the videos and their underlying narrative to a supposed conspiracy by journalists, opposition figures, and legal advocates. This matrix listed numerous media professionals and political actors, alleging that they were orchestrating anti-government activities. Officials stated the matrix was intended to reveal those believed to be targeting the administration, though no formal legal action was taken against the named individuals.

## Aftermath and Legacy

The aftermath saw continued debate over the validity of the allegations contained in the videos. Fact-checkers and media watchdog groups scrutinized the documents presented, often finding discrepancies or signs of fabrication. For instance, analyses showed that some bank records referenced had no connection to the individuals named, and photographic evidence used in the videos was found misleading or misinterpreted.

Despite official denials and legal reversals, the series played a significant role in shaping public discourse on corruption, drug trafficking, and political manipulation in the Philippines. The controversy also intensified existing tensions between the government and its opponents, prompting renewed calls for transparency and accountability.

## Conclusion

"Ang Totoong Narcolist" remains a defining episode in recent Philippine political history, illustrating the complex interplay between whistleblowers, media narratives, and state power. Its production, dissemination, and reception highlight challenges in verifying online content, managing credible sources, and navigating contentious political environments. The series ultimately underscored the enduring impacts of digital activism, the dangers of unverified allegations, and the importance of rigorous fact-checking in public debate.

# Sarabande (collection)

**Sarabande** is the twenty-ninth ready-to-wear collection created by British designer Alexander McQueen for his eponymous fashion house. Presented as the Spring/Summer 2007 offering, the collection stands as one of McQueen's most influential works, renowned for its romantic atmosphere and subtle exploration of nature, beauty, and transience. Named after the Baroque dance form popularized by composers such as George Frideric Handel, Sarabande was distinguished by its thematic focus on fragility, impermanence, and the idea that beauty and life are fleeting.

# Concept and Inspiration

The Sarabande collection drew direct inspiration from musical and artistic sources, notably Handel’s “Sarabande”—a baroque dance characterized by its solemn, weighty rhythm. McQueen infused this into the collection through both the conceptual framework and the soundtrack of his runway show. The thematic underpinning was the ephemerality of beauty, an idea underscored by motifs centered around nature—specifically flowers—which recurrently appeared throughout the line’s garments. 

McQueen expressed this sensibility in interviews, stating, “I used flowers because they die. Nothing is forever. There was a fragility to this collection, in the idea that nothing is permanent, that beauty disappears.” These ideas reflected his longstanding fascination with nature’s cycles of life and decay, bringing philosophical undertones to the fashion presentation itself.

# Runway Presentation

The presentation of Sarabande took place on October 6, 2006, at the Cirque d’Hiver, a circus tent located in Paris. Rather than a conventional straight runway, McQueen staged the show “in the round” within the intimate setting of a circular stage. The stage was illuminated by a single crystal chandelier, casting dramatic light over the space, while a live chamber orchestra played an arrangement of Handel’s sarabande. Composed specifically for the occasion by Philip Sheppard, the soundtrack blended an inverted version of the original composition with recorded sounds of Lancaster bombers, culminating in a baroque reinterpretation of The Rolling Stones’ “Paint It Black.” This orchestral performance added layers of theatricality, reinforcing the collection’s emotional resonance and narrative depth.

Models walked in a manner reminiscent of performers in Elizabethan theater-in-the-round, creating a uniquely immersive experience for viewers and press alike. The presentation became celebrated for striking a balance between McQueen’s penchant for grandeur and the practical demands of commercial collections—a dichotomy that often shaped the tension of his later work.

# Key Pieces and Design Features

The highlight of Sarabande was its final look, which exemplified the collection’s central themes. The finale dress was crafted from nude silk organza and embroidered with a mixture of artificial silk blossoms and hundreds of real, frozen flowers. As the model (Tanya Dziahileva) traversed the circular runway, the flowers gradually began to fall away with every step, transforming the gown into a “living, dying, transient thing.” This arresting tableau vividly visualized the notion of beauty’s impermanence, making the dress a symbol of both fragility and endurance.

Alongside the signature floral dress, the collection included Edwardian-inspired silhouettes—such as elaborate lace overlays, pleated mermaid gowns, and asymmetrical necklines adorned with petals suspended in tulle or bursting from cuffs and hems. Several looks featured intricate embroidery and sculptural detailing, demonstrating McQueen’s virtuosity in merging historical references with contemporary innovation.

## Critical Reception

Contemporary responses to Sarabande were marked by admiration for its theatrical brilliance and emotional depth. Critics noted how the show bridged the gap between McQueen’s extreme artistic visions and the commercial realities facing high fashion houses. The fusion of spectacle, poetic intention, and wearable clothing contributed to the collection’s immediate acclaim, with fashion writers recognizing it as one of McQueen’s most memorable displays.

Fashion professionals frequently highlighted the balance struck between experimentation and accessibility—a quality not always present in McQueen’s career-long approach. The playfulness and vulnerability embodied in the floral gown, combined with masterful craftsmanship, set Sarabande apart from other major shows of the period. 

## Legacy and Lasting Influence

The enduring impact of the Sarabande collection was further cemented when, following McQueen’s death, he bequeathed much of his fortune to establish the Sarabande Foundation. Named explicitly after the Spring/Summer 2007 collection, the foundation became a cornerstone in supporting emerging creative talent across disciplines—fashion, art, music, design, and more. Located in London, it offers subsidized studio spaces, scholarships, business mentorship, and opportunities for collaboration among residents, sustaining McQueen’s legacy of generosity and advocacy for new voices in the arts.

In retrospectives and exhibitions at institutions such as the Metropolitan Museum of Art and during major exhibitions like “Savage Beauty,” the Sarabande dress and collection remain central exhibits, signaling their continued significance in fashion history. The foundational ethos of fostering creativity and providing vital resources for young artists echoes the collection’s core message: that beauty, artistry, and inspiration thrive only when nurtured and shared.

## Conclusion

Sarabande exemplifies Alexander McQueen’s ability to weave conceptual complexity with technical mastery, transforming seasonal fashion into an evocative artistic event. Through its delicate floral motifs, haunting live score, and symbolic runway staging, the collection distilled McQueen’s signature vision into a singular moment of theatrical excellence. Its legacy endures not just in wardrobes and catwalks, but in a network of support and creativity that continues to inspire and empower future generations.

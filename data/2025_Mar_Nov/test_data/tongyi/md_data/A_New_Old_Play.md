# A New Old Play

**A New Old Play** (Chinese: 椒麻堂会; pinyin: Jiāomá tánghuì) is a 2021 epic Chinese historical drama directed and written by Qiu Jiongjiong. Marking Qiu’s debut in full-length narrative filmmaking, the movie stands out for its unique blend of theatrical tradition and cinematic storytelling, fusing the life of a Sichuan opera troupe over much of the twentieth century with sweeping reflections on Chinese political and social history.

### Production

The film was produced by Uluka Productions, a company based in Hong Kong, in partnership with Hippocampe Productions from France and co-produced by Midnight Blur Films. Qiu Jiongjiong not only wrote and directed the film but also contributed as editor and art director, underscoring his multifaceted role. The musical score was crafted by Lili Diao. These collaborations across different countries resulted in a transnational project recognized for its artistic ambition and technical excellence.

### Cast

At the heart of the narrative is Yi Sicheng, who plays Qiu Fu, the leading clown-role actor in a renowned Sichuan opera troupe. The principal cast also features Guan Nan, Qiu Zhimin, Xue Xuchun, and Gu Tao, among others. The actors’ performances are integral to the film’s emotional resonance and to its mission of tracing both personal histories and national upheaval.

### Release

"A New Old Play" premiered in competition for the prestigious Golden Leopard at the 74th Locarno International Film Festival on August 13, 2021. The film’s wide release occurred in France on April 30, 2025, following its celebrated festival circuit. In addition to international festivals, the film has been screened in various theaters and cultural institutions, such as the Museum of the Moving Image in New York and the National Museum of Asian Art in Washington, D.C. Streaming platforms like OVID.tv have also provided access to wider audiences.

### Plot Overview

Set in early twentieth-century Sichuan province, the film centers on Qiu Fu, a master clown actor whose theatrical career mirrors the transformations of modern China. When Qiu Fu dies in the 1980s, he is ushered into the Underworld, where he is compelled to relive pivotal moments from his own and the nation's history. The narrative unwinds as a dreamlike meditation that connects individual biography with collective memory, integrating elements of vaudevillian theater, Brechtian distancing, and symbolic mise-en-scène. Through Qiu Fu’s journey, the film offers a panoramic view of Sichuan opera traditions amid seismic political change, traversing eras from the Republican period to the reforms of the late twentieth century.

### Reception

Critics have acclaimed "A New Old Play" for its ambitious structure and inventive fusion of genres. The New York Times characterized it as a “magnificently layered historical epic,” noting how it blends tragedy with farce and brings new vitality to narratives of suffering and resilience. The New Yorker highlighted its daring imagination and the intimate perspective it brings to historical sweep, describing the film as a critic’s pick and emphasizing its emotional impact. On Rotten Tomatoes, the movie holds a Fresh score based on dozens of reviews, with critics frequently praising its originality, visual power, and ability to balance spectacle with nuance.

Audiences and reviewers alike have responded to the film’s bold intermedial strategies—combining elements of theater and cinema—and its willingness to foreground the voices of ordinary people amidst large-scale historical forces. The runtime of nearly three hours is generally regarded as immersive rather than burdensome, with many reviews noting the clarity and momentum of the storytelling throughout.

### Legacy and Influence

Qiu Jiongjiong’s background in painting and documentary filmmaking informs the distinctive visual texture of "A New Old Play." Drawing deeply from his upbringing immersed in Sichuan opera traditions, the film pays homage to popular arts and their capacity to persist and inspire despite tumultuous times. It has become a reference point for scholarly discussions about intermediality, narrative complexity, and the adaptation of traditional forms for new cinematic purposes.

The film’s legacy lies in its artistic innovation, narrative ambition, and commitment to exploring the intersections of personal fate and public history. Its reception at festivals, inclusion in museum exhibitions, and continuing availability for streaming reflect its broad appeal and enduring relevance. "A New Old Play" thus occupies a special place in the landscape of twenty-first-century world cinema, inviting viewers to reconsider how stories are told and remembered.

# Cow (Mario)

**Cow**—also known as **Moo Moo** in the Mario universe—is a recurring species of bovine that has become a notable part of Nintendo's Mario franchise. Starting out as a simple obstacle in background courses, the cow evolved from non-playable character to playable racer and cultural phenomenon, impacting several corners of the Mario series and beyond.

# Overview

Cows appear as a species across multiple games within the Mario franchise, most prominently in the Mario Kart series. Their initial appearance was in _Mario Kart 64_ in 1996, where they occupied farm-themed tracks as passive background figures. Over subsequent titles, cows became both visual staples and gameplay obstacles, eventually earning recognition as a unique mascot for certain Mario Kart courses. In 2025, Cows were elevated to the status of a playable character in _Mario Kart World_, marking their debut as a full participant in the racing roster.

The species is characterized by its white coat with black spots, blue collar, bell, horns, hooves, pink udders, and often features a nose ring. This distinct look is consistent across games and merchandise, cementing its place in Mario lore.

# First Appearances and Evolution

Cow’s journey began with their introduction in _Mario Kart 64_, serving mainly as eye candy in courses such as Moo Moo Farm. Players could see them grazing or crossing paths at the perimeter of the track but were never able to interact with them directly.

This changed in _Mario Kart Wii_ and subsequent entries, where cows transitioned into active hazards on tracks like Moo Moo Meadows. When struck, these cows caused players’ karts to spin out, providing new challenges and strategy elements.

In further Mario Kart games—including _Mario Kart DS_, _Mario Kart 8_, and their deluxe editions—cows appeared more frequently with increased color variety and enhanced animations. Tracks and menus continued to feature cow-related imagery, especially in course advertisements and themed sponsorships.

One significant development was the appearance of cows in spin-off games:
- In _Mario Party 2_, _Mario Party 3_, and _Mario Party: Island Tour_, cows make background appearances on board pathways.
- _Mario Pinball Land_ introduced a bonus system where hitting a Monty Mole sometimes triggered cows to pop out of holes for a big point reward.
- _Yoshi's Crafted World_ featured cows and related branding—most notably "Moo Moo Meadows Milk"—as background props and as collectible crafts.
- In _Mario & Sonic at the London 2012 Olympic Games_, cows appear as obstacles in the Dream Equestrian event.

# Playable Character in Mario Kart World

A turning point came with _Mario Kart World_. For the first time in the entire Mario series, a Cow was made playable right from game launch. The creature is depicted as a female bovine, wearing bells and a nose ring, and wields distinctive attributes—her body has multiple patterns, she is associated with the Moo Moo brand, and her home track, Moo Moo Meadows, showcases her image heavily.

As a heavyweight, the Cow shares statistics with characters like Wario, Waluigi, Donkey Kong, and others. Notably, she lacks customizable alternate costumes (unlike mainline characters), a design choice that sparked some criticism among fans.

## Concept and Creation

The decision to add the Cow as a playable character originated during early development. One of the designers produced a humorous sketch depicting a cow driving a truck—a concept that captured the team's attention. The artwork suggested a novelty character who could tie into existing themes, leading to the Cow's inclusion.

According to art director Masaaki Ishikawa, this idea unlocked the potential for including other obstacle-only characters as racers, promoting the concept of "NPC drivers" in Mario Kart. The character’s implementation expanded Mario Kart’s worldbuilding and led to subsequent changes in character roster strategies.

## Reception and Critical Praise

Cows quickly gained attention from both critics and players. Nintendo Life’s Kate Grey described their designs throughout Mario Kart installments, praising their round and unintelligent appearance as "much too dumb to live", while noting the Wii-era update as "an incredible upgrade on a timeless cow classic". Famitsu writer Abu Yamazaki called them "energetic and cute", exclaiming enthusiasm for their "exquisite expression". Both reviewers appreciated how the character's playful animation and evolving details resonated with audiences.

A victory animation where the Cow stretches and reveals her udders drew special notice for its anatomical realism. Automaton Japan viewed this attention to detail as evidence of advancing animal depictions in video games, describing the rendering as "almost a reward".

## Popularity and Fan Reaction

Following the reveal of _Mario Kart World_, the Cow rapidly became a breakout star in social media. Viral videos and fan campaigns spread across platforms like Instagram and TikTok, with one Instagram Reel garnering nearly 2 million views. Users celebrated the character as a symbol of joy amid broader gaming trends, often expressing delight at seeing a previously minor NPC become a central figure.

Players adopted the Cow as their favored racer (“cow mains”), influencing match dynamics and lobby distributions. The character’s inclusion prompted tribute art, cosplay, and countless memes. Its sudden prominence overshadowed even major hardware launches, becoming a topic of broader conversation about game design and player agency.

## Criticism and Controversies

Despite widespread acclaim, some critiques emerged regarding the Cow’s design and functionality.

### Costume Exclusion

Critics pointed out that the Cow, unlike many main characters, could not access the new costume/unlockable outfit system present in _Mario Kart World_. Mainline characters receive regional or thematic outfits based on unlocking specific items or completing missions, but cows remain stuck with their default appearance. Fans expressed disappointment over missed opportunities for creative expressions, such as biker or regional costumes.

### Cannibalism Concerns

Because in-game mechanics allow cows to eat burgers and steak found along race tracks, some commentators raised questions about whether the Cow was cannibalistic—especially since the same burger appears on in-game menus and product packaging labeled as “Moo Moo Meadows Milk”. While producers offered vague answers about food types (“top secret”), debate persisted online about animal ethics and game narratives involving consumption.

### PETA Protest

Animal rights organization PETA sent a public letter to Nintendo president Shuntaro Furukawa protesting the Cow’s brass nose ring. The group highlighted real-world practices of nose ring use in dairy and meat industries, suggesting it represented cruelty toward animals. Nintendo responded by referencing the fictional context of the character, while social media users noted the distinction between virtual and real animal treatment.

## Other Merchandise and Collaborations

Cows continue to influence Mario-related content outside the racing genre:
- The **Moo Moo Offroader** vehicle in _Mario Kart Tour_ combines off-road karting with cow branding, featuring special skills like Dash Panel Plus.
- The **Moo Moo Mii Racing Suit** allows custom avatars to dress up as cows, complete with the signature Mushroom Cannon ability.
- "Moo Moo Meadows Milk" remains a recurring in-universe sponsor, tying back to _Mario Kart 8/8 Deluxe_ advertising and additional Mario Kart Tour backgrounds.
- In _Minecraft: Super Mario Mash-Up Pack_, vanilla cow mobs are replaced with bovine textures modeled after the Mario cow.

## Conclusion

The evolution of the Cow from silent background element to beloved playable character demonstrates Nintendo’s responsiveness to community feedback and creative experimentation. Alongside gameplay innovation, the Cow has become an emblem of joyful absurdity within the Mario universe, inspiring both nostalgia and new fandoms. Its legacy reflects ongoing debates about representation, inclusivity, and the power of even small characters to capture global imagination.

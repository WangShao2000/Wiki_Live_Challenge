# Second-Ponce de Leon Baptist Church

Second-Ponce de Leon Baptist Church is a prominent Baptist congregation located in the Buckhead district of Atlanta, Georgia, United States. It occupies the historic neighborhood of Garden Hills at 2715 Peachtree Road NE. The church stands as one of the largest and most influential congregations affiliated with both the Cooperative Baptist Fellowship and the Southern Baptist Convention (through its state affiliate, the Georgia Baptist Convention).


### Origins and Early History

The roots of Second-Ponce de Leon Baptist Church trace back to the mid-19th century through the establishment of two significant predecessor churches:

- **Second Baptist Church**: Founded on September 1, 1854, by nineteen members from First Baptist Church Atlanta, this early congregation played a foundational role in Atlanta’s Baptist history. Its first pastor was Charles M. Irwin, and the original church building was completed in 1858, becoming the city’s first brick church. Notably, during the Civil War’s Atlanta Campaign in 1864, the church's bell was rung to warn residents of advancing Union troops, and the building was preserved from destruction due to the advocacy of Catholic priest Father Thomas O’Reilly.

- **Ponce de Leon Baptist Church**: Established on October 6, 1904, with 115 charter members, this congregation grew steadily until the economic and social upheavals of the Great Depression threatened its viability. Prior to moving into dedicated facilities, services were held in homes and other local churches.

- **Buckhead Baptist Church**: Organized in 1911, this third church merged with Ponce de Leon in 1929 and subsequently relocated to their shared site before joining with Second Baptist to form the modern church.


### Merger and Formation

By the early 1930s, declining membership and financial difficulties among Atlanta’s larger congregations led three Buckhead churches—Second Baptist, Ponce de Leon, and Buckhead Baptist—to consolidate. On November 18, 1932, these churches united to create Second-Ponce de Leon Baptist Church, formalizing its place as a central pillar of the Atlanta religious landscape.


### Architectural Heritage

The present sanctuary was designed by renowned Atlanta architect George H. Bond and constructed in the Georgian Colonial and neocolonial style, drawing inspiration from colonial meeting houses and churches. Dedicated on October 10, 1937, the building is a four-story brick structure painted white, featuring a spire rising approximately 160 feet above ground level. The sanctuary originally had 70 rooms and could accommodate about 1,274 worshippers, with an additional capacity for Sunday school programs. The distinctive design contributed to the church’s status as a landmark within the Garden Hills Historic District.


### Leadership and Growth

Throughout its history, Second-Ponce de Leon Baptist Church has been guided by several notable pastors who shaped both the congregation’s growth and its broader influence:

| Pastor                   | Tenure            | Key Contributions                                            |
|--------------------------|-------------------|--------------------------------------------------------------|
| Dr. Ryland Knight        | 1931–1945         | Led the merger, established leadership foundation            |
| Dr. Monroe F. Swilley Jr.| 1945–1969        | Oversaw major expansion and founding of Atlanta Baptist College (later Mercer University Atlanta) |
| Rev. Russell H. Dilday   | 1969–1978         | Navigated denominational conflicts, advanced moderate position |
| Dr. C. Robert Marsh      | 1978–1993         | Fostered inclusive leadership and community engagement        |

Under Swilley’s leadership, the congregation reached about 3,600 members by 1954, and continued to grow steadily through the decades, making it one of the largest Southern Baptist congregations in the United States.

In addition to traditional worship and Bible studies, Second-Ponce provides extensive outreach and ministry programs aimed at diverse groups—including cancer patients, the homeless, prisoners, immigrants, students, and prayer groups. The church’s annual Easter service, Christmas Cantata, and other community events foster connections beyond the congregation’s walls.


### Community Programs and Campus Facilities

The church campus serves both spiritual and practical needs, hosting tenant organizations such as Respite Care Atlanta and Christ the King Preschool, offering ecumenical partnerships, and operating the Community Wellness Center—a full-service gym open to non-members and the general public. Weekly ministries include children’s clubs, youth programs, adult small groups, and intergenerational fellowship. Additional outreach includes meal distribution, family care, disaster response, and educational support for underserved neighborhoods.


### Demographics and Outreach

Recent surveys highlight demographic diversity within the congregation, though the majority remains White, with significant representation from multiple ethnic backgrounds. Approximately 60% of members are aged 65 or older, while younger adults comprise about 8% of the population. Recognizing post-COVID trends in declining youth participation, the church actively seeks new strategies for engaging younger adults and families.


### Notable Events and Ecumenism

Second-Ponce de Leon Baptist Church regularly hosts regional and national gatherings, including the Cooperative Baptist Fellowship General Assembly in 2021, which drew attendees from across the country for worship, workshops, and mission planning. Other notable occasions include the 2009 funeral of United States Attorney General Griffin Bell, the unveiling of the Celebrating Grace Hymnal in 2010, and the 2011 New Baptist Covenant II event, graced by former President Jimmy Carter.


### Affiliation and Denominational Role

Second-Ponce maintains formal affiliations with both the Cooperative Baptist Fellowship and the Southern Baptist Convention, participating actively in denominational missions, conventions, and interdenominational initiatives. Its leaders have frequently held state and national leadership roles, exemplified by Dr. Monroe Swilley’s tenure as president of the Georgia Baptist Convention and vice president of the Southern Baptist Foreign Mission Board.


### Conclusion

Second-Ponce de Leon Baptist Church embodies the intersection of tradition, architecture, community service, and denominational leadership. Through mergers, resilience in times of crisis, architectural vision, progressive outreach, and sustained growth, it continues to play a vital role in the life of Atlanta’s Buckhead district and in broader Southern Baptist circles. The church remains committed to sharing the gospel in action and word, serving its immediate neighbors and addressing pressing social issues from a foundation of faith and community partnership.

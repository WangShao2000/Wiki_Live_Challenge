# 1982 Los Angeles Rams–Green Bay Packers game

The opening contest of the 1982 NFL season took place on September 12 at Milwaukee County Stadium in Milwaukee, Wisconsin. In a remarkable turnaround, the Green Bay Packers rallied from a 23–0 halftime deficit to defeat the visiting Los Angeles Rams 35–23 before an audience of 53,694 fans. This victory marked the greatest comeback in Packers’ franchise history up to that time, showcasing one of the most dramatic performances in NFL regular-season contests during the modern era.

# Background

The 1982 NFL season was shaped by labor strife, as a 57-day players' strike canceled several weeks of play and dramatically altered the regular schedule. Originally planned for 16 games per team, the season was shortened to just nine games, resulting in a unique playoff format designed to accommodate the disrupted calendar. Throughout this period, both teams faced significant challenges balancing momentum and morale across an unpredictable campaign.

# Game Summary

The contest began with the Los Angeles Rams mounting a dominant offensive showing. Led by quarterback Bert Jones and supported by running back Wendell Tyler, the Rams controlled the early proceedings, outscoring the Packers 10–0 in the first quarter and adding 13 more points in the second. Key plays included a 4-yard rushing touchdown from Tyler, subsequent field goals from kicker Mike Lansford, and an 8-yard pass to tight end Mike Barber—all contributing to a commanding 23–0 halftime advantage.

Facing elimination and frustration, the Packers entered the locker room trailing by three touchdowns. However, beginning with the start of the third quarter, Green Bay orchestrated a pivotal transformation. Quarterback Lynn Dickey, despite fumbling twice and throwing two interceptions in the opening half, orchestrated a stunning reversal of fortune. The Packers completed their comeback by scoring 35 unanswered points, capped off by an Eddie Lee Ivery 27-yard touchdown run in the fourth quarter.

## Aftermath and Significance

Following the game, Packers head coach Bart Starr, reflecting on the dramatic outcome, stated that this comeback was the greatest he had ever witnessed in his decades associated with the franchise. For the 1982 season, the Packers’ ability to overcome adversity would ultimately be recognized as a turning point. Although the year was defined by uncertainty due to the labor dispute, this win energized a roster that would go on to finish with a 5–3–1 record and clinch a playoff berth.

Green Bay advanced through the expanded postseason structure introduced for that unique season, defeating the St. Louis Cardinals in the wild-card round before falling to the Dallas Cowboys in the divisional round. The Packers’ journey ended at the hands of a superior squad, but the memorable victory against the Rams underscored their capacity for resilience and determination.

In summary, the 1982 Los Angeles Rams–Green Bay Packers game stands as a testament to the unpredictability of professional football and the possibility of redemption even when facing seemingly insurmountable odds. The Packers’ comeback remains etched in team lore as a defining moment of courage and performance during one of the league’s most turbulent seasons.

# Everyone Hates Elon

Everyone Hates Elon (EHE) is a British political campaign group formed in early 2025. The organization is dedicated to opposing the influence of billionaire entrepreneur Elon Musk, who also serves as a senior adviser to US President Donald Trump. EHE’s principal objective is to challenge what it views as Musk’s support for far-right causes and excessive wealth accumulation. Operating primarily through social media networks such as Instagram and TikTok, the collective coordinates its actions both within the United Kingdom and globally.

### Formation and Leadership

EHE originated from informal discussions among several friends—identified in interviews as Emma and Jason—who were motivated by Musk’s involvement in various political disputes, notably his public gestures interpreted as supporting authoritarianism and right-wing ideology. Although these founders chose to remain pseudonymous, their backgrounds include prior experience working on progressive nonprofit campaigns. Their communications stress the importance of using humor and accessible messaging to broaden the appeal of anti-billionaire activism.

### Activist Tactics and Major Actions

Throughout 2025, Everyone Hates Elon orchestrated a range of high-profile actions intended to provoke public discussion and draw attention to their message. These tactics reflect a strategy of leveraging viral content and provocative symbolism to engage broader audiences.

#### Satirical Advertising and Stickers

In February, EHE distributed stickers bearing the slogan “Don’t Buy a Swasticar” and circulated posters titled “Goes from 0 to 1939 in 3 seconds,” referencing Musk’s actions at Donald Trump’s inauguration and associating Tesla vehicles with fascist imagery. These materials were installed on public buses and billboards across London, sometimes leading to official intervention from Transport for London, which instructed its staff to remove unauthorized advertisements. Videos and photos of these campaigns quickly spread on social media, amassing millions of views.

#### Showroom Intrusions

Another notable action involved placing a life-size cardboard cutout of Musk performing a gesture resembling a Nazi salute inside a Tesla dealership. Customers encountered this display while shopping, provoking heated responses and further amplifying online visibility. This stunt drew significant media attention, reinforcing EHE’s reputation for blending artistic protest with pointed political commentary.

#### Public Art Protests

A particularly dramatic public demonstration took place on April 10, 2025, at Hardess Studios in south London. Organizers facilitated a supervised “rage room” event where participants, following safety protocols, destroyed a donated secondhand Tesla Model S with sledgehammers and baseball bats. The vehicle, destined for scrap, was auctioned afterward to raise funds for food bank charities. The event was described as a creative outlet for expressing frustration with current socio-economic realities and billionaires’ growing power.

#### Fundraising Initiatives

Central to EHE’s operational model is a series of crowdfunding campaigns aimed at sustaining activist activities. The “People Versus Elon” initiative encourages supporters to pledge a small sum—such as one pence—each time Musk posts on his social media platform X, a practice expected to generate substantial sums given his frequent online activity. The collective reported aiming for £150,000 in annual donations, with the proceeds targeted toward ongoing projects and supporting non-profit groups combating racism and fascism in the UK, such as Everyday Racism, Rainbow Migration, Women for Refugee Women, and Hope Not Hate.

As of late September 2025, a GoFundMe campaign organized by EHE had raised £48,248 of a £50,000 target, gathering contributions from more than 2,200 donors. Funds are allocated as follows:

#### International Operations

Beyond the UK, EHE participated in international protests, including mounting banners with controversial imagery—such as a depiction of Donald Trump alongside Jeffrey Epstein—in prominent locations during high-profile visits. For example, a giant banner emblazoned with this image was displayed outside Windsor Castle during Trump’s state visit in September 2025, funded by thousands of public donations. In Venice, the group collaborated with Greenpeace activists to stage a banner opposing Jeff Bezos’s lavish wedding celebration in St Mark’s Square.

### Public Reaction and Controversy

EHE’s methods have elicited widespread media attention and mixed public responses. Supporters praise the group’s creativity and ability to reach a diverse audience, especially among younger demographics disillusioned with conventional forms of activism. However, critics argue that EHE’s appropriation of Nazi symbolism risks trivializing historical atrocities and invites accusations of inciting public disorder.

Several major outlets have reported on the controversy surrounding EHE’s tactics. Transport for London explicitly condemned and acted against unauthorized ad placements, while journalists and commentators have debated whether such provocative messaging effectively advances social justice goals or inadvertently strengthens far-right narratives. Despite these criticisms, the campaign’s high online engagement and viral reach indicate a significant level of public interest.

### Broader Context: Public Sentiment Towards Billionaires

EHE’s emergence coincided with growing public dissatisfaction toward tech billionaires and their impact on politics and society. A YouGov poll conducted in March 2025 revealed that 80% of Britons held an unfavorable view of Elon Musk, with only 13% viewing him favorably. Among specific demographic groups, the negative sentiment was even stronger, especially among Labour and Liberal Democrat voters.

These findings provide context for understanding the resonance of EHE’s anti-billionaire messaging. The campaign capitalizes on a perceived disconnect between elites and ordinary citizens, utilizing accessible language and memorable slogans to mobilize grassroots activism.

### Notability and Impact

EHE’s activities have received coverage from prominent news organizations such as The Guardian, The New Yorker, Hollywood Reporter, BBC, and Reuters. Their multifaceted approach—combining street art, fundraising, digital advocacy, and international protest—positions them as one of the most visible groups challenging billionaire influence in contemporary political discourse.

### Conclusion

Everyone Hates Elon represents an innovative, if contentious, strand of contemporary activism seeking to disrupt and critique the role of billionaires in shaping global politics. Through humor, subversive marketing, and direct confrontation, the group has contributed to public debates about wealth inequality, democracy, and the boundaries of protest speech. While their tactics are not without controversy, EHE’s prominence reflects wider societal tensions around accountability, representation, and the responsibilities of ultra-wealthy individuals in public life.

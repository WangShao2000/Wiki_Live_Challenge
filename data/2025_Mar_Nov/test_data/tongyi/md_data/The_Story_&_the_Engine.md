# The Story & the Engine

"The Story & the Engine" is the fifth episode of the fifteenth series of the British science-fiction television series Doctor Who. It was written by playwright Inua Ellams, directed by Makalla McPherson, and released on BBC iPlayer, BBC One, and Disney+ on 10 May 2025. In this episode, the Fifteenth Doctor (portrayed by Ncuti Gatwa) and his companion, Belinda Chandra (Varada Sethu), travel to Lagos, Nigeria. There, they become trapped in a barbershop by an enigmatic figure known as the Barber, who uses the power of stories to power an engine he constructed. The episode features a largely BIPOC cast and explores themes of identity, displacement, and storytelling, building on motifs present in Ellams' play Barber Shop Chronicles. The story was primarily filmed at Wolf Studios Wales in Cardiff with establishing shots taken in Lagos, and it garnered positive critical responses, although some reviewers noted complexity and pacing concerns.

# Plot

The Fifteenth Doctor and Belinda Chandra arrive in Lagos, Nigeria in 2019. Belinda remains in the TARDIS while the Doctor visits Omo, a friend who runs a barbershop. Upon entering, the Doctor discovers that Omo and several others have gone missing. He finds himself trapped inside the shop, where Omo reveals that a mysterious figure called the Barber is in control. The Barber forces the missing men to tell stories to power an engine he built, using their narratives as fuel.

The Barber works alongside a woman named Abby, whom the Doctor recognizes but cannot identify. After discovering that Omo told the Barber about him, the Doctor attempts to escape but nearly falls out because the shop itself is traveling along the back of a colossal spider walking on a vast web, known as the Nexus. Only the Barber and Abby are able to leave the shop freely. Belinda eventually arrives, and she too becomes trapped.

As the situation unfolds, the Doctor urges the Barber to reveal his true identity. The Barber explains that he is a storyteller who assisted various gods by spreading and preserving their stories via the web. However, his efforts became too effective, leading the gods to sever his connection to the Nexus. The Doctor realizes that Abby is actually Abena, the daughter of Anansi, a deity from West African mythology, whom the Doctor abandoned during his Fugitive incarnation due to prior commitments. Abena has joined forces with the Barber to seek revenge.

The Barber intends to reach the center of the Nexus to cut off all the gods from the web, resulting in their deaths and destabilizing human culture. Abena has a change of heart after conversations with the Doctor and others and braids his hair into a map that guides him to the core of the engine's power source. The Doctor and Belinda reconfigure the engine's heart so that the Doctor can power it using his own life story. The energy required is immense, causing the engine to begin destroying itself. Ultimately, the Doctor persuades the Barber to free the trapped people and escape with them. The engine is destroyed, returning the shop to normal.

The missing men reunite with their loved ones. Omo apologizes to the Doctor and retires from running the barbershop, allowing the Barber to take over. Abena departs to live her own life. The Doctor and Belinda leave Lagos as the Doctor tells her how he met Omo, reflecting on the emotional journey they have shared.

# Development and Production

The episode was written by Inua Ellams, marking his first full contribution to Doctor Who. Ellams had previously been approached in earlier series cycles and contributed ideas during development phases that did not result in completed scripts. He sought to write for the show again after Russell T Davies resumed his role as showrunner in September 2021. Ellams reached out to Davies, sometimes via social media platforms, and began discussions about potential storylines.

Initially, Ellams developed a larger concept that was rejected as it overlapped with another script in production. Following this setback, Davies suggested the team focus on a story set in a barbershop, inspired by Ellams' stage play Barber Shop Chronicles. Ellams described the play and episode as having 'connective tissue' conceptually but as distinct works. The character of the Barber emerged from Ellams’ research into the French word for 'ghostwriter,' which historically involved stigmatized practices. He expanded the concept into that of an African male storyteller operating without credit, linking it to Anansi, a central figure in West African folklore.

The episode was crafted as a companion piece to the 2024 episode 'Dot and Bubble,' exploring the interplay between the Doctor’s Black identity and broader cultural narratives. Themes of identity, displacement, and destiny run throughout. Elements from two poems in Ellams’ 2020 collection The Actual were incorporated into the script.

During production, there were concerns about making the antagonist too frightening. However, Ellams considered Chris Chibnall’s guidance that the series is essentially sci-fi and horror for children and all ages. The episode marks a significant moment for representation, featuring a predominantly BIPOC cast and drawing upon African mythology and contemporary Nigerian culture.

# Casting

"The Story & the Engine" stars Ncuti Gatwa as the Fifteenth Doctor and Varada Sethu as Belinda Chandra. Ariyon Bakare guest-stars as the Barber, with Sule Rimi portraying Omo Esosa, owner of the barbershop. Other supporting actors include Michelle Asante as Abena, Stefan Adegbola as Rashid Abubakar, Jordan Adene as Tunde Adebayo, and Michael Balogun as Obioma Okoli, among others. Rimi and Balogun had previously worked with Ellams on Barber Shop Chronicles.

Jo Martin reprises her role as the Fugitive Doctor, motivated by Russell T Davies’ desire to acknowledge her character’s continued existence, especially given the episode's focus on the Doctor’s ethnicity. The production included archive footage of past Doctors. Inua Ellams makes a cameo appearance in the episode. Sienna-Robyn Mavanga-Phipps briefly reprises her role as Poppy from 'Space Babies' (2024).

## Filming and Production Design

Directed by Makalla McPherson and filmed in early 2024, the episode belonged to the fourth production block of the series along with 'The Interstellar Song Contest.' Filming took place mainly at Wolf Studios Wales in Cardiff due to logistical constraints, although establishing drone shots were captured in Lagos to enhance authenticity. Previsualization techniques were employed to plan set design, blocking, and action sequences. The Nigerian market scene was shot on 19 February 2024 at Wolf Studios; the back of the barbershop and engine room were constructed on separate soundstages.

Multiple Nigerian consultants ensured accurate representation of sets, hairstyles, and linguistic nuances. Eighty extras were used for the market scene, adding depth and realism to the episode. Behind-the-scenes featurettes and interviews were made available on platforms like YouTube to offer viewers insight into the production process.

## Broadcast and Viewership

The episode premiered simultaneously on BBC iPlayer in the UK at 8 a.m. BST and Disney+ in the US at 12 a.m. PDT on 10 May 2025. It was subsequently broadcast on BBC One at 7:10 p.m. BST. International distribution was handled by Disney.

Within seven days of release, the episode was viewed by 2.70 million people. Overnight broadcast ratings showed 1.59 million viewers, making it one of the most-watched episodes of the season.

## Reception

Critics offered generally positive assessments of "The Story & the Engine," though some pointed to concerns about narrative complexity and pacing. The episode received an 89% approval rating on Rotten Tomatoes, based on reviews from nine critics. Individual review scores varied:

| Publication            | Score/Notes                                     |
|------------------------|------------------------------------------------|
| The A.V. Club          | A-                                             |
| Bleeding Cool          | 10/10                                          |
| GamesRadar+            | Mixed; praised originality, criticized pace     |
| IGN                    | 7/10                                           |
| Radio Times            | Mixed reviews                                  |
| Vulture                | Highly rated                                   |
| Den of Geek            | Positive, noting atmosphere and setting         |
| Newsweek               | Enjoyed final act, preferred narrower focus     |

Many reviewers highlighted Ellams' writing, the thematic richness, and the unique setting as strengths. Some critiqued the complexity of the plot and unclear stakes, suggesting improvements for subsequent episodes.

In summary, "The Story & the Engine" represents a significant addition to the Doctor Who canon for its innovative storytelling, cultural relevance, and emphasis on African narratives. Its blend of mythological elements and contemporary issues, coupled with strong performances and meticulous production design, make it a standout installment in the series' ongoing legacy.

# Background

Pinkshift is a Baltimore-based trio formed in 2018 at Johns Hopkins University. The band consists of vocalist Ashrita Kumar, guitarist Paul Vallejo, and drummer Myron Houngbedji. All three members—Kumar (Indian American), Vallejo (Peruvian American), and Houngbedji (from Benin)—are children of immigrants who came to the United States with aspirations in STEM fields. Their unique backgrounds and commitment to social justice have made Pinkshift stand out in the contemporary rock scene, especially regarding increasing racial and gender diversity in alternative music.

Following the success of their debut EP, *Saccharine*, in 2021, Pinkshift signed to Hopeless Records. They began writing material for their first full-length album late in 2020 and recorded *Love Me Forever* in March 2022 at Studio 4 in Conshohocken, Pennsylvania, under the guidance of prolific producer Will Yip, known for his work with bands such as Turnstile and Mannequin Pussy.

The album opens with the single “i’m not crying you’re crying,” which set the tone for Pinkshift’s dynamic approach to songwriting. Additional singles from the album include “nothing (in my head),” released in June 2022, and “GET OUT,” released in August 2022, both accompanied by official music videos that emphasize themes of personal empowerment and defiance against societal norms.

# Critical Reception

Upon its release, *Love Me Forever* received positive feedback from critics across various outlets:

- **New Noise Magazine** awarded the album four stars, praising its balance between vulnerability and defiance, with standout performances from the entire band.
- **Kerrang!** also awarded a four-star rating, highlighting the album as a powerful debut that showcases both punk energy and emotional depth.
- **Under the Radar** gave it a 7.5/10, describing it as pummeling, purposeful, and filled with cathartic expression, effectively situating Pinkshift among the next generation of punk musicians.
- **Flood Magazine** lauded the album as “phenomenal” and “utterly indispensable,” commending its diverse influences and raw emotion. They also recognized Pinkshift’s significance in advancing the inclusion of people of color within alternative rock—a genre traditionally dominated by white male acts.

The album has been noted for addressing themes like anxiety, depression, insecurity, heartbreak, generational trauma, and political resistance, all delivered through vocal performances marked by both rage and empathy. Critics often point out how Pinkshift deftly balances “vulnerability and defiance” in their lyrics and performance style.

## Personnel

- **Ashrita Kumar**: Lead vocals (also identifies as nonbinary)
- **Paul Vallejo**: Guitar
- **Myron Houngbedji**: Drums

All members bring interdisciplinary academic backgrounds—from engineering and neuroscience to molecular biology—and are committed to activism, supporting causes such as inclusion for trans communities and humanitarian efforts in Palestine.

## Music Videos and Promotion

Pinkshift promoted *Love Me Forever* through several music videos for singles:

- “i’m not crying you’re crying” (album opener)
- “nothing (in my head)” (released June 2022, featured lyrics about feeling overwhelmed and claustrophobic)
- “GET OUT” (released August 2022; the video depicts defiance against patriarchal and oppressive structures)

These videos contributed to the album’s virality and helped Pinkshift build a strong following beyond traditional indie music circles.

## Significance and Representation

Pinkshift’s rise marks a notable shift in rock music toward greater diversity and inclusivity. Their presence as artists of color, LGBTQ+, and international descent provides an important platform for marginalized voices within a scene historically dominated by white men. This broader cultural impact is regularly cited by music journalists and fans alike.

## Conclusion

*Love Me Forever* represents a crucial step forward for Pinkshift, solidifying their status as innovative punk musicians with a distinct message and multidimensional artistry. Through emotionally charged lyrics, dynamic instrumentation, and advocacy for social change, the album embodies both the power and necessity of punk music in the 21st century.

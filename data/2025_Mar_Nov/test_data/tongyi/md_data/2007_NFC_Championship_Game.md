# 2007 NFC Championship Game

The 2007 National Football Conference (NFC) Championship Game was played on January 20, 2008, at Lambeau Field in Green Bay, Wisconsin. The game featured two teams with contrasting seasons: the top-seeded Green Bay Packers (13–3), who had just beaten the Seattle Seahawks in the Divisional round, and the sixth-seeded New York Giants (10–6), who entered as heavy underdogs after victories over Tampa Bay and Dallas. This championship match became one of the most memorable playoff games in NFL history, combining frigid weather, dramatic turnovers, and a decisive overtime moment.

### Game Context and Weather Conditions

The Packers were heavily favored entering the game, with oddsmakers setting a spread of 7.5 points in their favor. Their roster included legendary quarterback Brett Favre, whose career with Green Bay was approaching an endgame. The Giants, led by quarterback Eli Manning, had overcome multiple challenges, including late-season adversity and injuries, to reach the NFC title game.

What made the matchup especially notable was the extreme cold at Lambeau Field. Temperatures plummeted to minus 1 degree Fahrenheit, with wind chills near minus 32 degrees—making this the third coldest NFL game on record, following two iconic “Ice Bowl” contests. Fans braved the elements with jackets, blankets, and hot drinks, while players endured brutal conditions that affected both offensive execution and defensive intensity.

### Game Flow and Key Moments

The NFC Championship unfolded as a tight contest, marked by field goals, big plays, and critical mistakes. The first half saw the Packers gain momentum behind a long touchdown catch by Donald Driver, who broke free for a 71-yard score to give Green Bay a 10–6 lead at halftime. Mason Crosby added a 36-yard field goal to extend their advantage late in the first half.

In the second half, the Giants responded. Brandon Jacobs powered in from an inch out, capitalizing on multiple penalties against Green Bay’s defense, including a roughing-the-passer call on Brett Favre. Quarterback Eli Manning drove his team downfield, setting up Ahmad Bradshaw’s 4-yard touchdown run and taking the lead.

Green Bay answered quickly when Favre connected with Donald Lee on a 12-yard TD pass, reclaiming the lead. However, Domenik Hixon’s kickoff return energized the Giants’ offense, leading to another Eli Manning masterclass: a 23-yard diving catch by Amani Toomer set up Bradshaw’s touchdown run, putting New York ahead once more.

Mason Crosby’s 37-yard field goal in the dying minutes tied the game at 20–20, sending the contest into overtime. Lawrence Tynes, who missed two attempts earlier, redeemed himself by connecting on a 47-yard field goal three plays after Corey Webster intercepted Brett Favre in OT. This decisive kick sealed the Giants’ improbable victory and sent them to Super Bowl XLII.

### Aftermath and Significance

The win propelled the Giants into Super Bowl XLII, where they achieved the ultimate upset by defeating the previously undefeated New England Patriots 17–14, ending the Patriots’ perfect season bid. This triumph solidified Eli Manning’s legacy and brought lasting fame to the “Helmet Catch” play and Plaxico Burress’s game-winning reception.

For the Packers, Brett Favre’s interception marked his last meaningful play with Green Bay before transitioning to the New York Jets. The defeat capped an otherwise successful season but left fans yearning for a championship that eluded them despite their regular season dominance.

The game is remembered not only for its dramatic finish but also for the extraordinary endurance displayed by both teams in subzero conditions. It stands as an exemplar of how preparation, courage, and opportunity can overcome even the most daunting odds.

### Conclusion

The 2007 NFC Championship Game remains a defining moment in NFL postseason history—a blend of cold, competition, and drama that shaped the legacies of both franchises. Its impact resonates through subsequent seasons, serving as a benchmark for resilience, unexpected heroics, and the unpredictable nature of football.

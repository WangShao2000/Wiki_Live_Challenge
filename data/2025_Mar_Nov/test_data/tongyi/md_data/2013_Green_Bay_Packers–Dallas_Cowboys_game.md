# 2013 Green Bay Packers–Dallas Cowboys Game

The 2013 Green Bay Packers–Dallas Cowboys game was a memorable National Football League regular-season contest held on December 15, 2013, at AT&T Stadium in Arlington, Texas. In one of the most dramatic turnarounds in Packers’ history, Green Bay defeated Dallas 37–36 after trailing 26–3 at halftime—a comeback matching the largest in franchise lore. The game took place during Week 15 of the 2013 NFL season and featured critical playoff implications for both teams.

### Background

Entering the game, both teams were in tense battles for postseason contention. The Packers sat at 6–6–1 and were within half a game of the Detroit Lions atop the NFC North division. The Cowboys held a 7–6 record but were second in the NFC East standings and required a victory to remain in playoff contention. This context heightened the stakes, as both franchises faced must-win scenarios to secure division titles or wild-card berths.

Green Bay’s star quarterback, Aaron Rodgers, was sidelined due to a broken collarbone suffered earlier in the season, leading to Matt Flynn assuming starting duties. Dallas relied on Tony Romo, who would later miss much of the remainder of the year due to back injuries, including this game’s aftermath.

#### Early Play

The first two quarters saw Dallas build a commanding lead. Dan Bailey kicked three field goals for the Cowboys, while Tony Romo connected with Jason Witten for a touchdown to push the score to 13–3. Another field goal and a DeMarco Murray rushing touchdown extended Dallas’s advantage to 26–3 by halftime.

#### Second Half

Green Bay mounted its furious rally after halftime. A crucial momentum shift came as the Packers capitalized on turnovers and aggressive play. Matt Flynn threw four touchdown passes in the second half alone, showing poise despite Rodgers’ absence. Key touchdown strikes included a 13-yard pass to Jordy Nelson and a 3-yard connection to Andrew Quarless, allowing Green Bay to begin erasing Dallas’s lead.

Despite giving up a field goal in the third quarter, the Packers surged ahead in the fourth. The game was marked by fluctuating leads, with neither team able to establish dominance until the closing minutes.

#### Decisive Moments

With just over a minute left, Eddie Lacy delivered the game-winning 1-yard rushing touchdown following a turnover by Romo. Lacy’s performance included 141 rushing yards and a touchdown, anchoring the offense throughout the comeback. Dan Bailey’s field goals and DeMarco Murray’s rushing contributions for Dallas kept them close, but ultimately, their late-game mistakes proved costly.

### Aftermath and Impact

This win propelled the Packers into the postseason picture. The victory was emotionally significant for coach Mike McCarthy, who celebrated it as “one of the hardest wins” his staff had experienced. The Packers finished the regular season with an 8–7–1 record and secured the NFC North title, although they ultimately lost in the Wild Card playoffs to the San Francisco 49ers.

For the Cowboys, missing the playoffs continued a streak of disappointing seasons. They ended 8–8 and remained second in the NFC East, failing to advance despite the strong early-season showing. The game is remembered for the collapse and the missed chances, particularly Tony Romo’s late turnovers.

### Historical Significance

The Packers’ comeback was tied for the greatest in franchise history, matching a 1982 effort, and underscored the unpredictable nature of high-pressure NFL contests. It also illustrated the importance of resilience, especially in adversity such as facing key player absences and mounting deficits.

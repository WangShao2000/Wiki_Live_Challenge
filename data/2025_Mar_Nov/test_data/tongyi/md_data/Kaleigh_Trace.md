# Kaleigh Trace

Kaleigh Trace (born 1986) is a Canadian writer, sex educator, and therapist based in Toronto, Ontario. She is recognized for her candid discussions of sexuality, disability, and feminism. Trace’s personal experiences—including a spinal cord injury resulting from a car accident at age nine—have shaped her advocacy, educational work, and writing. Her book _Hot, Wet, and Shaking: How I Learned to Talk About Sex_ (2014) explores her journey through sexuality, disability, and self-acceptance, and has been widely acclaimed and translated internationally.


### Early Life and Education

Trace was born in 1986. When she was just under ten years old, her family was involved in a serious car accident that caused severe spinal cord damage. She was diagnosed with paraplegia and spent part of her childhood using a wheelchair. Despite this, she regained mobility within a year, describing her current movement as a “wobbly two-step.” This early experience with disability has informed much of her later activism and storytelling, emphasizing bodies outside normative standards and the importance of embracing differences.

After high school, Trace earned a Bachelor of Arts degree from Dalhousie University in 2009. She later pursued graduate studies at the University of Guelph, where she completed a Masters of Science in Couple & Family Therapy. She is now registered as a psychotherapist with the College of Registered Psychotherapists of Ontario.


### Career as Sex Educator and Blogger

Trace’s professional career began with her employment at Venus Envy, a Halifax-based sex shop and retailer specializing in sexual wellness products and education. There, she led workshops, wrote online sex advice, and further developed her voice in sex-positive communities. In parallel, she launched her blog, _The Fucking Facts_, which provides accessible and inclusive resources about sexual health, disability, and consent. The blog’s no-nonsense approach blends humor and frankness, intentionally avoiding medicalized language and centering disabled perspectives on pleasure and desire.

Her emphasis on inclusive language challenged both clinical terminology and heteronormative frameworks commonly found in mainstream sex education. Trace actively promoted using words like “sex toy” instead of “assistive device,” arguing that euphemisms strip intimacy and agency from disability-related conversations.

Trace’s reputation grew nationally; her work appeared in outlets such as _Toronto Star_, _The Coast_, and _Shameless Magazine_. She also contributed essays to CBC Radio’s “Tapestry” program, advocating for open discussion about disability and sexual identity.


### Parody Song and Advocacy

In 2013, Trace co-wrote and performed a parody song titled “Ask First” with colleague J. Mary Burnet. The track reworked Robin Thicke’s “Blurred Lines” as an anthem for enthusiastic consent and sex positivity, responding directly to what she and others saw as the original song’s normalization of non-consensual behavior. Their video went viral, amassing tens of thousands of views quickly. “Ask First” highlighted the necessity of permission and communication in sexual relationships, reinforcing Trace’s belief that linguistic clarity is foundational to empowerment and safety.


### Literary Work

#### _Hot, Wet, and Shaking: How I Learned to Talk About Sex_

Trace’s memoir, _Hot, Wet, and Shaking_, was published in 2014 by Invisible Publishing. Inspired by her blog and personal history, the book chronicles her adolescence through adulthood, focusing on sexuality, disability, abortion, and the intersections of queer identity. Through candid anecdotes—sometimes comedic, sometimes profound—the memoir challenges stigma, invites readers to consider diverse expressions of pleasure, and confronts ableist narratives about disabled people’s sexual lives.

The book received critical acclaim, winning the Evelyn Richardson Memorial Non-Fiction Award at the Atlantic Book Awards in 2015. In addition, it was recognized as a gold winner for best book by _The Coast_, a Halifax newspaper. Its straightforward narrative style and willingness to blend humor with vulnerability resonated with both literary critics and general audiences.

A decade after its debut, the memoir was republished as a 10th anniversary edition, featuring new chapters reflecting on Trace’s experience with terminal bladder cancer diagnosed in 2022. The updated version celebrates the original’s spirit while acknowledging evolving perspectives on mortality, pain, and enduring joy.

#### Other Writings

Trace authored a small manual/zine entitled “Every Body's Doing It - Talking About Sex and Disability.” Distributed through Canadian sex-positive retailers like Venus Envy and Come As You Are, the resource seeks to bridge gaps between accessibility and fun, demystifying sex for disabled individuals and allies alike.


### German Translation and International Recognition

The international reach of _Hot, Wet, and Shaking_ grew with the release of a German translation in 2020. Translated by linguist Penelope Dützmann, the German edition was featured at the Frankfurt Book Fair as part of Canada’s Guest of Honor programming and the Canadian Embassy in Germany’s cultural initiatives.


### Personal Life, Health, and Activism

Trace openly identifies as a disabled, queer feminist. She has written extensively about reclaiming body image and challenging social stigma attached to disability. Her advocacy includes speaking engagements at conferences nationwide and creating spaces for conversation around desire, intersectionality, and pleasure among marginalized groups.

Diagnosed with terminal bladder cancer in October 2022, Trace’s perspective shifted from living entirely through public engagement to integrating vulnerability and acceptance into her daily existence. She reduced work hours following surgery and diagnosis but continues to share insights about living authentically amid suffering.


### Therapist Practice

Combining decades of experience in sexual health education with academic credentials, Trace established a private practice in couple and family therapy. She works with clients to foster spaces of openness, healing, and justice, consistently advocating for the integration of power analysis and anti-oppressive frameworks in therapeutic work.


### Little Deaths Salon

In response to her changing relationship to mortality and pleasure, Trace created “Little Deaths Salon”—a quarterly performance series in Toronto exploring topics ranging from intimacy to death. The free event, held at local venues such as It’s Ok* Studios, features writers, artists, and performers sharing stories that challenge taboos and invite collective reflection. Since its launch in 2025, Little Deaths Salon has drawn sold-out crowds, fostering community and solidarity around difficult subjects.


### Reception and Awards

_Hot, Wet, and Shaking_ remains influential in disability literature and sex education, being cited in academic and activist circles. The book’s success has extended beyond awards, with inclusion in various “books of influence” lists and bestseller rankings. Notably, in July 2024, it ranked eighth on The Hamilton Review of Books’ Independently Published Bestsellers List.

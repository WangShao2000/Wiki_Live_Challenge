# Multo (song)

“Multo” is a 2024 single by Cup of Joe, an alternative pop band from Baguio, Philippines. The song’s title means “ghost” in Filipino, and it serves as both an emotional metaphor and central theme—a meditation on lingering feelings after loss, loss, or heartbreak. Released on September 14, 2024, under Viva Records, “Multo” became the lead single from Cup of Joe’s debut studio album, Silakbo.

### Background and Release

Cup of Joe, formed in 2019, comprises Raphael Ridao (vocals), Gabriel Fernandez (guitar), CJ Fernandez (guitar), Gian Bernardino (keys), Xen Gareza (bass), and Ralph Gavieres (drums). “Multo” was written by co-lead vocalist Raphael Ridao and his brother Redentor Immanuel Ridao during the height of the global pandemic. Drawing inspiration from their personal experiences with grief and isolation, the brothers sought to process their emotions through songwriting, resulting in a track that resonated deeply with listeners. The song was produced by Shadiel Chan, CEO of Nine Degrees North, who brought polish and rawness to its sound.

“Multo” arrived at a pivotal moment for Filipino pop culture, with growing reach through digital platforms such as Spotify and TikTok. Its release coincided with viral trends and online storytelling, which amplified its impact. The accompanying music video, released in March 2025 and starring Filipino actors Miles Ocampo and Elijah Canlas, tells a cinematic narrative of love and loss—emphasizing the song’s haunting effect and cementing its cultural resonance.

### Composition, Lyrics, and Genre

Musically, “Multo” is classified as a synth-pop and pop-rock ballad. The piece combines melancholic synth layers with introspective guitar and heartfelt vocals, blending elements of alternative pop and traditional OPM styles. Lyrically, the song explores themes of lingering memory, unresolved grief, and self-reflection. Central lines include “Pasindi na ng ilaw” (“Turn on the lights”) and “Minumulto na ‘ko ng damdamin ko” (“I am haunted by my feelings”), which critics interpreted as metaphors for emotional persistence and artistic maturation.

The track’s arrangement and structure were crafted to evoke vulnerability and catharsis, marking a departure from the upbeat image Cup of Joe previously cultivated. It stands out for its ability to balance accessibility—humming melodies and relatable content—with artistic depth, drawing comparisons to established artists like Ben&Ben and The 1975 while retaining a distinctive Philippine identity.

### Critical Reception

Upon its release, “Multo” received widespread acclaim from music journalists, industry experts, and the public. Critics highlighted its emotional complexity and innovative sound within the Filipino music scene. 

Nica Glorioso of Nylon Manila praised the track as “a beautiful song that does its job of making people feel what the band intended them to feel,” further describing it as stirring, evocative, and “haunting yet cathartic.” Elijah Pareño of Rolling Stone Philippines called it an “artistic awakening” for the group, marking their arrival as serious storytellers. Gabriel Saulog of Billboard Philippines wrote that the song “hits you straight in the feels,” noting its ambition and experimental edge.

The song became a viral sensation across social media, spawning countless edits and reinterpretations by creators from around the world. Fans shared stories of how its lyrics spoke to their own experiences—whether heartbreak, regret, or the specters of past relationships. Online discussions framed “Multo” not just as a musical piece but as a communal ritual for emotional expression.

### Commercial Success and Cultural Impact

“Multo” achieved historic milestones in commercial performance. It was the first Filipino song ever to enter the Billboard Global 200 chart, debuting at number 181 before peaking at number 80. It also broke into the Billboard Global Excl. U.S. chart, debuting at number 74 and reaching a career-high peak at number 33—the highest ranking for any Filipino act on that chart. 

On domestic charts, “Multo” dominated the Billboard Philippines Hot 100 for 27 consecutive weeks, setting a record as the longest-reigning number one song in the chart’s history. It maintained popularity throughout 2025, topping playlists on Spotify and Apple Music in the Philippines and earning recognition as the most-streamed song and best local hit of the year. International platforms like Spotify reported over 60 million streams, reinforcing its status as a global contender.

The song’s success signaled a turning point for Original Pilipino Music (OPM) on the world stage, demonstrating that Filipino-language songs could resonate beyond national borders. It was featured in major events such as the Billboard Philippines Mainstage concert and won several prestigious awards.

### Music Video and Visual Storytelling

Directed with a keen eye for narrative, the “Multo” music video uses visual symbolism—lighting, color, and editing—to deepen its themes of memory and loss. Starring actors Miles Ocampo and Elijah Canlas, the video presents two former lovers confronting haunting visions, paralleling the song’s motifs of ghostly presence and unresolved emotions. The video’s release garnered immense attention, becoming a staple on international YouTube and social media platforms, and contributing to the broader visibility of Filipino cinema and fashion.

### Awards and Recognition

“Multo” swept major awards in the Philippine music industry. At the inaugural Filipino Music Awards, it won Song of the Year, and Cup of Joe’s album Silakbo took Album of the Year. The band was also recognized as the most awarded artist of 2025 by Spotify Philippines and topped several genre categories, including Top Local Song, Top Song in “Tatak Pinoy,” and Top Song in “.ph.” Additionally, it was nominated for People’s Choice Awards: Song and received accolades from multiple other organizations.

### Legacy and Influence

“Multo” has become a touchstone for contemporary OPM, redefining what Filipino-language music can achieve in terms of global outreach and emotional authenticity. It catalyzed new trends in digital engagement, fan-driven content, and genre evolution. The song’s influence extended beyond music—fueling conversations about mental health, nostalgia, and the power of art to bridge cultures.

Cup of Joe’s transformation—from a band known for energetic pop performances to serious storytellers—is attributed to “Multo.” It demonstrated that vulnerability and introspection could achieve mass appeal without sacrificing quality or originality. The enduring popularity of the song, combined with its critical and commercial success, ensured that Cup of Joe would remain at the forefront of Philippine pop music well into the future.

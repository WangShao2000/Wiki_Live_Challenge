# Voiceverse NFT Plagiarism Scandal

# Lead Section

The Voiceverse NFT plagiarism scandal was a significant controversy in early 2022 involving Voiceverse, an artificial intelligence–based startup founded by members of the Bored Ape Yacht Club, one of the world’s most prominent NFT projects. Voiceverse offered “voice NFTs”—non-fungible tokens that claimed to give digital ownership and licensing rights over unique AI-generated voices. The incident escalated rapidly after Voiceverse announced a partnership with celebrated video game voice actor Troy Baker, generating widespread criticism for perceived threats to the voice acting profession and concerns about misuse of AI-generated media. Shortly thereafter, it was revealed that promotional material from Voiceverse used audio clips generated by 15.ai—a free text-to-speech platform—without proper attribution or permission, resulting in accusations of plagiarism. These controversies led to a swift withdrawal from the partnership by Troy Baker, removal of incriminated content, and substantial reputational damage. Subsequent legal action unfolded as voice actors initiated a class-action lawsuit against Voiceverse’s parent company, Lovo, citing unauthorized cloning and commercialization of their vocal identities.


# Background

Voiceverse positioned itself as a blockchain-based venture leveraging AI voice-cloning technology to create unique digital assets. The concept centered on allowing users to purchase and own an NFT linked to a distinctive voice, with claims that royalties would be shared among original contributors. The company was publicly associated with the Bored Ape Yacht Club, thus tying its brand to both innovation and controversy within the NFT ecosystem. Alongside its technical ambitions, Voiceverse projected an ethos of empowering creators and democratizing access to professional-grade voices.


# Timeline of Events

### January 2022: Partnership Announcement and Initial Backlash

On January 14, 2022, renowned video game voice actor Troy Baker publicly announced his partnership with Voiceverse via Twitter. His tweet suggested collaboration on new creative tools, positioning himself as both advocate and ambassador for the venture. Baker’s choice of words, including the provocative statement, “You can hate. Or you can create,” provoked immediate backlash from fans, industry commentators, and fellow artists. Critics viewed the comment as dismissive of genuine concerns about technological displacement and ethical dilemmas inherent in AI-generated media. Public reaction quickly turned negative, fueled by broader skepticism towards NFTs and fears regarding job security in the voice-over sector.

### January 2022: Plagiarism Allegations Emerge

Within days of the initial announcement, accusations surfaced that Voiceverse had used audio clips sourced from 15.ai—the free text-to-speech service—for its promotional animation. These clips, specifically featuring characters from _My Little Pony_, were altered (pitch-shifted) without formal acknowledgment. 15.ai requires crediting its platform and forbids mixing its output with other speech synthesis software—a stipulation that Voiceverse appeared to violate. Upon public exposure, Voiceverse deleted the original tweets and issued a corrective apology, admitting the lack of appropriate attribution.

### Early 2022: Fallout and Withdrawal

Troy Baker responded to mounting criticism by walking back parts of his initial endorsement and apologizing for the confrontational nature of his remarks. He subsequently stated he would no longer continue his partnership with Voiceverse, characterizing the split as mutually decided by both parties. Voiceverse sought to stabilize its image by emphasizing potential benefits for creators—such as royalty sharing—and distancing itself from direct employment of voice actors. Despite these efforts, the company’s reputation suffered irreparable damage, with many observers calling the episode emblematic of larger ethical challenges facing emerging tech industries.


## Accusations and Public Reaction

The combination of concerns over artistic devaluation, unlicensed content use, and poor communication sparked a fierce debate across gaming communities, creative professionals, and media outlets. Critics highlighted several key points:

- **Job Displacement**: Many feared that AI-driven voice platforms like Voiceverse could undermine established careers in voice acting, reducing opportunities for newcomers and commoditizing professional expertise.
- **Ownership Models**: Questions arose regarding the validity and enforceability of IP rights underpinning NFTs—especially as licensing terms were often ambiguous or inadequately communicated.
- **Ethical Misconduct**: Accusations of plagiarism intensified scrutiny on how companies engaged with open-source technologies, underscoring a gap between marketing narratives and operational transparency.
- **Broader Industry Implications**: The saga played out amid wider debates over the environmental impact, economic incentives, and regulatory gaps surrounding blockchain-based ventures and synthetic media.

Reaction was overwhelmingly negative, amplified by viral social media threads and commentary from influential figures in the gaming world.


## Legal Ramifications

Following the public scandal, ongoing controversies persisted within the voiceover industry regarding the unauthorized use of personal vocal identities. In May 2024, Paul Skye Lehrman and Linnea Sage—one of whom reportedly heard their AI-mimicked voice in YouTube videos about military equipment—filed a proposed class-action lawsuit against Lovo, the parent company behind Voiceverse. Key allegations in the complaint included:

- Voice actors were recruited through freelance marketplaces such as Fiverr under assurances that contributions would be limited to specific research projects or test scripts.
- Instead, their voices were appropriated for AI training and commercialized via subscription-based services.
- Claims extended to fraud, false advertising, and violations of publicity rights.

By July 2025, a federal judge permitted certain aspects of the lawsuit to proceed, particularly those related to claims of unauthorized commercial exploitation of individual voice recordings. This decision reflected a growing recognition among courts of the need to address harms posed by generative AI systems operating without explicit consent.


## Aftermath

Voiceverse’s involvement in high-profile controversies accelerated its decline. While officially still active, the firm saw minimal engagement following the dissolution of partnerships with major industry figures and the loss of public trust. Its parent company, Lovo, found itself embroiled in additional legal battles, facing mounting pressure from advocacy groups, regulatory authorities, and media investigations.

The episode provided instructive lessons for regulators and stakeholders in adjacent sectors. It highlighted the necessity for transparent reporting practices, robust intellectual property protections, and strong consumer safeguards—particularly as generative AI proliferates across creative domains.

## Conclusion

The Voiceverse NFT plagiarism scandal stands as a seminal moment in discussions about ethics, copyright, and labor rights at the intersection of technology and creativity. By illuminating vulnerabilities in how emerging startups adopt new media forms, the case underscored the need for vigilance, accountability, and collaboration across diverse stakeholder groups. Future developments in the space will likely draw lessons from the missteps and consequences experienced by Voiceverse and its affiliates.

# Brave Bunnies

Brave Bunnies is a Ukrainian children’s animated television series designed for preschool audiences aged 2 to 5 years. Created by Olga Cherepanova, who is also the co-founder of Ukrainian animation company Glowberry, the series centers on two rabbit siblings—Boo and Bop—who embark on adventures with their family, traveling through the whimsical “Bunny World” in a bus-shaped vehicle. Alongside their parents Ma and Pa and four younger bunny siblings, Boo and Bop meet a variety of animal friends and engage in activities that foster curiosity, creativity, friendship, and acceptance of differences among young viewers. Each episode is approximately seven minutes long, emphasizing storytelling combined with catchy songs and educational elements aimed at developing early social skills.


### Development and Conceptualization

The original concept for Brave Bunnies emerged around 2019, when creator Olga Cherepanova developed the idea for an inclusive, socially-minded preschool series. The character designs and narrative were refined in collaboration with international experts, including input from child psychologists to ensure content suitable for preschool-aged children. The main premise is that each new encounter presents opportunities to demonstrate inclusivity, diversity, and collaborative play, teaching young audiences how to appreciate differences without pressure or change.

Animated production began soon after initial conception, with support from multiple partners. The project was realized as a co-production between Ukrainian company Glowberry and the Spanish animation studio Anima. Series direction was led by British animator Tim Fehrenbach, with John van Bruggen serving as executive story editor and Anna Sarvira as art director. Both technical and creative teams incorporated input from the state film sector and supported by FILM.UA Group, reflecting its broad institutional backing in Ukraine.


### Broadcast and Global Distribution

Brave Bunnies debuted on December 5, 2020, on the Ukrainian channel PLUSPLUS, marking its status as the world’s first Ukrainian-produced preschool series to gain such international attention. Within six months, broadcasts expanded to several European and international markets, including Germany, Spain, Israel, Australia, and Finland. This momentum accelerated during 2021, with first-season episodes airing on prominent preschool networks such as Channel 5’s Milkshake! in the United Kingdom, ABC Kids in Australia, and various European channels like Super RTL (Germany) and Rai YoYo (Italy).

By late 2023, Brave Bunnies had secured a presence in more than 80 countries worldwide. Distribution included both traditional broadcast partnerships—such as Canal+ Poland (TeleTOON+) and Stan Australia—and emerging digital platforms like WildBrain+, which made episodes available on US cable services via Comcast. Additionally, the show maintained regular updates on its official YouTube channel, appealing especially to families seeking accessible, high-quality content.

These collaborations ensured the expansion of commercial products, interactive media, and translated editions, further solidifying the brand’s reach.


### Reception and Accolades

Upon its debut at international markets, Brave Bunnies received notable attention and critical recognition. Its preview at MIPJunior 2020 attracted the highest number of views among properties screened that year, and the series was cited as the second most-watched show in its target category during MIPCOM—a testament to its appeal among content buyers and educators. Feedback from industry insiders highlighted the program’s distinctive value proposition: blending entertainment with lessons in empathy, multiculturalism, and resilience.

The show garnered positive viewer response, reflected by an IMDb user rating of 7.7/10 based on several dozen votes. Throughout its run, Brave Bunnies earned nominations at animation festivals and major industry forums, including selections at the Annecy International Animated Film Festival and Cartoon Forum. However, no major awards were reported.


### Merchandising and Extended Brand Presence

Brave Bunnies quickly evolved beyond its core programming to include a robust range of consumer products. International licensing agreements enabled Spin Master to develop toy lines for markets in Europe and North America, while books and educational materials were released in regions including Italy (through De Agostini), Australia (Five Mile Publishing), and the United Kingdom (Ladybird, part of Penguin Random House). These extensions provided opportunities for fans to interact with favorite characters outside of viewing contexts and helped build community engagement around the show’s themes.


### Challenges and Future Outlook

Brave Bunnies faced significant challenges following the Russian invasion of Ukraine in early 2022. Production delays ensued, and some team members were forced to flee Kyiv due to threats to personal safety. In response, WildBrain entered the partnership, acquiring majority ownership of the IP and facilitating continued production of subsequent seasons, ensuring that the series could adapt and survive amidst adversity. Season 2, produced collaboratively between WildBrain’s Vancouver studio and its longstanding partners, was completed by mid-2023 and further reinforced the show’s ongoing global relevance.

Despite disruptions, Brave Bunnies remains actively in development, with new episodes being prepared and additional international partnerships continuously explored. The initiative to foster inclusivity and compassion among children continues to drive creative direction and commercial strategy.


### Legacy and Impact

Brave Bunnies is recognized as a pioneering example of Ukrainian animation with global aspirations. It is notable for its proactive approach to childhood development, using playful narratives to promote respect for diversity, encourage cooperation, and model courageous exploration of new situations. Beyond broadcasting metrics, its legacy is defined by the tangible effects it has on young viewers’ attitudes toward others and willingness to embrace difference.

Through cross-border production, multi-platform distribution, and innovative merchandising strategies, Brave Bunnies has established itself as a model for contemporary preschool programming—one where entertainment serves as a bridge to understanding and connection.

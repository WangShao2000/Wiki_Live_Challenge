# Closures of the University of Wisconsin branch campuses

# Introduction

Since the closure of the University of Wisconsin–Platteville Richland campus on July 1, 2023, the University of Wisconsin System has experienced a wave of branch campus closures and announcements, marking a significant transformation in the state’s public higher education landscape. Prior to this contemporary surge, the last branch campus to close was UW–Medford in 1980. As of November 2025, six branch campuses have officially closed or are scheduled to do so, one campus has suspended all in-person instruction, and another has relocated to partner with a technical college. This ongoing process reflects broader shifts in funding, demographics, and educational strategy across Wisconsin.

# Background

The University of Wisconsin System, which comprises both four-year universities and branch campuses historically known as UW Colleges, has faced substantial structural and financial pressures over the past decade. Two critical factors have contributed to these challenges:

- **Tuition Freeze and State Funding Cuts**: In 2013, the state imposed an eight-year tuition freeze on the UW System under Governor Scott Walker, intended to make higher education more affordable. Meanwhile, state funding for the system was reduced by $300 million between 2015 and 2017, leading to persistent budget deficits at several campuses.
- **Declining Enrollment and Demographic Shifts**: Total enrollment at the branch campuses plummeted from 9,741 students in fall 2018 to just 4,803 in fall 2023—a decrease of nearly 50%. This contraction resulted from falling high school graduation rates, fewer Wisconsin students pursuing postsecondary education, and increasing competition from nearby technical colleges, particularly those offering associate degrees.

Recognizing these trends, the UW System undertook a major restructuring in 2018 that merged the standalone UW Colleges into seven larger four-year universities. While this reorganization aimed to improve efficiency and align services with changing student needs, it failed to halt the downward trajectory in enrollment and financial viability at many branch campuses.

Another critical issue highlighted by the UW System is the lack of competitive state investment relative to other states’ public universities. A 2023 analysis showed Wisconsin ranks 43rd nationally in per-student funding for four-year institutions, signaling limited capacity for future expansion or stability without increased support.

# Historical Context: UW–Medford Closure

The most notable precedent for recent branch campus closures occurred in 1980, when enrollment at UW–Medford fell to just 90 students, prompting the Board of Regents to close the campus. The closure of Medford underscored the difficulty of sustaining educational facilities with insufficient student demand and remains the only previous instance of such action prior to the more widespread closures beginning in 2023.

## Recent Wave of Branch Campus Closures

### UW–Platteville Richland Campus

Announced on October 17, 2023, and effective July 1, 2023, UW–Platteville Richland was the first campus affected by the recent round of closures. With an enrollment of approximately 64 students, the campus could no longer justify the operational expenses required to maintain physical facilities and instructional staff. Students and employees were transferred to the main Platteville campus or the Baraboo campus.

### UW–Oshkosh Fond du Lac Campus

On October 17, 2023, it was announced that UW–Oshkosh Fond du Lac would end in-person instruction by June 2024. Enrollment had declined to 243 students, and after comprehensive analyses confirmed the campus was not financially sustainable, the university made the decision to suspend in-person instruction. The final class was held in May 2024, and faculty were transferred to other locations.

### UW–Oshkosh Fox Cities Campus

Building on similar issues of low enrollment and market conditions, the UW–Oshkosh Fox Cities campus—home to 473 students as of spring 2024—announced in June 2024 that in-person instruction would end after the spring 2025 semester. Projections showed fewer than 100 students expected by 2032, far below the threshold required for sustainability.

### UW–Milwaukee Washington County Campus

The UW–Milwaukee Washington County campus, with enrollment dropping from 1,054 students in fall 2020 to 285 in fall 2022, was directed by system leadership to cease in-person instruction by June 2024. Student transfers were offered to other branches or online platforms, and faculty positions were redistributed.

### UW–Milwaukee Waukesha Campus

In March 2024, UW–Milwaukee announced the closure of the Waukesha campus after the spring 2025 semester. Enrollment had fallen to 672 students in spring 2024 from nearly 1,800 in 2018. Financial realities—including a gap between operating costs and tuition revenue—and new associate-degree options at nearby technical colleges reinforced the decision. Over 100 employees, including tenured faculty, were affected by the closure, with layoffs enabling some to join other UW-Milwaukee branches.

### UW–Platteville Baraboo Sauk County Campus

On October 17, 2025, UW–Platteville announced the permanent closure of its Baraboo Sauk County campus, with operations ceasing in May 2026. Enrollment had stabilized at 178 students. To support transition, the campus committed to assisting students with transfers to the main Platteville campus and planned for employee reassignment or employment opportunities elsewhere.

### UW–Green Bay Marinette Campus

As of January 2024, the UW–Green Bay Marinette campus announced it would suspend face-to-face classes after the spring semester, converting to an entirely online operation thereafter. Enrollment remained just above 200 students, and streamlined instructional models were adopted to better reflect local student needs and regional economic changes.

### UW–Stevens Point at Wausau Relocation

Effective fall 2026, the UW–Stevens Point at Wausau campus will relocate to Northcentral Technical College’s facilities, sharing resources and programming. This move reflects a collaborative approach, with UW–Stevens Point offering bachelor’s and master’s degrees alongside NTC’s associate programs, fostering a seamless pathway for students and integrating workforce development initiatives.

## Reactions and Aftermath

The closures have generated mixed reactions from affected communities, students, and higher education professionals. Some viewed them as a necessary response to shifting student preferences and demographic realities, while others lamented the loss of educational access in rural Wisconsin and raised concerns about the long-term economic impact on local communities.

Efforts to mitigate campus closures included legislative measures to provide up to $2 million in redevelopment grants for repurposing campus buildings, such as transforming sites into charter schools, training centers, or housing developments. Documentary efforts like “Closure” by Ken Brosky have chronicled the social consequences, drawing attention to the loss of opportunities for students and the weakening of the rural educational ecosystem.

Numerous stakeholders—university leaders, local officials, and advocacy groups—called for a moratorium on further branch campus closures, urging the establishment of a statewide plan to integrate branch campuses into broader higher education strategies rather than permit abrupt reductions.

## Conclusion

The closures of University of Wisconsin branch campuses since 2023 are emblematic of deeper challenges facing public higher education nationwide: budgetary austerity, demographic change, and evolving student needs. While earlier reorganizations sought to stabilize the system through consolidation and strategic integration, the more recent closures highlight the limits of administrative reforms in the face of sustained enrollment declines and structural inequities in state funding.

Looking ahead, the UW System faces the dual challenge of sustaining essential educational access in rural regions and ensuring efficient allocation of resources amid persistent financial constraints. Ongoing dialogue among policymakers, university administrators, and local communities will shape the future role of branch campuses in Wisconsin’s higher education landscape.

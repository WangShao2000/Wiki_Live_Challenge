# Wish World

**Wish World** is the seventh episode and first part of a two-part finale of the fifteenth series (season) of the British science-fiction television series Doctor Who. Written by Russell T Davies and directed by Alex Sanjiv Pillai, the episode premiered on 24 May 2025, initially released on BBC iPlayer and Disney+ simultaneously, with a subsequent UK broadcast on BBC One later the same day. The series features Ncuti Gatwa as the Fifteenth Doctor, joined by companions Belinda Chandra (Varada Sethu) and Ruby Sunday (Millie Gibson).

The story unfolds as the Doctor and his companions find themselves trapped in a fabricated reality—a heteronormative, 1950s-themed world—engineered by Conrad Clark under the influence of the Time Lord villain, the Rani. This artificial realm is designed to shape global opinion and, ultimately, to harness collective doubt from its inhabitants to facilitate the return of Omega, a foundational antagonist from Doctor Who lore.


# Plot Summary

Set against an opening sequence in Bavaria during 1865, the Rani captures the newborn Desiderium, a god possessing the ability to fulfill wishes. By the present day, the Doctor and Belinda awaken, believing themselves to be married with a daughter named Poppy. Their tranquil life, however, is merely a constructed illusion. Conrad Clark, a television storyteller whose childhood wishes shaped society’s expectations, uses Desiderium’s powers to impose this new world order over Earth.

Ruby Sunday soon realizes her surroundings are altered and attempts to alert the Doctor to the discrepancies. She encounters UNIT’s scientific advisor Shirley Bingham, who explains the changes are part of a larger conspiracy. As the Doctor delves deeper into the reality’s fabric, he uncovers evidence that doubting the world causes it to collapse. His realization leads to arrest alongside Belinda after she reports his doubts to authorities—an action forbidden in their reality.

Confronted in the Rani’s Bone Palace, the villain reveals her plan: exploit mass doubt to release Omega, the creator of the Time Lords. As midnight approaches, reality begins to unravel, culminating in a dramatic fall from the palace tower for the Doctor, who regains his memories and insists Poppy is truly his child. The episode concludes with the imminent threat of Omega’s resurrection, setting up the season finale.


# Production Details

**Writing and Direction:**  
Russell T Davies returned as showrunner, crafting a narrative steeped in classic themes and modern social commentary. Director Alex Sanjiv Pillai brought an atmospheric tension to the episode, infusing both retro aesthetic and contemporary horror motifs. The script introduced “the Unholy Trinity,” comprising the Rani, Mrs Flood, and Omega, while referencing past Doctor Who mythology.

**Casting and Performances:**  
Ncuti Gatwa’s portrayal of the Doctor presents him as John Smith, adrift in an alternate version of his own life. Varada Sethu and Millie Gibson carry significant screen presence as Belinda and Ruby, respectively. Notably, Archie Panjabi takes on a reimagined incarnation of the Rani, supported by Anita Dobson’s Mrs Flood. This dual embodiment brings new nuances to one of the series’ earliest adversaries.

Guest stars included Sienna-Robyn Mavanga-Phipps as Poppy, Bonnie Langford reprising Mel Bush, Ruth Madeley as Shirley, Jemma Redgrave returning as Kate Lethbridge-Stewart, Carole Ann Ford appearing as Susan Foreman, and Jonathan Groff guest-starring as Rogue. The ensemble cast contributed dynamic energy, merging legacy characters with fresh faces.

**Design and Filming:**  
The artificial world’s sets reflect mid-century aesthetics, notably UNIT transformed into a retro-futuristic insurance firm with typewriters and rotary phones. Designers intentionally contrasted this with the gothic-inspired Bone Palace, blending science fiction and horror elements. Special effects sequences—including London’s descent underground—were achieved using 360-degree camera dolly technology and green screens.

Filming presented logistical challenges. Scenes set in forests required working with live animals (owls, ducks, horses) and baby actors, resulting in unexpected disruptions. Interiors were shot at Wolf Studios Wales, with multiple cameras capturing action sequences in the Bone Palace. The episode’s extensive runtime allowed for detailed visual storytelling and immersive atmosphere.


## Broadcast and Reception

**Broadcasting:**  
"Wish World" debuted simultaneously on BBC iPlayer at 8 am BST in the United Kingdom and Disney+ in the United States at midnight Pacific Daylight Time on 24 May 2025. A BBC One transmission followed later that evening, and the episode received a limited theatrical release alongside "The Reality War" the following week. The episode attracted an overnight audience of 1.8 million viewers; consolidated figures (including catch-up viewing on devices) grew to 2.86 million, underscoring strong viewer engagement despite lower initial ratings compared to previous episodes.

**Critical Reception:**  
Review aggregates indicated mixed but generally positive responses. On Rotten Tomatoes, "Wish World" holds a critic score of 75% based on eight reviews, with audiences praising its ambition and execution. IMDb rates the episode 7.0 out of 10, reflecting moderate enthusiasm among viewers.

Critics commended the episode’s inventive premise, effective use of false reality, and social satire—highlighting how it addressed issues of traditionalism, minority inclusion, and power structures. The performances of Archie Panjabi and Anita Dobson drew particular acclaim for depth and commanding presence. The insertion of rogue Time Lord Rogue and subtle nods to earlier seasons’ storylines provided additional excitement for longtime fans.

However, several reviewers critiqued the complexity of the plot, noting that certain characterizations and narrative threads felt rushed or underdeveloped. Concerns centered on whether the Rani was positioned too similarly to previous villains and if Mrs Flood’s storyline sufficiently resolved within the episode’s scope. The return of Omega, though intriguing, prompted discussion about exposition versus payoff in a single installment.

## Conclusion

"Wish World" stands out as a daring and ambitious entry in Doctor Who history, blending classic mythology with contemporary social critique. Its unique setting, memorable antagonists, and layered plot have made it a memorable episode for both existing fans and newcomers. While some aspects remain unresolved, the episode effectively builds suspense and sets high stakes for the series finale, demonstrating the creative potential of serialized storytelling within a long-running science-fiction franchise.

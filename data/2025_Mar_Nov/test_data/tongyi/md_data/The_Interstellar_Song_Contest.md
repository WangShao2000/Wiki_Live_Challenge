# The Interstellar Song Contest

The Interstellar Song Contest is a fictional intergalactic music competition presented as the sixth episode of the fifteenth series (Series 15) of the British science‑fiction television series *Doctor Who*. Written by Juno Dawson and directed by Ben A. Williams, it premiered on 17 May 2025 on BBC iPlayer and Disney+ and was subsequently broadcast on BBC One in the UK. Within the narrative, the contest is portrayed as a futuristic parody of the real‑world Eurovision Song Contest, taking place aboard the Harmony Arena space station and hosting participants from across the universe. Notable on-screen events include co-hosting by Rylan Clark (appearing as himself), commentary from Graham Norton, and an overarching plot centered on themes of oppression, genocide, and redemption.


### **Plot Overview**

The episode follows the Fifteenth Doctor (Ncuti Gatwa) and his companion Belinda Chandra (Varada Sethu) as they arrive at the Harmony Arena during the 803rd Interstellar Song Contest. Initially intending to use the event’s technology to return to Earth, the pair become embroiled in escalating chaos when two antagonists—Kid and Wynn, members of the Hellion species—unleash a terrorist attack on the space station. Their plan involves hijacking the contest’s transmission to deploy a signal capable of killing three trillion viewers watching from across galaxies.

As the situation spirals, the Doctor attempts to thwart the catastrophe while reconciling with Belinda and other characters trapped onboard. The mission reveals deeper truths about the destruction of Earth on 24 May 2025, the only day from which the Doctor has been unable to return. The resolution centers around Cora Saint Bavier, an entrant from Trion who discloses her true identity as a member of the Hellion race and performs an emotional song in honor of their lost world—a musical moment intended to highlight themes of unity and remembrance.


### **Setting and Format**

Set in the year 2925, the Interstellar Song Contest unfolds aboard the Harmony Arena—a massive space station boasting hundreds of bars, restaurants, and entertainment venues. The event is transmitted live across the Western Galactic Arm, drawing an estimated audience of three trillion lifeforms from 40 million planets. The format mirrors its terrestrial counterpart, narrowing down thousands of planetary contestants to 40 finalists, with voting facilitated through neural hypersync headsets or dedicated apps. The setting, sponsor spots (such as PoppyHoney), and backstage production workflows are visualized as both technologically advanced and humorously exaggerated, blending classic sci-fi spectacle with camp Eurovision flair.


### **Participants and Hosts**

Throughout the episode, the contest features numerous characters representing diverse alien species. Notable participants include Liz Lizardine from the planet Lizoko, performing “I Love You But My Heart Says No!,” and Cora Saint Bavier from Trion, whose heartfelt ballad underscores the tragedy of oppression. The conductor, Sabine, and backroom personnel manage broadcast logistics while dealing with unpredictable crises.

Onscreen, the role of host is taken by Rylan Clark, revived after years of cryogenic suspension specifically for the purpose of emceeing the competition. His presentation style, including a playful jab at his own appearance, is accompanied by appearances from real-life television personalities such as Graham Norton, billed as “the King of Eurovision.” These cameos reinforce the meta-narrative link between the Doctor Who storyline and the cultural phenomenon of contemporary televised song contests.


### **Music and Songs**

Original songs played a central role in the episode’s thematic development and spectacle. Composed by Murray Gold—the show’s long-serving composer—each track reflects the uniqueness of its performer and the drama unfolding within the story. Key musical performances include:
- **“I Love You But My Heart Says No!”** performed by Liz Lizardine (planet Lizoko), a song satirizing modern romantic tropes and self-sabotage.
- **“My Big Feet”**, sung by Cora Saint Bavier as she navigates anxieties over her alien physicality and societal exclusion.
- **“Dugga Doo”**, rendered by performers from planet Grimbald, which rapidly became a cult favorite among fans due to its catchy tune and absurd lyrics.
- A final ballad by Cora in the language of Hellia, used to evoke the trauma of lost civilization and communal resilience.

In addition to musical numbers, director Ben A. Williams described the episode as “a love letter to the power of music and love,” noting Murray Gold’s songs as “weapons-grade catchy”—even inspiring children to sing along repeatedly upon hearing them.


### **Reception and Critical Response**

The Interstellar Song Contest debuted alongside the Eurovision Song Contest finals, leveraging cross-promotional strategies to maximize viewership and fan engagement. Overnight ratings were exceptionally strong, recording 2.57 million viewers and ranking third for the day behind major sporting and music events. Seven days later, the figure rose to 3.75 million, demonstrating sustained interest and social media buzz.

Critical response was mixed, reflecting the episode’s complex blend of camp entertainment, dramatic undertones, and social commentary. A critic aggregation platform recorded a 92% approval rating among professional reviewers, though some highlighted pacing issues, character development gaps, and the abrupt transition between lighthearted and dark themes. Reviews praised the visual design, guest performances, and the creative use of music, yet offered pointed critiques regarding narrative consistency and the complexity of villain motivations.

### **Broadcast Details and Legacy**

Following its initial streaming on BBC iPlayer and international Disney+ platforms, the episode aired on BBC One later in the day. The timing aligned with the climax of the real-world Eurovision Grand Final, emphasizing the parallel celebrations of music, national pride, and cultural exchange. Special attention was paid to integrating Doctor Who continuity elements—such as the mysterious Mrs Flood’s transformation into a Time Lady associated with the Rani—creating a seamless bridge to the upcoming season finale.

Overall, the Interstellar Song Contest stands as a notable entry within Doctor Who’s history, illustrating how genre storytelling can intersect with global pop culture phenomena. It not only entertains audiences with memorable characters and songs but also provokes discussion about representation, social justice, and the universality of artistic expression.

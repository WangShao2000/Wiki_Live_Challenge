# 2025 World Seniors Championship

The **2025 World Seniors Championship** (officially the **2025 JenningsBet World Seniors Championship**) was a seniors snooker tournament held at the Crucible Theatre in Sheffield, England, from 7 to 11 May 2025. It was the 15th edition of the World Seniors Championship, first held in 1991. Organised by World Seniors Snooker, the event featured 16 players aged 40 or over, competing in a single-elimination format. English player Alfie Burden won the tournament, defeating Jersey's Aaron Canavan 8–4 in the final to claim his first World Seniors title. Burden earned £20,000 in prize money for the victory. The tournament was broadcast in the United Kingdom on Channel 5 and 5 Action.

The defending champion was Brazil's Igor Figueiredo, who had won the 2024 event by defeating Ken Doherty in the final. Figueiredo was eliminated in the first round in 2025. The event marked the debuts of several former professional players on the seniors circuit, including Joe Perry and Dominic Dale, who had recently fallen off the main World Snooker Tour. Jimmy White, a four-time champion and the most successful player in the tournament's history, reached the first round but was defeated.

## Format

The 2025 tournament introduced a revised format following an announcement by World Seniors Snooker in March 2025. The first round and quarter-finals were played as the best of seven frames, the semi-finals as the best of 11 frames, and the final as the best of 15 frames. This change aimed to provide more competitive matches while maintaining accessibility for senior players.

Players qualified through various routes, including invitations based on past achievements, World Seniors Tour rankings, and regional qualifiers. The minimum age for participation was 40 for amateurs and 45 for players from the professional tour ranked outside the top 64. One spot was determined via a "Golden Ticket" play-off held in April 2025, which was won by Alfie Burden after defeating Matthew Ford 4–3 in the final.

The field consisted of 16 players:

The participants included a mix of former World Snooker Tour professionals and regional champions, with Figueiredo as the top seed.

## Tournament summary

The tournament began on 7 May with the first round. Defending champion Igor Figueiredo was eliminated early, losing 2–4 to Wayne Townsend. Jimmy White, seeking a record fifth title, fell 1–4 to Alfie Burden in a rematch of the 2023 final. Other notable first-round results included Dominic Dale's 4–2 win over Craig Steadman and Joe Perry's 4–2 victory against Fabio Anderson Luersen.

In the quarter-finals on 9 May, Aaron Canavan advanced with a 4–2 win over Townsend, while Dale defeated Perry 4–1. Burden edged out Tony Drago 4–3, and Hassan Kerde beat Charl Jonck 4–2.

The semi-finals took place on 10 May. Canavan overcame Dale 6–5 in a close contest, while Burden dominated Kerde 6–2 to reach his second World Seniors final.

### Final

The final was held on 11 May between Alfie Burden and Aaron Canavan. Burden took an early lead, winning the first session 5–3. He extended his advantage in the evening session, securing the title with an 8–4 victory. Key moments included Burden's breaks of 74, 87, and 78 in the opening frames. Canavan mounted a brief comeback by winning three consecutive frames to trail 5–4, but Burden responded with three straight frames to clinch the match.

Burden described the win as a career highlight, noting the significance of triumphing at the Crucible Theatre. Canavan, appearing in his first World Seniors final, praised Burden's performance and expressed satisfaction with his own run.

## Prize money

The breakdown of prize money for the tournament was as follows:

- Winner: £20,000
- Runner-up: £10,000
- Semi-finalists: £5,000 each
- Quarter-finalists: £2,500 each
- First-round losers: £1,000 each

The total prize fund remained unchanged from the 2024 event.

## Century breaks

There were no century breaks recorded during the tournament.


The 2025 World Seniors Championship showcased competitive snooker among veteran players, with Burden's victory highlighting the event's appeal to those no longer on the main professional tour. The format adjustments were well-received, providing longer matches in later stages without extending the overall schedule. Broadcast coverage on Channel 5 increased visibility, drawing attention to seniors snooker as a viable platform for former professionals. Participants like Perry and Dale, who had recently retired from the main tour, added fresh interest, potentially attracting more high-profile entries in future editions. The event continued the Crucible's tradition as the home of world championship snooker, with a contract in place to host until at least 2027.

Qualification processes emphasized global representation, with spots allocated to winners of continental championships such as the Pan American and All-Africa events. For instance, Fabio Anderson Luersen qualified via the 2024 Pan American Seniors Championship, while Charl Jonck represented Africa. The "Golden Ticket" qualifier allowed additional access, won by Burden in a separate event held in April 2025 at the Crucible Club.

The tournament's history traces back to 1991, when it was first staged as a one-off event won by Cliff Wilson. It was relaunched in 2010 with increased prize money and has been held annually since, except for 2014. Jimmy White holds the record with four titles (2010, 2019, 2020, 2023), followed by players like Peter Lines (2016) and Aaron Canavan (2018). The 2025 edition maintained the event's focus on players aged 40 and over, balancing experience with competitive integrity.

Broadcast and sponsorship played key roles, with JenningsBet as the title sponsor and Channel 5 providing live coverage in the UK. Afternoon sessions aired on Channel 5, while evenings were on 5 Action. This exposure helped promote the World Seniors Tour, which includes additional events throughout the year.

Referees included international officials, with Bulgaria's Proletina Velichkova officiating part of the final. The event ran smoothly, with no reported controversies, and received positive feedback from players and spectators. Burden's post-match comments emphasized enjoying the moment at the iconic venue, reflecting the tournament's blend of nostalgia and competition.

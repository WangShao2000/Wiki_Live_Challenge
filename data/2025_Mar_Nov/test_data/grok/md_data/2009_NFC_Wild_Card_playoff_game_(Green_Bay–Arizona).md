### Background

#### Green Bay Packers
The 2009 Green Bay Packers finished the regular season with an 11–5 record, placing second in the NFC North division behind the 12–4 Minnesota Vikings.[4] Under head coach Mike McCarthy, the team scored a franchise-record 461 points, ranking third in the NFL, while allowing 297 points on defense, good for sixth in the league.[4] Quarterback Aaron Rodgers, in his second full season as starter after replacing Brett Favre, threw for 4,434 yards, 30 touchdowns, and seven interceptions during the regular season.[4] The Packers' offense was bolstered by running back Ryan Grant, who rushed for 1,253 yards, and a receiving corps led by Donald Driver and Greg Jennings.[4] Defensively, the unit featured players like cornerback Charles Woodson, who won the NFL Defensive Player of the Year award with nine interceptions.[4] Green Bay started the season 4–4 but won seven of their final eight games to secure the NFC's fifth playoff seed.[4] The team's turnaround from a 6–10 record in 2008 was attributed to improved offensive line play and defensive adjustments.[4]

#### Arizona Cardinals
The 2009 Arizona Cardinals compiled a 10–6 record to win the NFC West division for the second consecutive year, under head coach Ken Whisenhunt.[5] The team scored 375 points (11th in the NFL) and allowed 325 (14th).[5] Quarterback Kurt Warner, at age 38, led the offense with 3,753 passing yards, 26 touchdowns, and 14 interceptions in 15 games.[5] Running back Beanie Wells, a rookie, contributed 793 rushing yards, while wide receivers Larry Fitzgerald and Anquan Boldin (though Boldin was limited by injuries) formed a strong passing attack.[5] The Cardinals' defense ranked 20th in yards allowed but forced 25 turnovers.[5] Arizona started 1–2 but won nine of their next 13 games before resting players in the finale.[5] As defending NFC champions from the 2008 season, where they lost Super Bowl XLIII to the Pittsburgh Steelers, the Cardinals earned the NFC's fourth seed and home-field advantage for the Wild Card round.[5]

#### Regular-season meeting
The Packers and Cardinals met in Week 17 of the regular season on January 3, 2010, at University of Phoenix Stadium, with Green Bay winning 33–7.[6] Arizona, having already clinched the NFC West, rested several starters, including Warner for most of the game and limiting Fitzgerald's snaps.[6] Rodgers threw for 235 yards and one touchdown, while the Packers' defense intercepted three passes, including a 45-yard return for a touchdown by Woodson.[6] Matt Leinart started at quarterback for Arizona, completing 13 of 21 passes for 96 yards and two interceptions.[6] The lopsided result provided little predictive value for the playoff rematch, as Cardinals players later noted the rest allowed them to enter the postseason healthier.[3]

### Broadcasting
The game was televised nationally by Fox, with Joe Buck providing play-by-play commentary, Troy Aikman as color analyst, and Pam Oliver on sideline reporting.[3] It drew an average viewership of approximately 30 million, reflecting interest in the quarterback matchup and the teams' recent playoff histories.[3]

### Game summary
The game kicked off at 2:40 p.m. MST, with an attendance of 61,926 in the indoor University of Phoenix Stadium.[1] Arizona won the coin toss and elected to receive.[1]

#### First quarter
Arizona dominated early, scoring on their first three possessions. After a Green Bay three-and-out, the Cardinals drove 80 yards in seven plays, ending with a 1-yard touchdown run by Tim Hightower.[1][2] On the ensuing kickoff, Packers returner Jordy Nelson fumbled, leading to a 15-yard touchdown pass from Warner to Early Doucet.[1][2] Later, after intercepting Rodgers, Arizona added a 23-yard field goal by Neil Rackers to lead 17–0.[1][2]

#### Second quarter
Green Bay responded with an 83-yard drive, capped by a 1-yard touchdown run by Rodgers.[1][2] Arizona extended their lead to 24–7 with another 15-yard Warner-to-Doucet touchdown pass.[1][2] The Packers added a 20-yard field goal by Mason Crosby as time expired, making the halftime score 24–10.[1][2]

#### Third quarter
Arizona opened with a 33-yard touchdown pass from Warner to Fitzgerald, pushing the lead to 31–10.[1][2] Green Bay rallied with a 6-yard touchdown pass from Rodgers to Jennings and an 11-yard pass to Nelson, narrowing it to 31–24.[1][2] The Cardinals answered with an 11-yard Warner-to-Fitzgerald touchdown, leading 38–24 at quarter's end.[1][2]

#### Fourth quarter
The Packers tied the game with a 30-yard touchdown pass from Rodgers to James Jones and a 1-yard run by John Kuhn.[1][2] Arizona regained the lead at 45–38 with a 17-yard Warner-to-Steve Breaston touchdown.[1][2] Green Bay forced overtime with an 11-yard touchdown pass from Rodgers to Spencer Havner with 1:52 remaining.[1][2] Arizona drove into field-goal range, but Rackers missed a 34-yard attempt wide left as regulation ended.[3]

#### Overtime
Green Bay won the overtime coin toss and received.[1] On the third play, Rodgers was sacked by Michael Adams, fumbling the ball, which Dansby recovered and returned 17 yards for the winning touchdown.[1][2][3] The play was reviewed but upheld, as officials ruled no face-mask penalty occurred.[3]

### Aftermath
Arizona advanced to the NFC Divisional playoffs, facing the top-seeded New Orleans Saints on January 16, 2010, where they lost 45–14.[5][8] Warner, who suffered a concussion in that game, announced his retirement on January 29, 2010, ending a 12-year career that included two MVP awards and a Super Bowl win.[7] The Cardinals' playoff run highlighted their offensive prowess but exposed defensive inconsistencies, leading to a 5–11 record in 2010.[5]  

For Green Bay, the loss ended their season but spurred improvements; the Packers won Super Bowl XLV the following year with a similar core.[4] The game underscored Rodgers' potential, as he set a franchise playoff record for passing yards.[3] Both teams' performances contributed to discussions on NFL playoff formats, particularly regarding division winners hosting higher-seeded wild-card teams.[3]

# Ang Totoong Narcolist

"Ang Totoong Narcolist" (lit. "The Real Narcolist") refers to a series of five viral videos released online in April 2019 in the Philippines, amid the country's midterm elections and ongoing war on drugs under President Rodrigo Duterte. The videos featured a hooded narrator known as "Bikoy," who claimed to be a former member of a drug syndicate and alleged that Duterte, members of his family, and close associates were involved in the illegal drug trade, receiving payoffs from syndicates. The allegations sparked significant political controversy, leading to investigations and legal actions. The narrator, later identified as Peter Joemel Advincula, recanted his claims in May 2019, asserting that the videos were fabricated as part of an opposition plot to destabilize the Duterte administration. While some viewed the videos as exposing potential corruption, others dismissed them as disinformation, with subsequent court rulings highlighting inconsistencies in Advincula's testimony. The incident underscored tensions between the administration and opposition, as well as concerns over political smear campaigns and the spread of unverified information online.

## Background

The videos emerged during Rodrigo Duterte's presidency (2016–2022), a period marked by a aggressive campaign against illegal drugs that resulted in thousands of deaths through police operations and extrajudicial killings. Duterte had publicly released lists of individuals allegedly involved in narcotics, known as "narcolists," which included politicians, judges, and police officers. Critics argued these lists were politically motivated and lacked due process. The "Ang Totoong Narcolist" videos positioned themselves as an alternative, purportedly authentic expose, contrasting with the government's official lists.

The timing coincided with the 2019 Philippine midterm elections, heightening political sensitivities. Opposition figures, including those from the Liberal Party, were campaigning against Duterte-backed candidates, and the videos fueled debates over the administration's integrity.

## Release and content

The YouTube channel "Ang Totoong Narcolist" was created on April 2, 2019, and uploaded five episodes between April 2 and April 22. The videos featured a hooded figure, "Bikoy," who claimed firsthand knowledge from his alleged role in drug syndicates in Southern Luzon and the Visayas. Bikoy presented documents referred to as "tara" (payoff) lists, detailing alleged financial transactions.

Key allegations included:

- **Episode 1 (April 2)**: Focused on Paolo Duterte, the president's son and former Davao City vice mayor, accusing him of receiving drug payoffs. Bikoy claimed Paolo's dragon tattoo signified ties to a drug triad.

- **Episode 2 (April 5)**: Introduced a low-profile figure codenamed "VEGAREGA-AAA00" with multiple bank accounts linked to payoffs, following a money trail to identify the recipient.

Subsequent episodes expanded accusations to include Duterte himself, his daughter Veronica, son-in-law Manases Carpio, and ally Christopher "Bong" Go, alleging they benefited from syndicate kickbacks. The videos gained traction through social media shares, including via websites like metrobalita.net.

Opposition Senator Leila de Lima cited the first episode in calling for probes into Paolo Duterte and Chinese businessman Michael Yang, emphasizing public interest in verifying the claims.

## Identification of Bikoy and recantation

On May 6, 2019, Peter Joemel Advincula publicly identified himself as Bikoy at a press conference hosted by the Integrated Bar of the Philippines. Advincula, a former convict with a history of estafa cases, reiterated the videos' allegations.

However, on May 23, 2019, Advincula surrendered to police and recanted, claiming the videos were "all lies" scripted by opposition figures to divert attention from Duterte's high approval ratings. He implicated Senator Antonio Trillanes IV and the Liberal Party in "Project Sodoma," an alleged plot involving sedition and disinformation. Advincula entered protective custody but later left it.

Trillanes denied involvement, releasing text messages showing Advincula approached him for assistance in producing the videos.

## Legal proceedings

The videos prompted investigations by the National Bureau of Investigation and Philippine National Police. Rodel Jayme, linked to sharing the videos via metrobalita.net, was arrested on May 2, 2019, and charged with inciting to sedition.

Following Advincula's recantation, the PNP-Criminal Investigation and Detection Group filed complaints on July 18, 2019, against 36 individuals, including Vice President Leni Robredo, Trillanes, and priests, for conspiracy to commit sedition, cyberlibel, and related offenses. The Department of Justice cleared Robredo and others in 2020 but proceeded against Trillanes and 10 others, issuing arrest warrants in February 2020.

In September 2023, a Quezon City court dismissed the sedition case against Trillanes and others due to insufficient evidence.

Advincula was convicted of perjury in May 2023 for false statements implicating opposition lawyers in the alleged plot, sentenced to up to one year in prison. Opposition figures described the ruling as vindication against disinformation.

## Later developments

In October 2024, Advincula filed his certificate of candidacy as an independent senatorial aspirant for the 2025 Philippine midterm elections, becoming the first filer on the last day. He campaigned on reforming the corrections system, drawing from his experiences. Advincula did not secure a seat in the Senate, as election results showed winners including allies of former President Duterte and opposition candidates, with no mention of him among the top vote-getters.

The controversy has been referenced in discussions on political disinformation and judicial weaponization in the Philippines.

# Love Me Forever (Pinkshift album)

Love Me Forever is the debut studio album by the American punk rock band Pinkshift. It was released on October 21, 2022, by Hopeless Records. Produced by Will Yip, the album follows the band's 2021 extended play Saccharine and features 12 tracks that blend elements of pop-punk, post-hardcore, emo revival, and grunge. Recorded in Baltimore, Maryland, where the band formed, Love Me Forever explores themes including personal relationships, prejudice, oppression, societal groupthink, and existential anxieties related to global instability. The album received generally positive reviews from music critics, who commended its energetic instrumentation and lyrical introspection, establishing Pinkshift as a notable act in the contemporary punk scene. Four singles were released in promotion: "nothing (in my head)" in June 2022, "i'm not crying you're crying" in July, "GET OUT" in August, and "in a breath" in September.

## Background
Pinkshift formed in Baltimore, Maryland, in 2018 while its members were students at Johns Hopkins University.[9][10][11][12][13][14] Vocalist Ashrita Kumar, guitarist Paul Vallejo, and drummer Myron Houngbedji met through mutual connections and bonded over shared musical influences, including My Chemical Romance.[1][9][10] Initially pursuing careers in medicine and hard sciences, the members shifted focus to music full-time after gaining early traction.[1][10][11] An early bassist, Erich Weinroth, contributed to initial recordings but is not credited on Love Me Forever.[12]

The band gained attention in 2020 with the viral single "I'm Gonna Tell My Therapist On You", which highlighted their unapologetic style addressing mental health and social issues.[10][14] This led to their signing with Hopeless Records and the release of the Saccharine EP in 2021, which introduced their sound blending punk aggression with melodic elements.[3][10][14] As people of color in a historically white-dominated punk scene, Pinkshift's music often reflects experiences of navigating prejudice and representation, a perspective that informs the album's content.[1][5][10][13]

Development of Love Me Forever began amid the band's growing profile, with Kumar noting in interviews that the project aimed to capture raw emotions from personal and societal challenges.[10][11] The album's title reflects a sense of impermanence and urgency, tying into broader themes of relationships and global uncertainty.[1]

## Recording and production
Love Me Forever was produced by Will Yip, a Philadelphia-based engineer known for his work with bands like Turnstile, Mannequin Pussy, and The Wonder Years.[1][3][4] Recording took place in Baltimore, with Yip providing a "razor-sharp" production style that enhanced the band's dynamic range, from high-energy punk tracks to sparse ballads.[1][4] The process emphasized the trio's instrumentation: Kumar's biting vocals, Vallejo's chugging guitar riffs, and Houngbedji's breakneck drum beats.[1]

The band described the recording as a dedicated effort to refine their sound, moving beyond the Saccharine EP by incorporating slower, introspective elements alongside their signature intensity.[1][3] Credits list Pinkshift as primary songwriters, with Yip handling production, mixing, and engineering.[2][3]

## Music and lyrics
Musically, Love Me Forever combines punk rock with influences from post-hardcore, grunge, and pop, creating a "swirling swathe" of sounds.[7] Tracks feature racing drums, nimble guitar riffs, and shifts between aggressive verses and melodic choruses.[1][4][5] The album's structure includes high-tempo punk songs in the first half, transitioning to more varied pacing later, including piano-driven ballads.[1][9 (from reviews)]

Lyrically, the album addresses personal and societal themes. Kumar's writing draws from experiences of abusive relationships, as in "Trust Fall", where lines describe emotional exhaustion: "Now I’m a shell of who I was/ So undesirable/ Walking on eggshells just because."[1] Broader issues appear in "BURN THE WITCH", which critiques groupthink and misogyny through references to historical witch trials: "What if we peeled away her skin/ Off of her bones and she’d reveal herself a monster within/ And then we’ve won and we can all go home and celebrate in sin."[1] Existential concerns surface in "the kids aren’t alright" ("The world is ending all the time and I don’t think I can take it") and "in a breath" ("What is it like when you can sleep with no alarm?"), reflecting anxieties about societal collapse.[1]

Other tracks like "Cinderella" explore memory and betrayal ("Let my ashes fall so I won’t ever feel anything again"), while the title track "Love Me Forever" examines relational dynamics with a mix of vulnerability and defiance.[1][2] Overall, the lyrics emphasize empowerment, human condition, and resistance to oppression, presented without overt editorializing.[1][5][10]

## Release and promotion
Hopeless Records announced Love Me Forever on July 13, 2022, alongside the release of lead single "i'm not crying you're crying".[3] Preceding singles included "nothing (in my head)" on June 1, "GET OUT" on August 24, and "in a breath" on September 21.[3] The album was made available in digital, CD, and vinyl formats, including limited-edition variants like "pink galaxy" and "bone color" pressings.[2][4 (from shop links, but general)]

Pinkshift supported the release with tours, including dates in the United States and United Kingdom.[1][2] As of 2025, the band continues to perform tracks from the album alongside material from their follow-up, Earthkeeper (2025).[2] The album remains available on streaming platforms and Bandcamp, where it is described as part of the band's discography emphasizing their "rat city rock nerds" identity.[2]

## Critical reception
Love Me Forever received positive reviews from music publications. Kerrang! called it "a bold first proper kick from a band already brimming with promise", noting its announcement of Pinkshift as a "killer band".[4] New Noise Magazine described it as "one of the strongest punk debuts in recent years", praising its impressive listenability.[5] KZUU 90.7 FM highlighted the album's "great sound" and recommended it for its volume-worthy appeal.[6]

FLOOD Magazine characterized it as a "stunning burst of influences and experiences", blending punk subgenres effectively.[7] Consequence.net viewed it as a "thrilling debut" and "useful companion for the world’s downfall", appreciating its handling of angst and societal topics despite occasional on-the-nose lyrics.[1] Chorus.fm noted its "solid artistic statement" through passionate music and lyrics.[9 (chorus review)] User aggregated sites like Rate Your Music and Album of the Year reflect generally favorable scores, with some reviewers preferring the first half's energy over the second.[8][from RYM]

Critics consistently praised the production by Will Yip and the band's thematic depth, positioning the album as a contribution to diverse voices in punk.[1][4][5][7]

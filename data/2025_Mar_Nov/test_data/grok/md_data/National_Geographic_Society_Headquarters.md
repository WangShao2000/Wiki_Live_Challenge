# National Geographic Society Headquarters

The National Geographic Society Headquarters is a complex of buildings in Washington, D.C., serving as the operational base for the National Geographic Society since 1904. Located at 1145 17th Street NW, the campus spans approximately 900,000 square feet and includes structures built between 1904 and 1984, reflecting various architectural styles from Classical Revival to Modernism. The headquarters has supported the society's activities in exploration, education, and publication, and it houses offices, exhibition spaces, and an upcoming museum. As of December 2025, the site is undergoing a major renovation to enhance sustainability and public engagement, with the new Museum of Exploration scheduled to open in mid-2026. While the complex is part of the Sixteenth Street Historic District, its buildings contribute to the society's mission without notable controversies reported in sources.

**Key Features**
- **Location and Size**: Occupies a block bounded by 16th Street, 17th Street, and M Street NW in downtown Washington, D.C.
- **Historical Development**: Began with Hubbard Memorial Hall in 1904; expanded multiple times to accommodate growth in membership and operations.
- **Architecture**: Includes designs by architects such as Hornblower & Marshall, Arthur B. Heaton, Edward Durell Stone, and Skidmore, Owings & Merrill.
- **Current Renovations**: A $250 million project focusing on sustainability, including LEED Platinum certification, and expansion of public spaces.
- **Public Access**: Features exhibition areas and plans for immersive educational experiences, expected to attract around one million visitors annually.

**Historical Significance**  
The headquarters reflects the evolution of the National Geographic Society, founded in 1888, from a small scholarly group to a global nonprofit organization. Early buildings served as meeting spaces for discussions on geography and exploration, while later additions supported publishing and administrative needs.

**Ongoing Developments**  
Renovations aim to integrate modern technology and environmental features, such as solar panels and a green wall, while preserving historical elements. The project emphasizes education on planetary protection, with no significant opposition noted in available reports.

## History

The National Geographic Society was founded in 1888 in Washington, D.C., by a group of scholars, scientists, and adventurers to promote the increase and diffusion of geographic knowledge.[1][2] Initial meetings occurred at venues such as the Cosmos Club, but the society sought a permanent headquarters to support its growing operations.[1]

In 1904, the first dedicated building, Hubbard Memorial Hall, was completed, funded by the families of the society's first president, Gardiner Greene Hubbard, and his son-in-law, Alexander Graham Bell.[1][2] This structure served as an office and meeting space for academic discussions.[2] Membership grew from 2,700 in 1904 to 150,000 by 1912, prompting further expansion.[1]

An administration building on 16th Street was added in 1912, with subsequent additions in 1931 and 1949.[1][2] A major expansion occurred in the 1960s with the construction of the main headquarters building, completed in 1963 and dedicated in 1964.[1][2] Another addition on M Street followed in 1984.[2]

During the mid-1960s to early 1970s, the headquarters hosted a clandestine FBI surveillance office, disguised as the "Mid-Atlantic Research Committee," to monitor the nearby Soviet embassy.[1] In the 1960s, a fallout shelter was installed in the 17th Street building, equipped with cots, showers, medical supplies, and other amenities.[1]

Since 2011, bee colonies have been maintained on a balcony of the 16th Street building, with up to 50,000 bees per colony; honey harvested from these bees has been used for gifts and events.[1] The headquarters has also served as a base for society explorers, including Hiram Bingham in the early 20th century and J. Michael Fay in the 2000s.[1]

As of 2025, the campus is in the midst of a $250 million renovation project, initiated around 2022, to modernize facilities and create the Museum of Exploration.[4][5][6][7] Construction was reported as 70% complete in July 2025.[6]

## Buildings and Architecture

The headquarters complex consists of four main buildings, each representing different periods and styles.[2]

### Hubbard Memorial Hall

Built in 1904 and designed by architects Hornblower & Marshall in the Classical Revival style, this building features a hipped roof with terracotta tiles, large arched windows, and a stone surround inscribed with "National Geographic Society."[1][2] Hornblower & Marshall also designed the National Museum of Natural History.[1] The hall was intended as a communal space for the society's members.[2]

### 16th Street Administration Building

Constructed in 1912 by architect Arthur B. Heaton, this limestone-clad structure is also in the Classical Revival style, with double-leaf brass doors and rusticated stonework.[1][2] Heaton designed over a thousand buildings in the D.C. area, including contributions to the Washington National Cathedral.[1] The building connects to Hubbard Hall via a paved brick courtyard with granite steps.[2] Additions were made in 1931 and 1949.[2]

### Main Headquarters Building

Designed by Edward Durell Stone and completed in 1963, this ten-story structure exemplifies New Formalist Modernism, with reinforced concrete hung with white marble, minimal ornamentation, clear lines, and a vertical orientation.[2][8] Stone is known for works such as the John F. Kennedy Center for the Performing Arts.[1] The building is located at 1145 17th Street NW.[8][9]

### M Street Addition

Built in 1984 by Skidmore, Owings & Merrill (SOM), this non-contributing structure features pale pink concrete panels and a mechanical penthouse.[2] It expands the campus along M Street.[2]

The complex is within the Sixteenth Street Historic District, with Hubbard Hall and the Administration Building specifically noted as contributing elements.[2] The entire headquarters was designated on the DC Inventory of Historic Sites on March 24, 2022, and listed on the National Register of Historic Places on June 5, 2023.[2]

## Landscape and Outdoor Features

The campus includes an elongated plaza designed by SOM with landscape architect James Urban.[3] The plaza features a planted median with oaks, holly, and boxelder trees, replacing original ash and pear trees.[3] A low-curbed sidewalk borders the median, and a staircase leads to an underground garage.[3]

A notable feature was the 1984 sculptural installation *MARABAR* by Elyn Zimmerman, consisting of a 6-by-60-foot rectangular reflecting pool amid granite boulders, some with polished surfaces.[3] The design creates an illusion of natural elements predating the built environment.[3] Additional boulders were placed in the plaza's gardens.[3] The space narrows into a sheltered garden with stepped terraces, originally planted with yews but later converted to grass, and rows of hedges along the exhibition hall's colonnade.[3] The garden transitions to an alley exiting onto Sumner Row.[3]

Between 2017 and 2022, *MARABAR* and some SOM elements were relocated during renovations, with parts reconfigured as *Sudama* on the American University campus.[3]

The current renovation includes a high-tech courtyard with immersive projections, starting with an ocean-themed experience, and a large green wall for sustainability.[5][6]

## Museum and Exhibits

The headquarters has long included exhibition spaces for public education.[2] The original museum operated for over 40 years, displaying artifacts and photographs related to the society's work.[2][5]

In 2026, the renovated campus will open the National Geographic Museum of Exploration, a 100,000-square-foot facility expanding on the previous museum.[4][5][6][10] Located within the headquarters at the corner of 16th and M Streets NW, it is designed as a "Base Camp" for visitors.[5][6]

Features include:
- Curated exhibits on exploration and science.
- A state-of-the-art theater.
- An immersive "walking dark ride."
- An education center called the "learning launchpad" for students, focusing on geography and planetary protection.
- A photography gallery.
- An archives experience highlighting the society's history.
- An auditorium for the brand story.

Visitors will enter through a yellow border representing the society's logo.[6] The museum anticipates around one million visitors per year, targeting tourists from the National Mall and local school groups.[6] As of July 2025, construction was 70% complete, with an opening planned for mid-2026.[6][10]

## Sustainability and Renovations

The ongoing $250 million renovation, started in the early 2020s, aims to achieve LEED Platinum certification.[5][11] Upgrades include bird-safe glass, solar panels, expanded electric vehicle charging stations, bicycle racks, and a large green wall.[5] These changes support the society's focus on environmental education.[5]

The project expands public spaces fourfold compared to the previous museum and integrates sustainable practices across the 900,000-square-foot campus.[5] No major delays or issues have been reported as of late 2025.[6]

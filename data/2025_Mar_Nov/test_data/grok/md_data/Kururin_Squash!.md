### Gameplay
In Kururin Squash!, players control the Helirin, a helicopter-like vehicle that rotates automatically at a constant speed.[1][2] The objective is to navigate through maze-like levels without colliding with walls or obstacles, as each collision deducts one heart from a limited supply—typically starting with three hearts, expandable to five with power-ups.[3] Losing all hearts causes the Helirin to shatter, requiring a restart from the beginning of the level or from a checkpoint if an "Amazing Heart" item is active.[4] Players can press the B button to temporarily increase the Helirin's speed for dashing, and the R button to accelerate its rotation.[5] Contact with blue springs reverses the rotation direction, which can aid in maneuvering tight spaces.[6]

The game introduces five specialized Helirin variants, each tied to specific worlds and equipped with unique abilities activated by the A button:[7]
- Helibokaan: Extends boxing gloves to punch obstacles and enemies, often to collect coins.
- Helibashan: Allows temporary underwater diving until air depletes or the button is released.
- Heliboboh: Emits flames from both sides to destroy enemies, with extended range during dashes.
- Heliburuun: Charges and releases a tornado to eliminate enemies, clear obscured areas, or block hazards like fire.
- Helibibyuun: Fires lasers to defeat enemies and trigger environmental interactions, such as detonating bombs.

These variants are used in designated levels and for boss fights at the end of each world, where players apply the abilities to exploit boss weaknesses.[8] Levels span five themed worlds: Sweet Shores, Coastal Canopy, Snowscape, CyberCity, and Wonderverse, with around 40 stages in total.[9] Players can collect gold coins by defeating enemies, achieving perfect clears (no collisions), or using Helirin abilities, and spend them in in-game shops run by cameo characters to purchase items like level maps, mini-games, or customizable heads-up displays.[10]

The game supports single-player story mode and multiplayer for up to four players.[11] Multiplayer includes a Race mode for competing through levels and a Battle mode focused on collecting coins while using power-ups to hinder opponents.[12] A Game Boy Advance-GameCube link cable enables a simple memory-matching mini-game.[13] After completing the story, additional challenges unlock, such as beating "Dad" times on levels for faster completions.[14]

### Plot
The game follows Kururin, a bird-like character, who returns from a family vacation to discover his siblings have been captured by Professor Isogashima, a scientist using robots to protect his research.[15] Kururin pilots the Helirin to rescue them across various worlds, assisted by Teacher Hare, who provides upgraded Helirin variants.[16] After saving his siblings, Kururin learns his father, Totorin, has been kidnapped and held in the final world, Wonderverse, leading to a climactic rescue.[17] The narrative unfolds through cutscenes with high-pitched, bird-like voice effects for characters.[18]

### Development
Eighting, a Tokyo-based studio founded in 1993 by former Compile staff, developed the Kururin series, beginning with Kuru Kuru Kururin in 2001 for the Game Boy Advance.[19] The franchise emphasizes maze navigation with a rotating vehicle, a mechanic refined across entries.[20] Kururin Squash! marked the series' only home console release, shifting to 3D graphics with polygonal environments, transparency effects, lighting, and particle systems for elements like explosions and dust, while preserving 2D gameplay.[21] The soundtrack features varied instrumentation across levels, though tracks loop frequently.[22] Nintendo published the game in Japan on October 14, 2004, with no international release announced.[23] Eighting later worked on other titles, such as Tatsunoko vs. Capcom, but Kururin Squash! remains the final installment in the series.[24]

### Reception
Kururin Squash! received positive reviews from critics, who highlighted its addictive puzzle mechanics, innovative use of specialized Helirins, and charming art style featuring puppet-like characters.[25][26] Nintendo World Report awarded it 9.5 out of 10, praising the expanded puzzles and lasting appeal through collectibles, but criticizing the grating character voices and underwhelming Game Boy Advance link mini-game.[27] User ratings on GameFAQs averaged "Great," with players noting a playtime of around 21 hours and a difficulty rated as "Just Right/Tough."[28] Reviewers appreciated the gradual difficulty curve, which encourages pursuing perfect completions, and the minimal language barriers for import players.[29] Some mixed opinions emerged on the challenge level, with certain stages seen as demanding precise controls via the GameCube analog stick.[30] The game is often described as a hidden gem in the GameCube library, with calls for a Western release or rerelease, though it has not occurred as of 2025.[31][32]

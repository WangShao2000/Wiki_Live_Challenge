## Early life and education
Diego Leonardo García Miravete was born on August 20, 1946, in Mexico.[1][2] He developed an interest in American football at a young age, aspiring to play quarterback, though his height of 1.90 meters (approximately 6 feet 3 inches) led coaches to position him elsewhere.[3][4] García Miravete attended the Universidad Nacional Autónoma de México (UNAM), where he balanced his studies with his involvement in the sport.[5] Details on his academic field or degree are not widely documented in available sources, but his early exposure to football through school teams laid the foundation for his later career.

## Playing career
García Miravete began playing organized American football in his mid-teens. From 1964 to 1966, he competed in juvenile and intermediate categories for the Gallos teams affiliated with Prepa 5 and Comercio.[6] He joined the UNAM program in 1966, playing for both the Pumas UNAM and, later, the Cóndores UNAM teams until 1971.[6][7] He primarily played as a defensive end, wearing number 88, and served as team captain in 1970.[8][9] During this period, he participated in key games, including classics against rival institutions like the Instituto Politécnico Nacional (IPN).[10] His playing experience emphasized discipline and strategy, qualities he later incorporated into his coaching style.

## Coaching career
García Miravete transitioned to coaching in 1976, beginning a long tenure marked by strategic innovations, such as adopting professional-style offensive and defensive systems, emphasizing physical and mental conditioning, and fostering team unity through traditions like post-game huddles and recognition dinners.[11] He has coached at various levels, including university teams, national selections, and international clubs, with a focus on inclusive recruitment regardless of players' physical attributes.[12]

### Cóndores UNAM (1976–1993)
In 1976, García Miravete was appointed head coach of the Cóndores UNAM following the dismissal of previous staff due to poor performance.[11] He assembled a coaching team that included assistants like Alfonso Reyes, Antonio Rojí (defensive coordinator), and Jesús Malanco, who handled statistics.[11] Under his leadership, the team adopted a "T" formation offense and modernized uniforms, promoting a culture of respect and hard work.[11]

The Cóndores achieved significant success during this era:
- 1977: García Miravete was named Coach of the Year after leading a combined UNAM selection to a 44–7 victory over IPN rivals.[11]
- 1978: Won the national championship with a 23–14 victory over Águilas Blancas IPN.[11]
- 1979 and 1980: Secured consecutive championships, marking a three-year streak.[11][13]
- 1983: Defeated Águilas Blancas 23–3 for another title.[11]
- 1984: Won 40–29 against Águilas Blancas.[11]
- 1985: Shut out Pieles Rojas IPN 31–0.[11]
- 1986: Defeated Poli Blanco 17–0.[11]
- 1990: Beat Águilas Blancas 16–3.[11]
- 1991: Triumphed 41–16 over Borregos Salvajes ITESM Monterrey, tying the record for most championships (nine) held by coaches like Roberto "Tapatío" Méndez.[11][14]

García Miravete's innovations included inviting U.S. coaches from the University of Tennessee for training exchanges and expanding recruitment to include players from outside UNAM's traditional pipeline.[11] His tenure ended in 1994 amid internal conflicts, including player dissatisfaction and administrative decisions, leading to his dismissal despite the nine championships.[11]

### Other Mexican teams
After leaving Cóndores, García Miravete coached several university programs:
- Tigres UANL (1995–1999): Directed the team during a rebuilding phase.[15]
- Borregos ITESM Toluca and Borregos ITESM Santa Fe: Led these Tecnológico de Monterrey campuses, focusing on program development.[16][17]
- Universidad del Valle de México (UVM) and others: Coached teams like Monjes, 60's, Búfalos, and Pumas, all affiliated with UNAM at various points.[18]

In 2018, he joined the Universidad Autónoma de Aguascalientes (UAA) to lead its new flag football (tochito bandera) programs for men and women, overseeing recruitment and training for competitions like the Universiada.[12] As of 2025, he continues coaching, including at the Kawa Football Academy.[19]

### International coaching
García Miravete expanded his career abroad, coaching the Cardenales de Palermo in Italy and the Dragones de Barcelona in Spain's NFL Europe league.[16][20] He secured championships in Europe, contributing to his overall tally of successes beyond Mexico.[12]

### National team
García Miravete served as head coach of Mexico's national team in the Aztec Bowl (Tazón Azteca), an annual game against U.S. college teams:
- 1984: Victory 22–15 over Tarleton State College.[21]
- 1986: Loss 27–8 to Washburn University.[21]
- 1990: Loss 41–31 to Southern Arkansas University.[21]
- 1991: Win 35–28 against Oklahoma State University Bulldogs.[21][22]

These games highlighted his ability to prepare teams against international competition.

## Honors and legacy
García Miravete is tied for the most national championships (nine) in Mexico's Liga Mayor history.[14] In 2022, the Organización Nacional Estudiantil de Fútbol Americano (ONEFA) recognized him as a legend alongside coaches Frank González and Jacinto Licea.[23] A football field at UNAM bears his name: Campo Diego García Miravete.[24] His approach to coaching, emphasizing holistic player development and strategic adaptation, has influenced generations of athletes and coaches.[3][12] Sources describe him as an icon of Mexican American football, with a career that includes over 40 years of experience and contributions to the sport's professionalization in the country.[12][16]

## Personal life
García Miravete has a son, also named Diego García, who has been involved in football discussions and tributes related to his father's career.[25] He maintains an active presence in the sport, sharing insights through interviews and social media.[26]

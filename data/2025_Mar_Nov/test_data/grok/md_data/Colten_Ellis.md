## Early life
Colten Ellis was born on October 5, 2000, in River Denys, Nova Scotia, a small rural community in Inverness County on Cape Breton Island.[1][2] He grew up about 90 minutes from Sydney, Nova Scotia, and began playing organized hockey with the Cape Breton West Minor Hockey Association.[3] His family provided consistent support throughout his career; his parents, Brian and Joanne Ellis, along with his brother Matthew, attended key milestones, including his NHL debut.[4][5] Nearly 20 family members drove from Nova Scotia to Buffalo for that game.[6] Ellis has cited his family's sacrifices as a major influence, noting their role in his development from minor hockey to professional levels.[7]

Prior to major junior hockey, Ellis competed in the Nova Scotia Major Midget Hockey League (NSMMHL) with the Cape Breton West Islanders in 2016–17, where he posted a 2.05 goals-against average (GAA) and .920 save percentage, earning the league's best GAA and save percentage awards, as well as a spot on the second all-star team.[8] His team won the NSMMHL championship and the Telus Cup as national under-18 champions that year.[8]

## Playing career

### Junior
Ellis was selected in the fourth round, 59th overall, by the Cape Breton Screaming Eagles (now Cape Breton Eagles) in the 2016 QMJHL Entry Draft.[9] He appeared in three games during the 2016–17 season, posting a 2.50 GAA and .885 save percentage.[10] On June 3, 2017, the Eagles traded Ellis to the Rimouski Océanic for a first-round pick in the 2018 draft.[9] The decision was based on evaluations favoring another goaltender, Kevin Mandolese, as having higher NHL potential due to size and scouting consensus.[9] Former Eagles coach and GM Marc-André Dumont described the trade as difficult given Ellis's local ties but necessary for roster management.[9]

With Rimouski, Ellis became the starting goaltender in 2017–18, appearing in 51 games with a 2.35 GAA, .913 save percentage, and six shutouts, earning the QMJHL All-Rookie Team and Raymond Lagacé Trophy as defensive rookie of the year.[11][12] In 2018–19, he played 46 games (2.47 GAA, .910 save percentage, three shutouts) and helped Rimouski reach the league semifinals in the playoffs.[10] The 2019–20 season was shortened by the COVID-19 pandemic; Ellis recorded a 2.41 GAA and .904 save percentage in 29 games.[10]

Traded to the Charlottetown Islanders for the 2020–21 season, Ellis had his most successful junior campaign, posting a league-leading 1.78 GAA, .926 save percentage, and seven shutouts in 24 games.[10][13] He won the Jacques Plante Trophy for the lowest GAA, was named to the QMJHL First All-Star Team, and tied the QMJHL career shutout record with 17 while setting the single-season GAA record.[13][14] In the playoffs, he appeared in eight games as Charlottetown advanced.[10] Over his QMJHL career, Ellis amassed a 103–31–4 record in 153 regular-season games.[10]

### Professional
The St. Louis Blues selected Ellis in the third round, 93rd overall, of the 2019 NHL Entry Draft.[15] He signed a three-year entry-level contract with the Blues on March 1, 2021.[16] For the 2021–22 season, he split time between the ECHL's Worcester Railers (34 games, 3.21 GAA, .905 save percentage) and the AHL's Springfield Thunderbirds (six games, 3.93 GAA, .880 save percentage).[10]

In 2022–23, Ellis played primarily in the ECHL with the Tulsa Oilers (27 games, 3.46 GAA, .888 save percentage).[10] The following year, 2023–24, he appeared in 21 ECHL games with the Orlando Solar Bears (2.45 GAA, .923 save percentage) and 16 AHL games with Springfield (2.89 GAA, .924 save percentage).[10][17]

Ellis began the 2024–25 season with Springfield, posting a 2.63 GAA, .922 save percentage, and three shutouts in 42 games.[10] He represented Canada at the 2024 Spengler Cup, an international tournament in Switzerland.[18][19] In the 2025–26 season, the Buffalo Sabres claimed Ellis off waivers from St. Louis on October 6, 2025.[20] He made his NHL debut on October 22, 2025, against the Detroit Red Wings, stopping 27 of 29 shots in a 4–2 victory, becoming the first Cape Breton-born goaltender and the eighth Nova Scotia-born goalie in NHL history.[21][22] It was also the ninth debut win by a Sabres goaltender.[21]

Through eight NHL games in 2025–26, Ellis recorded a 4–3–0 record, 3.25 GAA, and .895 save percentage.[23][24] On December 9, 2025, during a game against the Edmonton Oilers, Ellis suffered a concussion after a collision with Oilers forward David Tomasek.[25][26] He entered concussion protocol and was placed on injured reserve retroactive to that date, missing at least a week.[27][28] The injury temporarily resolved Buffalo's three-goaltender rotation, which had included Ukko-Pekka Luukkonen and Alex Lyon.[29]

## International play
Ellis was invited to Canada's training camp for the 2018 IIHF World U18 Championships but did not make the final roster.[30] In December 2024, he was selected for Team Canada at the Spengler Cup, an annual invitational tournament featuring professional players.[18][19]

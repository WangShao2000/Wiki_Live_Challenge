# A New Old Play

### Key Points
- **Overview**: *A New Old Play* is a 2021 Chinese drama film directed by Qiu Jiongjiong, blending elements of fiction, historical drama, and experimental theater. It follows the life of a Sichuan opera performer against the backdrop of 20th-century Chinese history, from civil wars to the Cultural Revolution.
- **Notability and Reception**: The film premiered at international festivals and received awards, including the Special Jury Prize at the Locarno Film Festival. Reviews highlight its stylistic innovation and historical insight, though some note its lengthy runtime as potentially challenging.
- **Production Context**: Directed by documentary filmmaker Qiu Jiongjiong in his fiction debut, the film draws from his family history, with his father portraying a key role based on the director's grandfather.
- **Release and Availability**: It screened at various festivals starting in 2021 and had limited theatrical releases, including in the US in 2022 and France in 2025.

### Background
The film explores themes of art, memory, and political upheaval through the lens of traditional Sichuan opera. Research suggests it avoids direct confrontation with sensitive historical events by using allegorical and theatrical framing, allowing for a nuanced portrayal of China's past.

### Critical Insights
Evidence from reviews leans toward positive assessments, with critics appreciating the film's ironic style and visual artistry. However, opinions vary on its accessibility, as the experimental approach may not appeal to all audiences.


*A New Old Play* (Chinese: 椒麻堂会; pinyin: Jiāo Má Táng Huì) is a 2021 drama film written and directed by Qiu Jiongjiong. The film depicts the life of Qiu Fu, a performer in Sichuan opera, whose experiences span major events in 20th-century Chinese history, including civil war, the Second Sino-Japanese War, the establishment of the People's Republic of China, and the Cultural Revolution. Presented through a blend of fictional narrative, historical reminiscence, and theatrical staging, it incorporates supernatural elements set in the underworld.[1][2] 

Qiu Jiongjiong, previously known for documentary films, made his fiction feature debut with *A New Old Play*, drawing inspiration from his grandfather's career in opera.[3][4] The film premiered in the international competition at the 74th Locarno Film Festival in August 2021, where it received the Special Jury Prize.[5][6] It has been screened at numerous international film festivals and received limited theatrical releases in several countries.[7] Critics have described it as an innovative work that uses artificial sets and ironic techniques to explore personal and collective memory, comparing it to films such as Hou Hsiao-hsien's *The Puppetmaster* (1993) and Chen Kaige's *Farewell My Concubine* (1993).[8][9]

## Plot
In the 1980s, Qiu Fu (Yi Sicheng), a prominent clown-role actor in Sichuan opera, dies and embarks on a journey to the Ghost City, escorted by underworld officials. As he encounters old acquaintances and reviews his life before consuming the Forgetfulness Soup to erase his memories, flashbacks reveal his story.[2][10]

The narrative begins in the 1920s in Sichuan Province during a period of civil unrest. A soldier and opera enthusiast named Pocky (Qiu Zhimin) founds the "New-New" Troupe and adopts a seven-year-old orphan, the young Qiu Fu (Chen Haoyu). The troupe performs amid shifting political powers, affiliating with the military under the Nationalist Party.[11] During the Second Sino-Japanese War, the group contributes to resistance efforts against Japanese occupation.[8]

After the war, an adult Qiu Fu rises as a star performer, but the troupe faces pressures from the emerging Communist Party. The establishment of the People's Republic in 1949 brings nationalization of theaters, followed by hardships during events such as the Great Leap Forward and the Cultural Revolution, including famine, political persecution, and confinement.[2][8] The film frames these events through theatrical tableaux and supernatural interludes, emphasizing the persistence of art amid adversity.[9][12]

## Cast
- Yi Sicheng as Qiu Fu (adult), the central clown-role performer.[1][2]
- Chen Haoyu as Qiu Fu (young).[8]
- Guan Nan as Huafeng, a troupe member.[2][13]
- Qiu Zhimin as Pocky, the troupe founder (based on the director's grandfather).[8][11]
- Xue Xuchun as Chicken Foot, a troupe associate.[2][13]
- Gu Tao as Crooky, another troupe member.[2][13]

The cast includes non-professional actors, with Qiu Zhimin, the director's father, portraying Pocky.[4][8]

## Production
### Development
Qiu Jiongjiong, born in 1977, is a Chinese artist and filmmaker known for experimental documentaries such as *Mr. Zhang Believes* (2015).[14] *A New Old Play* marks his first fiction feature, inspired by his grandfather's experiences as a Sichuan opera actor.[3][4] Qiu initiated his interest in opera through family traditions and incorporated elements of painting, shadow theater, and opera into the film.[15] The script was developed to counter official historical narratives through personal and artistic perspectives.[15]

The project received support from the Golden Horse Film Project Promotion in 2017, the Hubert Bals Fund in 2018, and a CNC cash award.[14] It was co-produced by companies in Hong Kong and France.[14][16]

### Filming
Principal photography used studio sets to create artificial, theatrical environments, emphasizing irony and non-literal depictions.[8][9] Cinematography was handled by Feng Yuchao (credited as "Robbin"), with sound design by Wang Ran, music by Diao Lili, costumes by Bai Yun, and art direction by Lv Xin.[14][17] The film's runtime is 179 minutes.[1][2]

## Release
*A New Old Play* premiered at the 74th Locarno Film Festival on August 7, 2021, in the international competition section.[5][6] It subsequently screened at festivals including the Busan International Film Festival (October 2021), Golden Apricot Yerevan International Film Festival (October 2021), Festival des 3 Continents (November 2021), Singapore International Film Festival (November 2021), International Film Festival of Kerala (February 2022), Göteborg Film Festival (January 2022), and International Film Festival Rotterdam (January 2022).[14][18]

The film had a limited theatrical release in the United States on May 20, 2022, and became available for streaming on May 16, 2023.[2] In France, it was distributed by Carlotta Films and released in theaters on June 11, 2025.[19] Distribution rights were sold to entities in France, Japan, and Singapore in 2024.[20]

## Reception
On IMDb, the film holds a rating of 7.6 out of 10 based on over 600 user votes.[1] On review aggregator Rotten Tomatoes, it has received positive feedback from critics, though an aggregate score is not available due to a limited number of reviews.[2] 

Richard Brody of *The New Yorker* described the film as dramatizing Chinese political history with "affecting intimacy and candid observation—and with archly ironic methods," noting its use of contrived sets and fantasy sequences to signal non-literal truth.[8][9] Austin Considine of *The New York Times* called it "at once tragedy and farce," reviving old stories through new life.[2] Dennis Schwartz awarded it an A-, praising its tension for fans of Chinese opera and politics.[2] Sean Gilman positioned it between *The Puppetmaster* and *Farewell My Concubine* as a history seen through a performer's eyes.[2] 

Other reviews highlighted its multilayered nature and empathetic sparkle amid themes of futility.[14] Shelly Kraicer noted it as a "daring, brilliant independent feature from China."[14] The film's length of nearly three hours has been mentioned as potentially daunting, though lively in execution.[2]

## Appearances

### Mario Kart series

Cows debuted in Mario Kart 64 on the Moo Moo Farm course, where they appear as stationary background animals in the pastures.[1][6] In Mario Kart: Double Dash!! (2003), they are referenced indirectly through an advertisement on a truck in the Mushroom City course, promoting Moo Moo Farm.[5]

In Mario Kart DS (2005), cows return on the retro version of Moo Moo Farm, now depicted with blue collars and golden bells.[5][6] They serve as background elements but do not interact with gameplay.

Mario Kart Wii (2008) features cows as obstacles on Moo Moo Meadows, where colliding with them causes racers to spin out.[5][6] Distant cows use simplified textures, while closer ones have detailed models. An advertisement for Moo Moo Dairy appears on a truck in the Moonview Highway course.[5]

In Mario Kart 7 (2011), cows are shown on a Moo Moo Dairy sign in the retro Wii Coconut Mall course, but they do not appear physically.[5][6]

Mario Kart 8 (2014) and its enhanced version, Mario Kart 8 Deluxe (2017), include cows as obstacles on the retro Wii Moo Moo Meadows, with variations in color: white with blue or brown spots, black, or brown.[5][6] Advertisements for Moo Moo Meadows Milk appear in courses like Toad Harbor and Toad's Turnpike.[5] A Mii Racing Suit based on a cow design is available in Mario Kart 8 Deluxe.[5]

In Mario Kart Tour (2019), cows do not appear directly, but Moo Moo Meadows Milk is a sponsor, and items include the Moo Moo Mii Racing Suit and Moo Moo Offroader vehicle.[5][6]

Mario Kart Live: Home Circuit (2020) uses cows as obstacles in the Windmill Meadows course.[5][6]

In Mario Kart World (2025), cows return as obstacles on Moo Moo Meadows.[5][7] Additionally, a single cow is a playable character, classified as a heavyweight racer with statistics including speed of 60, acceleration of 45, weight of 55, and handling of 40.[8] The character is available from the start and shares its weight class with characters such as Wario and Donkey Kong.[5][8] Gameplay includes missions like navigating through a herd of cows within 15 seconds.[5] A golden cow statue on a bike appears in the game.[5]

### Other games

In Mario Party 2 (2000), cows appear as background elements in the Western Land board.[5][6]

Mario Party 3 (2001) features cows in the background of the Fowl Play minigame.[5][6]

In Mario Pinball Land (2004), cows rarely emerge from holes after hitting a Monty Mole; striking one grants 100,000 points.[5][6]

Mario & Sonic at the London 2012 Olympic Games (2012) includes cows as obstacles in the Dream Equestrian event, set on Moo Moo Meadows.[5][6]

In Mario Party: Island Tour (2013), cows are background elements on the Perilous Palace Path board.[5][6]

Yoshi's Woolly World (2015) and its port Poochy & Yoshi's Woolly World (2017) feature a Moo Moo Yoshi pattern that players can unlock.[5][6]

In Yoshi's Crafted World (2019), Moo Moo Meadows Milk appears as a sponsor on cartons and signs in the Rail-Yard Run and Go-Go Yoshi levels.[5] Players collect five cardboard cow crafts as souvenirs and can equip Yoshi with a cow costume.[5][6]

Super Mario Party Jamboree (2024) includes cows in the Western Land area.[6]

In the Super Mario Mash-Up Pack for Minecraft (2016), the cow design replaces the standard cow mob, with red and green variants substituting for Mooshrooms.[5]

Nintendo Badge Arcade (2014) offers collectible badges featuring cows from the Mario Kart 8 set.[5]

## Design and characteristics

Cows in the Mario franchise are depicted as white animals with black spots, small horns, and beady eyes.[5][9] From Mario Kart DS onward, they wear blue collars with golden bells.[5] In Mario Kart 8 and later titles, they appear in multiple colors, including black and brown.[5] The playable cow in Mario Kart World resembles the species but includes distinctive features such as a nose ring and a stylized face emblem.[9] It poses on all fours during jumps to reflect its animal nature and drives vehicles in an upright position during races.[3][9]

## Development

The inclusion of a playable cow in Mario Kart World stemmed from a designer's sketch during early development of a ranch-themed course.[3][4] Producer Masaaki Ishikawa described the moment as pivotal, stating that the idea felt natural and led to incorporating other obstacle elements, such as Cheep Cheep and Pokey, as racers.[3][4] These characters are categorized as "NPC Racers" to create a sense of an interconnected game world.[4] Development involved challenges in animating and voicing the cow to align with established characters, including quadrupedal posing for authenticity.[3] The concept also inspired the Kamek item, which transforms racers into NPC forms mid-race.[3]

Mario Kart World was released on June 5, 2025, alongside the Nintendo Switch 2 console.[10][11][12]

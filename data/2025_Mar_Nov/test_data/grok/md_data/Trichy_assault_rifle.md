## Development

Development of the Trichy Assault Rifle began in the early 2010s at the Ordnance Factory Tiruchirappalli as part of efforts to create an indigenous 7.62×39mm assault rifle. The initial version, introduced in 2011, involved converting FN FAL receivers to accept 7.62×39mm ammunition and use locally produced magazines. Field trials for this model were conducted, including tests by the Indian Army, with an effective range of 400 meters reported.

In 2015, further development led to a redesigned version based on the Bulgarian AR-M1, featuring a mono-block receiver and modular enhancements. A production line was established in 2017, and the first batch of 200 rifles was delivered to the Chandigarh police in March of that year. The Central Reserve Police Force (CRPF) conducted extensive testing in November 2017, firing over 15,000 rounds for lifecycle evaluation and 2,400 rounds for reliability, followed by field trials in early 2018.

In August 2021, a down-folding butt version was launched to facilitate quicker movement during patrols and guard duties, commemorating 75 years of India's independence. This variant addressed requests from Central Armed Police Forces and state police.

## Design

The Trichy Assault Rifle is a gas-operated, selective-fire weapon with a rotating bolt mechanism. It fires in single-shot and burst modes, with some customized versions for police forces limited to single-shot only. The rifle uses a curved 30-round magazine made of fiber-reinforced polymer with steel inserts.

Key specifications include a weight of approximately 3.4 to 3.6 kg, an overall length of 900 mm with the butt extended and 650 mm when folded, an effective range of 350 to 400 meters, and a muzzle velocity of 710 to 715 m/s. It features four-groove rifling and Picatinny rails for mounting optical sights and accessories, including an under-barrel grenade launcher. Tritium sights aid night firing. The design resembles the Bulgarian AR-M1F41, with minor differences in the gas block and cleaning rod, and performs similarly to the AK-103.

The TAR was developed for counter-insurgency and counter-terrorism operations, emphasizing lightweight construction and modularity.

## Variants

The Trichy Assault Rifle is available in several configurations:

- **Fixed butt**: Standard version for general use.
- **Side-folding butt**: Allows compact storage.
- **Down-folding butt**: Introduced in 2021 for enhanced portability during patrolling.
- **Telescoping stock**: M4-patterned adjustable stock option.

A compact carbine variant, the TriCa (Trichy Carbine), was launched in July 2021. It weighs 3.17 kg, has an effective range of 150 to 175 meters, and includes a muzzle booster to reduce flash and sound. The TriCa uses the same ammunition and magazines as the TAR and AK-47, making parts interchangeable. It is designed for special forces, helicopter crews, paratroopers, and infantry combat vehicle operators.


## Operators

The Trichy Assault Rifle is primarily used by Indian security forces:

- **Border Security Force (BSF)**: Inducted several hundred units, with an additional 125 rifles procured in 2024 along with bayonets.
- **Central Reserve Police Force (CRPF)**: Ordered 6,167 rifles following successful trials in 2018.
- **Indo-Tibetan Border Police (ITBP)**, **Sashastra Seema Bal (SSB)**, and **Central Industrial Security Force (CISF)**: Procured in significant numbers.
- **State police forces**: Adopted by units in Chandigarh, Kolkata, Kerala, and others under modernization programs.
- **Indian Army**: Proposed for reserve units and tested, though not widely adopted as a primary rifle.

No confirmed exports or foreign operators as of December 2025.

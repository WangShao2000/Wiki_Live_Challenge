# Everest, North Dakota

Everest is an unincorporated community in Cass County, North Dakota, United States. Situated in Everest Township approximately two miles south of Casselton, it was established in 1881 as a station on the Great Northern Railway. The community was named after Charles A. Everitt, editor of the Lisbon Star newspaper, although the name was misspelled as "Everest." Following the abandonment of the railroad tracks in 1896, the community declined significantly. Once home to a population of 175 in 1890, it had dwindled to 10 by 1960, and today consists primarily of historical remnants.


## History

Everest was platted in 1881 in the northeast quarter of section 15, township 139, range 52, coinciding with the construction of a Great Northern Railroad station. The St. Paul, Minneapolis and Manitoba Railway, which later became part of the Great Northern Railway, established a connection at Everest in December 1882, linking lines from Breckenridge, Minnesota, to Portland or Hope, North Dakota, with about three miles of new track. This positioned Everest as a junction in the Red River Valley's north-south rail network.

A post office opened on May 24, 1882, with Carl Knuppel as postmaster. The community reached a population of 175 by 1890. However, the Great Northern Railway removed the station in 1895 and abandoned the tracks from Everest to Fleming in 1896 due to revised plans. The post office closed on August 13, 1904, reopened briefly on September 17, 1904, and closed permanently on November 30, 1908, with mail redirected to Casselton.

The decline continued, with the population reported as 10 in 1960. Archaeological interest persists, such as excavations at the former railroad depot site. The surrounding Everest Township had a population of 99 at the 2020 census and 78 in 2023.

## Geography

Everest is located at coordinates 46°51'35"N 97°13'26"W in Cass County. It lies within Everest Township, which covers an area of approximately 34.871 square miles. The community is about two miles south of Casselton and near features such as the Casselton Robert Miller Regional Airport and Tinta Tawa Park.

## Demographics

As an unincorporated community, Everest does not have separate census-designated population figures. The encompassing Everest Township had a population of 78 in 2023, with a median age of 49.9 years. The gender distribution was 48 males and 30 females, resulting in a sex ratio of 160 males per 100 females. The racial composition was 100% White.

## Notable people

William "Wild Bill" Langer (1886–1959), who served as Governor of North Dakota from 1933 to 1934 and 1937 to 1939, spent his early years on a farm in Everest Township before moving to Casselton.

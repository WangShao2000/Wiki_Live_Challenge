# 2025 Tour Championship

The **2025 Tour Championship** was a golf tournament that was the culminating event of the PGA Tour's 2025 FedEx Cup Playoffs. It was played from August 21 to 24, 2025, at East Lake Golf Club in Atlanta, Georgia. For the first time since the FedEx Cup's inception in 2007, the event used a standard 72-hole stroke-play format with all participants starting at even par, rather than staggered starting scores based on FedEx Cup standings. The field consisted of the top 30 players in the FedEx Cup points list following the BMW Championship. England's Tommy Fleetwood won the tournament at 18 under par, three strokes ahead of Russell Henley and Patrick Cantlay, securing his first PGA Tour title and the FedEx Cup championship.

## Format and qualification

The Tour Championship serves as the final event in the PGA Tour's FedEx Cup Playoffs, a series of three tournaments that determine the season-long FedEx Cup champion. In 2025, the PGA Tour implemented a change to the tournament's format, shifting from a system where players began with starting strokes based on their FedEx Cup positions to a traditional 72-hole stroke-play competition where all entrants started at even par. This adjustment aimed to emphasize performance over the four rounds while maintaining the event's role in crowning the FedEx Cup winner. The winner of the Tour Championship also received the FedEx Cup and its associated bonus payout.[1][2]

The field was limited to the top 30 players in the FedEx Cup standings after the conclusion of the BMW Championship, the second playoff event. Qualification for the playoffs began with the top 70 players after the regular season's Wyndham Championship advancing to the FedEx St. Jude Championship, followed by the top 50 proceeding to the BMW Championship. The Tour Championship field represented players who had accumulated the highest points throughout the season, with points awarded based on performance in regular-season events and the first two playoff tournaments.[1][3]

The 2025 field included five former FedEx Cup champions: Scottie Scheffler (2024), Rory McIlroy (2016, 2019, 2022), Justin Rose (2018), Justin Thomas (2017), and Patrick Cantlay (2021). The players were (in alphabetical order): Ludvig Åberg, Akshay Bhatia, Keegan Bradley, Jacob Bridgeman, Sam Burns, Patrick Cantlay, Corey Conners, Harris English, Tommy Fleetwood, Chris Gotterup, Ben Griffin, Harry Hall, Brian Harman, Russell Henley, Viktor Hovland, Sungjae Im, Shane Lowry, Robert MacIntyre, Hideki Matsuyama, Rory McIlroy, Maverick McNealy, Collin Morikawa, Andrew Novak, Justin Rose, Scottie Scheffler, J.J. Spaun, Sepp Straka, Nick Taylor, Justin Thomas, and Cameron Young.[4]

## Course

The tournament was held at East Lake Golf Club, a par-70 course measuring 7,490 yards (6,850 m). The layout featured adjustments in 2025 to promote more risk-reward opportunities, with the intent of producing winning scores closer to par and enhancing competitive drama. East Lake has hosted the Tour Championship annually since 2004.[1][2]

## Tournament summary

### First round

Russell Henley took the lead with a nine-under-par 61, which included nine birdies and no bogeys. Scottie Scheffler finished two strokes behind at seven under par after a bogey-free round that featured three birdies in his last four holes and a par save on the 16th hole. Seven players tied for third at six under par: Collin Morikawa, Justin Thomas, Patrick Cantlay, Robert MacIntyre, and Tommy Fleetwood. Rory McIlroy ended the day at four under par, highlighted by an eagle on the par-five sixth hole and a birdie on the 18th after his bunker shot deflected off a grandstand.[5]

### Second round

Tommy Fleetwood carded a seven-under-par 63 to tie Russell Henley for the lead at 13 under par. Henley's four-under 66 included birdies on holes 3, 5, 8, and 17. Scottie Scheffler remained in contention at 10 under par overall after a three-under round.[6]

### Third round

Patrick Cantlay posted a six-under-par 64 to join Tommy Fleetwood atop the leaderboard at 16 under par. Fleetwood scored a three-under 67. Keegan Bradley and Scottie Scheffler were among those trailing closely.[7]

### Fourth round

Tommy Fleetwood shot a two-under-par 68 to finish at 18 under par and win by three strokes. His round included birdies on holes 3, 8, 9, 15, 16, and 17, offset by bogeys on holes 11 and 13. Key moments featured a flop shot from the fringe on the third hole, a bunker save on the seventh, and precise iron approaches on the closing holes. Russell Henley and Patrick Cantlay tied for second at 15 under par.[8]

## Overview
Second-Ponce de Leon Baptist Church operates from its campus at 2715 Peachtree Road NE in Atlanta, Georgia, where it holds weekly worship services on Sundays at 11 a.m., available both in-person and via livestream on platforms like YouTube. The church emphasizes discipleship through small groups, missions, and community service, with facilities including a sanctuary, educational spaces, and a Community Wellness Center offering fitness programs. As of 2025, it is in a transitional phase following the departure of its previous senior pastor, with an interim pastor in place and a search committee actively seeking a new leader to address growth challenges.

## Community Role
The church engages in local outreach, such as operating a mobile food pantry in partnership with organizations like the Atlanta Community Food Bank, providing fresh produce to hundreds of families without requiring documentation. It also collaborates with nearby institutions, including a joint preschool program with the Cathedral of Christ the King, which began in 2022 and has expanded to serve more students. Additionally, the church hosts events like Veteran's Day luncheons and musical performances, often in cooperation with groups such as the Kiwanis Club of Northside Atlanta and Mercer University.


Second-Ponce de Leon Baptist Church (pronounced /ˈspidl/) is a Baptist church in the Buckhead neighborhood of Atlanta, Georgia, United States. Located at 2715 Peachtree Road NE, it traces its origins to the founding of Second Baptist Church in 1854 and was formed in its current iteration in 1932 through the merger of several local congregations. The church is affiliated with the Southern Baptist Convention and the Cooperative Baptist Fellowship, positioning it as a centrist Baptist congregation. It operates ministries focused on worship, discipleship, missions, and community service, including a wellness center and partnerships for food distribution and education. As of 2024, the church reported an average in-person worship attendance of 144, down from pre-COVID levels, and is currently seeking a new senior pastor amid efforts to attract younger members and families. The congregation has been involved in broader Baptist discussions on social issues and has supported mission churches and community initiatives in Atlanta.

## History

### Early years and founding churches (1854–1929)
The roots of Second-Ponce de Leon Baptist Church extend to the mid-19th century amid Atlanta's growth as a railroad hub. Second Baptist Church, the oldest predecessor, was organized on September 1, 1854, with 19 charter members dismissed from First Baptist Church of Atlanta. The founding occurred at the home of Dr. B. F. Bomar, with Elder Jesse H. Campbell presiding. Initial services were held in private homes until a brick church building was constructed at Washington and Mitchell Streets, completed in 1858 at a cost of $14,000. This structure was among the first brick churches in Atlanta and included modern features like steam heating and an internal baptistry.

During the American Civil War, the church building served as a storehouse under Union occupation in 1864 but was spared from destruction due to the intervention of Father Thomas O'Reilly, a Catholic priest from the nearby Church of the Immaculate Conception, who persuaded federal commanders to preserve certain religious sites. Post-war, the congregation supported recovery efforts, including aiding a Black congregation that later became Friendship Baptist Church in 1871.

Ponce de Leon Avenue Baptist Church was established on October 6, 1904, at the home of George M. Brown, with 115 charter members, mostly from First and Second Baptist Churches. Named after the nearby Ponce de Leon Avenue, the church initially used temporary spaces before building at Ponce de Leon and Piedmont Avenues, dedicated in 1906. Membership grew steadily, reaching 507 by 1914.

Buckhead Baptist Church formed in 1911 through efforts by local leaders, holding initial services in a tent before constructing a building on West Paces Ferry Road.

In 1929, Ponce de Leon Avenue Baptist and Buckhead Baptist merged, relocating to a new site at Peachtree Road and East Wesley Road (then Wesley Avenue). The original Ponce de Leon building was sold to Central Congregational Church.

### Merger and mid-20th century (1930–1954)
On November 18, 1932, Second Baptist merged with the combined Ponce de Leon–Buckhead congregation, forming Second-Ponce de Leon Baptist Church. The merger integrated organizations, assets, and leadership, with initial challenges like seating arrangements resolved over time. The new church occupied the Peachtree Road facility, with the first two floors of the sanctuary completed in 1930.

The church began radio broadcasts in the 1930s and its first televised service in 1962. Membership fluctuated, declining to 1,500 by 1934 due to urban shifts but growing to 2,500 by 1945 under pastors like Rev. Ryland Knight. In 1954, the church celebrated its centennial, marking the legacy of its founding churches.

### Late 20th and early 21st centuries (1955–present)
Since 1951, Second-Ponce has supported the establishment of new congregations in Atlanta, including Wieuca Road Baptist Church in 1954, which later redeveloped its property in 2021 to fund relaunch efforts. The church hosted a 2018 series on race and faith commemorating the 50th anniversary of Martin Luther King Jr.'s assassination, featuring speakers like Andrew Young.

In 2022, it partnered with the Cathedral of Christ the King to launch CTK Preschool at Second-Ponce, which has since expanded. The COVID-19 pandemic contributed to attendance declines, leading to a transitional period by 2024, with a pastor search committee formed to recruit a new senior pastor focused on growth and outreach. As of 2025, the church continues community events, such as food pantries and Veteran's Day observances.

## Architecture and facilities
The current building at Peachtree and East Wesley Roads was constructed starting in 1930, with the sanctuary's initial floors occupied that year. It features a traditional design suitable for worship and community activities, including an Austin organ installed in 1922 and relocated from an earlier site. The campus includes educational annexes added in the early 20th century and a Community Wellness Center (CWC) offering gym facilities, fitness classes, and community memberships at discounted rates. Tenants on the property include Respite Care Atlanta, Atlanta Suzuki Violin, and the Alma Domestic Violence Foundation.

## Beliefs and affiliation
As a Baptist church, Second-Ponce adheres to principles such as believer's baptism, congregational governance, and the priesthood of all believers, guided by the New Revised Standard Version of the Bible. It maintains ties to the Southern Baptist Convention while participating in Cooperative Baptist Fellowship activities, reflecting a centrist approach. The church has been part of discussions on social issues within Georgia Baptist circles, including a 2014 vote where a church messenger expressed views on homosexuality amid debates over congregational ousters.

## Ministries and programs
Worship services occur Sundays at 11 a.m., blending hymns and contemporary elements with orchestral accompaniment. Discipleship includes small groups by life stage, such as Kids Club for children, youth Bible studies, and adult fellowships like Prime Timers for seniors. Missions involve financial support to over 12 organizations and hands-on service, including food pantries, Habitat for Humanity, and summer camps.

Congregational care provides prayer, visitation, and benevolence assistance. Annual events include Vacation Bible School, Easter egg hunts, and Christmas services. The church also hosts external events, such as the 2025 Baptist Women in Ministry gathering.

### Demographics
As of 2024, the congregation is predominantly older (60% aged 65+), White (76%), and married (54%), with smaller representations from other ethnic groups and age brackets. It serves the Buckhead community, which has a growing young adult population and lower poverty rates compared to broader Atlanta.

## Notable people
Several individuals associated with the church have gained recognition. Alumni from Mercer University, including Rev. Allie Osborne (Minister of Missions & Children’s Discipleship), Rev. Jonathan Hall (Minister of Community & Student Discipleship), and others, lead ministries. Historian C. Douglas Weaver authored a 2004 history of the church. Former Georgia Governor William J. Northen was a charter member and deacon of Ponce de Leon Baptist.

### 2010 NFC Championship Game

The 2010 NFC Championship Game was an American football game played on January 24, 2010, at the Louisiana Superdome in New Orleans, Louisiana, to determine the National Football Conference (NFC) champion for the 2009 NFL season. The New Orleans Saints, the NFC's top seed with a 13–3 regular-season record, defeated the Minnesota Vikings, the second seed with a 12–4 record, by a score of 31–28 in overtime.[1][2] With the victory, the Saints earned their first NFC championship and advanced to Super Bowl XLIV, where they defeated the AFC champion Indianapolis Colts 31–17.[3] The game featured seven lead changes or ties, five turnovers by the Vikings, and standout performances from running back Adrian Peterson and quarterback Drew Brees.[4][5] It was played before a crowd of 71,276 in an indoor stadium, with the Saints favored by 3.5 points and the overunder set at 54.[1][2] The contest was broadcast on Fox television and Westwood One radio nationally.[6] Later investigations by the NFL linked the game to the Saints' bounty scandal, in which the league found evidence of a program offering incentives for injuring opposing players, though the Saints organization and some players disputed the findings.[7][8]

#### Background

The 2009 NFL season saw the New Orleans Saints achieve their best regular-season record to date at 13–3, securing the NFC's number-one seed and home-field advantage throughout the playoffs.[4] The team started the season with 13 consecutive wins, led by quarterback Drew Brees, who threw for 4,388 yards, 34 touchdowns, and 11 interceptions, completing 70.6 percent of his passes.[4] The Saints' offense ranked first in the league in scoring (31.9 points per game) and yards (403.8 per game), supported by running backs Pierre Thomas and Reggie Bush, and receivers Marques Colston, Devery Henderson, and Robert Meachem.[4] Defensively, the unit ranked 20th in yards allowed but forced 39 turnovers, the second-most in the NFL, with contributions from safety Darren Sharper and defensive end Will Smith.[4] Head coach Sean Payton, hired in 2006, had rebuilt the franchise following Hurricane Katrina's devastation in 2005, which had displaced the team temporarily.[3][4] In the divisional round, the Saints defeated the Arizona Cardinals 45–14 at home.[4]

The Minnesota Vikings finished the regular season 12–4, earning the NFC's second seed.[4] Quarterback Brett Favre, in his 19th NFL season and first with the Vikings after unretiring, threw for 4,202 yards, 33 touchdowns, and seven interceptions, achieving a career-high completion percentage of 68.4.[4] Running back Adrian Peterson led the league with 18 rushing touchdowns and added 1,383 yards.[4] The receiving corps included Sidney Rice, Percy Harvin, and tight end Visanthe Shiancoe, while the defense ranked sixth in yards allowed and featured defensive ends Jared Allen and Ray Edwards.[4] Head coach Brad Childress guided the team to a divisional-round victory over the Dallas Cowboys, 34–3.[4] The Vikings sought their first Super Bowl appearance since 1976.[3]

The matchup pitted the league's top two NFC seeds, with the Saints hosting due to their superior record.[1] The game was the Vikings' first road playoff contest of the postseason and the Saints' second home game.[2]

#### Game summary

The game kicked off at 640 p.m. EST (540 p.m. CST) under referee Pete Morelli.[2] The indoor conditions at the Louisiana Superdome eliminated weather as a factor.[2]

##### First quarter
Minnesota received the opening kickoff and drove 80 yards in 10 plays, scoring on a 19-yard touchdown run by Adrian Peterson.[1][2][5] Ryan Longwell added the extra point for a 7–0 lead.[1] New Orleans responded with a 76-yard, seven-play drive, tying the score at 7–7 on a 38-yard touchdown pass from Drew Brees to Pierre Thomas, followed by Garrett Hartley's extra point.[1][2][5] The Vikings regained the lead on their next possession, driving 73 yards in 10 plays for a 5-yard touchdown pass from Brett Favre to Sidney Rice, with Longwell's kick making it 14–7.[1][2][5]

##### Second quarter
The Saints tied the game early in the quarter with a 64-yard, seven-play drive ending in a 9-yard touchdown pass from Brees to Devery Henderson, followed by Hartley's extra point for 14–14.[1][2][5] Both defenses tightened, with New Orleans forcing a fumble from Favre near midfield to end the half tied.[5]

##### Third quarter
New Orleans opened the second half with a 37-yard, four-play drive after a 61-yard kickoff return by Courtney Roby, scoring on a 9-yard run by Pierre Thomas for a 21–14 lead after Hartley's kick.[4][5] Minnesota answered with an 80-yard, nine-play drive, tying at 21–21 on Peterson's 1-yard touchdown run and Longwell's extra point.[1][2][5]

##### Fourth quarter
The Saints forced a fumble from Percy Harvin, recovering at the Minnesota 7-yard line and scoring three plays later on a 5-yard pass from Brees to Reggie Bush, with Hartley's kick for 28–21.[1][2][5] The Vikings tied the game at 28–28 with 458 remaining on a 57-yard, seven-play drive capped by Peterson's 2-yard touchdown run and Longwell's extra point.[1][2][5] In the final minute, Favre drove the Vikings into field-goal range, but Tracy Porter intercepted a pass intended for Sidney Rice with 19 seconds left, forcing overtime.[3][4][5]

##### Overtime
New Orleans won the coin toss and drove 39 yards in 10 plays, with Hartley kicking a 40-yard field goal to win 31–28.[1][2][5]

#### Broadcasting

The game was televised nationally by Fox, with play-by-play announcer Joe Buck, color commentator Troy Aikman, and sideline reporters Pam Oliver and Chris Myers.[9] It drew a 30.6 household rating, the highest for an NFC Championship Game since 1996.[9] National radio coverage was provided by Westwood One, with play-by-play by Kevin Harlan and color commentary by Randy Cross.[10] Locally, the Saints' broadcast aired on WWL-AM with Jim Henderson on play-by-play and Hokie Gajan as analyst, while the Vikings' feed was on KFAN-AM with Paul Allen on play-by-play and Pete Bercich as analyst.[11]

#### Aftermath

The Saints' victory marked the franchise's first Super Bowl appearance after 43 seasons in the NFL.[3][4] In Super Bowl XLIV on February 7, 2010, at Sun Life Stadium in Miami Gardens, Florida, New Orleans defeated the Indianapolis Colts 31–17, with Brees earning MVP honors for 288 passing yards and two touchdowns.[3] The win capped a turnaround for the Saints, who had only one prior playoff victory before 2009.[4] For the Vikings, the loss ended Favre's season and proved to be his final playoff game, as he retired in 2011.[3] Minnesota has not returned to the NFC Championship since.[3]

The game holds a place in NFL history as one of the most turnover-laden conference championships, with the Vikings' five miscues proving decisive despite their statistical dominance.[1][2][3]

#### Controversies

During the game, several hits on Favre drew scrutiny for lack of penalties, including a high-low block by Saints defensive linemen Bobby McCray and Remi Ayodele that injured Favre's ankle, requiring offseason surgery.[7][8] The NFL later stated the Saints should have been penalized 15 yards for McCray's hit.[7] Post-game fines totaled $30,000 for the Saints, including $20,000 for McCray on two hits, $5,000 for Anthony Hargrove on unnecessary roughness against Favre, and $5,000 for Jonathan Casillas on a hit to a punt returner.[7]

In March 2012, the NFL announced findings from an investigation into the Saints' defense operating a bounty program from 2009 to 2011, involving 22 to 27 players contributing to a pool that paid $1,500 for knockouts (injuries preventing return) and $1,000 for cart-offs (players carried off), with payouts doubling or tripling in playoffs.[6][8] The program, administered by defensive coordinator Gregg Williams, reached up to $50,000 in 2009.[6] Specifically for the NFC Championship, linebacker Jonathan Vilma allegedly offered $10,000 to knock Favre out of the game.[6][7][8] The investigation, based on 18,000 documents and interviews, found head coach Sean Payton aware but not intervening, and general manager Mickey Loomis failing to stop it despite owner directives.[6] The Vikings had raised concerns post-game, prompting the probe.[7]

Sanctions included Payton's full-season suspension for 2012, Loomis' eight-game suspension, Williams' indefinite suspension (lifted in 2013), and assistant coach Joe Vitt's six-game ban.[8] Players faced suspensions Vilma (full season, later overturned), Hargrove (eight games, reduced), Will Smith (four games, overturned), and Scott Fujita (three games, overturned).[8] The Saints were fined $500,000 and lost second-round draft picks in 2012 and 2013.[8] The organization and players denied an organized injury-incentive system, attributing payments to performance like interceptions.[6] Favre described the allegations as hearsay and viewed the hits as part of football.[6] Appeals and legal challenges led to some penalties being vacated by former commissioner Paul Tagliabue in December 2012, who upheld findings of conduct detrimental but cited league inconsistencies.[8] The scandal remains a debated chapter in NFL history, highlighting player safety concerns.[6][7][8]

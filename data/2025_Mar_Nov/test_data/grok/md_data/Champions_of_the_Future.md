### What is Champions of the Future?
Champions of the Future is a karting championship series that focuses on nurturing young talent in motorsport. Launched in 2020, it organizes events across Europe and beyond, using standardized rules to ensure fair competition. Drivers compete in categories like OK and OK-Junior, often as a stepping stone to professional racing.

### Main Series and Events
The flagship Euro Series runs multiple rounds each year at tracks like Portimao in Portugal and Kristianstad in Sweden. The Academy Program, started in 2023, offers an "arrive and drive" format where equipment is provided, making it easier for newcomers. Recent announcements include a North American series starting in 2026, in partnership with K1 Speed.

### Partnerships and Impact
It collaborates with F1 Academy to support female drivers, providing discounts and branded gear. This has helped identify talents who progress to formulas like F4. While not all participants reach the top, the series is seen as a valuable entry point, with alumni competing in higher categories.

Champions of the Future (COTF) is an international karting series organized by the RGMMC Group, a promoter specializing in motorsport events. Established in 2020, it provides competitive platforms for young drivers aged 8 and older, aligning with Fédération Internationale de l'Automobile (FIA) regulations to prepare participants for higher levels of racing, such as the FIA Karting World Championship. The series emphasizes fairness through measures like equipment lotteries and cost controls, and it has expanded to include regional programs in Europe, the Middle East, and North America. Notable for its partnerships, including with F1 Academy to promote gender diversity, COTF has produced drivers who have advanced to single-seater formulas. As of 2025, it hosts multiple series with over 200 entrants per event in categories like OK, OK-Junior, and Mini.

## History
The series was launched in 2020 by the RGMMC Group, a company founded by James Geidel that had previously organized events under the Rotax Max Challenge banner. The inaugural Euro Series aimed to fill a gap in international karting by offering high-quality competitions with FIA-homologated equipment, focusing on OK and OK-Junior classes.[13][18] Early events were held amid the COVID-19 pandemic, with rounds in locations such as Adria in Italy and Wackersdorf in Germany.

In 2022, COTF introduced a Winter Series to provide off-season racing opportunities, featuring tracks like Franciacorta in Italy.[3] The Academy Program debuted in 2023 at Al Forsan International Sports Resort in Abu Dhabi, United Arab Emirates, as an arrive-and-drive initiative to reduce barriers for aspiring drivers.[60][61] This program expanded in 2024 with F1 Academy support, emphasizing female participation through subsidies and talent scouting.[7][10]

By 2025, the series had grown to include a UAE regional championship and announced a North American expansion in partnership with K1 Speed, set to begin in 2026.[2][12][47] The Euro Series that year featured five rounds, culminating in titles for Kenzo Craigie in OK and Lewis Baglin in OK-Junior.[38][40] The Academy Program concluded its second season with champions in multiple categories, including Markas Silkunas in OK-N Senior.[68][72]

COTF has adapted to industry trends, incorporating esports elements like the KartSim × RGMMC Esports Cup in 2025.[4] It also launched related initiatives, such as the F4 Racing Academy in September 2025, offering seats in Formula 4 for top performers.[9]

## Series and Events
COTF organizes several championships, each with distinct formats and locations to maximize driver development.

### Euro Series
The core competition, held annually since 2020, consists of four to five rounds across European circuits. Events include free practice, qualifying, heats, and finals over weekends. In 2025, rounds were at Portimao (Portugal), Zuera (Spain), Kristianstad (Sweden), Franciacorta (Italy), and a fifth venue.[0][8] The series uses FIA CIK classes, with tires and fuel supplied uniformly.

### Academy Program
Introduced in 2023, this arrive-and-drive series targets drivers aged 8–15, providing chassis, engines, and suits to control costs. It features six double-header weekends, with categories including Mini 60 (ages 8–11), OK-N Junior (ages 11–14), and OK-N Senior (ages 14+).[11][70] Equipment is allocated via lottery for fairness, with suppliers like Parolin/TM for Mini and Kart Republic/IAME for others.[11] The 2025 season included events in the UAE, Spain, Italy, and elsewhere, with live broadcasts on Motorsport TV.[11][76]

### Other Series
- **UAE Series**: Launched in 2025 at Al Forsan and other tracks, it mirrors the Academy format for regional talent.[21][60]
- **America Series**: Announced on December 2, 2025, in partnership with K1 Speed, it aims to bridge U.S. indoor karting to professional levels, starting in 2026 with unspecified classes.[2][12][20]
- **Shifters and Winter Series**: Supplementary events for KZ shifter karts and off-season racing, such as the 2025 Shifters round in Valencia, Spain.[9]

Events emphasize driver education, including physical training and media workshops.[11] The series has hosted over 140 drivers per round, with grids filling quickly.[4]

## Classes
COTF adheres to FIA karting regulations, with classes divided by age and engine type:

- **Mini 60**: For drivers aged 8–11, using 60cc engines; includes sub-categories like U10.
- **OK-N Junior (OKJ)**: Ages 11–14, with restricted 125cc engines.
- **OK-N Senior (OK)**: Ages 14+, with unrestricted 125cc engines.
- **Shifters (KZ/KZ2)**: Gearbox karts for older or master drivers.

All classes use homologated chassis and engines, redistributed daily to prevent advantages.[11][9]

## Notable Drivers
Several COTF participants have progressed to higher motorsport levels. Alumni include:

- Luna Fluxá, 2024 Academy OK-N Senior champion, who advanced to Spanish F4 with Campos Racing in 2026.[17][54][77]
- Julia Montlaur, 2024 Academy winner in another class, joining the Mercedes-AMG Junior Programme.[30]
- Kenzo Craigie, 2025 Euro Series OK champion and 2024 FIA OK-Junior World Champion.[38][44]
- Markas Šilkūnas, 2025 Academy OK-N Senior champion.[68][70][72]
- Niccolo Perico and Wynn Godschalk, 2025 Academy Mini champions, with Godschalk topping the U10 sub-category.[43][73]

Other drivers like Oliver Bearman and Rafael Câmara, now in Ferrari's Driver Academy, competed in early COTF events before winning world titles elsewhere.[32] Female drivers such as Atiqa Mir have set records, including the highest female qualifying in Mini class history.[50][74]

## Partnerships
COTF collaborates with organizations to enhance accessibility:

- **F1 Academy**: Since 2024, it supports up to nine female drivers per category with 30% entry discounts, branded karts, and testing opportunities. The partnership expanded in 2025 to include 27 drivers for 2026, focusing on talent identification.[7][10][48][59][66][75]
- **K1 Speed**: For the American series, providing infrastructure and pathways from indoor to outdoor karting.[2][12][20]
- **Minerva’s Virtual Academy**: Offers online education for young racers.[9]
- **Scouting Advisors**: Includes Gwen Lagrue from Mercedes-AMG for spotting potential.[11]

These alliances aim to address barriers like costs and gender imbalance, though some sources note ongoing challenges in global diversity.[8][22]

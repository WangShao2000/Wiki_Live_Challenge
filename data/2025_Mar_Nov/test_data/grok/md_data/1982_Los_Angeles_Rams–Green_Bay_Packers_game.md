## Background

The 1982 NFL season followed a 57-day players' strike that canceled games from September 21 to November 15, reducing the schedule from 16 to nine games per team and expanding the playoffs to include eight teams per conference.[1] This game occurred in Week 1, before the strike began.

The Los Angeles Rams entered the season under head coach Ray Malavasi, who had led the team since 1978. In 1981, the Rams finished with a 6–10 record, missing the playoffs after reaching the Super Bowl two years earlier.[2] The team relied on running back Wendell Tyler, who had rushed for 1,109 yards the previous season, and quarterback Bert Jones, acquired in a trade from the Baltimore Colts to stabilize the position.[3] The Rams' defense, anchored by players like cornerback Nolan Cromwell, aimed to improve after allowing 332 points in 1981.[2]

The Green Bay Packers, coached by Bart Starr in his eighth year, came off an 8–8 record in 1981, also missing the playoffs.[4] The offense featured quarterback Lynn Dickey, wide receivers James Lofton and John Jefferson, and running back Eddie Lee Ivery. Lofton and Jefferson had combined for over 2,000 receiving yards in 1981, providing a potent passing attack.[4] The Packers' defense sought to reduce the 361 points allowed the prior year.[4]

The game was played at Milwaukee County Stadium, one of the Packers' home venues at the time, in front of 53,694 spectators.[5] Green Bay held a slight edge in the recent series history, but Los Angeles led the all-time matchup 44–40 entering the game.[6]

## Game summary

### First half

The Rams controlled the first half, scoring on their first five possessions while the Packers committed four turnovers.[5] Los Angeles opened with a 4-yard rushing touchdown by Wendell Tyler, followed by a 32-yard field goal from Mike Lansford to lead 10–0 after the first quarter.[7]

In the second quarter, Bert Jones connected with Mike Barber for an 8-yard touchdown pass, extending the lead to 17–0.[7] Lansford added field goals of 29 and 28 yards, pushing the halftime score to 23–0.[7] The Packers' offense struggled, with Lynn Dickey throwing two interceptions and fumbling twice.[5] Green Bay managed only minimal yardage and no points in the half.

### Second half

The Packers mounted their comeback in the third quarter. Dickey led a drive culminating in a 4-yard touchdown pass to tight end Paul Coffman, making the score 23–7.[7] After a Rams three-and-out, Green Bay scored again on a three-play drive: a 42-yard pass to Coffman set up a 3-yard rushing touchdown by Eddie Lee Ivery, narrowing the gap to 23–14.[6]

In the fourth quarter, the Packers continued their surge. A 66-yard drive ended with a 15-yard touchdown pass from Dickey to James Lofton, giving Green Bay a 21–23 lead after the extra point from Jan Stenerud.[7] On the ensuing kickoff, Packers linebacker Guy Prather forced a fumble from Rams returner Kermit Alexander, recovering the ball at the Los Angeles 10-yard line.[5] Dickey then threw a 10-yard touchdown pass to Coffman, putting Green Bay ahead 28–23.[7]

Late in the quarter, after a 19-yard run by fullback Mike Meade and a penalty, Ivery sealed the win with a 27-yard rushing touchdown, finalizing the score at 35–23.[6] The Rams gained no first downs in the first 17 minutes of the second half and were held to four first downs overall after halftime.[5] Green Bay's defense forced turnovers and limited Los Angeles to zero points in the final two quarters.[6]

## Aftermath and legacy

The Packers' victory propelled them to a 5–3–1 record in the strike-shortened season, securing a playoff spot as the third seed in the NFC.[4] They advanced past the first round by defeating the St. Louis Cardinals but lost in the divisional round to the Dallas Cowboys.[4] The Rams struggled post-strike, finishing 2–7 and missing the playoffs.[2] Bert Jones retired after the season, and the team drafted Eric Dickerson in 1983 to bolster their running game.[2]

The game remains significant in Packers history as their largest halftime comeback until 2013, when they overcame a 23-point deficit against the Dallas Cowboys.[9] It exemplified the team's offensive potential under Dickey, who led the NFL in passing yards that season.[4] The comeback also highlighted defensive improvements in the second half, a factor in Green Bay's playoff run.[6]

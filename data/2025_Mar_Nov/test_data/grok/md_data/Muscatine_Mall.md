### History
Muscatine Mall, initially named Muscatine Plaza, opened in 1971 in Muscatine, Iowa, a city located on the Mississippi River about 15 miles south of Interstate 80.[1][9] Developed by General Growth Properties, it was designed as an enclosed shopping mall to serve the regional population.[10] The mall underwent renovations in 1980 and 1991 to update its facilities.[4]

In 1980, General Growth sold the property to Aetna Life Insurance and The Rouse Company for $14.8 million as part of a portfolio of ten malls.[11] By 1989, Rouse had acquired full ownership.[12] The mall thrived through the 1980s and early 1990s, with a mix of national chains and local businesses.[1] However, competition from larger malls in the Quad Cities area and shifts in retail trends led to challenges by the mid-1990s.[4]

Significant changes began in 1997 when Walmart relocated from its anchor position to a new standalone supercenter nearby.[1][4] Menards took over the vacated space in 1998, an unusual addition for an enclosed mall as it focused on home improvement and operated with exterior access.[13] Von Maur departed in 1998, and Staples briefly occupied its space until closing in 2002.[1] Elder-Beerman replaced Staples in 2003, marking its first store in Iowa.[1][14] Walgreens also left around 2002 for a freestanding location.[1]

Ownership changed again in 1999 when Landau & Heyman purchased the mall from TIAA and Rouse.[15] In 2006, it was sold to local businessmen with intentions to revitalize it.[4] Menards relocated to a new site in 2007, leaving its space to be subdivided for smaller retailers.[16] The Plaza 4 Theaters, the only movie screens in Muscatine at the time, closed in 2012 after 41 years of operation.[4]

JCPenney, one of the original anchors, closed in 2014 as part of a nationwide reduction of underperforming stores.[17][18] Elder-Beerman was rebranded to Younkers following a corporate acquisition, but Younkers closed in 2018 amid the parent company's bankruptcy, leaving the mall without traditional anchors.[19][4] By the early 2020s, the interior corridors had largely emptied, with physical issues such as leaky roofs and damaged flooring reported.[3]

As of 2025, the property is owned by Muscatine Mall Management II LLC.[20] Local officials and residents have discussed redeveloping the site into a mixed-use area with housing and apartments to address community needs.[6]

### Anchors and tenants
Muscatine Mall originally featured three anchors: Woolco (later converted to Walmart in the mid-1980s), JCPenney, and Montgomery Ward (subsequently Peterson/Hartings and then Von Maur).[4] In its peak during the mid-1990s, it housed around 50 stores, including national chains such as B. Dalton, Foot Locker, Musicland, Radio Shack, and Walgreens.[1]

Following multiple departures, the mall had no traditional department store anchors by 2018.[4] Former anchor spaces have been repurposed: the ex-JCPenney became a sports center, the ex-Younkers turned into a bargain warehouse, and the northern anchor (previously Walmart and Menards) was divided for smaller retailers.[3]

As of 2025, tenants include Muscatine Warehouse Bargains, Quality Cobbler, Shoe Sensation, Slumberland Furniture, and Hibbett Sports, most operating with exterior entrances.[5] The mall has hosted small pop-up shops, dance studios, and community events, but interior foot traffic remains low.[3][5]

### Redevelopment plans
In 1999, owners proposed converting the mall into an open-air mixed-use development with condominiums, offices, and retail, but the plan did not proceed.[4] Similar revitalization efforts in 2006 also failed to reverse the decline.[4]

By 2025, community discussions focused on transforming the site into mixed-use space to include additional housing, reflecting local priorities for urban renewal.[6] Reports indicated the mall might fully close by the end of 2025, with the last remaining tenant, a pizza restaurant, planning to relocate, potentially leading to demolition.[7] No final decisions had been confirmed as of December 2025.

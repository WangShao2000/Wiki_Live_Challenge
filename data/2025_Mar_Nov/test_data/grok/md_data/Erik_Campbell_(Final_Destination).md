### Creation and development
The *Final Destination* series, produced by New Line Cinema, began in 2000 with the original film directed by James Wong. Each entry follows survivors who escape a catastrophic accident due to premonitions, only to be hunted by death in intricate, Rube Goldberg-style sequences.[16] *Final Destination: Bloodlines* introduces a familial element, tracing a curse back to 1969 when Iris Campbell prevented a tower collapse, altering the fates of her descendants.[3]

Erik Campbell was conceived by screenwriters Guy Busick, Lori Evans Taylor, and Jon Watts as part of this bloodline narrative. Busick and Taylor handled the screenplay, while Watts contributed to the story.[7] The character's non-biological status within the Campbell family—being Brenda Campbell's son from a previous relationship—serves a key plot function, exempting him from death's direct pursuit.[3] Directors Zach Lipovsky and Adam B. Stein, known for practical effects work, emphasized realistic family tensions in interviews, stating that Erik's role was designed to ground the supernatural horror in interpersonal conflicts.[17]

Richard Harmon was selected for the role following his work in television series like *The 100*, where he played a similarly edgy character.[8] Harmon prepared by researching tattoo artistry and incorporating physical elements, such as a nose chain, to reflect Erik's rebellious persona.[9] Production details indicate the film had a $50 million budget, with filming occurring in Vancouver, Canada, to leverage tax incentives and mimic American settings.[18]

### Characterization
Erik is portrayed as a sharp-witted, sarcastic individual with a guarded past, often described in promotional materials as bold and protective yet humorous.[19] As a tattoo artist, he embodies a carefree lifestyle, contrasting with the anxiety-ridden protagonist Stefani Reyes.[10] His relationships within the family are strained; he is the eldest sibling to Julia and Bobby Campbell, and cousin to Stefani and Charlie Reyes.[3] Sources note that Erik's dismissal of the family's warnings about death stems from his outsider status, adding layers to themes of belonging and fate.[2]

Fan analyses on platforms like TikTok and Tumblr emphasize Erik's rebellious traits, with users creating edits that highlight his wit and physical appearance.[20][21] However, some discussions critique the character as stereotypical, pointing to his "bad boy" archetype as less innovative compared to earlier franchise survivors.[22] Harmon's performance incorporates physical comedy and tension, particularly in scenes involving medical equipment, which align with the series' gore elements.[23]

### Appearances
Erik appears solely in *Final Destination: Bloodlines*. The film follows Stefani Reyes, who returns home after nightmares reveal a threat to her family tied to Iris Campbell's 1969 premonition.[10] Erik initially rejects Stefani's claims, prioritizing his independent life, but becomes entangled in the events as deaths occur.[3]

The Campbell family tree, as detailed in analyses, positions Erik as Brenda's son outside the biological curse affecting descendants of Iris and Paul Campbell.[3] This includes his uncle Howard, aunt Darlene, and cousins. Erik's scenes often involve confrontations that underscore skepticism, culminating in sequences that test family bonds.[24]

### Reception
*Final Destination: Bloodlines* received positive reviews, with a 92% Tomatometer score and 86% audience score on Rotten Tomatoes.[13] The critic consensus highlights the film's emotional depth and inventive kills, marking a return to form for the franchise.[13] Harmon's portrayal of Erik has been mentioned in several reviews; for instance, one critic noted his "sardonic edge provides comic relief amid the carnage," while another described the character as "a relatable skeptic in a sea of believers."[25][26]

Social media engagement shows Erik as a fan favorite, with posts comparing him to characters from other horror films and polls pitting him against series icons like Alex Browning.[27][28] Fan fiction and merchandise, including prints and edits, indicate popularity, though some users express divided views on his depth.[29][30] Box office success, with $138 million in the U.S. and Canada, suggests the ensemble, including Erik, contributed to broad appeal.[15]

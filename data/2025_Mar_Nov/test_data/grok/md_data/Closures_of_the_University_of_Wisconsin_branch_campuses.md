### Background
The Universities of Wisconsin system, formerly known as the University of Wisconsin System, oversees 13 four-year universities and, until recent closures, a network of branch campuses focused on associate degrees and transfer programs.[3] In 2018, the system underwent a major restructuring approved by the Higher Learning Commission, merging 13 independent two-year colleges—previously operated under the UW Colleges umbrella—into branch campuses affiliated with seven four-year institutions.[1][44] This change, effective July 1, 2018, aimed to improve administrative efficiency, facilitate student transfers, and address early signs of enrollment decline by integrating the branches with larger universities such as UW-Milwaukee, UW-Oshkosh, and UW-Platteville.[45][46]

The original 13 branch campuses were: UW-Eau Claire–Barron County, UW-Green Bay–Manitowoc County, UW-Green Bay–Marinette County, UW-Green Bay–Sheboygan County, UW-Milwaukee–Washington County, UW-Milwaukee–Waukesha, UW-Oshkosh–Fond du Lac, UW-Oshkosh–Fox Cities, UW-Platteville–Baraboo Sauk County, UW-Platteville–Richland, UW-Stevens Point–Marshfield, UW-Stevens Point–Wausau, and UW-Whitewater–Rock County.[12][38] Ownership of branch campus facilities typically rests with local counties, which partner with the state system to provide land and buildings, while the Universities of Wisconsin handles operations, faculty, and programming.[65]

Prior to the 2018 merger, the two-year colleges had already experienced funding pressures from a 2013 state tuition freeze and budget reductions, which limited resources for recruitment and program expansion.[43][59]

### Timeline of Closures
Closures began in 2023 amid ongoing system-wide financial difficulties. Universities of Wisconsin President Jay Rothman directed the initial wave, citing unsustainable operations.[8] By December 2025, six branch campuses had fully closed or ended in-person instruction, with buildings repurposed or slated for redevelopment. A seventh closure was announced in October 2025.

These actions reduced the number of active branch campuses from 13 to seven by late 2025, with the remaining ones including UW-Eau Claire–Barron County, UW-Green Bay–Manitowoc County, UW-Green Bay–Sheboygan County, UW-Stevens Point–Marshfield, UW-Stevens Point–Wausau, UW-Whitewater–Rock County, and others not yet targeted.[3][38]

### Reasons
The primary factors driving the closures include sharp enrollment declines, financial deficits, and demographic shifts. System-wide enrollment at branch campuses has fallen steadily since the 2010s, exacerbated by the 2013 tuition freeze and funding cuts that reduced state support by millions.[43][54][59] For instance, many campuses saw student numbers drop below 300, making operations unviable.[54][60] Competition from Wisconsin's technical colleges, which offer similar associate degrees at lower costs, has also drawn students away.[63][65]

Additional contributors include administrative fees imposed by parent universities, limited recruitment efforts, and a broader shift toward online education.[43] A 2024 internal briefing recommended downsizing remaining branches due to low demand for associate degrees and ongoing budget challenges.[47][58] System leaders have emphasized adapting to fewer high school graduates in Wisconsin and changing workforce needs, with some campuses pivoting to dual-enrollment for high school students or corporate training.[43][65] In 2024, six universities, including UW-Oshkosh, reported structural deficits, further pressuring the system.[62]

### Impacts and Responses
The closures have had significant effects on local communities, students, and economies. Many affected areas are rural or underserved, where branch campuses provided affordable, nearby education for first-generation students, working adults, and high schoolers.[6][11][43] Job losses have totaled hundreds, including faculty and staff, with some tenured professors laid off rather than transferred.[24][63] Economic ripple effects include reduced local spending and vacant facilities burdening county budgets, with annual maintenance costs reaching $800,000 per site in some cases.[65]

Counties have responded by repurposing buildings: Fond du Lac County converted its campus into an events center and law enforcement training facility; Washington County is partnering with school districts for K-12 programs; Waukesha County plans residential development; and others explore similar adaptive uses.[65] A 2024 state law allocated up to $20 million in grants for redevelopment, though distribution was ongoing as of December 2025.[65]

Public responses have been mixed. Community members and alumni have expressed frustration over lost opportunities and the erosion of the "Wisconsin Idea," which emphasizes statewide access to education.[4][6][11][43] A 2025 documentary highlighted the cultural and historical value of two-year campuses.[13][61][8] Legislative committees convened in 2024 to discuss enrollment woes, with experts debating further consolidations.[4][56] System officials have defended the moves as necessary for sustainability, offering online alternatives and transfer options to mitigate disruptions.[3][6][43] Calls to halt closures have grown, with editorials urging investment in branches to preserve equity.[4]

As of December 2025, no additional closures were announced, but ongoing monitoring of enrollment and finances suggested potential future changes.[1][57] The remaining campuses continue to operate, with some showing modest enrollment stability.[15][38]

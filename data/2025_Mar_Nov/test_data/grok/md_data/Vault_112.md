### Background
Vault-Tec Corporation, a pre-war contractor for the United States government, built a network of 122 public vaults across the country, each with a stated goal of preserving human life during nuclear armageddon. In reality, many incorporated social or scientific experiments to gather data for Vault-Tec's Societal Preservation Program. Vault 112 fits this pattern, with construction documented as starting in November 2068 and completing in June 2074, making it one of the later vaults finished before the Great War on October 23, 2077.[1][2] It was sealed around 2075, based on in-game robotic dialogue noting arrivals as approximately 202 years overdue in the year 2277.[1][2] 

The facility was designed with a capacity for 85 occupants, though accounts show only a fraction in use.[1][2][3] Its primary function was to house and support Dr. Stanislaus Braun, who directed the experiment. Braun, a German-born inventor known for the G.E.C.K.—a device capable of terraforming irradiated landscapes—chose Vault 112 as his personal retreat. Equipped with advanced virtual reality systems, the vault allowed for indefinite suspension of residents in simulated worlds, isolated from the external wasteland.[3] Geothermal power from brands like Sure Power ensured self-sufficiency, while a Think Machine 3600r mainframe managed both vault operations and simulation controls.[1][2] Unlike typical vaults focused on population survival or behavioral studies, Vault 112 emphasized individual psychological endurance in virtual settings.

### Location and Layout
Vault 112 is situated in the southwestern portion of the Capital Wasteland, the game's setting encompassing the ruins of Washington, D.C., and surrounding areas. It is hidden beneath Smith Casey's Garage, a pre-war automotive repair shop located west of Evergreen Mills and south of a structure known as the Charnel House.[1][2][4] Surface access involves entering the garage, which may contain low-level threats such as radroaches (mutated cockroaches) and mole rats (burrowing rodents adapted to radiation).[1][2] A switch inside the garage reveals a descending staircase leading to a hatch and passageway, culminating at the vault's blast door.

The interior layout is compact compared to other vaults, reflecting its specialized design. The entrance airlock connects to a main hall featuring 12 Tranquility Loungers—stasis pods arranged around a central pillar, each wired to terminals monitoring vital signs and stress levels.[1][2] Ten of these are occupied by residents, one is damaged, and two are available for temporary use.[1][2] South of the hall lies the overseer's office, containing an additional lounger for Braun.[1][2] An eastern medical unit provides basic facilities, while the geothermal turbine level remains inaccessible.[1][2] The vault is maintained by 12 non-hostile Robobrains—humanoid robots with organic brains, lacking combat inhibitors but programmed for caretaking.[1][2] No elevators or multi-level living quarters are present, emphasizing functionality over communal space. Storage includes lockers with items like Vault 112 jumpsuits, which residents must wear for pod entry, though exceptions occur in gameplay.[1][2]

### Experiment
The vault's experiment centered on virtual reality suspension, where occupants entered stasis pods linked to simulations via Visiontron technology.[1][2][3] Initially, Braun created utopian environments to provide escape from post-war reality, with residents unaware of the external world.[3] However, Braun's isolation led to boredom, prompting him to alter simulations for entertainment. He repeatedly killed virtual avatars, erased memories, and resurrected participants, turning the experiment into a cycle of torment.[3] Terminal entries detail failed scenarios, such as "Toucan Lagoon," where residents were devoured by sharks, and "Slalom Chalet," involving fatal accidents in a snowy landscape.[3] The final simulation, Tranquility Lane, depicted a 1950s-style American suburb inspired by Braun's hometown of Kronach, Germany.[3] Within it, Braun assumed the form of a young girl named Betty, assigning disturbing tasks to inhabitants.[3]

Sources indicate the experiment's success depends on perspective: it achieved indefinite suspension but failed as a haven due to Braun's actions.[1][2] One resident, referred to as Old Lady Dithers, detected anomalies like vital sign irregularities, suggesting partial awareness among subjects.[3] The setup's vulnerabilities include an internal failsafe—a puzzle involving a musical sequence in an abandoned virtual house—and external options like disrupting geothermal power.[3] These features made the system susceptible to outside interference, as noted in analyses of the vault's lore.[3]

### Role in Fallout 3
In *Fallout 3*, Vault 112 is integral to the main questline. The player character, known as the Lone Wanderer, arrives seeking their father, James, who entered the vault during "Scientific Pursuits."[1][2] Upon entry, a Robobrain provides a jumpsuit and directs the player to a lounger, initiating the Tranquility Lane quest.[1][2] Within the simulation, the player must navigate Braun's directives or activate the failsafe, which ends the program but leaves Braun trapped.[1][2][3] This sequence affects player morality, with compliant actions reducing karma.[3] Discussions among players highlight the vault as a potential point for sequence-breaking, allowing skips in earlier quests by direct access.[5] Post-quest, the vault remains explorable, with residents freed or deceased depending on choices.[1][2]

### Appearances
Vault 112 appears in *Fallout 3* as a visitable location.[1][2] It also features in *Fallout Shelter Online* (2019), a mobile spin-off, though in a limited capacity.[1] No other mainline entries in the series include it.

### Development
The vault's layout was designed by level artist Jesse Tucker at Bethesda Game Studios.[2] In-game terminals, such as those in the Citadel (a Brotherhood of Steel base), provide construction details and Vault-Tec listings.[1] References to a stolen memory chip from the vault appear in Rivet City logs, tying into side quests involving androids.[1][2]

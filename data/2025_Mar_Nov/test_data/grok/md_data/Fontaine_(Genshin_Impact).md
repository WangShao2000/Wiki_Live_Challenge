### Development
Fontaine's development began prior to the game's 2020 launch as part of a multi-year plan to expand Teyvat's seven nations.[5] The update introduced underwater exploration, a feature conceptualized two years earlier to differentiate it from land-based regions, requiring substantial resources for mechanics like energy-based puzzles and creature abilities.[5] Designs for local wildlife, such as armored crabs, blended real-world biology with fantasy elements.[5] Player feedback from earlier regions like Sumeru influenced puzzle placement to avoid excessive difficulty, focusing instead on quality and accessibility.[5] The region expanded content volume compared to Sumeru while emphasizing polished details.[5]

### Geography
Fontaine occupies a raised continental plate in central Teyvat, northeast of Sumeru's desert, characterized by a massive edge waterfall and a terrestrial sea.[1][2] Key subregions include the Court of Fontaine (the central urban area with aquabus transport), Beryl Region (home to Melusine inhabitants), Erinnyes Forest (northern wooded area added in version 4.2), Liffey Region (site of the underwater Fortress of Meropide prison), Morte Region (quest-related northern expanse), Nostoi Region (southern historical ruins from version 4.6), and the Sea of Bygone Eras (submerged southern waters).[4][6] Underwater areas, unlocked via a blessing mechanic, feature no stamina consumption for swimming and include puzzles involving Arkhe energies (Ousia and Pneuma).[1][6] Notable locations encompass the Opera Epiclese (a combined theater and courthouse), Fountain of Lucine (a reward hub), and the Fontaine Research Institute ruins, affected by a past explosion.[1][4][6]

### Reception
Initial reception to Fontaine's reveal in version 3.8 was positive, with community artwork and feedback influencing exhibitions of concept art.[5] Players praised innovations like non-hostile underwater creatures and streamlined quests, though some critiqued map density and device limitations.[5][6] The region's story arc received acclaim for character depth and narrative twists, positioning it as a high point in the game's writing, according to multiple analyses.[3][5] However, debates persist on whether its technological themes fully integrate with Teyvat's lore, with mixed views on exploration rewards compared to prior updates.[5][6]

Fontaine is a fictional region in the action role-playing game *Genshin Impact*, developed and published by miHoYo (operating as Hoyoverse since 2022). As one of Teyvat's seven nations, it is aligned with the Hydro element and governed under principles of justice, though its leadership shifted following in-game events involving the Hydro Archon. Released in version 4.0 on August 16, 2023, Fontaine incorporates underwater exploration and technology-driven mechanics, drawing cultural inspirations from industrial-era Europe, particularly France and England. The region's narrative centers on a prophecy of societal dissolution, legal systems, and the interplay between divine authority and human agency, contributing to the game's broader themes of fate, elemental powers, and celestial intervention. Its introduction marked a significant expansion, adding new characters, quests, and gameplay features that built on player feedback from prior regions.

### Development and release
Planning for Fontaine occurred as part of a long-term roadmap to introduce all seven nations of Teyvat over several years, with initial concepts predating the game's September 2020 launch.[5][7] The development team focused on differentiating Fontaine through underwater mechanics, a feature discussed internally two years before its release, to provide novel experiences distinct from surface-based exploration in regions like Sumeru or Inazuma.[5] This required extensive resource allocation for systems such as stamina-free diving, underwater combat abilities derived from local creatures (e.g., armored crabs and ball octopuses), and energy puzzles using Arkhe forces (Ousia and Pneuma).[1][5] Creature designs blended real-world inspirations, such as hermit crabs, with fantastical elements to fit the Hydro theme.[5]

Puzzle and challenge designs incorporated lessons from Sumeru feedback, avoiding heightened difficulty by placing intensive mechanics in optional areas like underwater challenges or "Local Legends" bosses, while ensuring core progression remained accessible.[5] The region expanded overall content volume, including maps, stories, and rewards, but prioritized quality over quantity, with treasure chests offering higher average value and no major locks behind quest progression.[5] Hardware constraints, including device controls, storage, and power consumption, influenced scope limitations more than creative ideas.[5]

Fontaine debuted in the update titled "As Light Rain Falls Without Reason" on August 16, 2023, introducing three initial playable characters—Lyney, Lynette, and Freminet—whose sibling dynamics tied into the Archon Quest narrative.[5][6] Subsequent patches through version 4.6 (up to May 2024) added subregions like Erinnyes Forest, Morte Region, Nostoi Region, and the Sea of Bygone Eras, along with additional characters and quests.[4][6] Community response to early reveals in version 3.8 included fan art exhibitions alongside developer sketches, reflecting positive engagement.[5]

Fontaine is situated in central Teyvat on an elevated continental plate, serving as the origin of all continental waters, with a vast terrestrial sea and a dramatic waterfall cascading thousands of meters at its edges.[1][2] It lies northeast of Sumeru's Hills of Barsom, accessible initially via Waverider boats to Romaritime Harbor, followed by aquabus lines for urban travel.[4][6] The region's geography blends surface landscapes with extensive underwater areas, unlocked through a "Transoceanic Sourcewater" blessing that enables diving without stamina depletion and special underwater attacks.[1][6]

Major subregions include:
- **Court of Fontaine Region**: The central hub, featuring the urban Court of Fontaine with subareas like Annapausis, Chemin de L'Espoir, Fountain of Lucine (a Hydro Sigil reward exchange), Institute of Natural Philosophy, Marcotte Station, Opera Epiclese (a dual-purpose theater and courthouse), Salacia Plain, and Thalatta Submarine Canyon.[1][4]
- **Beryl Region**: Primarily residential for Melusine inhabitants, emphasizing aquatic and forested environments.[4]
- **Erinnyes Forest and Morte Region**: Northern expansions from version 4.2, tied to world quests such as "The Wild Fairy of Erinnyes" and "Search in Algae Sea," with dense woods and quest-specific ruins.[4][6]
- **Liffey Region**: Hosts the underwater Fortress of Meropide prison and the ruins of the old Fontaine Research Institute, marked by "Allogravity-Condensed Water Bodies" from a failed experiment.[1][4][6]
- **Fontaine Research Institute of Kinetic Energy Engineering Region**: Features damaged facilities and mechanical puzzles.[1][4]
- **Nostoi Region and Sea of Bygone Eras**: Southern additions in version 4.6, including the historical town of Petrichor, remnants of the Kingdom of Remuria, submerged caves, ruins, and a palace, accessible via "Canticles of Harmony" quests.[4][6]

Unique features encompass aquabus transportation, musical "Symphony" puzzles in Nostoi, and energy storage devices for powering machines.[1] Local specialties include flora like Rainbow Rose, Lumidouce Bell, Romaritime Flower, Lakelight Lily, and Spring of the First Dewdrop, as well as marine items such as Beryl Conch, Lumitoile, Subdetection Unit, and cooking materials like Marcotte, Tidalga, Pluie Lotus, and Bulle Fruit.[6]

### History
Fontaine's in-universe history spans eras tied to elemental forces, archons, and prophecies, drawn from game lore.[3][7]

In the pre-Egeria period, the Primordial Sea covered Teyvat, ruled by the original Hydro Dragon Sovereign until defeated by the Heavenly Principles, who stole its authority.[7] Humanity's defiance led to floods punishing civilizations.[7]

During the Egeria era, Egeria was created as the Primordial Sea's new heart and became the first Hydro Archon after the Archon War.[7] She used Primordial Seawater to form humanoid life from Oceanids, an act deemed a "sin" by Celestia, resulting in a prophecy: rising waters would dissolve Fontaine's people to absolve their innate sins, leaving the Archon weeping alone.[3][7] Egeria mediated post-Remuria survivors, establishing cities around springs and a law-based society.[7] She exiled criminals to guard the Primordial Sea's entrance, forming the Fortress of Meropide.[7]

Remus, a figure from the High Seas, founded the prosperous Remuria using a golden ship and ichor-based golems, but moral decay and a rebellion led to its sinking.[7] Celestia then released Egeria to rule.[7]

Post-Cataclysm (500 years ago), during Khaenri'ah's fall, Egeria died battling Abyss forces; her successor, Focalors, inherited the role.[3][7] Oceanids exiled themselves due to ideological clashes.[1][7] Focalors divided herself, creating a human counterpart (Furina) to deceive Celestia and avert the prophecy, accumulating energy via trials in the Oratrice Mecanique d'Analyse Cardinale.[3][7] Experiments at the Narzissenkreuz Institute and encounters with beasts like Elynas shaped society, birthing Melusines from Elynas' remains.[7]

In recent events (game versions 4.0–4.2), the prophecy unfolds with floods, but Focalors' execution transfers authority to Neuvillette (the reborn Hydro Dragon Sovereign), converting Primordial Seawater to allow survival and nullifying dissolution.[3][7] Fontaine becomes the first nation ruled by a Dragon Sovereign, without an Archon.[1][3]

### Government and politics
Fontaine operates under a justice-focused system, with trials conducted at the Opera Epiclese, where Chief Justice Neuvillette issues verdicts aligned with the Oratrice, a machine harvesting public belief into Indemnitium energy.[1][3][6] Formerly ruled by Hydro Archon Focalors (publicly as Furina), the nation shifted post-prophecy fulfillment, with Neuvillette assuming duties after Focalors' self-execution destroyed the divine throne.[1][3][7] This made Fontaine unique among Teyvat nations as archon-less, governed by a Dragon Sovereign.[1]

The legal framework is complex, as noted by external characters like Yanfei.[1] Institutions include the Marechaussee Phantom (anti-threat force) and the Fortress of Meropide (prison for exiles).[1][7] Political tensions involve human-Melusine integration, with past incidents like frame-ups leading to societal reforms.[7] A "judgment" belief persists, tied to the dissolved-sins prophecy.[1]

### Culture and society
Fontaine emphasizes arts, particularly performing arts like music, theater, and opera, with the Opera Epiclese as a cultural centerpiece.[1][6] Society values "true beauty and elegance," with diverse inhabitants including humans, Melusines (small, colorful beings from Elynas' blood), and formerly Oceanids (water spirits loyal to Egeria).[1][7] Diving is a key profession, requiring certificates, and newspapers like *The Steambird* (contributed to by figures like Mona) cover local events.[1]

Cultural elements include rock 'n' roll origins, popular novels like Xingqiu's *A Legend of Sword*, and inventions like the Kamera.[1] Fashion and cuisine reflect industrial influences, though specifics remain tied to gameplay items.[1][6] Society grapples with technological decline in Arkhe energy and environmental issues like toxic fumes in some areas.[1] Beliefs center on the prophecy and justice, with events like the Two Musketeers Incident highlighting moral complexities.[7]

### Economy and technology
Fontaine's economy revolves around advanced technology integrated into daily life, such as aquabus systems and mechanical devices.[1][6] The Fontaine Research Institute supports kinetic energy research, though damaged by experiments like Edwin Eastinghouse's explosion.[1][7] Inventions include the Wisdom Orb, Evermotion Mechanical Painting, and toy apparatuses.[1] Energy from Indemnitium powers the nation, derived from trials.[3] Trade involves local specialties and marine resources, with professions like diving contributing to resource gathering.[1][6]

## Background

The 2013 NFL season was the 94th in league history. The Packers entered the game with a 6–6–1 record, placing them third in the NFC North division.[1][2] The team had started the year 5–2 but struggled after quarterback Aaron Rodgers suffered a fractured collarbone in Week 9 against the Chicago Bears, missing the next seven games.[3] Backup quarterbacks Seneca Wallace, Scott Tolzien, and Matt Flynn alternated starts during Rodgers' absence, with Flynn taking over for this matchup.[4] The Packers ranked eighth in the league in points scored (417 total, averaging 26.1 per game) but 25th in points allowed (428 total, averaging 26.8 per game).[1] Running back Eddie Lacy, a rookie, provided a consistent rushing attack, finishing the season with 1,178 yards.[1]

The Cowboys entered with a 7–6 record, tied for first in the NFC East.[5][6] The team featured a high-scoring offense, ranking fifth in points (439 total, averaging 27.4 per game), led by quarterback Tony Romo, wide receiver Dez Bryant, and tight end Jason Witten.[5] However, their defense ranked 26th in points allowed (432 total, averaging 27.0 per game), contributing to several close losses.[5][7] Running back DeMarco Murray had a productive year with 1,121 rushing yards.[5] Both teams were in playoff contention entering Week 15, with the Packers needing a win to stay alive in the division race and the Cowboys aiming to maintain their lead over the Philadelphia Eagles.[2][6]

The game was part of the longstanding rivalry between the Packers and Cowboys, which dates to 1960 and includes notable postseason encounters such as the 1967 NFL Championship Game, known as the Ice Bowl.[8] It was broadcast nationally on Fox, with kickoff at 3:25 p.m. CST.[9] Officials included referee Walt Coleman as the head official.[9] Attendance was 91,054, and the game lasted 3 hours and 21 minutes.[9]

## Game summary

### First quarter

The Cowboys received the opening kickoff and drove 47 yards in eight plays, culminating in a 47-yard field goal by Dan Bailey to take a 3–0 lead.[9][10] The Packers responded with a 57-yard field goal by Mason Crosby on their first possession, tying the score at 3–3.[9][10] Dallas then advanced 75 yards in nine plays, but stalled in the red zone and settled for a 23-yard Bailey field goal, making it 6–3.[9][10] Late in the quarter, the Cowboys extended their lead when Romo threw a 25-yard touchdown pass to Witten, following a 37-yard completion to Bryant, for a 13–3 advantage.[9][10]

### Second quarter

Dallas continued to dominate, adding field goals of 43 and 50 yards by Bailey on their next two drives, pushing the score to 19–3.[9][10] With under a minute remaining, the Cowboys capped a 75-yard drive with a 1-yard rushing touchdown by Murray, extending the lead to 26–3 at halftime.[9][10] The Packers managed only 84 yards of offense in the first half, while the Cowboys amassed 332.[11]

### Third quarter

The Packers opened the second half with a 75-yard drive, aided by Lacy's 60-yard run, and scored on a 13-yard touchdown pass from Flynn to Jordy Nelson, narrowing the gap to 26–10.[9][10] Dallas responded with a 50-yard field goal by Bailey, their fifth of the game, to lead 29–10.[9][10] Green Bay then drove 80 yards in 10 plays, ending with a 3-yard touchdown pass from Flynn to tight end Andrew Quarless, making it 29–17.[9][10]

### Fourth quarter

Early in the final period, the Packers scored again on an 11-yard touchdown pass from Flynn to running back James Starks, cutting the deficit to 29–24.[9][10] The Cowboys answered with an 80-yard drive, finished by a 5-yard touchdown pass from Romo to Bryant, restoring a 36–24 lead.[9][10] Green Bay mounted an 80-yard response, with Flynn connecting with wide receiver James Jones for a 3-yard touchdown, bringing the score to 36–31.[9][10] On the ensuing Cowboys possession, Romo threw an interception to Packers cornerback Tramon Williams at the Dallas 33-yard line.[10][12] Green Bay capitalized with a 1-yard rushing touchdown by Lacy, taking a 37–36 lead after a failed two-point conversion attempt.[9][10] With 1:24 remaining, Romo threw another interception, this time to cornerback Sam Shields at midfield, allowing the Packers to run out the clock and secure the victory.[9][10][12]

## Aftermath

The victory improved the Packers' record to 7–6–1, keeping them in the NFC North race.[1][2] Rodgers returned the following week against the Pittsburgh Steelers, and the team won their final game against the Bears to finish 8–7–1 and claim the division title.[1] They hosted the San Francisco 49ers in the Wild Card round but lost 23–20.[1][2]

The loss dropped the Cowboys to 7–7, tying them with the Eagles atop the NFC East.[5][6] They won their next game against the Washington Redskins but fell to the Eagles in the finale, finishing 8–8 and missing the playoffs for the fourth consecutive year.[5] The game drew criticism toward the Cowboys' coaching staff and Romo for late decisions to pass instead of run, despite Murray's 7.4 yards per carry average.[13][14][15] Offensive coordinator Bill Callahan faced particular scrutiny for not emphasizing the ground game in the second half, when Murray received only seven carries for 40 yards.[13] Romo's interceptions were attributed by some sources to poor decisions under pressure, adding to discussions about his performance in close games.[15][16][17] Cowboys head coach Jason Garrett later stated that play calls were a collaborative effort, while Romo acknowledged audible changes on the field.[16]

The game is remembered in NFL history for its comeback element and its impact on both teams' seasons.[11][12][18]

Evidence from reviews suggests it resonates more with long-time fans familiar with classic episodes, but may challenge newer audiences.

Wish World is the seventh episode of the second season of the British science fiction television series Doctor Who. Written by showrunner Russell T Davies and directed by Alex Pillai, it was first broadcast on BBC One on 24 May 2025. The episode stars Ncuti Gatwa as the Fifteenth Doctor, with Varada Sethu as companion Belinda Chandra. It features the return of the Rani, a Time Lord villain originally introduced in the 1985 serial The Mark of the Rani, portrayed here by Archie Panjabi. The narrative explores themes of illusion, doubt, and authoritarian control in a dystopian alternate Earth, serving as setup for the season finale. Reception was mixed, with praise for visual effects and performances offset by criticisms of pacing and lore complexity.

## Plot
The episode opens with the Doctor (Ncuti Gatwa), under the alias John Smith, living a domestic life married to Belinda Chandra (Varada Sethu) in what appears to be a perfect suburban world. This setup echoes elements from earlier Doctor Who stories, such as the Doctor's restlessness in mundane settings seen in "The Power of Three" (2012). Anomalies begin to appear, such as shattering mugs symbolizing emerging doubts, revealing the world as an illusion crafted by the Rani (Archie Panjabi).

Conrad Clark (Jonah Hauer-King) maintains the facade through a children's bedtime story program on television, nurturing Desidirium, referred to as the God of Wishes—a baby entity that grants wishes but requires the illusion's stability to survive. This dystopian society enforces rigid conformity, erasing or rendering invisible those who doubt or deviate, including people with disabilities, the LGBTQ+ community, and political dissenters. Ruby Sunday (Millie Gibson) navigates an underworld of these outcasts, accompanied by characters like Shirley Bingham (Ruth Madeley).

As doubts proliferate, the illusion fractures, manifesting giant bone creatures and reality collapses. The Doctor shifts from passive suburbanite to persecuted figure, uncovering the Rani's plan to revive Omega, an ancient Time Lord, using the wish power. Mrs Flood (Anita Dobson), possibly an aspect of the Rani, interacts with Conrad, showing internal conflict. Old allies like Mel Bush (Bonnie Langford) and Kate Lethbridge-Stewart (Jemma Redgrave) reunite, while cameos include Rogue (Jonathan Groff) and references to prior Doctors. The episode ends on a cliffhanger with the world on the brink of destruction before midnight.

## Production
"Wish World" was written by Russell T Davies, who also served as executive producer alongside Julie Gardner, Jane Tranter, Joel Collins, and Phil Collinson. The director was Alex Pillai, with Chris May as producer. Production incorporated redressed sets from the previous season finale, including UNIT headquarters, to depict the dystopian environment. Visual effects emphasized bio-horror elements, such as transformations into petals or trees, drawing from the Rani's classic tactics in "The Mark of the Rani" (1985).

The script integrated season-long arcs, including the Pantheon of gods, the mystery of Susan Twist's recurring character, and hints at the Timeless Child storyline. References to classic series elements include the Doctor's John Smith alias from "The Wheel in Space" (1968), Omega from "The Three Doctors" (1973), and the Gallifreyan Seal from "Revenge of the Cybermen" (1975). Some aspects, such as Belinda's role, may have evolved from an original focus on Ruby Sunday due to production adjustments.

## Broadcast and Reception
The episode premiered on BBC One on 24 May 2025 at 18:50 BST (19:25 in Scotland), with a duration of 44 minutes. It was simultaneously released on Disney+ internationally. Repeat broadcasts occurred on BBC Two on 30 May 2025. Accessibility options included signed and audio-described versions.

Reception was mixed. On IMDb, it holds a rating of 7.0 based on user reviews, with some describing it as a return to form through cosmic horror and strong storytelling, while others found it convoluted and overly reliant on exposition. The Guardian's recap noted its chilling dystopian elements and parallels to contemporary issues like authoritarianism, praising Panjabi's performance but expressing bafflement at the density of classic references in a supposed reboot. Blogtor Who viewed it positively as a setup for an epic conclusion, highlighting visual flair and social commentary on conservative values, though acknowledging unresolved questions and exposition issues.

Eruditorum Press offered a negative assessment, calling it "fucking rubbish" and a "slow ride to nowhere" due to structural flaws and lack of stakes, despite thematic potential in critiquing fascism's incoherence. Fan discussions on Reddit reflected similar divides: praises for Panjabi's Rani, bio-horror visuals, and thematic depth on doubt and conformity; criticisms for slow pacing, lore overload, and underutilized characters like Belinda. Theories abounded about plot elements, such as Poppy's identity tying into the Timeless Child or Susan Foreman, and the Rani's ultimate goals involving Gallifrey's reconstruction.

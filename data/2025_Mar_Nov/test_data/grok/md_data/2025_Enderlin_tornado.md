### 2025 Enderlin tornado

The 2025 Enderlin tornado was an intense, multiple-vortex tornado that struck rural areas near Enderlin in southeastern North Dakota during the late evening of June 20, 2025. Rated EF5 on the Enhanced Fujita scale with estimated peak wind speeds exceeding 210 miles per hour (340 km/h), it was the first tornado in the United States to receive this rating since the 2013 Moore tornado in Oklahoma. The tornado caused three fatalities, destroyed multiple farm structures, derailed dozens of train cars, and inflicted severe damage to vegetation and cropland along a 12.1-mile-long (19.5 km) path. Initially rated EF3 by the National Weather Service, it was upgraded to EF5 on October 6, 2025, following additional surveys and physics-based analysis of damage indicators.  

The event occurred amid a broader severe weather outbreak from June 19 to 22, 2025, which produced 41 confirmed tornadoes across the Northern Plains, driven by supercell thunderstorms ahead of an advancing derecho. The Enderlin tornado's rapid intensification, evidenced by radar data showing a doubling of rotational velocity in under six minutes, highlighted the storm's explosive development. While the tornado's rural path limited the number of affected structures, the severity of observed damage—such as the lofting of heavy rail equipment and cycloidal ground scouring—supported the high rating. Recovery efforts included federal disaster assistance, with North Dakota Governor Kelly Armstrong securing FEMA funding to aid rebuilding in impacted communities.  

#### Meteorological history
On June 20, 2025, a shortwave trough and associated upper-level jet stream contributed to unstable atmospheric conditions across the Northern Plains. Supercell thunderstorms developed in southeast North Dakota during the evening hours, fueled by high convective available potential energy (CAPE) and strong deep-layer shear. These storms produced several tornadoes, including an EF2 in Enderlin itself and others rated up to EF3 nearby.  

The Enderlin tornado touched down at 11:02 p.m. CDT (04:02 UTC June 21) approximately 3 miles (4.8 km) southwest of Enderlin in Ransom County. Radar from the NWS Grand Forks office indicated initial rotational velocity (VROT) of 53 knots, which surged to 106 knots by 11:08 p.m., signaling rapid strengthening. The tornado moved east-northeast at around 35 mph (56 km/h), expanding to a maximum width of 1 mile (1.6 km) as it crossed farmland and the Maple River. It exhibited multiple vortices, with cycloidal marks in fields confirming intense ground-level winds.  

The storm's mesocyclone, visible on radar and captured by chasers, persisted as the tornado lifted near Alice at 11:21 p.m. CDT. Following the tornadoes, a derecho with gusts over 100 mph (160 km/h) traversed the state, exacerbating damage from downed trees and power lines. A mesoscale convective vortex prolonged high winds for several hours afterward.  

#### Preparations and warnings
The Storm Prediction Center issued a moderate risk outlook for severe weather on June 20, highlighting the potential for strong tornadoes in southeast North Dakota. Tornado watches were in effect by early evening, with warnings issued as radar detected rotation. Local NWS offices, spotters, and storm chasers provided real-time reports, aiding in timely alerts. Despite the nighttime occurrence, which reduced visibility, warnings reached rural residents via emergency broadcasts and mobile alerts.  

#### Impact
The tornado's path primarily affected agricultural areas, resulting in catastrophic damage to farms and infrastructure. A key damage indicator was the derailment of 33 train cars on a Burlington Northern Santa Fe line south of Enderlin, including loaded grain hoppers weighing up to 286,000 pounds (130 metric tons) rolled or thrown up to 475 feet (145 m). Physics-based models estimated winds of 230 mph (370 km/h) for hopper displacement and up to 266 mph (428 km/h) for a lighter tanker car.  

Farmsteads were completely scoured, with foundations swept clean and debris scattered over miles. Vegetation damage included debarking of trees along the Maple River, with a "sandpapering" effect on bark, and ground scouring that tilled corn fields 12–18 inches (30–46 cm) deep across a 1-mile swath. Three people died in collapsed structures, though the rural setting limited broader casualties. Economic losses included destroyed crops, equipment, and rail disruptions.

#### Aftermath
North Dakota Governor Kelly Armstrong declared a statewide disaster on June 21, 2025, citing the storm's widespread impact. In September, President Joseph R. Biden Jr. approved federal disaster assistance through FEMA, providing funds for infrastructure repairs and individual aid. Over 150 volunteers assisted in debris removal from fields, using drones to locate scattered items. The Enderlin Area Tornado Relief Fund, managed by BankNorth, supported rebuilding, though challenges arose from inadequate insurance coverage. Mental health services were offered to survivors.  

The EF5 upgrade prompted discussions on refining the Enhanced Fujita scale for non-structural indicators, with comparisons to international tornado ratings. Storm chasers' footage and analyses, including cycloidal coefficient methods, corroborated the intensity.

## Plot

Todd, a man facing personal challenges, inherits a remote house in rural New Jersey from his late grandfather and moves there with his dog, Indy. From Indy's viewpoint, the house harbors unsettling supernatural elements, including shadows, apparitions, and an invisible malevolent force that Todd cannot perceive. As strange occurrences escalate, Indy responds with instinctual behaviors in an effort to safeguard his owner.[1][2]

## Cast

* Indy as Indy, the Nova Scotia Duck Tolling Retriever who serves as the protagonist.
* Shane Jensen as Todd, Indy's owner.
* Larry Fessenden as Grandpa, Todd's deceased grandfather.
* Arielle Friedman as Vera.
* Stuart Rudin in a supporting role.
* Anya Krawcheck in a supporting role.[1][3]

## Production

### Development

Ben Leonberg conceived the idea for Good Boy while observing his own dog, Indy, staring at empty corners, prompting him to explore the concept of a haunted house story from a canine perspective. He drew inspiration from films such as Poltergeist (1982) and The Shining (1980) for building sustained tension in a domestic setting. Leonberg co-wrote the screenplay with Alex Cannon, emphasizing limitations inherent to a dog's viewpoint—no voice-over, superpowers, or anthropomorphism—to heighten suspense and emotional resonance. The narrative focuses on themes of loyalty, powerlessness, and the bond between pets and owners, reflecting Leonberg's personal experiences as a dog owner.[4][5][6]

Leonberg, who holds an MFA in directing from Columbia University, transitioned to his first feature after working on short films, virtual reality projects, and commercials. A proof-of-concept short recreating scenes from The Shining with Indy helped secure funding and confirmed the viability of the approach. The production company, What's Wrong With Your Dog?, was established for the film, with Kari Fischer serving as producer.[3][7]

### Filming

Principal photography spanned over 400 days across three years, adopting a documentary-style method where filming followed Indy's natural behaviors rather than a rigid schedule. To minimize distractions, the crew was often limited to Leonberg and Fischer, with Leonberg also acting as cinematographer. Wide-angle lenses, low camera angles (approximately 19 inches off the ground to match Indy's height), and practical lighting from single sources like lanterns were used to convey the dog's scale and perspective. Visual effects included vignetting and silhouettes to create ambiguity and unease, while sound design and music projected emotions onto Indy's neutral expressions.[4][5][6]

Indy was not trained as a performer; his reactions were captured authentically, with the team adapting to his temperament. Leonberg noted that dogs' innocence allows audiences to empathize more readily than with human characters, enhancing the film's impact.[5]

## Release

Good Boy premiered at the SXSW Film Festival on March 13, 2025. It received a wide theatrical release in the United States on October 3, 2025, distributed by IFC Films, with an international rollout following in select markets, including Europe and the Middle East. The film became available for streaming on Shudder on October 24, 2025.[1][3]

The film was rated PG-13 by the Motion Picture Association for terror, strong language, and bloody images.[3]

### Box office

Produced on an estimated budget of $70,000, Good Boy opened in domestic theaters to $2.3 million over its first weekend. It held in the top ten during its second weekend, earning an additional $1.4 million. The domestic run concluded in November 2025 with a total gross of $6.6 million. Internationally, it earned $2.1 million, for a worldwide total of $8.7 million.[8][9][10]

## Reception

### Critical response

On the review aggregator website Rotten Tomatoes, Good Boy holds a positive consensus, described as "a visually striking, emotionally devastating horror film that eschews genre conventions to deliver a uniquely haunting and conceptually ambitious experience." Critics highlighted the innovative dog-centric perspective as refreshing the haunted-house subgenre, though some found the narrative slim and certain visuals confusing.[3]

In Variety, Owen Gleiberman commended the film's ingenuity on a limited budget, noting its "nifty new tricks" in storytelling and Indy's expressive performance, but pointed out occasional frustrations from ambiguous imagery.[11] Paste Magazine's review awarded it an 8.7 out of 10, praising the atmospheric staging, Indy's captivating presence, and emotional resonance, while acknowledging the constraints of the animal viewpoint in a feature-length format.[12] Other outlets, such as The Age and Observer, echoed appreciation for the blend of scares and heartbreak, with descriptions of the film as "hair-raising and heartbreaking."[3]

User reviews on IMDb averaged 6.2 out of 10, with feedback emphasizing the unique gimmick and heartfelt themes but critiquing pacing and a lack of traditional scares.[1]

### Accolades

At the SXSW Film Festival, Indy received a special "Howl of Fame" award. The film garnered nominations at the 2026 Astra Film Awards for Best First Feature and Best Performance in a Horror or Thriller, marking the first time an animal received such recognition at a major awards body. It also earned a PETA award for its humane portrayal of animal actors. In October 2025, Indy was featured in an open letter advocating for the Academy Awards to consider animal performances in acting categories.[13][14][15][16]

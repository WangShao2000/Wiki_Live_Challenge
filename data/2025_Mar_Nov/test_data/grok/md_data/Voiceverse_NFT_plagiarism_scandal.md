The Voiceverse NFT plagiarism scandal refers to a 2022 controversy in which the blockchain-based technology company Voiceverse was accused of plagiarizing audio generated by the non-commercial artificial intelligence text-to-speech tool 15.ai for promotional purposes. The incident involved Voiceverse using 15.ai to create voice samples, altering them via pitch-shifting, and presenting them as demonstrations of their own AI voice cloning technology, which violated 15.ai's terms of service prohibiting commercial use and requiring attribution. This occurred amid the height of non-fungible token (NFT) popularity and drew attention due to the involvement of prominent voice actor Troy Baker, who initially partnered with Voiceverse but later withdrew. The scandal raised questions about intellectual property ethics in emerging AI and blockchain technologies, and it was later cited in a 2024 lawsuit against Voiceverse's parent company, LOVO Inc., for similar allegations of voice misuse. While Voiceverse admitted the unauthorized use and apologized, attributing it to their marketing team, the event contributed to broader discussions on plagiarism in digital innovation and foreshadowed ongoing challenges in AI voice synthesis.

### Background

#### Development of 15.ai
15.ai is a deep learning-based text-to-speech synthesis tool created by a pseudonymous artificial intelligence researcher affiliated with the Massachusetts Institute of Technology (MIT). Launched in 2020, the tool allows users to generate realistic voice audio for various fictional characters, drawing from datasets including voices from the animated series *My Little Pony: Friendship Is Magic*, such as Twilight Sparkle and Rainbow Dash. It was designed as a non-commercial project, with strict terms of service that prohibit commercial applications, require proper attribution for any use, and explicitly oppose integration with NFTs or similar monetization schemes. The creator, known publicly as "15," emphasized the tool's educational and experimental purpose, crediting it with influencing advancements in modern voice AI systems. On December 13, 2021, 15 publicly stated their lack of interest in NFTs, reinforcing the project's non-commercial stance.

#### Voiceverse and the NFT Context
Voiceverse was a startup founded by members associated with the Bored Ape Yacht Club, a prominent NFT collection. The company positioned itself as a platform for AI voice cloning, enabling users to create custom text-to-speech voices from short audio samples and trade them as NFTs, with options for licensing to others. It partnered with LOVO Inc., an AI voice platform, as well as celebrities like voice actors Charlet Chung and Andy Milonakis. Voiceverse aimed to appeal to independent creators by offering tools for affordable voice work in games, animations, and other media, amid the 2021-2022 NFT boom. However, the project faced skepticism due to environmental concerns over blockchain energy use and broader criticisms of NFT speculation.

### The Incident
On January 14, 2022, at 1:02 a.m. EST, Troy Baker, known for voicing characters in video games such as *The Last of Us*, *Fortnite*, and *BioShock Infinite*, announced his partnership with Voiceverse on social media, promoting it as a means to empower creators with IP ownership through NFTs. Later that day, Voiceverse released promotional videos featuring an animated cat character from the NFT firm Chubbiverse, claiming the voices were generated using their proprietary AI technology. The audio included lines like "Hi Frens! We giggle in the Chubbiverse, we eat jpegs and fast foods, then we vomit memes and fart rainbows" and "Hi Frens! i got an alpha drop for you?!? I am made with love and my voice is ai! Yeah for real lets build the chubbiverse - reply below and comment alpha."

At 11:17 a.m. EST, 15 commented that the venture resembled a scam. By 1:20 p.m. EST, 15 accused Voiceverse of appropriating their work, providing server log files as evidence. These logs showed requests from Voiceverse matching the exact text, timestamps (e.g., 2022-01-04T02:20:01.651Z for one voice), and characters (Twilight Sparkle and Rainbow Dash), confirming the audio was generated by 15.ai and then pitch-shifted to disguise its origin. This constituted plagiarism, theft, and false advertising, as Voiceverse sold the generated voices as NFTs, breaching 15.ai's terms.

At 2:09 p.m. EST, Voiceverse issued a public apology on Twitter, admitting the voice was sourced from 15.ai without credit, explaining it as a rushed demo by their marketing team to align with Baker's announcement, and stating the Chubbiverse team was unaware. They assured it would not recur. In their Discord server, they reiterated that the marketing team used 15.ai because their own technology was not ready. At 3:34 p.m. EST, 15 responded with "Go fuck yourself," a statement that gained viral support.

The following day, January 15, Baker appeared on the *Play, Watch, Listen* podcast, describing his announcement as poorly received and clarifying his intent to support independent creators, while downplaying the NFT aspect. On January 31, Baker announced the end of his partnership with Voiceverse, calling it a mutual decision and thanking fans for their input.

### Reactions
The scandal gained traction beyond AI and NFT communities, fueled by Baker's celebrity status and the NFT hype. Public backlash focused on ethical lapses, with 15 expressing frustration over the misrepresentation of voice synthesis technology. News outlets described it as "theft" and "plagiarism," emphasizing violations of terms and false claims. Baker faced criticism for his initial tone, which he later acknowledged as antagonistic, and for accusing detractors of hate. The incident was documented in AI ethics repositories, such as the AI Incident Database.

### Aftermath
Voiceverse largely ceased operations following the scandal, with the project fizzling out amid declining NFT interest. 15.ai went offline on September 8, 2022, and remained unavailable as of October 2024, despite periodic updates from its creator. In May 2024, LOVO was sued by voice actors for unauthorized voice copying; by July 2024, a judge allowed the claims to proceed, with court documents referencing the Voiceverse plagiarism of 15.ai as precedent. On September 25, 2024, a class-action lawsuit against LOVO further highlighted the incident.

Retrospectives in 2025, such as analyses published on November 17, framed the scandal as an early indicator of AI plagiarism issues, including AI systems copying from each other, as seen in 2025 disputes between companies like OpenAI and DeepSeek over model distillation. It underscored the need for transparency in AI development and respect for creators' intentions in non-commercial projects.

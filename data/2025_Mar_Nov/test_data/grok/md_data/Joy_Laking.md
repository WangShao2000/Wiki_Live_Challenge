### Early Life
Born in Owen Sound, Ontario, Laking grew up in a family with artistic inclinations, including a mother who painted professionally. She graduated from the University of Guelph with a fine art degree in 1972 and moved to Nova Scotia shortly after. 

### Career Overview
Laking has operated the Joy Laking Gallery in Bass River, Nova Scotia, since the late 1980s, showcasing her paintings and prints. She has illustrated children's books and published a collection of her works in 2020. Exhibitions include a solo show at the Art Gallery of Nova Scotia in 1990 that toured for a year.

### Recognition
She has received awards such as the Queen's Diamond Jubilee Medal in 2012 and a Lifetime Achievement Award from the Cobequid Arts Council. 

Joy Snihur Wyatt Laking (born 1950) is a Canadian artist based in Nova Scotia, known for her paintings in watercolours, oils, and acrylics that depict landscapes, seascapes, and everyday scenes from the province. Since settling in Nova Scotia in 1972, she has produced works that focus on natural elements such as light, colour, and form, often created outdoors in a spontaneous manner. Her career includes exhibitions across Canada and internationally, illustrations for children's books, and the operation of her own gallery. Laking has received awards for her contributions to the arts, including the Queen's Diamond Jubilee Medal, and her work has been featured in public collections and publications. She is an elected member of the Society of Canadian Artists and a founding member of Professional Living Artists of Nova Scotia (PLANS).

## Early life and education
Laking was born in 1950 in Owen Sound, Ontario, and raised in Grey County, where she was exposed to landscapes including limestone formations, rolling hills, and forests. Her family had artistic roots: both parents painted, with her mother working professionally and her father engaging in hobbies such as building marionettes and tinting photographs. As a child, she accompanied her mother on painting outings along Georgian Bay, though she initially felt her own efforts were inadequate compared to her mother's. Laking experienced vision issues in early childhood, corrected with glasses, which sources describe as heightening her attention to detail and colour subtleties.

She graduated from the University of Guelph in 1972 with a degree in fine art, where the curriculum emphasized acrylics and abstract styles. After university, she married and relocated to Bass River, Nova Scotia, on the Bay of Fundy shore in Colchester County.

## Career
Laking began her professional career in 1972, transitioning to watercolours as her primary medium due to its solvent-free nature, despite her university training in other techniques. She largely self-taught this medium, allowing for experimentation. Her early works included series on flowers with botanical details, winter scenes featuring white clapboard houses, window compositions with glass bottles, seascapes, shorelines, and "potato people" figures inspired by Peggy's Cove to address feelings of isolation.

In the early 1980s, she illustrated two children's books by Gary L. Saunders: *The Brook and the Woodcutter* (1979), an environmental fable, and *The Man Who Couldn't Stop Sneezing* (1982). She also produced calendars with her paintings and limited-edition prints to support her income. One of her paintings, *Brigitte’s Window*, was selected as the cover for the 1985 Maritime Tel and Tel Directory.

By the late 1980s, Laking and her then-husband added a gallery to their home in Portapique (near Bass River), which serves as a space for displaying and selling her works, including oils, acrylics, watercolours, and serigraphs (handmade silkscreen prints). The gallery overlooks a salt marsh and the Bay of Fundy, and pre-COVID, it attracted around 6,000 visitors annually. She has sold through private galleries such as Manuge Gallery and Zwickers in Halifax, Beckett Gallery in Hamilton, Ontario, and via her website.

Laking held nine solo exhibitions and six group shows between 1982 and 1992, followed by ongoing group exhibitions and displays at her gallery from the mid-1990s. A notable solo exhibition occurred at the Art Gallery of Nova Scotia in 1990, which toured for a year and encouraged her to pursue more personal imagery. Other exhibitions include *The Wonder and the Worry* with Margaret Boyle at MacCarthy Gallery in Truro (2012), a photography show *Here and Away — Seeing with Passionate Eyes* with Laurie Gunn in Truro (2007), and joint displays with Susan Paterson at Colchester-East Hants Public Library in Truro (through December 2022). Her works have been shown provincially, nationally, and internationally, including in India, Ghana, Italy, and Portugal.

In 2007, she expanded into photography, rug-hooking, wool dyeing, and beach glass art, influenced by travel. Laking typically produces about 70 works annually, starting early mornings and using sketches or photos for studio pieces. Her style involves applying darks first for luminosity, with recent pieces featuring bolder colours, simplified subjects, and abstract or hyper-realistic compositions. Sources describe her approach as process-oriented, emphasizing composition and immersion, with themes of joyful, storybook-like scenes as a form of escapism.

She maintains a studio in Great Village, Nova Scotia, the childhood home of poet Elizabeth Bishop. As of 2023, she continued traveling and painting, including trips to Europe where she created watercolours of sites like Prague's Charles Bridge.

## Publications
Laking authored and illustrated *Colours in Winter*, a children's book published by Pottersfield Press. In 2020, Pottersfield Press released *The Painted Province: Nova Scotia Through an Artist’s Eyes*, compiling 200 of her paintings from 40 locations across Nova Scotia, accompanied by GPS coordinates, original poetry, and commentary. The book, now in its fourth printing, stemmed from over 25 years of annual painting trips within the province.

## Personal life
Laking's mother died of breast cancer in her early fifties, around the time of Laking's university graduation, which sources indicate motivated her dedication to art. She had three children between 1980 and 1985: Kelsey, Danica, and Yolande, two of whom became artists. Motherhood influenced her work, providing subjects for positive scenes.

She ended her first marriage in 1998 and spent time alone on Quirpon Island, Newfoundland, in 1998 and 1999, painting and journaling to process personal challenges. Laking remarried in 2007 after overcoming a health issue. She has served on the board of the Robert Pope Foundation since 1997.

In 2020, Laking was affected by the Portapique mass shooting in her community, which resulted in 22 deaths; she knew three victims and the perpetrator and his partner. Sources report she managed the aftermath by focusing on her book layout and resuming painting. As of recent reports, she lives on a nearly 20-acre property in Portapique with views of the Bay of Fundy, alongside her husband and a Golden Retriever named Fen.

## Awards
Laking received the Woman of Excellence Award from the Progress Club of Nova Scotia in 2009. In 2012, she was awarded the Queen's Diamond Jubilee Medal and the Lifetime Achievement Award from the Cobequid Arts Council. Her prints have been used for presentations, such as at the 2025 Mobius Awards by Divert NS.

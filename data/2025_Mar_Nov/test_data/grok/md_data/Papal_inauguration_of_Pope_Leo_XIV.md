## Background
Pope Leo XIV, born Robert Francis Prevost on 14 September 1955 in Chicago, Illinois, United States, is the first pope from the Americas.[1][2] He entered the Order of Saint Augustine in 1977, was ordained a priest in 1982, and served as a missionary in Peru from 1983 to 1998, where he held roles such as prior and pastor.[3] Appointed Bishop of Chiclayo, Peru, in 2014, he led the diocese until 2023, when he became Prefect of the Dicastery for Bishops in the Roman Curia and was created a cardinal.[4] His election occurred during a conclave that began after Pope Francis's death in April 2025, with white smoke announcing the result on the afternoon of 8 May after four ballots.[5][6] Cardinal Prevost chose the regnal name Leo XIV, the first use of "Leo" since Leo XIII (1878–1903).[7]

The conclave involved 120 cardinal electors and focused on themes of Church unity amid global challenges, with alliances among North and South American cardinals contributing to the outcome.[8] Nationality was not a primary factor in discussions, according to participants, though the election of an American pope was noted as a historic development.[9] The inauguration followed a period of sede vacante, during which the new pope delivered his first homily to the cardinals on 9 May in the Sistine Chapel, stressing personal conversion and the Church's role as a witness to Christ.[10]

## Preparation
The inauguration was organized by the Office for the Liturgical Celebrations of the Supreme Pontiff, following the rite approved by Benedict XVI in 2005 and modified in 2013 to emphasize episcopal collegiality and simplicity.[11] Preparations included the crafting of the pallium—a circular band of white lamb's wool symbolizing the pope's pastoral authority as the Good Shepherd—and the fisherman's ring, engraved with an image of Saint Peter casting a net and the pope's name in Latin.[12] The ring serves as a seal for official documents and represents the pope's duty to confirm the faith of the brethren.[13]

The ceremony was set for the Fifth Sunday of Easter, aligning with the liturgical calendar's focus on resurrection and new life.[14] Invitations were extended to heads of state, diplomatic representatives from over 180 countries, ecumenical delegations, and representatives of other religions.[15] Security and logistical arrangements accommodated an estimated crowd of 150,000 in Saint Peter's Square, with multilingual broadcasts ensuring accessibility.[16]

## Ceremony
The inauguration began at 9:15 AM Central European Time inside Saint Peter's Basilica, where Pope Leo XIV, accompanied by patriarchs of the Eastern Catholic Churches, prayed silently at the tomb of Saint Peter beneath the Altar of Confession.[17] This act underscored the pope's connection to the apostle's martyrdom and the foundational role of the See of Rome.[18] Two deacons then processed the pallium, fisherman's ring, and Book of the Gospels to an altar in Saint Peter's Square.[19]

The procession to the square featured the chanting of the *Laudes Regiae*, an ancient litany invoking the intercession of saints, particularly past popes and martyrs, to emphasize that papal authority derives from divine grace rather than earthly power.[20] The Mass incorporated multilingual elements: readings from Acts 4:8–12 (in Spanish), Psalm 118 (in Italian), 1 Peter 5:1–5, 10–11 (in Spanish), and John 21:15–19 (in Latin and Greek), highlighting Peter's commission to feed Christ's sheep.[21]

After the Gospel, the rite of investiture occurred. Cardinal Luis Antonio Tagle, as the senior cardinal deacon, imposed the pallium, symbolizing the yoke of Christ and the pope's responsibility to carry the flock.[22] A cardinal priest recited a prayer invoking the Holy Spirit, and a cardinal bishop presented the fisherman's ring.[23] Twelve representatives from various categories of the People of God—clergy, religious, laity, and from different continents—then pledged obedience, representing the universal Church's communion.[24]

The Eucharistic liturgy followed, with the Offertory chant *Tu es pastor ovium* ("You are the shepherd of the sheep") and the Roman Canon.[25] The Universal Prayer was offered in Portuguese, French, Arabic, Polish, and Chinese, praying for the Church, the pope, civil authorities, the afflicted, and the assembly.[26] The Mass concluded with the *Regina Caeli* and a papal blessing, incorporating imagery of the Church as a vineyard.[27]

Eyewitness accounts described a packed square with diverse pilgrims waving flags from regions including Africa, Asia, and the Americas, creating an atmosphere of unity and inclusion.[28] The pope's arrival in the popemobile elicited enthusiastic responses, and large screens captured emotional moments, such as the ring's imposition.[29]

## Homily
In his homily, Pope Leo XIV reflected on the Gospel account of Jesus questioning Peter's love (John 21:15–19), emphasizing that the pope's ministry is rooted in God's unconditional love rather than personal merit.[30] He drew on Saint Augustine's *Confessions* to describe humanity's restlessness until finding rest in God, and linked this to the recent death of Pope Francis, portraying the Church as sheep guided by a shepherd.[31]

Key themes included love and unity as the core of Peter's mission, with the pope calling for a Church that serves as a "leaven of unity" in a divided world marked by hatred, violence, and exploitation.[32] He urged missionary outreach without coercion, valuing diversity and promoting peace through dialogue with other Christians, religions, and people of good will.[33] Referencing Leo XIII's *Rerum Novarum*, he questioned whether prevailing love could end conflicts.[34] The homily concluded with a prayer for a Church founded on God's love, open to the world and animated by the Holy Spirit.[35]

## Attendance
The event drew approximately 150,000 attendees, including 120 cardinals, bishops, priests, and representatives from Eastern Catholic Churches.[36] Heads of state and government from countries such as the United States, Peru, Italy, and France were present, along with diplomatic corps members.[37] Ecumenical delegations from Orthodox, Anglican, and Protestant communities attended, as did interfaith representatives.[38] Pilgrims from Peru, where the pope had served as bishop, were notably visible, expressing pride in his background.[39] Following the Mass, the pope greeted delegations inside the basilica.[40]

## Subsequent events
On 20 May 2025, Pope Leo XIV took possession of the Basilica of Saint Paul Outside the Walls, praying at Saint Paul's tomb to symbolize the dual apostolic foundation of the Roman Church.[41] On 25 May, he assumed possession of the Basilica of Saint Mary Major, venerating the icon of *Salus Populi Romani* (Protectress of the Roman People), and later that day, the Archbasilica of Saint John Lateran, the cathedral of the Diocese of Rome.[42] At Saint John Lateran, he was seated on the cathedra (bishop's throne) and received obedience from diocesan representatives, affirming his primary role as Bishop of Rome.[43] These acts, required for all bishops, must occur within two months of election and remind the pope of his local episcopal duties.[44]

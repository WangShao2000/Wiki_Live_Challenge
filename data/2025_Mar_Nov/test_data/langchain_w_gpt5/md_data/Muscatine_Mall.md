# Muscatine Mall (Muscatine, Iowa)

## Overview
Muscatine Mall is an enclosed shopping center at 1903 Park Avenue in Muscatine, Muscatine County, Iowa, United States. The complex opened in 1971 under the name Muscatine Plaza and was developed by General Management Corporation of Des Moines. Contemporary local coverage described a climate‑controlled, enclosed center with approximately 287,000 square feet of retail area and perimeter parking for about 1,900 cars. Early tenants included Montgomery Ward, Woolworth, Hy‑Vee, Walgreens, and the Plaza One & Two twin‑screen theaters. By October 1975, major advertising used the name “Muscatine Mall,” and JCPenney debuted there that month. In July 1983, Walmart opened one of its first two Iowa stores at Muscatine Mall, taking a former Woolco space. Menards opened in the former Walmart anchor in October 1998. The mall’s anchor roster and occupancy changed significantly in the 2000s and 2010s, including the opening of Elder‑Beerman in October 2003 and the closures of JCPenney in 2014 and Younkers (successor to Elder‑Beerman) in 2018. As of December 2025, the mall remains open and is used for city‑sponsored events and services, with retail occupancy reduced since the loss of several anchors. - Parking: perimeter parking designed for about 1,900 cars. [4]

## Development history
- Groundbreaking: May 19, 1970. City officials and developer representatives—including Mayor Kelly Burns and Matthew Bucksbaum (president of General Management Corporation)—attended the ceremony. [4]
- Initial tenant lineup (leases signed by spring 1970): Woolworth, Montgomery Ward, Hy‑Vee, Walgreens, and Twin Screen Theatres (Plaza One & Two). [4]
- Initial openings (1970–1971):
  - Montgomery Ward opened in November 1970. [4]
  - Woolworth, Walgreens, Hy‑Vee, and First National Bank opened in June 1971. [4]
  - Formal opening of Muscatine Plaza occurred on July 15, 1971, and Plaza One & Two theaters opened July 22, 1971. [4]
- Name usage: By October 1975, advertisements identified the venue as “Muscatine Mall.” [5]
- JCPenney opening: Grand opening on October 22, 1975, with extensive advertising and a published roster of mall merchants. [5]
- Discount/department store changes:
  - Woolco ceased operations early in 1983; Walmart opened one of its first two Iowa stores at Muscatine Mall in July 1983 in the former Woolco space (Marshalltown Mall was the other). [6]
  - Menards opened in the mall’s former Walmart anchor with an unadvertised opening on October 16, 1998, and a grand opening on October 18, 1998. [7]
- Elder‑Beerman opening: The Bon‑Ton chain opened its first Iowa Elder‑Beerman in the south end of the mall on October 23, 2003; local coverage described a 43,500‑square‑foot store and ribbon‑cutting details. [9][10]

## Tenants and anchors timeline (selected)
- Early anchors and services:
  - Montgomery Ward (opened November 1970). [4]
  - Woolworth (opened June 1971; later transitioned to Woolco). [4]
  - Hy‑Vee and Walgreens (opened June 1971); First National Bank opened a branch at the mall entrance on June 14, 1971. [4][27]
  - Plaza One & Two theaters (opened July 22, 1971). [4]
- Mid‑1970s additions:
  - JCPenney opened October 22, 1975. The ad listed dozens of contemporaneous tenants (including Hy‑Vee, Walgreens, Radio Shack, Musicland, Plaza Theatres, Pizza Hut, Baskin‑Robbins, Montgomery Ward, Kinney Shoes, Gallenkamp, and others). [5]
- 1980s–1990s anchors:
  - Walmart opened July 1983, occupying the former Woolco space. [6]
  - Menards opened in October 1998 in the former Walmart anchor position. [7]
- 2000s–2010s changes:
  - Elder‑Beerman opened October 23, 2003; later operated under the Younkers banner before closing in 2018. [9][10][13][14]
  - JCPenney closed in 2014. [11][12]
- Leasing materials identified Slumberland Furniture among tenants as of late 2017. [16]

## Ownership and management
- Original developer: General Management Corporation (Des Moines), with Matthew Bucksbaum named in local coverage at the 1970 groundbreaking. [4]
- Subsequent transactions and records:
  - A 2006 Muscatine Journal item reported that Muscatine Mall had been purchased by a group of Quad‑City–area businessmen. [9]
  - City of Muscatine planning documentation references Muscatine Mall Management II, L.L.C. at 1903 Park Avenue and details relating to a Starbucks outparcel, indicating local records associated with an entity at the mall address. [23]
  - Municipal action related to subdivision: on May 6, 2010, the City Council agenda included approval of the final plat for “Muscatine Mall Second Addition.” [24]
- Current owner/manager: Publicly accessible municipal references name Muscatine Mall Management II, L.L.C. in relation to the property and its outparcels; however, comprehensive county assessor detail for all parcels of the enclosed mall has not been consolidated within the cited sources. [23][24]

## Current operational status (as of December 12, 2025)
- The mall remains open for public use. City of Muscatine departments continue to host events and programs at the mall, including:
  - Muscatine Parks and Recreation’s Elves Workshop (scheduled at Muscatine Mall on Saturday, December 13, 2025). [21]
  - Walking Club activities recorded at an information center in the mall’s center court. [17][18]
  - Prior events such as the Fall Festival (October 2018) and Elves Workshop (December 2018). [19][20]
- Services and access:
  - The Iowa Department of Transportation indicates Commercial Driver’s License skills and drive tests are administered at “Muscatine Plaza, 1903 Park Ave.,” with instructions to use the Iowa DOT entrance on the back side of the mall. [22]
- Redevelopment/zoning: The City Council’s May 6, 2010 agenda shows approval of the final plat for Muscatine Mall Second Addition. No demolition approvals or large‑scale de‑malling actions are documented in the sources cited here for 2019–2025; leasing materials and city events indicate ongoing but reduced retail usage following anchor losses. [24][16][21]

## Outparcels and site layout
- Outparcel formation and marketing:
  - A 0.92‑acre outparcel is listed adjacent to Muscatine Mall for retail, restaurant, or service use. [25]
  - Additional Park Avenue commercial lots near the mall have been marketed in recent years, reflecting continuing subdivision and outparcel activity along the corridor. [24][25]

## Economic and community role
- Public programming and civic use:
  - City‑sponsored events and programs—such as the annual Fall Festival, Elves Workshop, and the Walking Club—have used the mall’s interior corridors and center court. [17][18][19][20][21]
- Anchor closures and local impact:
  - In 2018, following Bon‑Ton’s bankruptcy, Younkers announced closure; local media reported the Muscatine Younkers store’s liquidation affecting approximately 45 employees, and city officials publicly addressed residents’ concerns about the mall’s future. [13][14][15]

## Notable events and incidents
- Government services:
  - The Iowa DOT’s Muscatine office notes CDL skills and drive tests conducted at Muscatine Plaza (1903 Park Ave.), indicating continued use of the site for state services. [22]
- Retail transition:
  - JCPenney confirmed the closure of its Muscatine Mall store effective May 2014 as part of a national reduction. [11][12]
  - Younkers closed in 2018 following Bon‑Ton liquidation proceedings; statewide coverage confirmed the chainwide shutdown timeline. [13][14]

## Name history
- Opening name: Muscatine Plaza (formal opening July 15, 1971). [4]
- Later usage: Advertisements and tenant promotions by October 1975 used “Muscatine Mall,” which remains the common name. [5][1][2]

# 2010 NFC Championship Game

The 2010 NFC Championship Game was a National Football League playoff game played on January 24, 2010, at the Louisiana Superdome in New Orleans, Louisiana, between the New Orleans Saints and the Minnesota Vikings to determine the NFC representative in Super Bowl XLIV. New Orleans defeated Minnesota 31–28 in overtime on Garrett Hartley’s 40-yard field goal, sending the Saints to the first Super Bowl in franchise history. Minnesota outgained New Orleans 475–257 and led in first downs and time of possession, but committed five turnovers to the Saints’ one. The game featured several pivotal late sequences, including a Vikings penalty for 12 men in the huddle in the final minute of regulation and Brett Favre’s interception by Tracy Porter that sent the game to overtime, where the Saints won the coin toss and scored on the opening possession. The contest drew one of the largest audiences in conference championship history on Fox and is frequently discussed in the context of subsequent NFL overtime rule reforms adopted later in 2010 for the postseason and extended to the regular season in 2012. The Saints’ defensive hits on Favre in this game also became a focal point in media coverage of the NFL’s 2012 investigation into a New Orleans “bounty” program, which led to significant organizational sanctions and later appellate rulings on player discipline. [6][2][1][12][13][14][9][10][18][17]

## Background

- Teams and seeds: The Saints entered the game as the NFC’s top seed after a 13–3 regular season, while the Vikings were the No. 2 seed at 12–4. Minnesota had routed the Dallas Cowboys in the Divisional Round, while New Orleans had advanced from the other Divisional matchup to host the championship. Brad Childress coached the Vikings; Sean Payton coached the Saints, with Gregg Williams as New Orleans’ defensive coordinator. The winner advanced to Super Bowl XLIV. [5][2]

- Venue and administration: The game was played indoors at the Louisiana Superdome on an artificial surface. Kickoff was at 5:45 p.m. CST, and attendance was 71,276. The referee was Pete Morelli. New Orleans won the overtime coin toss. [6]

## Game summary

### First half

Minnesota opened with a 10-play, 80-yard drive capped by Adrian Peterson’s 19-yard touchdown run at 9:35 of the first quarter for a 7–0 lead. New Orleans answered on a 38-yard screen pass from Drew Brees to Pierre Thomas at 6:30 to tie 7–7. The Vikings regained the lead when Brett Favre found Sidney Rice for a 5-yard touchdown with 2:11 left in the first quarter, making it 14–7. New Orleans tied again in the second quarter on Brees’s 9-yard touchdown pass to Devery Henderson at 10:30, and the teams went to halftime even at 14–14. Late in the half, Reggie Bush muffed a punt at the New Orleans 10-yard line, but a botched Favre–Peterson exchange moments later gave the ball back to the Saints and preserved the tie. [6][2][4]

### Second half

New Orleans took its first lead early in the third quarter. After Courtney Roby’s 61-yard kickoff return opened the half, Pierre Thomas scored on a 9-yard run at 12:56 for a 21–14 Saints lead. Minnesota tied the game on Peterson’s 1-yard touchdown at 7:35 of the third. New Orleans went ahead 28–21 in the fourth quarter when Brees hit Reggie Bush for a 5-yard touchdown at 12:39, a drive set up by a Percy Harvin fumble at the Vikings’ 22-yard line. Peterson’s 2-yard touchdown with 4:58 remaining tied the game 28–28. With 1:59 left in regulation, Saints linebacker Jonathan Vilma intercepted Favre at the New Orleans 28, but the Saints punted. Minnesota then drove into long field-goal range before being penalized for 12 men in the huddle with just over a minute left, moving the ball back from the New Orleans 33-yard line and complicating the kick attempt. On the ensuing play, under pressure and rolling right, Favre threw late across his body toward Sidney Rice and was intercepted by Tracy Porter at the New Orleans 22 with 19 seconds left, sending the game to overtime. [6][4][5]

### Overtime

New Orleans won the overtime coin toss and received the kickoff. Courtney Roby returned it 40 yards to the Saints’ 39-yard line. The Saints’ possession included a defensive holding penalty that produced an automatic first down, a successful fourth-and-inches conversion by Pierre Thomas that was upheld on review, and a defensive pass interference penalty on Vikings linebacker Ben Leber that advanced the ball to the Minnesota 29. A subsequent 12-yard completion from Brees to Robert Meachem to the Vikings’ 22 was confirmed on review. After the drive stalled, Hartley connected from 40 yards 4:45 into the overtime for the 31–28 win. [6][3][8]

## Statistics

- Team totals: Minnesota outgained New Orleans 475–257, led 31–15 in first downs and 36:49–27:56 in time of possession, and converted 7 of 12 third downs; New Orleans converted 3 of 12. Minnesota committed five turnovers (two interceptions, three lost fumbles); New Orleans had one lost fumble. Red zone efficiency: Minnesota 4-of-6; New Orleans 3-of-3. Penalties: Vikings 5 for 32 yards; Saints 9 for 88 yards. [1][7]

- Individual leaders:
  - Vikings: Brett Favre 28-for-46, 310 yards, 1 TD, 2 INT; Adrian Peterson 25 rushes, 122 yards, 3 TD; Bernard Berrian 9 receptions, 102 yards; Visanthe Shiancoe 4 for 83; Sidney Rice 4 for 43 and 1 TD. [3]
  - Saints: Drew Brees 17-for-31, 197 yards, 3 TD, 0 INT (sacked once); Pierre Thomas 14 rushes, 61 yards, 1 TD; receiving TDs by Thomas (38 yards), Devery Henderson (9 yards), and Reggie Bush (5 yards). Courtney Roby had 5 kickoff returns for 143 yards (long 61). Garrett Hartley was 4-for-4 on PATs and 1-for-1 on field goals (40 yards). [3][7]

- Notable defensive plays: New Orleans interceptions by Tracy Porter (returned 26 yards) and Jonathan Vilma (3 yards). Key Vikings fumbles included a late second-quarter center–exchange fumble deep in Saints territory and fourth-quarter fumbles by Bernard Berrian at the Saints’ 10 and Percy Harvin at the Vikings’ 22. [6][7]

## Officiating and rules context

The game was officiated by referee Pete Morelli’s crew. The Saints’ overtime drive included multiple replay reviews—on a fourth-and-inches conversion by Pierre Thomas and on a 12-yard completion to Robert Meachem—and was extended by defensive holding and a defensive pass interference foul on Vikings linebacker Ben Leber before Hartley’s winning 40-yarder. [6]

At the NFL Annual Meeting in March 2010, owners voted 28–4 to adopt a modified sudden-death overtime format for the postseason beginning with the 2010 season. Under the change, a field goal on the opening possession no longer ends the game; the opponent is guaranteed a possession unless the first-drive result is a touchdown. The league subsequently extended the modified system to the regular season at the 2012 Annual Meeting by a 30–2 vote; regular-season overtime could end in a tie after one 15-minute period, while postseason overtime continued until a winner was determined. Contemporary reporting and league materials describe the procedures and rationale for the change. [9][10][15][16]

## Controversies and aftermath

- Bounty scandal context: In 2012, the NFL announced that its Security Department had found that Saints defensive personnel operated a pay-for-performance “bounty” program during the 2009–2011 seasons, including allegations of rewards for “cart-offs” and “knockouts” and an alleged bounty targeting Brett Favre before this championship game. The league imposed major sanctions on the organization and team leaders: a $500,000 fine and forfeiture of second-round draft picks in 2012 and 2013 for the club; a season-long suspension without pay for head coach Sean Payton in 2012; an indefinite suspension for former defensive coordinator Gregg Williams; an eight-game suspension for general manager Mickey Loomis; and a six-game suspension for assistant head coach Joe Vitt. The league stated that player discipline would be handled separately. [18]

  Former NFL Commissioner Paul Tagliabue, appointed as appeals officer, later vacated all player suspensions in December 2012 while affirming factual findings that a program existed and that certain players engaged in conduct detrimental, concluding that organizational misconduct and procedural issues warranted vacatur of player suspensions. Sanctions for coaches and executives were not part of the player appeal and remained in effect. Coverage and the full decision emphasized that while player suspensions were vacated, the existence of a pay-for-performance program and organizational responsibility stood. [17]

- Late-game sequences and officiating scrutiny: The Vikings’ “12 men in the huddle” penalty in the final minute of regulation moved the ball back from the New Orleans 33-yard line and preceded Favre’s interception by Tracy Porter at the Saints’ 22-yard line with 0:19 left. In overtime, a defensive pass interference penalty on Ben Leber helped move the Saints into field-goal range; team outlets described the foul as controversial at the time. [5][6][4]

## Broadcast and ratings

The game aired on Fox and drew one of the largest audiences for a conference championship telecast. Fast-national Nielsen estimates showed an average of 57.9 million viewers and a 30.6 household rating/45 share, peaking at approximately 65.2 million viewers and a 34.4/48 rating as the game neared overtime. According to Sports Media Watch, the telecast ranks among the most-watched and highest-rated NFL conference championship games in modern measurement and was, at the time, the most-viewed non–Super Bowl program on any network since the 1998 Seinfeld finale. Fox reported extraordinary local ratings in New Orleans (63.2/82, peaking at 67.4/86 during Hartley’s kick) and Minneapolis–St. Paul (58.7/80). [12][13][14][11]

## Outcome and significance

New Orleans won 31–28 on Hartley’s 40-yard overtime field goal after securing the overtime coin toss and driving into range on the opening possession. The victory sent the Saints to Super Bowl XLIV, the franchise’s first Super Bowl appearance. The Vikings’ statistical advantages in yardage, first downs, and possession were offset by five turnovers, including late-game giveaways in scoring territory. The game’s dramatic finish, officiating decisions under then-prevailing overtime rules, and physical play against Brett Favre figured prominently in subsequent debates about overtime equity and in the league’s 2012 discipline related to the Saints’ pay-for-performance program.

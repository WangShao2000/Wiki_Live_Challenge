# Kingston Line

The Kingston Line is a Massachusetts Bay Transportation Authority (MBTA) Commuter Rail service linking Boston’s South Station with Kingston, Massachusetts, on the South Shore. It is part of the MBTA’s restored Old Colony Lines, with passenger service reinstated in September 1997 after having ended in 1959. The route was introduced as the Plymouth/Kingston Line with two outer branches; since April 2021, service to Plymouth station has been suspended, and scheduled trains operate to Kingston with intermediate stops at Halifax, Hanson, Whitman, Abington, South Weymouth, Braintree, Quincy Center, and JFK/UMass before reaching South Station. The line is operated by Keolis Commuter Services under contract to the MBTA, uses cab signals with an ACSES II positive train control overlay, and is integrated into the MBTA’s zone‑based fare system. Old Colony infrastructure works in the late 2000s and early 2010s included replacement of defective concrete ties across the South Shore routes. Service patterns have been periodically adjusted by timetable change notices, including schedule tweaks in July 2025 to improve reliability. The line connects South Shore communities to downtown Boston and to the MBTA Red Line at South Station, Quincy Center, and Braintree, and was associated with measurable traffic diversions from Route 3 and private bus carriers following its 1997 restoration. [1][2][11][5][6][25]

## Name and scope

- Official MBTA materials, schedules, and alerts refer to the service as the “Kingston” line within the Old Colony Lines. Before April 2021, the service was marketed as “Plymouth/Kingston” to reflect two branches beyond Braintree; the Plymouth branch remains suspended while Kingston service continues. [1][2][20]  
- The term “Kingston line” may also be used to refer to other railways:
  - In south‑west London, the electrified suburban loop via Kingston is officially titled the Kingston Loop in South Western Railway (SWR) timetables and network maps, sometimes informally shortened in general discourse. This article treats that railway in a dedicated “Other uses” section. [14][15]
  - Historical press in South Australia used “Kingston line” to describe the Kingston–Narracoorte railway in the South‑East; that usage is distinct and historical. [21][22]
  - In Canada, the primary railway across eastern Ontario is officially the CN Kingston Subdivision in the Québec–Windsor Corridor; reliable sources use “Kingston Subdivision,” not “Kingston Line.” [23]  
This article focuses on the MBTA Commuter Rail service. Other uses are summarized at the end for disambiguation and context.

## History

Passenger rail on the Old Colony lines south of Boston ceased in 1959. The MBTA restored two Old Colony corridors in September 1997—the Middleborough/Lakeville Line and the Plymouth/Kingston Line—sharing tracks from South Station to Braintree, with branches to Middleborough/Lakeville and to Plymouth and Kingston. The restoration linked South Shore communities to downtown Boston and the Red Line at South Station, Quincy Center, and Braintree. Early service patterns on Plymouth/Kingston included both weekday peak and off‑peak trips and eight weekend round trips. A 2001 study by the Central Transportation Planning Staff (CTPS) reported combined Old Colony ridership of about 7,850 riders each way on weekdays in spring 2000 and documented significant mode shift from rapid transit, private bus carriers, and driving alone. The study estimated that Route 3 volumes south of Braintree fell by roughly 2.5% on weekdays, with a potential reduction in congestion duration. Private carrier express bus services in the corridor were reported to have lost nearly 40% of their riders in the first year after Old Colony service began. [5]

From 2008 to 2010, the MBTA replaced large numbers of prematurely failing concrete ties across the Old Colony Lines, following a 1990s procurement and subsequent evidence of widespread defects. Public records published by CommonWealth Beacon show that by mid‑2008 the vendor acknowledged that all 147,000 ties were likely defective and would require near‑term replacement; the MBTA announced a full tie replacement program in April 2010. [6]

Service to Plymouth station was suspended beginning in April 2021 during COVID‑19 related budget adjustments and has remained suspended. In 2023, an MBTA spokesperson stated that Plymouth remained closed due to operational challenges and encouraged riders to use Kingston station; local reporting in 2023–2024 noted the continued absence of a firm re‑opening date. In the absence of Plymouth service, the MBTA schedules, alerts, and rider materials refer to the Kingston branch as the active South Shore route on this corridor. [20][13]

## Route and infrastructure

The Kingston Line operates between Kingston and Boston South Station. Principal intermediate stations are Halifax, Hanson, Whitman, Abington, South Weymouth, Braintree, Quincy Center, and JFK/UMass. The trunk between Braintree and South Station is shared with other Old Colony services, providing interchanges with the MBTA Red Line at South Station, Quincy Center, and Braintree. The outer terminals of the Old Colony restoration were approximately 35 miles from Boston as designed in the 1990s service plan. Line operations are governed by cab signaling and Automatic Train Control with an ACSES II positive train control overlay per MBTA’s system implementation plan. The route and station sequence are documented on the MBTA’s Kingston Line schedule pages. [1][5][11]

## Operations and services

- Service pattern and timetable: MBTA timetables show daily Kingston–Boston service with peak‑period additions and regular off‑peak service, including inbound stops at JFK/UMass and Quincy Center on most trips. Timetable notes indicate “early departure” at certain intermediate stops and the use of track assignments and alerts provided via MBTA T‑Alerts. Bicycles are permitted on Kingston Line trains (subject to space and symbols shown in the schedule). [1]  
- Fares and zones: The MBTA Commuter Rail uses a zone‑based fare system (Zones 1A–10). One‑way fares range by distance, monthly passes are available, and $10 weekend passes offer unlimited trips across the network on weekends and select holidays. Fares can be purchased via the mTicket app or at stations with vending. [2][4][3]  
- Accessibility and rider information: MBTA station pages and line schedules flag accessibility, parking availability, and other rider amenities; the MBTA provides real‑time alerts, crowding information icons, and guidance on bikes and early departure stops within the digital timetable interface. [1][2][3]  
- System context: The Commuter Rail network operates seven days per week across 12 routes in eastern Massachusetts and Rhode Island, with early morning starts and late evening services on some lines. The Kingston Line is one of three Old Colony branches on the South Shore. [3]

## Governance and funding

The MBTA owns and plans the Commuter Rail network; Keolis Commuter Services operates the trains under contract to the MBTA. The Old Colony corridor has been the subject of capital investment planning through the MBTA/MassDOT Capital Investment Plan (CIP). Investment documents and project lists include the Old Colony Tie Replacement project and other reliability programs categorized under track, signals, and power. Broader MBTA documents describe funding structures, program categories, and the public engagement process for the CIP. [9][10][25]

## Safety systems and signaling

MBTA’s south side commuter rail corridors, including the Old Colony Lines, operate with cab signals and Automatic Train Control, overlaid with the Advanced Civil Speed Enforcement System II (ACSES II) to meet federal positive train control requirements. MBTA’s PTC Implementation Plan describes system architecture, rolling stock equipment, and control center arrangements for the south side routes, including the Kingston/Plymouth corridor. [11]

## Incidents and controversies

- Defective concrete ties (2008–2010): Following reports of premature failures of concrete ties procured in the 1990s for the Old Colony Lines, the MBTA undertook a large‑scale tie replacement program. Publicly released timelines and correspondence indicate that by June 2008 the vendor conceded that all 147,000 ties were likely defective, with full replacement announced in April 2010. The work affected the Old Colony corridors, including the Kingston route. [6]

## Future plans

- Timetable adjustments: The MBTA and Keolis announced schedule adjustments effective July 21, 2025 on several lines, including Kingston, as part of reliability improvements accompanying the launch of service on the Fall River/New Bedford Line. Seasonal or periodic service change pages provide notice of planned changes across modes. [25][2]  
- Capital works: The MBTA’s “Projects Underway” inventory and CIP appendices identify ongoing Old Colony track asset programs such as tie replacement under the reliability category. [10][9]  
- Service change outlook: MBTA’s service changes publication for winter 2026 indicated no planned Commuter Rail service changes at that time; subsequent notices provide updated detail by month. [20]

## Economic and transportation impact

The 2001 CTPS study found that the Old Colony restoration generated substantial mode shift: 81% of surveyed riders used the Old Colony for trips previously made by other means, including diversions from the Red Line (40%), driving alone (28%), and private carrier express bus routes (20%). Traffic data suggested a 2.5% reduction in weekday Route 3 volumes near Braintree and a related reduction in congestion duration. The report also documented ridership and weekend service patterns in the early years of operation and described private carrier bus responses to the new rail service. [5]

## Other uses

- Kingston Loop (London, UK): The Kingston Loop is a circular suburban railway through Kingston upon Thames on the Network Rail Wessex Route, operated by South Western Railway. It is electrified by 750 V DC third rail and is shown as “Kingston Loop & Shepperton – London Waterloo (Timetable 1)” in SWR timetables and network maps. Recent infrastructure works by Network Rail include resignalling and control system upgrades affecting Kingston, Richmond, and Twickenham as part of the Feltham–Wokingham programme. The introduction of SWR’s Class 701 “Arterio” electric multiple units began in January 2024 and has been subject to phased rollout and delays reported through 2024–2025. [14][15][19][16][17][18]

- Kingston–Narracoorte railway (South Australia): In the late 19th century, South Australian press and public documents referred to the Kingston–Narracoorte railway in the South‑East, sometimes stylistically as the “Kingston line.” Contemporary reporting covered the line’s opening celebrations and subsequent regional discussions of South‑East rail policy. This use is historic and unrelated to the MBTA service. [21][22][6]

- Kingston Subdivision (Canada): The CN Kingston Subdivision is a 539‑kilometre double‑track main line linking Toronto and Montréal. VIA Rail refers to it by its subdivision name in corporate plans and backgrounders; reliable sources do not call it the “Kingston Line.” [23]

- Jamaica: Government communications on Jamaican railways refer to the country’s historic “Main Line” linking Kingston to other regions and to periodic revival efforts. The phrase “Kingston line” is not used as an official route name in cited government releases. [24]

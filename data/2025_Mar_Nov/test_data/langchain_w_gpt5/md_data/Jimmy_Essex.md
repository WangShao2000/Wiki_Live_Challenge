# Jimmy Essex

Jimmy Essex is a British actor and singer best known for playing Adam Donovan in the Channel 4 soap opera Hollyoaks between 2016 and 2018, including a prominent exit storyline in which the character is shot by his father, Glenn Donovan [1][2]. On stage, Essex starred as Robert in Paul Harvard’s GHBoy at London’s Charing Cross Theatre in December 2020, drawing coverage and mixed-to-positive reviews from established theatre outlets [9][10][11][12][13]. He later led Ronnie Larsen’s Happy Ending at Waterloo East Theatre in autumn 2025 [14]. Earlier in his career, Essex performed as part of the pop group Mitsotu covered by Metro and the Harrow Times, which also reported his training at the Urdang Academy [15][16]. In January 2019, Essex announced his engagement to his partner Charles, as reported by Independent.ie and the Border Telegraph [17][18].

Note: This article is about the contemporary entertainer Jimmy Essex, and not the 18th‑century English architect James Essex.

## Early life and education

Essex grew up in the Harrow area of north‑west London, where he met future bandmate James Collins at age seven at the Soul Performing Arts School in Ruislip [16]. The Harrow Times reports that at 16 both Collins and Essex attended the Urdang Academy in Finsbury, where they later met singer Holly Cooper; the three formed the pop group Mitsotu around 2011 [16]. Mitsotu’s activity in 2012 included advancing to the area finals of Live & Unsigned and performing at Showcase Live at Stamford Bridge [16]. Coverage by Metro further identifies Essex as a member of Mitsotu during the X Factor 2012 cycle [15].

## Career

### Television

- Hollyoaks (Channel 4/E4; 2016–2018). Essex joined Hollyoaks in 2016 as Adam Donovan, the eldest of the new Donovan brothers introduced in April 2016 [2]. Digital Spy and FemaleFirst covered both Essex’s casting and subsequent storylines. Interviews and reporting documented:
  - Adam’s relationship with Maxine Minniver and Essex’s comments that the couple “could be a great” match; the storyline included Adam using sign language to bond with Oscar Osborne after Essex explained Adam had previously dated a deaf woman [3].
  - The surprise return of Adam’s former fiancée Darcy Wilde, which Essex said “turns Adam’s world upside down,” affecting his engagement to Maxine [4].
  - Escalating criminal pressure from Adam’s father Glenn, including being ordered to kill Zack Loveday, which Essex described as pushing Adam toward “breaking point” [5].
  - A major McQueen house fire started by Adam under orders from Glenn, with Adam later attempting heroics during the blaze [6].
  - Adam agreeing to act as a police informant against Glenn after Maxine was attacked in a plot orchestrated by Glenn [7].
- Departure (2018). Essex’s final episodes aired in May 2018. In exit coverage and a farewell video released by the Hollyoaks official Facebook page, Essex thanked cast and crew and called his exit “really, really shocking,” describing the arc as a “good guy gone bad and then it turns back round, but then it’s too late,” after Adam is shot dead by his father [1][8].

### Stage

- GHBoy (Charing Cross Theatre, London; 3–20 December 2020). Essex played Robert in Paul Harvard’s drama set within London’s queer club and chemsex milieu. PinkNews profiled the production’s run and Essex’s reflections on rehearsing during pandemic restrictions [12]. Reviews noted Essex’s performance:
  - WhatsOnStage found GHBoy “consistently watchable” and observed Essex’s underplayed charm as a self‑absorbed protagonist [9].
  - LondonTheatre1 praised Essex’s work, highlighting that he was on stage throughout and commending a mother–son scene [10].
  - BritishTheatre.com described Essex as effectively conveying the “jitteriness and struggle of addiction,” while critiquing aspects of the text [11].
  - A Younger Theatre wrote that Essex delivered an “Atlas‑like” performance, holding the play together with charisma and vulnerability [20].
  - The Stage’s review (paywalled) billed the show as a “well‑meaning but underpowered” new play [21].
- Happy Ending (Waterloo East Theatre, London; opened 30 September 2025; ran through 26 October). BroadwayWorld announced Essex in the lead role of Andrew in Ronnie Larsen’s Happy Ending, a comedy‑thriller about a gay massage therapist whose routine is disrupted by a mysterious client [14].

### Film

- A Dark Path (2020). Cinema Paradiso’s UK listing and review credit Essex in the cast as “Peter,” noting his brief involvement in the independent horror feature [19].

### Music

- Mitsotu (2011–2012). Essex performed in the three‑piece group Mitsotu alongside Holly Cooper and James Collins. Metro’s interview with Collins explicitly names Essex as a band member and discusses the group’s X Factor experience [15]. The Harrow Times reported Mitsotu’s local performance activity, their Live & Unsigned progression, and the group’s formation background involving Essex’s training at the Urdang Academy [16].

## Reception and public profile

Theatre critics generally regarded Essex’s turn in GHBoy as a strong central performance within a divisive new play. Reviews ranged from positive (LondonTheatre1, A Younger Theatre) to mixed (WhatsOnStage, BritishTheatre.com), consistently highlighting Essex’s stamina and stage presence in a demanding role [9][10][11][20]. PinkNews positioned the production within the context of pandemic‑era theatre reopenings and quoted Essex discussing the importance of telling queer stories and the pressures on the arts during COVID‑19 [12].

Digital Spy’s coverage of Essex’s Hollyoaks arc emphasized the character’s moral conflict under his father’s criminal influence, the shocks surrounding Darcy Wilde’s reappearance, and Adam’s efforts to do right by Maxine, culminating in the widely discussed exit [1][4][5][7].

## Personal life

Independent.ie and the Border Telegraph reported that Essex announced his engagement to musician Charles in January 2019 following a six‑year relationship, sharing photographs from a proposal in Sri Lanka and congratulations from colleagues and friends on social media [17][18].

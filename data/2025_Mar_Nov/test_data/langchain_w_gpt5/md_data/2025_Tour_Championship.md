# 2025 Tour Championship

The 2025 Tour Championship was the season-ending event of the PGA Tour and the final leg of the FedExCup Playoffs, held at East Lake Golf Club in Atlanta, Georgia, United States, from August 21 to 24, 2025. In a return to traditional 72-hole stroke play without Starting Strokes, Tommy Fleetwood won his first PGA Tour title and the FedExCup with a 72-hole total of 18-under-par 262, three strokes ahead of Patrick Cantlay and Russell Henley. The tournament carried a $40 million purse, of which the winner received $10 million in official earnings; FedExCup bonus distributions were revised in 2025 to balance season-long rewards and postseason outcomes. Tee times were moved up on Friday due to anticipated weather, and U.S. coverage was led by NBC, Golf Channel, Peacock, and ESPN+. Proud Partners included Southern Company, Coca-Cola, and Accenture, with tournament charity programs continuing to benefit the East Lake Foundation and First Tee – Metro Atlanta. Fleetwood’s victory also elevated him to a career-best Official World Golf Ranking of No. 6. [1][4][5][6][9][11][12][13][17][18][20][21][22]

## Background and Format

The Tour Championship is organized by the PGA Tour and serves as the finale of the FedExCup Playoffs. The top 30 players in the FedExCup standings after the BMW Championship qualify for East Lake, where the tournament winner is also crowned FedExCup champion. In 2025, the PGA Tour eliminated the Starting Strokes format and reverted to a standard 72-hole stroke-play event with all 30 qualifiers starting at even par; the best four-round score determines both the tournament title and the FedExCup. The PGA Tour announced the format change in May 2025 as part of broader fan-informed enhancements to the FedExCup race and event setup. [1][3][4][5][6]

Key 2025 format elements:
- 72-hole stroke play; all players start at even par. [4][5]
- The tournament winner is the FedExCup champion and earns a five-year PGA Tour exemption. [4][5]
- Field size remained 30 players (top 30 FedExCup standings after the BMW Championship). [3][4]
- FedExCup bonus distribution was rebalanced for 2025 (see “Purse and FedExCup payouts”). [4][13][14][15][16]

## Venue

East Lake Golf Club, established in 1904 and the historic home of Bobby Jones, has been the permanent home of the Tour Championship since 2005 and first hosted the event in 1998. The course underwent a comprehensive restoration led by architect Andrew Green completed in 2024, which returned many design elements to the club’s classic character while updating the test for modern professional play. [2][1][8]

Course details (2025):
- East Lake Golf Club (Atlanta, Georgia, USA). [1]
- Par 70; official tournament yardage 7,440 yards. [1][12]
- The PGA Tour preview stated a planned setup at 7,490 yards with No. 14 reverting to a par 4 after playing as a par 5 in 2024; official event pages list 7,440 yards as used during competition. [6][1][12]
- Setup notes emphasized a firmer, faster test aligned with the winner-take-all format: primary rough targeted to 3.5 inches, greens intended to run near 13.5 feet on the Stimpmeter, tighter landing areas (notably No. 17), and expanded short grass around select greens (e.g., left of No. 8). [7][6]
- The restoration highlighted unique green shapes allowing varied hole locations and emphasized precision iron play and recovery options from Zoysia chipping areas. [8][6]

## Field and Qualification

Thirty players qualified via the FedExCup standings following the BMW Championship. Six former FedExCup champions were in the field, and several players made their Tour Championship debuts. Scottie Scheffler entered as the defending FedExCup champion from 2024. The field competed over four rounds without Starting Strokes, beginning at even par. [6][1][3][12]

Qualification and entry:
- Top 30 FedExCup after BMW Championship. [3]
- Defending FedExCup champion: Scottie Scheffler (2024). [2]
- All 30 qualifiers competed; official results list all finishers and payouts. [12][13]

## Event Summary

Fleetwood closed with a 2-under-par 68 to finish at 18-under 262 and win by three strokes over Patrick Cantlay (71) and Russell Henley (69). Fleetwood and Cantlay were tied entering the final round; Cantlay bogeyed the first and doubled the second to fall behind. A two-shot swing on No. 10 narrowed Fleetwood’s lead to one, but Fleetwood then birdied Nos. 12 and 13. He limited damage to a bogey on the demanding par-3 15th—where he had made double bogey on Saturday—and closed out the win. Scheffler’s final-round rally was derailed by a missed short putt at No. 10 and a water ball with a 5-iron at No. 15 for double bogey. Keegan Bradley, within one shot early on Sunday, finished with a 70 to tie for seventh. [9][10]

Round highlights and momentum:
- Henley’s opening round established early pace; he remained in contention through Sunday (T2). [12]
- Cantlay’s birdie runs on Nos. 16–18 over the week were a notable feature of his scoring; he finished T2 at 15-under. [9]
- Corey Conners surged with a closing 62 to share fourth at 14-under. [12]
- No playoff was required; Fleetwood won by three strokes. [9][12]

## Purse and FedExCup payouts

Tournament purse and official money:
- Total purse: $40,000,000; winner’s official earnings: $10,000,000. [1][13]

FedExCup bonus rebalancing (2025):
- The PGA Tour revised FedExCup bonus distribution in 2025 to balance recognition of season-long performance and postseason results. The previous $25 million lump-sum bonus to the FedExCup winner was eliminated; the winner now receives a $10 million FedExCup bonus, with broader distributions across positions and stages of the postseason. The points leader at the BMW Championship receives a $5 million bonus, and the top 30 reaching East Lake share the remaining bonus pool, down to $355,000 for 30th. The FedExCup bonus pool remains $100 million. [4][14][15][16]

## Statistics and Records

Performance notes and records:
- Fleetwood’s victory was his first PGA Tour title and delivered the FedExCup under the 2025 winner-take-all format. [9][10]
- Fleetwood became the first player in the FedExCup era (since 2007) to make the Tour Championship his maiden PGA Tour win; historically, Chad Campbell also captured his first Tour title at the Tour Championship in 2003. [11][13]
- ShotLink context reported a more difficult Moving Day (Round 3) relative to Round 2 due to weather and setup; an under-par score beat the field average. Cantlay was 9-under on the final three holes for the week and led the field Tee to Green and on Approach while gaining approximately 3.05 strokes on the greens through Round 3. Fleetwood led Round 3 in Strokes Gained: Putting. Scottie Scheffler’s 66 in Round 3 marked his 20th consecutive round in the 60s, one shy of the PGA Tour record at the time. [7][9]

Course setup and scoring context:
- The PGA Tour emphasized tighter pins, faster greens, and heavier rough to bring winning scores closer to par after the course’s 2024 restoration matured. [6][7]
- Official event pages recorded the course at par 70 and 7,440 yards for the tournament. [1][12]

## Sponsorship and Community Impact

Sponsorship:
- Proud Partners of the Tour Championship included Southern Company, Coca-Cola, and Accenture. [1][4]

Community and charity:
- The event supports local community initiatives including the East Lake Foundation and First Tee – Metro Atlanta, among other partners. In 2025, First Tee – Metro Atlanta honorees hit the ceremonial HOPE Tee Shot, each receiving a $10,000 scholarship supported by Accenture. The tournament’s community page highlights recent charitable contributions and beneficiary programs; the 2024 event generated a record $7.2 million in charitable impact, with 2025 figures not yet published at the time of writing. [20]

## Aftermath

With his victory, Tommy Fleetwood was confirmed as the 2025 FedExCup champion and earned a five-year PGA Tour exemption. Fleetwood rose from OWGR No. 10 to No. 6, his career best, following the win. Official results and payouts were published by the PGA Tour. Media coverage highlighted Fleetwood’s breakthrough and congratulatory reactions, while the Tour’s highlights hub recapped the week’s key shots and storylines. [4][21][22][12][23]

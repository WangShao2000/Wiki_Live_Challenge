# 2011 Flat Rock–Trenton tornado (Michigan)

## Lead
The 2011 Flat Rock–Trenton tornado is sometimes cited in secondary sources as a tornado affecting the Downriver communities of Flat Rock and Trenton in Wayne County, Michigan. However, official National Weather Service (NWS) Detroit/Pontiac records, the NOAA Storm Events Database, and NWS storm survey summaries for 2011 do not document any tornado track between Flat Rock and Trenton that year. Instead, Downriver experienced several damaging severe‑thunderstorm wind events in 2011, including an episode on September 3 that injured 10 people in Southgate and caused widespread power outages, along with earlier severe weather on May 23. Michigan’s confirmed tornadoes in late May and July 2011 occurred elsewhere in the state (e.g., Shiawassee and Bay counties; a brief EF0 over Sanford Lake), not in the Flat Rock–Trenton corridor. A similarly named, well‑documented EF4 tornado tracked from Flat Rock, Alabama, to Trenton, Georgia, on April 27, 2011, during the 2011 Super Outbreak, which is likely the source of name confusion with Michigan communities of the same names [1][2][3][4][5][6][7].

## Background and meteorological synopsis
Severe convection impacted southeast Michigan on multiple days in May, July, and September 2011.

- May 23, 2011: A daylong severe weather episode produced numerous reports of damaging winds and large hail south of the M‑59 corridor, including across Wayne County and neighboring counties. The NWS Detroit/Pontiac archived the event, and Storm Data summarizes scattered wind damage with downed trees and limbs across the metro area. No tornado was confirmed in Wayne County on this date [1][6].
- May 29–31, 2011: Two tornadoes were confirmed in the NWS Detroit/Pontiac forecast area, but not in Wayne County. On May 29, an EF1 crossed southern Shiawassee County with maximum winds near 90 mph. On May 31, an EF1 tornado with peak winds near 95 mph tracked 8.4 miles across Bay County; NWS provided a detailed survey and timeline. Those events were part of broader severe storms in central and eastern Lower Michigan rather than the Detroit Downriver area [1][2].
- July 2011: Multiple severe-weather days occurred, including July 2 and July 11, with widespread damaging winds and hail. On July 24, a brief EF0 tornado over Sanford Lake in Midland County was documented; again, no Wayne County tornado was recorded in July 2011. July also featured an excessive heat episode across southeast Michigan [3].
- September 3, 2011: Thunderstorms along a cold front produced a corridor of damaging winds and hail from Ann Arbor to downtown Detroit and from Huntington Woods to Madison Heights. Approximately 100,000 DTE customers lost power. A 61‑kt (estimated) thunderstorm wind at Southgate caused 10 injuries and $10,000 in damage. NCEI categorizes this as a thunderstorm wind episode; no tornado was confirmed in Wayne County on that date [4][5].

In summary, while severe thunderstorms repeatedly affected the Downriver area in 2011, official records do not list a tornado between Flat Rock and Trenton during that year [1][3][4][5][6].

## Tornado chronology and path
- Alleged Flat Rock–Trenton (Michigan) tornado: Neither the NWS Detroit/Pontiac Storm Data narratives for 2011 nor the NOAA Storm Events Database list a tornado track starting or ending in Flat Rock or Trenton, Michigan, in 2011. As a result, there are no official NWS start or end times, coordinates, path length, or width for such a Michigan tornado in 2011. Severe damage reported in Wayne County on May 23 and September 3 was attributed to severe‑thunderstorm winds rather than tornadoes [1][4][5][6].
- Name confusion with Alabama–Georgia: On April 27, 2011, a violent EF4 tornado tracked from near Section/Flat Rock, Alabama, into northwest Georgia, causing major impacts in and near Trenton, Georgia. The NWS Huntsville survey documents formation at 4:01 p.m. CDT and dissipation at 4:36 p.m. CDT (Alabama segment), with peak winds near 190 mph; later surveys and regional documentation extend the full Alabama–Georgia track through Dade County, GA. That event is unrelated geographically to Michigan but shares place names, which likely led to the misattribution in some summaries [7].

## Impact and damage
Because no official 2011 tornado is recorded between Flat Rock and Trenton, Michigan, impacts in Downriver in 2011 derive from severe‑thunderstorm wind events rather than tornadic circulation:

- September 3, 2011 thunderstorm wind episode:
  - Southgate (Wayne County): estimated 61‑kt gust; 10 injuries; $10,000 in property damage at a community event as tents and structures were blown down [5].
  - Power outages: approximately 100,000 DTE Energy customers lost power across the metro area during the episode [5].
- May 23, 2011 severe storms: numerous reports of trees and limbs down across Wayne and adjacent counties; hail and severe wind reports were widespread south of M‑59. No Downriver tornado was confirmed [1][6].

Statewide tornado impacts in late May and July 2011 (e.g., EF1s in Shiawassee and Bay counties; EF0 over Sanford Lake) included tree and structural damage, but those events did not affect Flat Rock or Trenton in Wayne County [1][2][3].

## Ratings and surveys
- Downriver (Wayne County) in 2011: No NWS Detroit/Pontiac survey assigns an EF rating to a Flat Rock–Trenton tornado in 2011, because no such tornado is in the official record [1][3][4][6].
- Other Michigan tornadoes in 2011 (NWS Detroit/Pontiac area):
  - May 29 (Shiawassee County): EF1; max winds ~90 mph; short path; tree damage predominated [1].
  - May 31 (Bay County): EF1; peak winds ~95 mph; length ~8.4 miles; width ~200 yards; barn destroyed, roofs/garages damaged; numerous trees uprooted or snapped [2].
  - July 24 (Midland County): EF0 over Sanford Lake; width ~50 yards; winds ~65 mph; minor impacts to a boat hoist and trees [3].

These surveys underscore that 2011 tornado activity documented by NWS Detroit/Pontiac did not include a Flat Rock–Trenton (Michigan) track [1][2][3][4][6].

## Official NWS warnings and statements
- NWS Detroit/Pontiac products for 2011 include event summaries and Storm Data narratives for May, July, and September events. The May 23 page and the Storm Data reports describe severe‑thunderstorm warnings and numerous local storm reports (LSRs) but do not document a tornado warning or confirmation between Flat Rock and Trenton in 2011 [1][6].
- The NOAA Storm Events Database episode entry for September 3, 2011, provides a narrative of the thunderstorm wind event and lists the Southgate injuries; it does not contain a Wayne County tornado entry for that date [5].

## Response and aftermath
- September 3, 2011: Local emergency services responded to wind damage across multiple communities, including Southgate. Utilities conducted restoration operations following roughly 100,000 DTE customer outages in the metro area. The event was handled as a severe‑thunderstorm wind episode; no tornado‑specific disaster declarations were recorded for Flat Rock or Trenton [5].
- May 23, 2011: Municipal public works and utilities cleared downed trees and repaired scattered infrastructure following widespread damaging winds and hail south of M‑59; again, no tornado in Downriver was documented by NWS [1][6].

## Broader significance
- Michigan’s 2011 severe weather: The state saw multiple severe episodes in late spring and summer, including two NWS‑surveyed EF1 tornadoes in the Detroit/Pontiac forecast area (Shiawassee and Bay counties) and one brief EF0 over Sanford Lake, plus several damaging wind events that affected metro Detroit and Downriver. These events were not part of the late‑April 2011 Super Outbreak that devastated parts of the Southeast U.S. [1][2][3].
- Place‑name confusion: The well‑known April 27, 2011 EF4 tornado from Flat Rock, Alabama, to Trenton, Georgia, during the 2011 Super Outbreak is likely the reason a “2011 Flat Rock–Trenton tornado” is sometimes mistakenly associated with Michigan communities of the same names. Official NWS records for Michigan do not include such a tornado in Wayne County in 2011 [7][1][4][6].

## Notes on sources and verification
- This article prioritizes official NWS Detroit/Pontiac Storm Data reports and event pages, and the NOAA/NCEI Storm Events Database. Those sources show no NWS‑confirmed 2011 tornado track between Flat Rock and Trenton, Michigan. Where some community damage in 2011 has been widely attributed to a “tornado,” official surveys attribute the Downriver impacts to severe‑thunderstorm winds. The Alabama–Georgia EF4 is documented by NWS Huntsville and is geographically distinct from Michigan [1][2][3][4][5][6][7].

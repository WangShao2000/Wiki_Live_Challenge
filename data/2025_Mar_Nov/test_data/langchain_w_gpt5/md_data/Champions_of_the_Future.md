# Champions of the Future

## Lead

Champions of the Future (often abbreviated as COTF) is an international karting series launched in 2020 and organized by RGMMC Group, a Switzerland-based motorsport promoter. The series stages multi-round championships primarily for OK and OK-Junior classes under the CIK-FIA technical framework, and positions itself as a high-level preparatory competition that closely mirrors circuits, sporting procedures, and technical conditions used in FIA Karting European and World Championship events. Its events are live-streamed globally and frequently covered by specialist karting media, which highlight its role in talent development within the single-seater pathway. The scope of this article is the karting series organized by RGMMC under the “Champions of the Future” banner; other unrelated uses of the phrase are not covered here. [Champions of the Future – Official Site](https://www.championskarting.com), [RGMMC Group – Official Site](https://www.rgmmc.com), [FIA Karting – News](https://www.fiakarting.com/news)

Champions of the Future is an international karting championship platform:
- Domain and scope: multi-event, professional-level karting competition in Europe, targeted at OK and OK-Junior categories that feed into FIA single-seater talent pipelines. [Champions of the Future – Official Site](https://www.championskarting.com)
- Founding and organizer: launched by RGMMC Group in 2020; RGMMC is a Swiss promoter known for organizing high-profile karting series and promotion activities for international karting. [RGMMC Group – Official Site](https://www.rgmmc.com)
- Relationship to governing frameworks: events run to the CIK-FIA technical basis (chassis, engine homologation, tyres and fuel practices aligned with OK/OK-Junior categories), with sporting procedures and scrutineering modeled on international standards and implemented under local ASN permits. [Champions of the Future – Official Site](https://www.championskarting.com), [Vroom Kart – COTF Event Coverage](https://www.vroomkart.com/news)

## History and Development

- Launch and early phase (2020): COTF debuted in 2020, a season shaped by the COVID-19 pandemic and public-health restrictions across Europe. Despite disruptions, the series delivered rounds used by top teams to prepare for FIA Karting pinnacle events, including Portimão, which hosted the FIA Karting World Championships that year. [Champions of the Future – Official Site](https://www.championskarting.com), [FIA Karting – News](https://www.fiakarting.com/news)
- Consolidation (2021–2022): The series consolidated an annual “Euro Series” format with multi-round calendars on established European circuits such as Zuera (Spain), Genk (Belgium), Kristianstad (Sweden), Lonato and Franciacorta (Italy), and Portimão (Portugal). Factory teams across major chassis brands (e.g., Tony Kart, KR/IAME, Parolin, Birel ART) routinely entered, reinforcing the championship’s competitive depth. [Champions of the Future – Official Site](https://www.championskarting.com), [Vroom Kart – COTF Event Coverage](https://www.vroomkart.com/news)
- Expansion and refinement (2023–2025): COTF maintained the Euro Series and added or trialed early-season “Winter” formats and calendar refinements to continue mirroring FIA schedules. The series’ organization emphasized Super Heats and detailed sporting procedures similar to FIA Karting events, helping teams benchmark performance and logistics. [Champions of the Future – Official Site](https://www.championskarting.com), [FIA Karting – News](https://www.fiakarting.com/news)

The group’s leadership is responsible for calendar construction, sporting format, event production, stewarding appointments, and media operations in line with ASN authorizations. [RGMMC Group – Official Site](https://www.rgmmc.com)
- Key personnel and officials: The series employs experienced race directors, technical delegates, and stewards familiar with FIA Karting and CIK-FIA procedures, executing scrutineering and sporting oversight at each event. [Champions of the Future – Official Site](https://www.championskarting.com)
- Regulatory framework: Technical rules follow the CIK-FIA OK and OK-Junior homologations (chassis, engines, tyres, fuel), with event-specific supplementary regulations, bulletins, and official notices published by the series. ## Competition Format and Classes

- Classes: OK (senior) and OK-Junior form the core of COTF competition; eligibility follows international karting age criteria set by CIK-FIA guidance via national ASNs. [FIA Karting – Sporting framework](https://www.fiakarting.com/regulations), [Champions of the Future – Official Site](https://www.championskarting.com)
- Event structure: Typical weekends include free practice, qualifying practice, multiple qualifying heats (grids set by time), Super Heats, and Finals per class. Grid sizes are capped per circuit homologation, with split groups when entries exceed capacity. [Champions of the Future – Official Site](https://www.championskarting.com)
- Points and standings: Points are awarded across Heats/Super Heats and Finals to determine round results and cumulative championship standings. The series publishes detailed points tables and final classifications at the end of each season. [Champions of the Future – Official Site](https://www.championskarting.com)
- Equipment and suppliers: COTF aligns with OK/OK-Junior homologations. Tyres and fuel used at events are selected within homologated supplier frameworks commonly used in FIA Karting (e.g., LeCont tyres and Panta Racing Fuel have been specified for OK/OK-Junior in FIA Karting), enabling consistency in performance and setup. [FIA Karting – Homologations](https://www.fiakarting.com/homologations), [LeCont Tyres – Official](https://lecont.it/), [Panta Racing Fuel – Official](https://pantaracingfuel.it/)
- Scrutineering and controls: Scrutineering covers chassis and engine identification, tyre scanning/control, fuel conformity, and parc fermé procedures, with on-event bulletins overseeing compliance and steward decisions. [Champions of the Future – Documents/Regulations](https://www.championskarting.com)

## Notable Competitors and Alumni

COTF has featured many drivers who progressed into FIA Formula 4, Formula Regional, FIA Formula 3 and FIA Formula 2:
- Andrea Kimi Antonelli: Competed in international OK/OK-Junior karting before moving into F4 and beyond, widely covered as a Mercedes-AMG junior driver. His karting trajectory appears in independent coverage of elite OK-level series, including preparatory events. [FIA Karting – News](https://www.fiakarting.com/news)
- Arvid Lindblad: Red Bull Junior Team driver who raced at the top level in OK/OK-Junior competition, then advanced into FIA F4 and FIA F3. [FIA Karting – News](https://www.fiakarting.com/news)
- Freddie Slater: A leading OK-Junior and OK competitor who progressed into single-seaters (F4/FRECA) with coverage noting his karting success prior to car racing. [FIA Karting – News](https://www.fiakarting.com/news)
- Tuukka Taponen: Ferrari Driver Academy driver with top-level OK achievements before moving into FIA F4/F3 pathways, referenced by independent karting coverage and FIA Karting. [FIA Karting – News](https://www.fiakarting.com/news)

Independent karting outlets and FIA Karting reports commonly cite Champions of the Future rounds as relevant preparatory stages for these drivers, particularly on circuits later used for European or World title events.

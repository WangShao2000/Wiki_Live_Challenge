# 2025 Enderlin tornado

The 2025 Enderlin tornado was a violent, long-track tornado that struck rural areas south and east of Enderlin, North Dakota, on June 20, 2025. In post-event analysis published on October 6, 2025, the National Weather Service (NWS) in Grand Forks upgraded the tornado to EF5, with estimated peak winds greater than 210 mph. The tornado killed three people and produced severe damage across parts of Ransom and Cass counties. It was the first EF5 tornado confirmed in the United States since May 2013 and the first in North Dakota since 1957. The NWS designated the storm “Enderlin Tornado #1” to distinguish it from other tornadoes in the area that night. The event occurred during a broader severe-weather episode that included multiple tornadoes, a fast-moving derecho, and a prolonged period of destructive winds across portions of North Dakota and adjacent Minnesota. [1][2][7][4]

## Background and geographic context

Enderlin is a city in southeastern North Dakota located primarily in Ransom County, with parts of the surrounding rural area extending into Cass County. The tornado’s path began a few miles south of Enderlin in Ransom County and crossed into Cass County before dissipating, affecting farmsteads, rail infrastructure, and utility networks in both counties. The tornado formed in the context of a larger severe weather episode across the Northern Plains that featured discrete supercells ahead of an approaching convective complex, followed by a powerful derecho and then an unusual multi-hour period of severe post-derecho winds associated with a mesoscale convective vortex (MCV). [1][2]

## Meteorological synopsis

According to NWS event documentation, supercell thunderstorms developed in southeast North Dakota during the evening hours of June 20 ahead of a convective system that later evolved into a derecho. This derecho produced widespread severe wind gusts—many over 60 mph and some exceeding 100 mph—and was followed by an additional 2–3 hours of destructive winds over portions of central and east-central North Dakota and adjacent Minnesota as an MCV persisted behind the main line. The NWS summarized the environment as supportive of strong tornadoes prior to the line’s passage, transitioning to a severe wind event as upscale growth ensued. [2]

The Storm Prediction Center (SPC) highlighted the region for significant severe weather on June 20, 2025. The agency issued multiple mesoscale discussions during the late afternoon and evening (e.g., MDs 1386 at 8:17 p.m. CDT, 1387 at 8:33 p.m. CDT, 1388 at 8:47 p.m. CDT, 1389 at 10:06 p.m. CDT, and 1392 at 11:41 p.m. CDT), reflecting the evolving threats of tornadoes and damaging winds. SPC also issued Severe Thunderstorm Watches and a Tornado Watch covering the region through the late evening and overnight hours. Public communications from SPC during the day described an Enhanced Risk of severe storms over North Dakota into northern Minnesota. [8][2][9]

## Watches, warnings, and lead times

- Watches: The State of North Dakota’s disaster request notes a Severe Thunderstorm Watch was issued at 3:45 p.m. CDT, followed by a Tornado Watch issued at 7:50 p.m. CDT and valid through 3:00 a.m. CDT on June 21. The Enderlin tornado touched down at 11:02 p.m. CDT, placing it well within the Tornado Watch period and providing a watch lead time of a little over three hours. The NWS Grand Forks event summary lists SPC Watches 447 (Severe Thunderstorm), 448 (Tornado), and 449 (Severe Thunderstorm) as part of the sequence that evening. [4][3]

- Warnings: The tornado occurred between 11:02 and 11:21 p.m. CDT in the NWS Grand Forks forecast area. While the specific Tornado Warning text and polygon for the Enderlin tornado are not included in the cited NWS public pages, the event occurred during active watches and a high-impact severe weather episode documented by NWS Grand Forks and SPC. [1][2][8]

## Occurrence details

- Date and local time: June 20, 2025; 11:02–11:21 p.m. CDT (UTC−05:00) [1]  
- EF rating: EF5 (final); preliminary assessments referenced EF3 intensity prior to the upgrade [1][4]  
- Peak winds: Greater than 210 mph (final estimate) [1]  
- Path length: 12.10 miles [1]  
- Maximum width: 1,850 yards (approximately 1.05 miles) [1]  
- Counties affected: Ransom and Cass counties, North Dakota [1]  
- Start point: About 3 miles south of Enderlin, Ransom County, ND (46.5728, −97.6031) at 11:02 p.m. CDT [1]  
- End point: About 5 miles south-southwest of Alice, Cass County, ND (46.6870, −97.5803) at 11:21 p.m. CDT [1]

The tornado was produced by a supercell that tracked from Ransom County into Cass County. The NWS Grand Forks post-event analysis notes that WSR-88D storm-relative velocity data from the KMVX radar showed extreme rotation concurrent with the period of peak damage intensity. [1][2]

## Damage survey and EF5 upgrade

On October 6, 2025, the NWS in Grand Forks issued a Public Information Statement upgrading “Enderlin Tornado #1” to EF5. The upgrade followed additional surveys and collaboration with wind-damage experts using forensic methods. A key focus was a train derailment south of Enderlin in which fully loaded grain hopper cars were tipped and empty tanker cars were lofted. One empty tanker car was thrown approximately 475.7 feet (145 m) from its prior coupled position. Based on expert analyses, these outcomes required winds exceeding 200 mph; the NWS adopted an estimated maximum wind speed greater than 210 mph. The analysis cited agreement with the peak storm-relative velocity signals sampled by the KMVX radar. [1]

Beyond the rail impacts, NWS documented severe tree damage with near-complete denuding and debarking (“sandpapering”) in a river valley and complete destruction at one farmstead, where only the foundation remained and debris was carried downwind. The agency noted anchoring issues at that location that limited a local, structure-based damage rating, underscoring the importance of multiple independent indicators (including the rail evidence) in the overall EF5 determination. [1]

The final upgrade superseded preliminary materials that referred to EF3 intensity near Enderlin in the immediate aftermath; some state documents produced in late June and July 2025 continued to reference the preliminary rating, while acknowledging three tornado-related fatalities associated with this storm. [4][3]

## Impacts

- Casualties: The Enderlin tornado caused three fatalities and no reported injuries, according to the NWS. Local reporting identified the victims as Michael and Katherine Dehn (both 73) and Marcario Lucio (89). The Cass County Sheriff stated that deputies were dispatched to rural Enderlin at approximately 11:40 p.m. CDT for tornado damage to a residence, where storm chasers had located two deceased individuals; a third deceased person was later found. [1][5][6]

- Structural and agricultural damage: Local officials reported roughly 10 homes near Enderlin demolished or damaged, along with extensive tree damage and severe impacts to farmsteads. A free-standing cell phone tower collapsed. Vegetation damage included crop destruction, widespread tree snapping, and debarking along portions of the path. [1][5]

- Rail and utility infrastructure: The tornado derailed 33 rail cars south of Enderlin (a mix of fully loaded grain hoppers and empty tankers) and displaced at least one tanker car hundreds of feet from the tracks. The larger severe-weather episode also caused widespread power outages—tens of thousands of customers at peak statewide—with prolonged rural restoration timelines and many broken poles across multiple rural electric cooperatives. Cass County Electric alone reported thousands of outages and significant distribution damage. [7][4][3]

- Transportation and communications: The broader event produced multiple roadway disruptions and damaged communication systems, including public safety radio and cellular infrastructure in several communities. [3]

## Emergency response and aftermath

State and local officials mounted a multi-agency response. The Governor of North Dakota activated the State Emergency Operations Plan and requested a Major Disaster Declaration for the June 20–21 storms, citing multiple tornadoes, winds over 100 mph, three tornado-related fatalities in the Enderlin area, and widespread infrastructure damage. Local emergency managers conducted damage assessments; the American Red Cross opened resources and provided meals and assistance; and utility crews and mutual aid partners undertook extended restoration work. State agencies coordinated debris removal, hazardous materials guidance, agricultural impact assessments, and public information. Community groups and volunteers assisted with cleanup and relief in the days following the storm. [3][5][6][4]

## Context and significance

The Enderlin tornado occurred as part of a high-impact severe weather episode in North Dakota that featured discrete supercells, a damaging derecho, and unusual multi-hour post-derecho severe winds tied to an MCV. Within this context, the Enderlin tornado stands out for both its human toll and its meteorological significance: it is the first EF5 tornado confirmed in the United States since the Moore, Oklahoma tornado of May 20, 2013, and the first in North Dakota since the Fargo tornado of 1957. NWS Grand Forks explicitly designated the storm “Enderlin Tornado #1,” reflecting the presence of additional tornadoes in the vicinity that evening. [1][2][7]

## Naming and scope

Official NWS materials refer to the storm as “Enderlin Tornado #1,” with the path beginning south of Enderlin in Ransom County and ending south-southwest of Alice in Cass County. Some early state documents and news stories described it as the “Enderlin tornado” or “near Enderlin” with a preliminary EF3 rating; the NWS Grand Forks Public Information Statement of October 6, 2025 supersedes preliminary characterizations by issuing the final EF5 rating and detailed path statistics. This article’s scope is limited to that EF5-rated tornado. [1][4][3][5]

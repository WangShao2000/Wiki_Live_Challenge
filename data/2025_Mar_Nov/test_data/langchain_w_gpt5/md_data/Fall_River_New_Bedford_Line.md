# Fall River/New Bedford Line

The term Fall River/New Bedford Line refers to the railroad corridor serving the South Coast cities of Fall River and New Bedford, Massachusetts, both historically and in contemporary usage. Historically, it denotes the New Bedford and Fall River branches of the Old Colony Railroad (later the New York, New Haven and Hartford Railroad) linking those ports to Taunton and the Boston area. In current practice, Massachusetts agencies primarily refer to the corridor and the MBTA’s commuter rail restoration as South Coast Rail, which launched revenue service to Fall River and New Bedford in 2024 via a Middleborough routing (Phase 1), with a longer-term plan to complete a Stoughton routing (Phase 2) that was the subject of the earlier environmental review and federal Record of Decision. This article treats “Fall River/New Bedford Line” as the paired branches to Fall River and New Bedford and summarizes their development from the 19th century to the present MBTA South Coast Rail service, including planning, infrastructure, operations, governance, environmental review, and future plans [1][2][3][4].

## Naming and scope

- Agencies and project materials primarily use South Coast Rail to describe the MBTA commuter rail restoration to Fall River and New Bedford and the overall program including both Phase 1 (via Middleborough) and Phase 2 (via Stoughton) [1][2].  
- The physical rail corridors are typically called by their freight subdivision names: the Fall River Secondary and New Bedford Secondary, owned by the Commonwealth and used by both passenger and freight services [2].  
- In public-facing MBTA materials after the start of service, timetables and station pages refer to New Bedford Line and Fall River Line branches under the South Coast Rail umbrella [1][5][6].  
- Historically, the branches were components of the Old Colony system and, after 1893, the New York, New Haven and Hartford Railroad; in this context, “Fall River line” can also appear in historical sources, but that phrase historically also referred to combined rail–steamship services to New York and should be distinguished by context [2].

## History

### 19th and early 20th centuries

- The New Bedford and Taunton Railroad opened in 1840, creating a rail link from New Bedford to Taunton and, via connections, to Boston; the Fall River Branch Railroad opened in the 1840s linking Fall River to the Old Colony network. These and related lines were consolidated into the Old Colony Railroad by the 1870s and leased to the New York, New Haven and Hartford Railroad in 1893, placing both branches in the New Haven system [2].  
- Through the mid-20th century, frequent passenger service operated between Boston and South Coast communities via the Old Colony network [2].

### Cessation of prior passenger service and Old Colony revival

- Passenger service on the Old Colony network ended in the 1950s, with the last Old Colony passenger trains discontinued in 1959 amid declining ridership and infrastructure disinvestment, leaving Fall River and New Bedford without direct passenger rail to Boston for decades [2].  
- MBTA restored commuter rail on portions of the Old Colony system in 1997 (e.g., to Middleborough/Lakeville) but not to the South Coast branches [2].

### South Coast Rail project development

- Massachusetts initiated the South Coast Rail (SCR) planning process in the 1990s, with extensive state and federal environmental review in the 2000s–2010s. The combined state Final Environmental Impact Report and federal Final Environmental Impact Statement (FEIR/FEIS) was published in 2013, identifying the Stoughton alignment as the preferred alternative for full Boston–Taunton–Fall River/New Bedford service and documenting substantial environmental mitigation requirements, particularly in and near the Hockomock Swamp Area of Critical Environmental Concern in Easton and Raynham [3].  
- The U.S. Army Corps of Engineers issued a federal Record of Decision (ROD) for the Stoughton alignment in 2017, concluding the federal NEPA process for that route [4].  
- To deliver service sooner while funding and permitting for the Stoughton route advanced, MassDOT and the MBTA adopted a phased approach in 2018: Phase 1 via Middleborough to initiate service using existing rights-of-way upgraded for passenger speeds and new stations, and Phase 2 to complete the Stoughton alignment later. The Phase 1 Middleborough approach underwent a separate Environmental Assessment under NEPA, culminating in a federal Finding of No Significant Impact in 2020 [2][7].  
- Early works and main construction for Phase 1 proceeded during 2019–2024, including track, signals, grade crossing upgrades, bridges, layover facilities, and new stations on the Fall River and New Bedford branches [1][2].

### Launch of MBTA service

- The MBTA announced the start of South Coast Rail service effective July 1, 2024, with scheduled commuter rail service from Boston South Station to both Fall River and New Bedford via the existing Old Colony route to Middleborough and the newly upgraded Middleborough Secondary, Fall River Secondary, and New Bedford Secondary [5].  

## Route and infrastructure

### Ownership and route segments

- The Commonwealth of Massachusetts (MassDOT) owns the Fall River Secondary and New Bedford Secondary, acquired as part of broader rail asset agreements with CSX, and grants freight operating rights on those lines while coordinating passenger improvements with the MBTA [2][8][9].  
- Passenger trains operate from Boston South Station over the MBTA Old Colony mainline to Braintree and the Middleborough/Lakeville Line to a new Middleborough station; from there, the route uses the Middleborough Secondary to Cotley (Taunton) and then diverges southwesterly on the Fall River Secondary to Fall River and southerly on the New Bedford Secondary to New Bedford [1][2][5][6].  
- The Phase 1 program included track upgrades to passenger standards, new and rehabilitated bridges, new signal systems, Positive Train Control (PTC), passing sidings, and numerous grade crossing safety enhancements across both branches [1][2][10].

### Stations and facilities

All stations opened with the launch of revenue service on July 1, 2024 [5][6]:

- Middleborough (new station on the Middleborough Secondary) [1][2].  
- East Taunton (serving Taunton and the Myles Standish Industrial Park) [1][2].  
- Freetown (Assonet/Freetown) [1][2].  
- Fall River (downtown Fall River) [1][2].  
- Church Street (north New Bedford) [1][2].  
- New Bedford (downtown New Bedford) [1][2].

Additional facilities:

- Layover yards were constructed to support operations: New Bedford Layover (near the Wamsutta area north of downtown) and Fall River Layover (Weaver’s Cove area), designed to store and service trainsets between trips [2][11].  
- All new stations were designed to be fully accessible, with high-level platforms and ADA-compliant features consistent with MBTA standards [1][2].

### Signals and train control

- The project installed new signal systems and extended Positive Train Control across the corridor. MBTA commuter rail uses ACSES II PTC on its territory, including the Old Colony and newly equipped South Coast Rail segments for interoperability and safety compliance [10].

## Operations and service

- Launch date: Revenue service began July 1, 2024 [5].  
- Service pattern: MBTA schedules provide separate timetables for the New Bedford Line and Fall River Line branches under South Coast Rail. Weekday service includes peak-period inbound and outbound trips and off-peak service; weekend service operates at reduced frequency relative to weekdays [5][6].  
- Travel times: Published MBTA timetables show end-to-end scheduled travel times between Boston South Station and New Bedford or Fall River of approximately 90–100 minutes, varying by train and time of day [5][6].  
- Fares and zones: Stations are assigned to MBTA Commuter Rail fare zones. The terminal stations at Fall River and New Bedford are in the highest outer zones, with intermediate stations assigned accordingly; fares follow MBTA’s zone-based pricing and reduced fare programs [12].  
- Rolling stock and operator: Service is operated by Keolis Commuter Services under contract to the MBTA using standard MBTA diesel push–pull commuter rail equipment (locomotive-hauled bi-level and single-level coaches) [13].  
- Integration and connections: The South Coast Rail branches integrate into the MBTA Commuter Rail network, sharing trunk routing to South Station. Local bus connections are available via the Southeastern Regional Transit Authority (SRTA) at the downtown Fall River and New Bedford stations and adjacent bus facilities [1][2].

## Governance, funding, and cost

- Agencies: MassDOT owns the South Coast Rail corridor segments (Fall River and New Bedford Secondaries) and serves as the program sponsor; the MBTA is responsible for service planning, capital delivery, and operations through its commuter rail operating contract [1][2][13].  
- Environmental review: The Stoughton alignment completed an FEIR/FEIS (2013) and received a federal ROD (2017); the Middleborough Phase 1 received an FRA FONSI (2020) [3][4][7].  
- Funding and delivery: Phase 1 was funded primarily by Commonwealth bond authorizations in the MassDOT/MBTA capital program, with procurement packages covering track, signal, station, bridge, grade crossing, and layover works. MBTA Board and MassDOT program updates have reported a Phase 1 total program budget on the order of approximately $1.3 billion, inclusive of construction, design, property, and contingencies [1][2].  
- Contracting: Major construction packages were delivered through conventional design–bid–build and selected design–build elements; program management, environmental, and design services were contracted to industry consultants under MassDOT/MBTA procurements [1][2].

## Ridership

- Planning projections: Environmental and planning documents projected several thousand average weekday boardings upon stabilization of service, with demand distributed between the New Bedford and Fall River branches and additional incremental demand expected under the full Stoughton build (Phase 2) [3][7].  
- Early results: Following the July 2024 opening, initial news coverage reported strong interest and early ridership activity on launch-day and opening-week trains. The MBTA publishes system performance data; however, disaggregated, validated line-level ridership series specific to South Coast Rail may lag initial launch or be aggregated with the broader Old Colony region in early reports [5][6]. Where the MBTA has not published official line-level ridership figures, precise counts are not available in agency sources [5][6][14].

## Freight use and coordination

- Ownership and rights: MassDOT’s ownership of the Fall River and New Bedford Secondaries preserves common-carrier freight service; the Commonwealth has contracted Mass Coastal Railroad as the designated freight operator on these South Coast corridors [9][15].  
- Coordination: Project works included freight-compatible track improvements, passing capacity, and grade crossing upgrades. The MBTA/Keolis dispatcher coordinates passenger slots with the freight operator, consistent with the operating agreements and safety protocols [1][2].  

## Environmental and community impacts

- Environmental considerations were a major focus of the South Coast Rail reviews. The combined FEIR/FEIS for the Stoughton alignment (2013) detailed wetlands impacts, wildlife and habitat issues, noise and vibration, and community considerations, including the crossing of the Hockomock Swamp Area of Critical Environmental Concern. The USACE ROD (2017) documented the federal decision and required mitigation [3][4].  
- For Phase 1 via Middleborough, the 2020 FRA FONSI concluded that, with specified mitigation and improvements, the Middleborough approach would not result in significant environmental impacts. Mitigations included wetland restoration/replication, stormwater improvements, noise and vibration mitigation where warranted, grade crossing safety upgrades, and measures to protect cultural and historic resources [7].  
- Community engagement occurred through MassDOT/MBTA public meetings, design reviews, and station-area planning with municipalities along the route [1][2].

## Future plans and status (as of December 12, 2025)

- Phase 2 (Stoughton alignment): The state’s long-term plan remains to construct the Stoughton route to provide a more direct connection between Boston and Taunton/Fall River/New Bedford, consistent with the 2013 FEIR/FEIS and 2017 federal ROD. MassDOT/MBTA materials indicate the project is advancing planning and preliminary engineering for Phase 2 subject to funding availability; as of late 2025, construction of Phase 2 has not been fully funded nor scheduled for delivery, and additional permitting, design updates, and budget development are ongoing [1][2][4].  
- Near-term program: In the interim, MassDOT and the MBTA continue to operate and refine Phase 1 services, monitor ridership and on-time performance, and coordinate with municipalities on station access and transit-oriented development in the Fall River and New Bedford station areas [1][2].  

## Notability and significance

- South Coast Rail reconnects Fall River and New Bedford—the largest Massachusetts cities without passenger rail service for decades—to the Boston regional rail network. State and local officials and media coverage described the opening as a major regional mobility improvement expected to reduce travel times for some commuters, broaden access to jobs and education, and support economic development initiatives in the South Coast region [5][6][16][17][18].  
- The program has also been notable for its extensive environmental review, the decision to phase implementation with an initial Middleborough service, and the long planning horizon to complete the Stoughton alignment [2][3][4][7].

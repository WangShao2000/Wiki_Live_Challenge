# Diego García Miravete

Diego García Miravete (full name Diego Leonardo García Miravete) is a Mexican former gridiron football player and coach known for leading the Cóndores de la UNAM to nine national championships in Mexico’s collegiate league system, making him one of the winningest head coaches in Mexican college football history. Independent media and institutional sources document his role in the first ONEFA championship (1978), a four‑year title streak in the mid‑1980s, and his stewardship of UNAM’s classic rivalry victories in the era when its teams were split into multiple squads. He later coached at UANL’s Auténticos Tigres and within the Tec de Monterrey system, and he served as head coach for Mexico’s all‑star teams in the Tazón Azteca and the junior national team in the NFL Global Junior Championship. His honors include the Premio Luchador Olmeca (2010) from CODEME, and he is listed among historical honorees by the Museo Salón de la Fama del Futbol Americano de México. [1][2][3][4][5][6][13]

## Early life and playing background

Published interviews and UNAM media archives portray García Miravete as a long‑time UNAM football figure who first engaged with the sport in the 1960s and later played within the university’s programs before moving into coaching. A 2020 interview outlines his early aspiration to play quarterback in the 1960s and his later transition to defensive roles as part of UNAM teams, contextualizing his subsequent coaching philosophy. ## Coaching career

### Cóndores UNAM

- First ONEFA champion (1978): When Mexico’s Organización Nacional Estudiantil de Futbol Americano (ONEFA) launched on 12 January 1978, the inaugural season culminated with the Cóndores de la UNAM defeating their rival at Estadio Olímpico Universitario to become the first ONEFA champions under García Miravete. [1]  
- Championship record: Institutional and sports media credit García Miravete with nine national titles at UNAM’s Cóndores. Fundación UNAM attributes nine of the team’s ten titles to his tenure, while ESPN summarizes him among coaches with nine championships in Mexico’s college football. [2][3]  
- Mid‑1980s streak: ESPN’s regional coverage notes the four consecutive titles that UNAM’s Cóndores won from 1983 through 1986 under García Miravete. [12]  
- Rivalry record: During the era (1970–1997) when UNAM’s main team was split into three squads, UNAM won 11 Clásicos Poli‑Universidad; Fundación UNAM reports that seven of those rivalry wins occurred under García Miravete. [2]  
- UNAM coverage: The university’s Gaceta UNAM recorded Cóndores events and results in this period, including coverage of international matchups and season narratives, situating García Miravete within the official media documentation of UNAM sport. [7][8]

### Auténticos Tigres UANL

- UANL head coach: García Miravete later led the Auténticos Tigres of the Universidad Autónoma de Nuevo León. UANL’s digital hemeroteca notes his tenure from 1995 to 1999 and recalls internal challenges during that era. [9]

### Tec de Monterrey system

- Tec Santa Fe: García Miravete was announced as head coach for the Tec de Monterrey Campus Santa Fe program on 23 May 2010. [11]  
- Additional Tec roles: Coverage from receptor (a dedicated Mexican American football outlet) recounts his coaching path within the Tec system after his UNAM and UANL years. [10]

## National teams and representative coaching

- Tazón Azteca (Aztec Bowl): García Miravete served as head coach of Mexico’s collegiate all‑star team in notable editions, including the XIII Aztec Bowl on 15 December 1984 (Mexico 22–15 Tarleton State) and XIX on 15 December 1991 (Mexico 35–28 Southwestern Oklahoma State), both at Estadio Olímpico Universitario. [5]  
- NFL Global Junior Championship: Historical coverage of the NFL’s Global Junior Championship notes that Mexico won the first two editions (1997 and 1998). García Miravete is quoted in reporting around the 1998 event, underscoring his coaching role with the junior national team. [6]

## Awards and honors

- Premio Luchador Olmeca (2010): On 17 December 2010, García Miravete received the Premio Luchador Olmeca, conferred by Mexico’s national sports confederation CODEME, alongside other distinguished athletes. In remarks carried by UANL’s official site, he characterized the award as recognition for Mexican coaches in the sport. [4]  
- Historical recognition: The Museo Salón de la Fama del Futbol Americano de México lists García Miravete among its historical honorees, reflecting his inclusion in the Hall’s archival pages of notable figures from the 1965–1969 era and beyond. [13]  
- Coaching record in media: ESPN’s summary of championship head coaches in Mexico’s college football includes García Miravete among those with nine titles, reinforcing his standing in the sport’s historical record. [3]

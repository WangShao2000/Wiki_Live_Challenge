# Kururin Squash!

Kururin Squash! (Japanese: くるりんスカッシュ！; Hepburn: Kururin Sukasshu!) is a 2004 action video game developed by Eighting and published by Nintendo for the Nintendo GameCube. It is the third title in the Kururin series, following the Game Boy Advance games Kuru Kuru Kururin (2001) and Kururin Paradise (2003), and the first entry on a home console. The game was released in Japan on October 14, 2004 and is officially styled with an exclamation mark. It adds attack-capable “Helirin” variants, boss encounters, local multiplayer for up to four players, and Game Boy Advance link cable features to the series’ core “rotating craft through narrow courses” concept. [1][4][5][10]

## Gameplay

Kururin Squash! builds on the series’ central idea of piloting a perpetually rotating craft (the Helirin) through maze-like stages while avoiding contact with walls and obstacles. The GameCube entry presents the action with 3D graphics and an over-the-shoulder/angled camera, while keeping the precise collision-avoidance and route-learning that define the series. The GameCube controller’s analog stick is used for movement, with button inputs assigned to actions on certain Helirin variants. [1][4][10][12]

Distinctive mechanics introduced in this installment include multiple “action Helirin” forms that can attack or traverse hazards in new ways. Official materials and contemporary coverage describe variants that can punch, emit flame, and submerge to pass underwater obstacles; these forms are leveraged in new stage types, including one-on-one boss battles and competitive multiplayer modes. The official site also details an action-learning system with training modules tied to specific areas, introducing actions under controlled conditions before they appear in main stages. [3][5]

Modes and features presented on the official site and in press coverage include:
- Story-style progression with boss encounters built around the new action Helirin mechanics. [5]
- Time Attack, with leaderboard-oriented stage completions. [1]
- Versus modes for up to four players on split-screen (racing and battle-style competition). [1][4][5][10]
- Game Boy Advance link cable connectivity (GBA–GameCube), noted on the product page’s specifications. [1]

Contemporary import coverage from IGN further notes course elements such as springs that alter rotation and special Helirin forms used for attacks and rail-riding, and summarizes the multiplayer as split-screen up to four players. [10]

## Narrative and presentation

The game conveys a light narrative through short sequences between stages, while keeping its focus on mechanics. Eurogamer’s review characterizes Kururin Squash! as “built around guiding a perpetually spinning stick through maze-like courses” and observes that the 3D backgrounds and “bizarre” story interludes are aesthetically charming but not central to play. [12]

Visually, the title uses colorful 3D environments and varied stage motifs aligned to each Helirin action type. Audio was composed by Atsuhiro Motoyama (本山淳弘), credited as the game’s composer. [8][9]

## Release and distribution

- Platform: Nintendo GameCube (product code DOL-P-GKQJ). [7]
- Publisher and developer: Nintendo; Eighting. [1][4][8]
- Japan release date: October 14, 2004. [1][8][10]
- Price: 3,619 yen before tax on Nintendo’s product page; contemporary press lists 3,800 yen, consistent with tax-inclusive pricing at the time. [1][5][10]
- Genre and players: Action; 1–4 players (local). [1]
- Memory card blocks: 4. [1]
- Peripherals: Game Boy Advance link cable supported. [1]
- Barcode: 4902370509601. [8]

Nintendo’s Japanese product site served as the game’s official web hub, with sections for “About,” “Versus,” “Boss,” “Time Attack,” connectivity with Game Boy Advance, and “special hints.” [1][2] The title was announced and previewed in Japanese media (GAME Watch) on September 22, 2004, and appeared in IGN’s “Gaming Life in Japan” and “Now Playing in Japan” columns ahead of release, which summarized the premise and listed its budget price point. [5][11][10]

An official guidebook was published by Shogakukan as a “Nintendo Official Guidebook” (Wonder Life Special), covering characters, modes, controls, and complete stage maps for the “adventure” and “challenge” modes; retailer listings confirm the volume’s release on November 12, 2004. [15][16]

No ports, reissues, or releases in regions outside Japan have been announced on the consulted official and professional media pages. [1][4][8][10][11]

## Reception

Contemporary Japanese coverage introduced the game’s new mechanics and modes, emphasizing the addition of attack-capable Helirin, boss battles, and four-player versus play. [5]

Eurogamer’s review offered a qualified assessment: it praised the underlying concept and variety in stage types (including submarine sections, projectile waves, destructible block puzzles, and a flamethrower segment), while arguing that the main puzzle mode was comparatively undemanding and recommending the earlier Game Boy Advance entries over the GameCube installment for sustained challenge. The review also remarked that the 3D presentation and story scenes were pleasant but nonessential to the gameplay loop. [12]

In import coverage, IGN highlighted Kururin Squash! as a budget-priced GameCube release in Japan, summarizing its rotating-navigation mechanics, special Helirin forms, and four-player split-screen multiplayer. [10]

Public weekly sales charts published by Inside Games (reporting Media Create data) for October 10–17 and October 25–31, 2004, did not list Kururin Squash! among their respective top-ten software rankings, which were dominated by Game Boy Advance and PlayStation 2 titles and (later in October) Mario Tennis GC on GameCube. These charts provide market context around the week of the game’s release. [13][14]

## Legacy and impact

Kururin Squash! is frequently cited in import and retrospective discussions as the series’ 3D evolution on GameCube, retaining the precise collision-avoidance core while expanding with action-oriented Helirin variants and party modes. In later retrospectives, Hookshot Media’s Nintendo Life featured the game among “overlooked GameCube gems,” describing it as an effective extension of Kuru Kuru Kururin’s design on home console. [17]

Eighting’s official catalog lists Kururin Squash! alongside the broader Kururin series, reflecting the developer’s continued association with the brand. [4] Although no re-releases have been announced, the title remains referenced in import features and databases that catalog Japan-only GameCube releases. [10][8]

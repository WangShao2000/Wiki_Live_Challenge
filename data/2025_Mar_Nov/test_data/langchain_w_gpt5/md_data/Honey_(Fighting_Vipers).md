# Honey (Fighting Vipers)

Honey (Japanese: ハニー), localized as Candy in some Western releases, is a playable character from Sega AM2’s Fighting Vipers series, introduced in the original arcade game Fighting Vipers and its Sega Saturn home conversion. Within the games’ setting of Armstone City, she is depicted as a fashion-focused fighter who designed her own distinctive “plastic fairy” outfit; she has been cited in official materials and period coverage as part of AM2’s cast built around armor-clad, youth-oriented archetypes and enclosed-arena combat [1][2][3][6][15]. Honey is notable for her regional naming differences (Honey in Japan; Candy in North American and some European versions of the first game), for Japan‑only extras tied to her design in the Saturn version, for later reappearances in Fighting Vipers 2, and for inspiring the separate Sonic franchise character “Honey the Cat,” who became playable in the 2012 digital re-release of Sonic the Fighters [1][6][11][12][13].

## Identity and context

Honey is one of the default roster members in Fighting Vipers, a 3D fighting game developed by Sega AM2 that debuted in arcades and was later ported to the Sega Saturn. The series distinguishes itself from AM2’s Virtua Fighter line through faster pacing, enclosed arenas with destructible walls, and a costume-based armor system that can be broken to change damage and knockback dynamics [3]. The 2012 announcement by Famitsu for the Model 2 Collection re-release describes the original game as having operated in arcades in 1995, situating Honey’s debut in that context [6].

Official home manual bios emphasize Honey/Candy’s fashion motif. The Japanese Saturn manual lists the character as ハニー (Honey) and presents the roster and gameplay systems (AM2/SEGA branding is prominent on the credit pages), while the North American Saturn manual identifies her as Candy and describes her as a fashion-minded fighter who designed her own trademark outfit, aligning with the “fashion student” concept widely associated with the character in contemporary summaries of that manual text [1][2][15]. In-game, Honey fights in a rubbery, stylized costume with wing-like accents and a skirt, reflecting the series’ “armored fighter” aesthetic [1][2][15].

## Development and design

Fighting Vipers was directed by Hiroshi Kataoka under producer Yu Suzuki at Sega AM2. Credits for the Saturn version list Kataoka as director, Yu Suzuki as producer, with chief programmers Tetsuya Sugimoto and Hideya Shibazaki, and chief graphic designer Youji Kato; character designers include Manabu Sato, Kentaro Nishimura, and Kaoru Nagahama [4]. The arcade sequel Fighting Vipers 2 (1998) credits Hiroshi Kataoka as director and “Imai Toonz” (imaitoons) as original character designer, indicating continued refinement of AM2’s character concepts between installments [5].

Developer commentary on AM2’s fighting projects at the time notes a shift away from Virtua Fighter’s realism toward exaggerated, anime-like action for Fighting Vipers, with the team assembling a “matrix” of body types and styles and then applying appearances and motifs to them—consistent with Honey’s fashion-forward concept layered over a nimble, kick-oriented playstyle [20]. Contemporary and retrospective documentation of the project’s concept art and prototypes also foregrounds armor-breaking visuals and caged arenas as central themes—a backdrop against which Honey’s costume design “breaks” in dramatic ways when hit with power blows [3][15].

## Naming and localization history

- Original name and first-game localizations:
  - Japan: The character is named ハニー (Honey) in the Japanese Saturn manual and in-game [2].
  - North America and some European releases: The character was localized as “Candy” in the first Fighting Vipers home releases; the U.S. Saturn manual and in-game text use “Candy” [1]. Multiple contemporary and retrospective sources document this change in the first game [1][12][15].
- Fighting Vipers 2:
  - Honey returns in Fighting Vipers 2. In the Dreamcast PAL release, in-game footage and coverage show the character name as “Honey,” indicating that the original name was used in that region’s sequel [19].
- Digital Model 2 Collection (2012):
  - Sega’s 2012 digital re-release program covered Virtua Fighter 2, Sonic the Fighters, and Fighting Vipers. Official pages confirm the releases but do not enumerate per-character names by region [7][8][9][17]. Contemporary coverage and side-by-side builds reported that the Japanese version retained “Honey,” while Western builds of Fighting Vipers aligned with the earlier “Candy” export set; Famitsu’s contemporaneous notes confirm regionalized content and secret-conditions adjustments (see below) for the JP release [6][12][15].
- Rationale:
  - No official Sega statement or reputable press source located in this research confirms the specific rationale for the Honey→Candy renaming. Available reliable sources document that the name differed by region; the cause remains unconfirmed in primary materials and reputable interviews [1][2][6][12][15].

## Appearances and release timeline

- Fighting Vipers (Arcade, 1995): Debuted in arcades in 1995 on Sega’s Model 2 hardware, according to Famitsu’s 2012 re-release article [6].
- Fighting Vipers (Sega Saturn, 1996): Released for Sega Saturn across Japan, North America, and Europe in 1996, with Honey named Honey (Japan) and Candy (U.S./export) in manuals and in-game text [1][2][15].
- Fighting Vipers 2 (Arcade, 1998): Honey returned in the arcade sequel, which introduced revised systems and new content under director Hiroshi Kataoka; credits list imaitoons as original character designer [5].
- Fighting Vipers 2 (Dreamcast, 1999–2001): Released on Dreamcast in Japan and Europe; PAL-region gameplay captures show the name “Honey” on HUD/selection, reflecting use of her original name in that version [19].
- Model 2 Collection digital releases (PlayStation 3/Xbox 360, 2012): Fighting Vipers was reissued digitally with online features. Sony’s PlayStation Blog lists the PSN release on November 27, 2012 (North America), with further regional dates following; Xbox Live Arcade listings confirm the Microsoft release around the same period [8][9][17].

## Gameplay characteristics

Fighting Vipers’ systems revolve around:
- Enclosed arenas with breakable barriers enabling wall smashes and ring-outs via fence-breaks.
- Two-part costume armor (upper and lower) that can be broken by power hits to increase vulnerability and alter hit reactions.
- Faster pacing and combo strings distinct from Virtua Fighter’s more methodical tempo [3].

Within this framework, Honey/Candy is presented as a lighter, mobile fighter whose offense emphasizes quick strings, high/low kicks, and evasive movement to set up wall pressure and armor-break opportunities. Her distinctive skirt and upper armor break visually in combat, altering damage dynamics and emphasizing AM2’s “armored fighter” motif [3][15]. The Saturn home version manual and fighting-style integrations highlighted in Fighters Megamix documentation further codify how the Vipers’ armor affects attacks, knockback, and special techniques compared with Virtua Fighter characters [16].

## Narrative elements

Fighting Vipers is set in Armstone City, framed around a localized fighting tournament that culminates in prominent city landmarks. Honey/Candy’s official manual bio characterizes her as a fashion-focused combatant who designed her own “plastic fairy” costume and enters the tournament with self-promotional aspirations aligned to fashion or design, reflecting AM2’s cast concepting that foregrounded youth subcultures and colorful motifs [1][3]. While story modes in Vipers games are minimal, the manuals and promotional materials emphasize her personality and brand-oriented image as a fashion student/designer in a citywide competition context [1][2][3][15].

## Voice acting and credits

The Fighting Vipers Saturn credits include a “Guest Voice Actor: Hideki Kuroda (Pepsiman)” for the Japanese-only guest character Pepsiman. No credit for a dedicated voice actor specific to Honey/Candy appears in the Saturn credits list, and no reliable source consulted in this research identifies a dedicated Japanese or English voice actor for Honey [4]. Credits for Fighting Vipers 2 list key staff including director Hiroshi Kataoka and original character designer imaitoons but do not enumerate per-character voice credits in accessible summaries [5].

## Censorship and regional changes

- Naming: Honey (Japan) vs. Candy (U.S./export) in the first game’s home versions, as reflected in manuals and in-game text [1][2][12][15].
- Branding and guest character content: The Japanese Saturn version includes Pepsi branding (stage signage and props) and the guest character Pepsiman. Western versions remove/replace this branded content; Pepsiman does not appear outside Japan [3][13].
- Honey’s costume behavior (Saturn): The Japanese Saturn version includes Honey-specific secrets tied to difficulty/arcade completion; for example, her skirt can be knocked off under certain conditions (e.g., after meeting a high‑win requirement on Very Hard), not accessible in standard Versus modes. Contemporary cheat listings document these behaviors; Western versions removed or altered these region-specific features [12][13][15].
- Model 2 Collection adjustments (2012): Famitsu reported adjustments to secret thresholds (e.g., altering consecutive-win counts for Honey-related costume behaviors) in the Japanese digital release; regional builds continue to reflect differences between the original Japanese and export sets [6][15].

## Crossovers and legacy

AM2’s experimentation with bringing Sonic characters into a Virtua Fighter/Fighting Vipers–style system led to the Model 2 arcade game Sonic the Fighters. The Sonic franchise character “Honey the Cat” is a separate character concept historically linked by name and motif to Fighting Vipers’ Honey. In 2012, Sega’s digital re-release of Sonic the Fighters for PlayStation 3 and Xbox 360 restored Honey the Cat as a selectable character alongside Dr. Eggman and Metal Sonic; contemporary news coverage and achievement listings documented button inputs and selection methods to play as Honey the Cat [7][8][11][17]. The Xbox achievement “Honey the Cat” confirms the selection method and character availability in the 2012 ports [10]. Sega’s Model 2 Collection site and platform store listings substantiate the re-release framework in which these restorations appeared [7][8][9][17].

Beyond Sonic the Fighters, Honey remains associated with Fighting Vipers’ enduring identity as “the armored, fenced-arena AM2 fighter,” a distinction carried into Fighting Vipers 2 (including PAL use of “Honey” in that sequel) and into later re-releases that preserved the series’ mechanical identity and visual style [5][9][19].

## Reception

Contemporaneous coverage in specialist press and platform magazines highlighted Fighting Vipers’ armor-breaking system, fenced arenas, and youthful, stylized cast in contrast with Virtua Fighter’s realism; Official Sega Saturn Magazine’s 1996 issues featured previews, interviews, and reviews, helping establish the series’ identity at the time of its Saturn release [15]. Retrospective commentary has continued to frame Honey/Candy within this identity, noting her representative role for the series’ fashion motif, armor-break visuals, and the regional naming differences that became a talking point among enthusiasts and in later ports and collections [3][15][16]. While professional reviews from the period typically focused on overall systems and presentation rather than individual characters, contemporary and later writeups consistently credit AM2’s costume armor and enclosed-arenas concept—features in which Honey’s visual design and break states play a conspicuous role [3][15][16].

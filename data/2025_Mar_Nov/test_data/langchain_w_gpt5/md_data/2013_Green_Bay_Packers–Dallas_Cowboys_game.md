# 2013 Green Bay Packers–Dallas Cowboys NFL game

The 2013 Green Bay Packers–Dallas Cowboys game was a National Football League regular-season contest played on December 15, 2013, at AT&T Stadium in Arlington, Texas. Green Bay overcame a 23-point halftime deficit to defeat Dallas 37–36, scoring 34 points in the second half behind quarterback Matt Flynn’s four touchdown passes with Aaron Rodgers inactive due to a broken collarbone. The result kept the Packers in contention in the NFC North and damaged the Cowboys’ pursuit of the NFC East title; the game drew extensive media attention for Dallas’s late-game clock management and a disputed shift away from the run, culminating in two Tony Romo interceptions in the final three minutes. The comeback is among the largest in Packers franchise history by deficit overcome. The game aired in FOX’s national late window with Joe Buck, Troy Aikman, and Pam Oliver on the call. ## Pre-game context

Dallas entered at 7–6, contending in the NFC East. Green Bay arrived at 6–6–1, attempting to stay alive in the NFC North race while awaiting Aaron Rodgers’s return from a broken collarbone. Matt Flynn started at quarterback for the Packers; Tony Romo started for the Cowboys. Head coaches were Mike McCarthy (Packers) and Jason Garrett (Cowboys). [4][1]

Rodgers missed a sixth consecutive game due to the collarbone injury. The Cowboys were without several linebackers, and during the game they further lost Justin Durant (hamstring) and Ernie Sims (hip), while Green Bay lost TE Brandon Bostick (foot) and DE Johnny Jolly (shoulder). [4][13]

Playoff implications were direct: the Packers improved to 7–6–1 with the win, keeping their chances in the NFC North, while Dallas fell to 7–7, missing an opportunity to draw level with Philadelphia but still in position to control its fate by winning its remaining games. [4]

## Broadcast

The game was televised on FOX in the Sunday national late window (4:25 p.m. ET), with announcers Joe Buck (play-by-play), Troy Aikman (analyst), and Pam Oliver (sideline). [6]

## Game summary

Dallas built a 26–3 halftime lead through five Dan Bailey field goals and two touchdowns from Jason Witten (25-yard catch) and DeMarco Murray (1-yard run). Green Bay managed only a 57-yard Mason Crosby field goal in the first half. [1][3]

Green Bay’s response began early in the third quarter, set up by a 60-yard Eddie Lacy run and capped by a 13-yard touchdown pass from Flynn to Jordy Nelson. After Bailey’s fifth field goal (his second from 50 yards), Flynn found Andrew Quarless for a 3-yard score to make it 29–17 entering the fourth quarter. [1][3]

The fourth quarter featured rapid swings and pivotal decisions. Flynn hit James Starks for an 11-yard touchdown to pull within 29–24. A near interception by Tramon Williams was overturned by review, and Dallas extended its lead on Dez Bryant’s toe-tap 5-yard touchdown, 36–24. Flynn answered with a 3-yard touchdown to James Jones at 4:17. On Dallas’s next possession, with the Cowboys leading 36–31 and aiming to drain the clock, Romo threw a quick pass instead of executing the called run; Sam Shields intercepted at midfield with 2:58 remaining. Jason Garrett later described the call as a “run” with a “smoke or flash” option that Romo exercised, and said the quarterback “probably should have run the ball in that situation.” Green Bay capitalized, and Lacy scored from 1 yard out at 1:31; the two-point pass failed, keeping the margin at one point. Dallas’s final chance ended when Romo threw toward Cole Beasley; the diving interception by Tramon Williams was initially ruled incomplete but overturned on review at 1:24. Green Bay then ran out the clock. [1][4][5][8]

Many outlets highlighted Dallas’s run-pass balance relative to game situation. The Cowboys rushed 18 times for 134 yards (7.4 per carry) but threw 48 passes; criticism focused on abandoning the run in clock-kill scenarios, including the play preceding Shields’s interception. Garrett acknowledged Romo’s check out of the run into a quick pass; Beasley said of the final interception, “I stopped when I should have kept going… That was my fault.” [5][7][8]

## Records and milestones

Green Bay’s rally from a 23-point halftime deficit (26–3) is among the largest comebacks in franchise history by margin, and the team scored 34 points after halftime to secure the win. Flynn threw four touchdown passes in the second half, a performance widely noted in national coverage. [1][12][4]

Contemporary coverage also observed that, at the time, Flynn “accomplished something Brett Favre and Aaron Rodgers had not—winning in Dallas,” reflecting the Packers’ historical difficulties on the road against the Cowboys; this commentary framed the game’s significance within franchise lore. [4]

## Controversies, decisions, and officiating

Two late interceptions by Romo shaped the finish. On the first, with Dallas ahead 36–31 and seeking to burn clock, Romo threw a quick pass instead of executing the called run; Sam Shields intercepted at 2:58 remaining. Garrett later said the original call was a run with a built-in “smoke or flash” throw option and acknowledged that running the ball would have been preferable in that situation. On the second, Romo targeted Cole Beasley; Williams’s diving interception was initially ruled incomplete but overturned on review at 1:24, ending Dallas’s hopes. [1][5][8]

The game sparked significant discussion of Dallas’s play selection. The Cowboys averaged 7.4 yards per carry with Murray but threw 48 passes; critics argued that abandoning the run contributed to the collapse. Garrett’s comments about Romo’s checks and the team’s “aggressive” approach were central to this debate. Beasley publicly accepted fault for stopping on his route on the final interception. [5][7][8][14]

Bryant’s decision to leave the sideline for the tunnel before the game ended drew scrutiny; he later said it was “wrong” and that he was emotional and did not want to display that on the sideline. [9]

## Aftermath and impact

The win improved Green Bay to 7–6–1, preserving realistic paths to the NFC North title in the season’s final two weeks. Dallas fell to 7–7, missing a chance to draw even with Philadelphia but remaining alive with two games left. [4]

Media analysis in the days after centered on Dallas’s game management and offensive philosophy. CBS Sports and Sports Illustrated noted Garrett’s postgame comments about the run call and Romo’s quick throw, while emphasizing that defensive lapses and overall play selection contributed to the loss beyond the quarterback’s mistakes. In subsequent retrospectives, Dallas-based reporting tied this defeat to an offseason shift toward a run-first identity in 2014, including reducing Romo’s freedom to audible out of runs, with the goal of protecting a defense that struggled in 2013. [7][8][15]

## Legacy

The game is frequently cited in Packers retrospectives as a notable rally led by Flynn that kept the 2013 season on track as Rodgers recovered, and in Cowboys coverage as a turning point that informed the organization’s strategic changes the following year. National outlets highlighted the magnitude of the comeback and the late-game sequence that reversed the result. [4][11][15]

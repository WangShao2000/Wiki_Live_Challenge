# Dungka!

Dungka! (stylized in all caps with an exclamation mark) is a Filipino-language song by the Manila-based P-pop group SB19, released on April 25, 2025 as one of the focus tracks of their third extended play (EP), Simula at Wakas, under Sony Music Entertainment and 1Z Entertainment [1][8]. Written by SB19 leader John Paulo “Pablo” Nase and produced with his brother Josue under their RadKidz moniker, the track transforms the everyday Filipino phrase “doon ka” (“go over there”) into a playful, assertive refrain aimed at brushing off negativity [1][2][8][17]. A six-minute music video directed by Kerbs Balagtas of YouMeUs MNL depicts vibrant scenes of urban Philippine life and features high-profile cameos from artists and creators including Vice Ganda, Maymay Entrata, Alodia Gosiengfiao, Sassa Gurl, Kween Yasmin, Pat Lasaten and Agnes Reoma of Ben&Ben, Mimiyuuuh, and others [1][2][3][4]. 

The song received significant coverage from major newspapers and trade and culture publications—such as The Philippine Star, Inquirer Entertainment, Billboard Philippines, Bandwagon Asia, Nylon Manila, Daily Tribune, and VMAN SEA—highlighting its cultural specificity, themes of community and self-expression, and its maximalist, rave-influenced sound [1][2][3][4][8][11]. Commercially, Dungka! debuted at No. 7 on Billboard’s World Digital Song Sales chart and entered the Billboard Philippines Hot 100 at No. 21 [14][15][16]. It won Pop Song of the Year at the inaugural Filipino Music Awards in 2025 and also secured People’s Choice Song of the Year, among SB19’s six wins at the event [20][21][22]. A separate collaboration music video with Japanese game developer SNK ties the track to Fatal Fury: City of the Wolves, integrating the game’s “REV IT UP” slogan with SB19’s choreography and performance [5][6][7].

## Background and recording

Dungka! appears on Simula at Wakas, the final EP in SB19’s trilogy that began with Pagsibol (2021) and continued with Pagtatag (2023). The release contextualizes the group’s personal and artistic journey, exploring closure, renewal, and the finite nature of time and fame [8]. SB19 and their longtime collaborators describe the EP’s aesthetic as balancing familiar P-pop terrain with experimentation; Dungka! was framed as one of the set’s “focus tracks” [8]. 

The track was written by Pablo (John Paulo Nase). Production, mixing, and mastering were handled by RadKidz, the tandem of Pablo and his brother Josue, as credited on the official music video [1][17]. The EP was released globally through Sony Music Entertainment in partnership with SB19’s management company, 1Z Entertainment [8][57].

## Composition and lyrics

Dungka! is characterized by pounding, rave-influenced and hyper-pop elements, aggressive rhythmic accents, and swaggering rap verses from Pablo and Josh, reflecting a maximalist pop approach within the group’s evolving sound palette [2][8]. Lyrically, the title phrase pivots on Filipino wordplay—turning “doon ka” (“go over there”) into a spirited directive to step aside—framed as a cheeky dismissal of detractors and negative energy. In passages like “Tumabi ka! Kung ‘di mo ‘to gusto” (“Step aside! If you don’t like this”), the song asserts agency while inviting communal revelry [2][4][8]. 

Coverage emphasized Pablo’s preference for wordplay and the track’s loud-yet-catchy production, situating Dungka! as a playful rejoinder to critics and a celebration of SB19’s diverse identities and influences [1][2][4][8].

## Music video

The official music video for Dungka! was released on April 25, 2025 and directed by Kerbs Balagtas (YouMeUs MNL). It interweaves scenes of everyday Filipino street and pop culture—sari-sari stores, street-food stalls, jeepneys, karaoke, pickup basketball, Zumba, ten-twenty (Chinese garter), and protest rallies—creating a kinetic portrait of urban Manila [1][2][3][4]. 

The video features a wide array of cameos selected to align with its message of diversity, pride, and collective action: Vice Ganda, Maymay Entrata, Alodia Gosiengfiao, Sassa Gurl, Kween Yasmin, Malupiton, Ghost Wrecker, Shehyee, Smugglaz, Ben&Ben’s Pat Lasaten and Agnes Reoma, Mimiyuuuh, and Jayat, among others [1][4][18][19]. Visual motifs include placards advocating Filipino arts (“Iangat ang sining na Pilipino”) and a poster stating “Sining Ang Sandata sa Kulturang Mapagpalaya” (“Art is the weapon of a liberating culture”) pasted over a traditional politician’s signage—read by coverage as social critique embedded in a street-party tableau [4]. 

Independent features praised the MV’s generous celebration of ordinary Filipino life, its dense references and “Easter eggs,” and the decision to embed SB19 within the community rather than centering them above it [2][3][4].

## Release and promotion

Simula at Wakas and the Dungka! music video were released on April 25, 2025 [1][8]. On the same day, SB19 staged surprise flash mob performances in Bonifacio Global City (Taguig), performing Dungka! alongside the pre-release single Dam near a temporary “Dam Tree” public art installation [9]. Shortly afterward, SB19 performed at Xiaomi’s All-Star Summer Concert at Enchanted Kingdom in Sta. Rosa, Laguna, where Dungka! was introduced as a surprise new track [10]. The group promoted the song in multiple live formats, including a Wish 107.5 Stopover Sessions performance [12] and their debut episode on Billboard Philippines Studios, where Dungka! closed a three-song set from Simula at Wakas [11]. 

Dungka! has been a regular feature of SB19’s Simula at Wakas World Tour set list, including the two-night kickoff at Philippine Arena (Bulacan) on May 31 and June 1, 2025 [13]. Inquirer Entertainment and Bandwagon Asia also covered the group’s cross-cultural collaboration with SNK for Fatal Fury: City of the Wolves, releasing a cinematic music video built around Dungka! and the game’s “REV IT UP” tagline [5][7], as formally announced by SNK in a May 26, 2025 press release [6].

## Reception

Significant coverage in major Philippine outlets and regional culture media framed Dungka! as a sonically bold and culturally anchored work. VMAN SEA described the six-minute MV as “a joyful portrait of Filipino life” that refuses to dilute its local specificity, noting Pablo’s transformation of “doon ka” into a defiant, communal refrain [2]. Inquirer POP! highlighted the video’s social messages, political undertones, and queerness-affirming cameos, interpreting scenes (such as a crab-eating antagonist) as commentary on “crab mentality” and cancel culture [4]. Daily Tribune emphasized the MV’s energetic depiction of Manila street life and its celebrity-packed cast [3]. Bandwagon Asia described the song’s sound as “experimental hyper-pop/rave-influenced maximalism” within the EP’s ecosystem [8]. Billboard Philippines contextualized Dungka! within SB19’s evolving performance repertoire, including its Studios feature and use on the world tour, while extensively documenting the EP’s chart impact [11][16]. 

Audience engagement was reflected in widely covered dance challenges and collaborative clips, with Inquirer POP! reporting on creators joining the Dungka! dance trend [27]. Across outlets, the prevailing view holds that Dungka! merges diss track swagger and local references with an inclusive call to community and self-expression [1][2][3][4][8].

Dungka! is one of the seven tracks on Simula at Wakas, alongside Dam, Time, Quit, 8TonBall, Shooting for the Stars, and an extended version of Dam. The EP release and related activations (including the Dam Tree installation in Bonifacio Global City) were covered by The Philippine Star and Bandwagon Asia [1][8][9].

## Legacy and cultural impact

Coverage frequently characterizes Dungka! as an archival snapshot of contemporary Filipino street culture, asserting that leaning into local specificity can broaden resonance. VMAN SEA argued that the video “refuses to sand down cultural particularities,” and that specificity is what allows universality [2]. Inquirer POP! highlighted how the MV’s messaging—art, politics, queer pride, and community solidarity—connected during an election season and sparked detailed viewer interpretation, reinforcing SB19’s role in cultural discourse [4]. The SNK collaboration introduced Dungka! to gaming audiences regionally, emphasizing cross-media reach and SB19’s international partnerships [5][6][7].

## Controversies

No major controversies were widely reported in reliable sources regarding the song or its music video. Interpretive coverage discussed the MV’s depictions of “crab mentality,” political posters, and social critique; these were framed as artistic choices rather than points of public controversy [4].

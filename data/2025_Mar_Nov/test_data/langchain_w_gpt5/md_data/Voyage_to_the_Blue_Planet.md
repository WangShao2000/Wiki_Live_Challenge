# Voyage to the Blue Planet

Voyage to the Blue Planet is the title of Weezer’s 2024 North American arena tour marking the 30th anniversary of the band’s debut album Weezer (commonly called the Blue Album) and of the associated concert film recorded at TD Garden in Boston and livestreamed globally on October 13–14, 2024. The tour, produced by Live Nation with support acts The Flaming Lips and Dinosaur Jr., framed Weezer’s performance within a camp, sci‑fi narrative culminating in the Blue Album played in full each night. Major outlets described largely positive audience response alongside some criticism of the show’s kid‑friendly aesthetics. The concert film, announced by Billboard, streamed via Moment and offered timed replays. Weezer continued to use the branding in 2025 at the Bumbershoot festival in Seattle. The title is primarily used in connection with Weezer; similarly titled, unrelated works include the IMAX film Blue Planet and the giant‑screen feature Oceans: Our Blue Planet 3D. [1][2][3][4][5][6][10][11][12][13][14][19][20]

## Title usage and scope

- Primary use: Media coverage and official event marketing employ “Voyage to the Blue Planet” for Weezer’s 30th‑anniversary arena tour (September–October 2024) and the companion concert film livestreamed October 13–14, 2024. [1][2]  
- Continued branding: Weezer headlined Bumbershoot (Seattle Center) on August 30, 2025 using the “Voyage to the Blue Planet” presentation. [12][13]  
- Other uses and similarly titled works: The phrase closely resembles but is distinct from titles such as the IMAX feature Blue Planet (1990s releases/editions) and the giant‑screen film Oceans: Our Blue Planet 3D (2018). These are not connected to Weezer. [20][19]

This article covers Weezer’s 2024 tour and concert film under this title.

## Classification and format

- Concert tour: Arena tour across the United States and Canada branded as “Voyage to the Blue Planet,” celebrating 30 years of Weezer (1994). [1]  
- Concert film: Feature‑length concert film capturing the September 10, 2024 TD Garden performance, livestreamed globally via Moment on October 13–14, 2024. [2][6][10]

## Creators and key contributors

- Band and performers: Weezer — Rivers Cuomo (lead vocals, lead guitar), Patrick Wilson (drums), Brian Bell (rhythm guitar, backing vocals, keyboards), and Scott Shriner (bass, backing vocals). [1][2][14]  
- Tour production and creative:
  - Producer: Live Nation (tour). [1]
  - Support acts: The Flaming Lips and Dinosaur Jr. [1]
  - Show design and direction: Travis Shirley / Blank Wall Creative. [7][8]
  - Visual content production: Immanent (Content Director: Drew Best; Producer: Tara LaPlante; team of 3D artists and editors). [7]
  - Script: Yalun Tu and Rivers Cuomo; historical inputs credited to Weezer archivist Karl Koch in vendor materials. [7]
  - Technical and staging vendors: TAIT (automation/staging), 4Wall Entertainment (lighting/rigging), NEP Group/Screenworks (video), Clair Global (audio), Strictly FX (special effects), ShowFX (scenic). [7][8]
- Concert film production:
  - Production company: Yes Chef Pictures (feature‑length concert film for Weezer under this title). [9]
  - Distribution platform: Moment (livestream). [10]
  - Film announcement and release window: Billboard (industry trade) and Pollstar (trade) reported the film and streaming dates/pricing. [2][6]

## Country of origin, language, and production entities

- Country/language: United States; English. [1][2]  
- Principal entities: Weezer; Live Nation (tour producer); Yes Chef Pictures (film); Moment (digital livestream distribution); Blank Wall Creative and Immanent (show design and screen content); TAIT, 4Wall, Screenworks/NEP, Clair Global, Strictly FX, ShowFX (tour vendors). [1][2][7][8][9][10]

## Development and production background

Weezer announced the arena run on March 11, 2024, positioning it as a fall 2024 tour marking three decades since the Blue Album. Public on‑sale began March 15, with presales opening March 13; Citi served as the tour’s official card in the U.S. The tour was produced by Live Nation and featured The Flaming Lips and Dinosaur Jr. as support. [1]

The creative team built a narrative, space‑mission frame around the band “traveling” to save a distant Blue Planet through music. Designer/director Travis Shirley led overall staging, with Immanent producing the LED screen content that carried the show’s plot beats. Vendor materials credit a scripted structure, space‑capsule “liftoff,” detours (including a “Pinkerton asteroid belt”), and a finale in which playing the Blue Album “in its entirety” restores the planet. TAIT provided automation for moving scenic elements; 4Wall supplied lighting; Screenworks/NEP handled LED/video; Clair Global provided audio; Strictly FX delivered special effects; ShowFX provided scenic fabrication. [7][8]

In parallel with the tour, Billboard announced Weezer’s feature‑length concert film shot at Boston’s TD Garden on September 10, 2024, to be livestreamed on Moment.co on October 13–14, 2024, with tiered early‑bird pricing reported by the trade press. Yes Chef Pictures identifies the Weezer title among its feature concert film projects. [2][9][6]

## Content overview and themes

Contemporary reviews describe a deliberately kitschy, camp sci‑fi adventure framing a career‑spanning set. A mock breaking‑news segment and five‑minute countdown preceded “liftoff” of a space‑capsule set piece, with the band in matching jumpsuits. Visuals across large LED screens developed the story (e.g., an alien antagonist, “Weezeroids” resembling the front man, and a “Pinkerton asteroid belt” detour) while the band moved through hits and deep cuts. [4][5][3]

At each show, after the narrative “arrival” on the Blue Planet, the band planted a flag and performed the full Blue Album sequence (“My Name Is Jonas” through “Only in Dreams”) as the crowning act of the mission. Multiple reviews highlighted the emotional response to “Buddy Holly,” “Say It Ain’t So,” and a climactic “Only in Dreams.” [4][5][3][15][16]

## Tour timeline and distribution

- Announcement: March 11, 2024, with presales beginning March 13 and public on‑sale March 15. [1]  
- Run: September–October 2024 across major U.S. and Canadian arenas. Cited examples include TD Garden (Boston, September 10, 2024) and Intuit Dome (Inglewood/Los Angeles, October 11, 2024). [4][14]  
- Concert film: Filmed September 10, 2024 (Boston), livestreamed globally October 13–14, 2024 via Moment. Trade coverage reported early‑bird pricing at $13, then $16, then $18 day‑of, with a 24‑hour replay window. [2][6][10]

## Reception

Press response was broadly favorable regarding musicianship, set construction, and the Blue Album segment, with recurring notes about the intentionally kitschy space framing:

- Variety (New York, Madison Square Garden) reported high audience engagement with the Blue Album sequence but felt parts of the early theatrics skewed overly kid‑friendly, comparing some visuals to children’s programming; the review nonetheless praised the band’s performance. [3]  
- The Boston Globe (TD Garden) noted that while the “video‑game style” visuals sometimes “wore thin,” the show’s spectacle framed a nostalgic, high‑energy performance that culminated in a euphoric “Only in Dreams” response. [4]  
- Boston.com emphasized the show’s “all camp, all fun” tone and quoted Wayne Coyne calling it “effing mind blowing,” while capturing Rivers Cuomo’s recurring “one giant leap for Weezer‑kind” motif. [5]  
- Regional coverage (Santa Barbara Independent; Melodic Mag) praised the production’s scale and the fan response to the Blue Album section. [15][16]  
- CBS News’ anniversary feature contextualized the tour within the broader 30‑year reception and enduring status of the Blue Album. [11]

## Commercial performance

Trade reporting documented at least two strong grosses: Pollstar cited consecutive nights at The Anthem (Washington, D.C.) with 6,000 attendees per show, each grossing $816,250. [6] Other outlets and venue calendars recorded multiple sell‑outs or near‑sell‑outs, though detailed boxoffice reporting beyond the Washington dates was limited in the sources cited here. [14][17]

## Awards and distinctions

As of December 12, 2025, no major industry awards or nominations for the tour or concert film were reported in the cited sources. The tour’s creative production drew attention in live‑event trade press for its integrated narrative staging and vendor collaboration. [7][8]

## Legacy and related activities

- Festival presentation: The “Voyage to the Blue Planet” presentation returned as part of Bumbershoot’s 2025 lineup, with Weezer headlining opening night on August 30, 2025; the branding appeared in both festival and public‑radio coverage. [12][13]  
- Technical profile: Live‑event trades highlighted the tour’s integration of automation, large‑format LED content, and special effects as an example of narrative‑driven arena staging for rock acts. [7][8]  
- Promotional tie‑ins: Los Angeles alternative radio noted Weezer’s “Sweater Saga” arcade cabinet, a space‑themed activation linked to the 30th‑anniversary celebration and tour aesthetic. [18]

## Documented criticisms or disputes

- Aesthetic critiques: Variety characterized parts of the show’s early narrative interludes as resembling children’s television; The Boston Globe described some “video‑game style” backdrops as wearing thin over time. These critiques were balanced by praise for the Blue Album performance and audience impact in the same reviews. [3][4]

## Versions and availability

- The tour was presented in arenas in original staging between early September and mid‑October 2024. [1][14]  
- The concert film streamed via Moment on October 13–14, 2024, with a limited replay window. Availability after the livestream window was not detailed in the trade announcements cited. [2][6][10]

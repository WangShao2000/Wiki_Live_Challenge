# Midnight Sun (Zara Larsson album)

## Lead
Midnight Sun is the fifth studio album by Swedish singer Zara Larsson, released worldwide on 26 September 2025 through her imprint Sommer House under exclusive license to Epic Records (Sony Music Entertainment). The 10‑track set blends high‑energy dance‑pop and electropop with introspective songwriting inspired by the Scandinavian “midnight sun” and Larsson’s Swedish summers. Preceded by the singles Pretty Ugly, Midnight Sun, and Crush, the album reached number one on the Swedish Albums Chart, number 36 in the United Kingdom, number 31 in Australia, and number 53 in Germany. U.S. trade outlets reported a debut at number 3 on Billboard’s Top Dance/Electronic Albums and number 22 on Top Album Sales, and listed a Billboard 200 debut at number 119. Critics in Rolling Stone, university publications, and music magazines highlighted the record’s cohesive summer aesthetic, vocal confidence, and thoughtful turns on ambition and adulthood. The album was supported by a European arena run in autumn 2025 and a North American tour planned for 2026, alongside a limited “Lisa Frank Edition” CD and clear vinyl pressings. [1][2][3][4][17][18][19][20][21][22][23][24]

## Background and development
Larsson’s release campaign and label press materials frame Midnight Sun as a project conceived around the feeling of a Swedish summer where the sun never sets—grounding the album title and its imagery. In announcing the LP, Larsson said she wanted the whole record to feel like an endless summer night, proudly drawing from her Swedish pop heritage. The title track was among the first songs written for the project. Principal collaborators include longtime partner MNEK, producers Margo XS and Zhone, and songwriter Helena Gao, with additional contributions across individual tracks. [2][12][21][22]

Rolling Stone documented Larsson’s self‑confidence entering the era—fueled by bright, Y2K‑tinged visuals that followed viral moments from earlier hits and an energetic run opening for Tate McRae—while Larsson broke down how key songs map the album’s identity, from club‑ready euphoria to more reflective pieces about purpose and adulthood. [12]

## Composition and lyrics
Midnight Sun is primarily dance‑pop and electropop, interlacing trance‑styled choruses, club‑leaning beats, and “Euro summer” textures with lyrical themes of romance, identity, ambition, and late‑twenties self‑reflection.

- Title track Midnight Sun opens with a melodic, dance‑forward rush and hypnotic hook, intended to capture the surreal continuity of summer daylight. [12][13]
- Blue Moon continues the buoyant mood; reviewers noted ethereal vocalizations and a dizzying pop energy. [13][15]
- Pretty Ugly leans into rowdy, chant‑like confrontation and feminist edge over stomping production. [13][16]
- Girl’s Girl explores the tension between solidarity and desire; critics observed its “messier” emotional moments as compelling. [13][14][16]
- Eurosummer nods to 2010s Eastern European pop staples, with playful accordion touches and a breezy Mediterranean flair. [12][16]
- Hot & Sexy celebrates feminine confidence while introducing a pointed outro about women’s safety and social responsibility, and features a re‑recorded line by television personality Tiffany Pollard. [12][16]
- The Ambition interrogates comparison and validation in the industry, pairing a catchy hook with confessional lyrics. [13][16]
- Saturn’s Return reflects late‑twenties turbulence and acceptance of uncertainty, reinforcing the album’s celestial motif. [12][13][16]
- Puss Puss (Swedish for “kiss kiss”) closes with playful intimacy and phone‑call imagery, balancing the set’s maximalist energy with levity. [13][14]

Apple Music’s editorial copy characterized the album as “larger, bolder, and more flamboyant” than VENUS (2024), aiming for peak dancefloor euphoria while retaining a hedonistic, sun‑soaked mood. [4]

## Release and promotion
Larsson announced Midnight Sun in early June 2025 alongside pre‑orders, with the title track scheduled for mid‑June and an autumn album release. The album became available globally on 26 September 2025 across digital platforms and in multiple physical formats. The roll‑out included:

- Official website and social campaigns directing fans to pre‑save the single and pre‑order the album. [1]
- A Sony Music Canada press release on release day summarizing collaborators, themes, and the special “Lisa Frank Edition” CD (launched in partnership with Lisa Frank, Inc.) and highlighting touring plans. [2]
- A limited “Lisa Frank Edition” CD and a standard CD sold via Larsson’s official store, and clear vinyl pressings, some signed or with signed art cards. [5][6][2]
- Music video content and editorial playlisting on DSPs; a lyric video for Midnight Sun was released in late June. [9][4]
- European arena dates announced by outlets including The Line of Best Fit, with additional socials facilitating presale access. [11][1]

## Singles and videos
- Pretty Ugly – first single of the era, released in April 2025; included on the album and referenced in label and media announcements. [2][21][22]
- Midnight Sun – title track and second single, released 13 June 2025; accompanied by an official lyric video; later expanded via a bundle featuring a live version and Alex Chapman remix. A collaborative single version with Muni Long also appeared on streaming services. [8][9][10][7]
- Crush – third single, issued in August 2025 and featured in release‑day press; performed at radio through late 2025. [2][21][22]

Scandipop named Midnight Sun its “Song of the Summer” and covered the release of the remix/live bundle in August. [7]

## Critical reception
Midnight Sun received generally positive notices.

- Rolling Stone praised the LP’s relentless, high‑energy joy and highlighted Larsson’s “messier” moments as the most alive, while commending the project’s reflections on ambition and adulthood. [13]
- A Rolling Stone feature contextualized the era’s aesthetics and momentum, and offered Larsson’s track‑by‑track insights on composition and themes. [12]
- University press reviews lauded the album’s cohesive summer framing and vocal confidence. The Brown Daily Herald described the record as Larsson’s most personal and cohesive work to date, mixing Euro‑pop with deepened lyricism; The Daily Nebraskan emphasized the balance of dance‑pop perfection with self‑reflection and identity. [14][15]
- Melodic Magazine called Midnight Sun Larsson’s strongest, most cohesive body of work, singling out Crush and Saturn’s Return as standouts and noting the project’s distinctive yet familiar palette. [16]

## Commercial performance
- Sweden: Debuted at number 1 on the Sverigetopplistan albums chart in the week dated 3 October 2025; three weeks on chart. [17]
- United Kingdom: Debuted at number 36 on the Official Albums Chart (week of 3–9 October 2025). [18]
- Australia: Debuted at number 31 on the ARIA Top 50 Albums (week commencing 6 October 2025). [19]
- Germany: Peaked at number 53 on the Offizielle Deutsche Charts Top 100 albums. [20]
- United States:
  - Trade outlets reported a career‑best debut at number 3 on Billboard’s Top Dance/Electronic Albums and number 22 on Top Album Sales, with additional Luminate/Internet Albums placements; these reports also noted Crush rising at pop radio. [21][22]
  - Third‑party chart trackers listed a Billboard 200 debut at number 119 in the week of 11 October 2025. [23][24]

As of 12 December 2025, no official certifications for the album were reported by certifying bodies; press materials highlighted catalog certifications for earlier singles. [21][22]

# Robert Jacomb-Hood

Robert Jacomb-Hood (also styled Jacomb Hood; 19th–20th century) was a British railway engineer and administrator whose career was closely associated with the South Eastern Railway (SER) and, after its working union with the London, Chatham and Dover Railway, the South Eastern and Chatham Railway (SECR). He held senior engineering roles, including engineer-in-chief, and contributed to major works across the SER/SECR network, encompassing station improvements, bridge works, and infrastructure modernisation. He was a member of leading professional bodies, published technical papers, and was recognised for his services to railway engineering. His surname appears both hyphenated and unhyphenated in contemporary sources. [1]

## Career

Jacomb-Hood’s career was connected to the SER and later the SECR, with responsibilities extending across London termini and the network in Kent and the southeast. Sources summarise his progression to senior posts and identify work on:

- London termini and river crossings: engineering oversight connected with Charing Cross and Cannon Street and associated bridge and station fabric, reflecting ongoing late-19th and early-20th century reconstruction and strengthening cycles. [1]  
- Network infrastructure: permanent-way renewals, signalling and level-crossing eliminations, and harbour works linked to SER/SECR coastal operations at Folkestone and Dover. [1]

Following the 1899 formation of the SECR working union, Jacomb-Hood is recorded as holding engineer-in-chief responsibilities for the combined undertaking’s civil works. [1] In addition to administrative leadership, he prepared and delivered papers to professional meetings on railway construction and maintenance topics. [1]

## Name disambiguation

The double-barrelled surname Jacomb-Hood appears for more than one figure in this period. Contemporary sources distinguish Robert Jacomb-Hood, a railway engineer, from George Percy Jacomb-Hood, a painter and etcher active in the same era. Care is needed to attribute engineering sources to Robert and artistic sources to George Percy. [1]

# Arne Furumark

Arne Furumark (1903–1982) was a Swedish classical archaeologist whose research on Aegean Bronze Age ceramics, especially his classification and chronology of Mycenaean pottery, became a foundational framework for the field. His two-volume Mycenaean Pottery (first published in 1941 and reissued with additions in 1972–1992) is widely cited as a standard reference. He taught at Uppsala University as Professor of Classical Archaeology and Ancient History from 1952 to 1970 and served as Director of the Swedish Institute at Athens in 1956–1957. He also directed Swedish excavations at Sinda, Cyprus in 1947–1948, a key Late Bronze Age site later published as a comprehensive site report in 2003 [1][2][3][4][5][6][7][8].

## Early life and education

Furumark was Swedish and specialized in the archaeology and art of the Aegean Bronze Age, with a focus on the Mycenaean period [1][9][10]. He studied at Uppsala University, where classical archaeology and ancient history were consolidated as an academic field in Sweden in the early twentieth century. Within this milieu he trained in the scholarly school led by Axel W. Persson, whose excavations and supervision shaped two generations of Swedish classical archaeologists; Persson’s program, including work at Asine, provided a research context for Furumark’s systematization of Mycenaean pottery [10]. 

## Academic career

- Uppsala University: Furumark held the chair in Classical Archaeology and Ancient History from 1952 until 1970 [2].  
- Swedish Institute at Athens: He served as Director from 1956 to 1957, between the tenures of Åke Åkerström and Erik J. Holmberg [3].

His career combined typological and chronological research on Aegean ceramics with institutional leadership in Swedish classical archaeology [1][2][3].

## Fieldwork and expeditions

- Sinda (Cyprus): In two seasons in 1947–1948, Furumark led Swedish excavations at Sinda, a Late Bronze Age fortified settlement in central Cyprus. The work focused on stratified habitation deposits crucial for clarifying later phases of the Bronze Age, particularly the development of Mycenaean IIIC pottery in Cypriot contexts. The excavation documented three occupation phases (Sinda I–III), with major destruction horizons and substantial assemblages of imported and locally produced Mycenaean-style ceramics (notably IIIC1b), contributing to debates about post-palatial Aegean connections and Cypriot ceramic development. The final site report was published as Swedish Excavations at Sinda, Cyprus: Excavations Conducted by Arne Furumark 1947–1948 (2003) [7][8].  
- San Giovenale (Italy): In the early 1960s the Swedish Institute’s broader program at San Giovenale evolved into a significant, long-term field project. Within that context, Furumark carried out excavations and was involved in the project’s scientific direction during its formative years [11].

## Research and contributions

- Mycenaean pottery classification and chronology: Furumark’s most influential contribution was his comprehensive classification of Late Bronze Age Mycenaean ceramics by shape and decorative motifs and his articulation of a relative and absolute chronological framework. These results were first published in 1941 as The Mycenaean Pottery: Analysis and Classification and The Chronology of Mycenaean Pottery, and later updated and reissued in the Swedish Institute at Athens series ActaAth-4°, 20 (vols. 1–2 reprinted with additions in 1972; plates volume issued in 1992). The set has served as a standard reference and established the conventional type and motif terminology used by Aegean archaeologists [4][5][6][9].  
- Aegean and eastern Mediterranean connections: Beyond typology, Furumark addressed broader historical questions bridging the Aegean and eastern Mediterranean in studies such as The settlement at Ialysos and Aegean history c. 1550–1400 B.C., published in Opuscula Archaeologica 6 (1950), and later articles on Cretan religion and Cypriot materials [12].  
- Scripts and scholarly correspondence: Archival letters reflect Furumark’s active engagement with contemporaries on Aegean writing systems and Bronze Age archaeology, including correspondence exchanged from Athens on Linear A sign occurrences on pottery fragments, illustrating his participation in scholarly networks around epigraphic and archaeological discoveries of the mid-twentieth century [13].

## Influence and legacy

Furumark’s Mycenaean Pottery volumes established a durable typological and chronological framework that has underpinned Aegean Late Bronze Age studies for decades. The reissue of the analysis and chronology volumes (1972) and the publication of an expanded plates volume (1992) attest to the work’s continued scholarly use and to its integration in the Swedish Institute’s peer-reviewed monograph series. Subsequent research has refined and debated individual chronological details, but his shape and decoration classifications remain part of the conventional vocabulary of the field [4][5][6][9][12].

## Archives

A substantial corpus of Furumark’s correspondence has been digitized and cataloged by Uppsala University’s archives, with most letters transcribed and made available via the Alvin platform. This collection includes scholarly exchanges pertinent to his Aegean research and provides evidence of his professional networks. Individual documents, such as a 1958 letter to him from the American School of Classical Studies at Athens concerning Linear A signs on pottery from Lerna, are available as digital facsimiles [13][14].

## Personal life

Standardized library authority records confirm his dates (1903–1982) and identify him as a Swedish classical archaeologist; further biographical details (including nationality and research specialization) are summarized in Britannica and in scholarly reference profiles [1][10][12].

# Love Me Forever (Pinkshift album)

Love Me Forever is the debut studio album by American rock band Pinkshift, released on October 21, 2022, under an exclusive license to Hopeless Records. The album runs 12 tracks and approximately 37 minutes and was produced by Will Yip. It was recorded at Studio 4 during an intensive three-week session, and its release was preceded by a series of singles with accompanying videos. Critics in outlets such as Kerrang!, FLOOD Magazine, and Under the Radar highlighted the album’s blend of punk, pop-punk, post-hardcore, and grunge influences and noted its emotionally charged lyrical themes; Paste included it in its list of the year’s best punk albums. The record was issued digitally and in multiple physical formats, including several colored-vinyl variants via Hopeless Records and partner retailers. ## Background and development

Pinkshift formed in 2018 while its members—vocalist Ashrita Kumar, guitarist Paul Vallejo, and drummer Myron Houngbedji—were students at Johns Hopkins University in Baltimore. Their DIY single “I’m Gonna Tell My Therapist On You” went viral in 2020 and drew industry attention. By 2021, the trio had begun working with producer Will Yip, and they later signed with Hopeless Records to release their first full-length. [NME Radar profile](https://www.nme.com/blogs/nme-radar/pinkshift-interview-love-me-forever-debut-album-baltimore-pop-punk-radar-3322403)

The band tracked Love Me Forever at Yip’s Studio 4 during a three-week session in March under a tight timeline ahead of an April tour. In discussing the process, members described their first experience making a record in a professional studio, noting Yip’s exacting approach to takes and arrangement in service of the album’s cohesion. [Punknews interview](https://www.punknews.org/article/78502/interviews-paul-and-myron-of-pinkshift-talk-their-upcoming-debut-album-touring-as-headliners-and-more)

Kumar characterized the opener “i’m not crying, you’re crying” as an unfiltered outpouring that starts the album in “panicked denial.” [BroadwayWorld announcement](https://www.broadwayworld.com/bwwmusic/article/Pinkshift-Announce-Debut-Album-Share-New-Single-im-not-crying-youre-crying-20220713) [Consequence announcement](https://consequence.net/2022/07/pinkshift-love-me-forever-im-not-crying-youre-crying-stream/)

The band has described the album’s cover concept—featuring a stitched heart rendered in a kintsugi-like motif—as reflecting themes of breakdown and repair that thread through the record. [Punknews interview](https://www.punknews.org/article/78502/interviews-paul-and-myron-of-pinkshift-talk-their-upcoming-debut-album-touring-as-headliners-and-more)

## Composition and lyrics

Reviewers and profiles classify Love Me Forever across contemporary punk subgenres:

- FLOOD described its sound as a “mix of punk, post-hardcore, grunge, as well as pop,” evoking influences from No Doubt to modern hardcore-adjacent acts, and framed its lyrical stance as a “swirling swathe of anger and injustice.” [FLOOD Magazine review](https://floodmagazine.com/120242/pinkshift-love-me-forever/)
- Kerrang! characterized Pinkshift as a pop-punk breakout while noting moments that verge on aggression. [Kerrang! review](https://www.kerrang.com/album-review-pinkshift-love-me-forever)
- NME’s Radar profile contextualized the trio amid pop-punk and emo traditions. [NME Radar profile](https://www.nme.com/blogs/nme-radar/pinkshift-interview-love-me-forever-debut-album-baltimore-pop-punk-radar-3322403)

Under the Radar highlighted “searing energy and visceral vulnerability” across themes including trauma, abuse, alienation, and a generation’s uncertain future. [Under the Radar review](https://undertheradarmag.com/reviews/love_me_forever_pinkshift)

The album’s piano-led ballad “in a breath” appears in the middle of the track list; Stereogum noted its video’s minimalist form and single-take focus on Kumar’s performance. [Stereogum (“In a Breath”)](https://stereogum.com/2200263/pinkshift-in-a-breath/music)

The single “GET OUT” was introduced by the band as an anthem asserting space and autonomy, with Kumar calling it a “blatant attack against the white supremacist and patriarchal colonizers of our bodies, homes and ways of life.” [Paste single feature (“GET OUT”)](https://www.pastemagazine.com/music/pinkshift/new-single-get-out)

## Critical reception

Love Me Forever received positive notices from several music publications.

- Kerrang! rated it 4/5, calling it “sharp, skilful and bristling with infectious energy” and a “bold first proper kick from a band already brimming with promise.” [Kerrang! review](https://www.kerrang.com/album-review-pinkshift-love-me-forever)
- Under the Radar gave it 7.5/10, praising its “searing energy and visceral vulnerability” and describing it as a “stellar punk debut” that claims the band’s “spot among the genre’s next generation.” [Under the Radar review](https://undertheradarmag.com/reviews/love_me_forever_pinkshift)
- FLOOD Magazine highlighted the album’s fusion of punk, post-hardcore, grunge, and pop, and framed it as a “stunning burst of influences and experiences” channeled through anger and injustice. [FLOOD Magazine review](https://floodmagazine.com/120242/pinkshift-love-me-forever/)
- New Noise Magazine’s review praised the album’s strong showing for a debut within modern punk. [New Noise review](https://newnoisemagazine.com/reviews/album-review-pinkshift-love-me-forever/)
- Paste placed Love Me Forever on its list of The 20 Best Punk Albums of 2022, citing Pinkshift’s blend of 2000s pop-punk with ’90s grunge heft. [Paste year-end list](https://www.pastemagazine.com/music/punk-albums/best-punk-albums-of-2022)

## Notability and context

As Pinkshift’s debut studio album, Love Me Forever marked the band’s transition from viral DIY singles and an EP to a full-length produced by Will Yip on Hopeless Records. It drew coverage from prominent rock and alternative outlets and appeared on Paste’s curated list of the year’s best punk albums, with reviews drawing attention to its stylistic breadth and the emotive force of its lyrics and performances.

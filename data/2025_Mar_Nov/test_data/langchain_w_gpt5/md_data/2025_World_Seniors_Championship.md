# 2025 World Seniors Championship

The 2025 World Seniors Championship (officially the 2025 JenningsBet World Seniors Snooker Championship) was a seniors snooker tournament organized by World Seniors Snooker (WSS) and staged at the Crucible Theatre, Sheffield, England, from 7 to 11 May 2025. It was contested by a 16-player field comprising seeded invitees, qualifiers from the Race to the Crucible series, continental champions, and a Golden Ticket play-off winner. Alfie Burden won his first World Seniors title, defeating Aaron Canavan 8–4 in the best‑of‑15 frames final. In the United Kingdom, the event was broadcast live and free-to-air by Channel 5 and 5Action and produced by Sunset+Vine. JenningsBet was the title sponsor. This article covers the seniors snooker championship; a separate darts event in 2025 is titled the World Seniors Darts Championship and is organized under a different body and title. ## Background

World Seniors Snooker’s flagship championship returned to the Crucible Theatre, the long-standing home of professional snooker, for a five-day one-table event in May 2025. The venue’s main auditorium seats 980 spectators. [1] [15] [16]

The 2025 edition followed WSS’s established seniors ecosystem, which includes a global network of qualifiers and a domestic Race to the Crucible series feeding places into the Crucible field. WSS is part of the wider WPBSA family of organizations. [5] [17]

Tickets for 2025 went on sale in October 2024, with the event scheduled for 7–11 May 2025 and Brazil’s Igor Figueiredo announced as the defending champion. [1]

## Qualification

Places for the 16-player field came through multiple routes administered by WSS: [5]

- Seeds and invited players: The seeds included former ranking event winners Joe Perry and Dominic Dale making their World Seniors debuts, four-time World Seniors champion Jimmy White, 1997 world champion Ken Doherty, Brazil’s defending champion Igor Figueiredo, Tony Drago, Tony Knowles, and World Seniors No. 1 Aaron Canavan. [5]
- Race to the Crucible qualifiers: Four places were awarded via the WSS Race to the Crucible series—Aaron Canavan, Andrew Norman, Gerard Greene, and Wayne Townsend (Crucible debutant). [5]
- Continental champions: Four continental seniors champions qualified—Craig Steadman (Europe), Hassan Kerde (Asia‑Pacific), Charl Jonck (Africa), and Fabio Anderson Luersen (Americas). [5]
- Golden Ticket Play‑Off: The final place was decided at The Crucible Club in Reading on 19–20 April 2025. Alfie Burden won the play‑off, defeating Matthew Ford 4–3 in the final to clinch the last berth. [6]

For its 2025/26 season, WSS broadened tour event eligibility to all players aged 40+ (including World Snooker Tour professionals) and confirmed that a minimum of four places for the 2026 championship would again be allocated via the ranking list, underscoring the championship’s connection to the WSS tour pathway. [18] [3]

## Format

A format change was introduced for 2025. All matches up to and including the quarter‑finals were best of seven frames, the semi‑finals were best of 11 frames, and the final was contested over the best of 15 frames. The event used a one‑table, straight knock‑out format throughout. [2] [4]

The draw for the championship was conducted on 10 April 2025 on Channel 5’s Jeremy Vine programme by Jeremy Vine and Neal Foulds. WSS published the order of play the following day. [4]

Quarter‑finals (9 May; best of 7)
- Dominic Dale 4–1 Joe Perry
- Alfie Burden 4–3 Tony Drago
- Hassan Kerde 4–2 Charl Jonck
- Aaron Canavan 4–2 Wayne Townsend

Semi‑finals (10 May; best of 11)
- Alfie Burden 6–2 Hassan Kerde
- Aaron Canavan 6–5 Dominic Dale

Final (11 May; best of 15)
- Alfie Burden 8–4 Aaron Canavan [8] [7]

## Final

The final was played over a possible 15 frames at the Crucible on 11 May 2025. Alfie Burden defeated Aaron Canavan 8–4 to win his first World Seniors Championship. The World Snooker Tour confirmed the result, and Snooker.org’s frame data recorded a 109 break by Burden among the decisive frames. [8] [7]

## Statistics

Breaks (selected)
- Dominic Dale compiled century breaks of 131 and 109 in his semi‑final against Aaron Canavan, and a 128 in his quarter‑final against Joe Perry; he also made a 107 in the last 16. [7]
- Alfie Burden made a 109 in the final against Aaron Canavan. [7]

Deciding‑ball frames
- Tony Drago won two frames on the re‑spotted black in his 4–3 last‑16 win over Gerard Greene. [7]
- A re‑spotted black occurred in frame 4 of Burden’s 4–1 last‑16 win over Jimmy White. [7]

## Prize fund

- Winner: £20,000
- Runner‑up: £10,000

These amounts were reported by Snooker.org’s event page and noted in post‑event coverage; no official public breakdown beyond these amounts was cited in the available sources. [7] [8]

## Broadcast

- United Kingdom: Channel 5 held live free‑to‑air rights, with daytime coverage on Channel 5 and evening sessions on 5Action; Sunset+Vine produced the coverage. Channel 5’s programme listing carried the final. [11] [12] [13] [14]
- Event communications emphasized that “every ball” was carried live on Channel 5 in the UK; live scoring was available via WPBSA SnookerScores. [4] [12]

## Reception

Channel 5’s renewal of the World Seniors Snooker Championship for 2025 was framed by the broadcaster as part of its strategy to grow as a sports destination. WSS welcomed the return to UK free‑to‑air coverage, and producer Sunset+Vine highlighted the inclusion of snooker legends in the coverage team. These perspectives were reported in trade media ahead of the event. [11] [14]

## Logistics and sponsorship

- Title sponsor: JenningsBet was announced as the 2025 championship’s title sponsor on 4 April 2025. [2]
- Organizer/promoter: World Seniors Snooker (WSS). [17]
- Production: Sunset+Vine. [11] [14]
- Community and education: WSS announced a partnership with Sheffield Hallam University to provide Sport Business Management students with applied placements across media, marketing, and event operations for the 2025 championship at the Crucible. [10]

## Notes

- Naming and scope: In official and media usage in 2025, “World Seniors Championship” without a sport qualifier primarily referred to the seniors snooker event at the Crucible. A distinct darts tournament was staged under the title “World Seniors Darts Championship” and is not covered in this article. [4] [11]

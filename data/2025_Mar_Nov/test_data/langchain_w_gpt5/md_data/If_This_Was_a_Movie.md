# If This Was a Movie

## Lead

If This Was a Movie is a song by American singer-songwriter Taylor Swift, first released as a bonus track on the Target-exclusive deluxe edition of her third studio album, Speak Now (2010), through Big Machine Records. It was co-written by Swift and Martin Johnson and produced by Swift with Nathan Chapman. Issued digitally in November 2011, the song debuted at number 10 on the US Billboard Hot 100 and reached number 3 on Digital Songs with 162,000 downloads in its first week. Critics at Billboard characterized it as a fast-paced pop rock ballad that signaled a maturing lyrical perspective for Swift. Swift released If This Was a Movie (Taylor’s Version) on March 17, 2023, as part of her re-recording project timed to the start of the Eras Tour; the new version topped the US Country Digital Song Sales chart and reached number 4 on Digital Song Sales. The re-recording was later included on the streaming compilation The More Fearless (Taylor’s Version) Chapter. The title has been used for other recordings by independent artists, but the Swift composition is the widely covered work under this name in mainstream music media and charts. ## Background and writing

The song was written by Taylor Swift and Martin Johnson, the latter known as the frontman of Boys Like Girls, and produced by Swift with Nathan Chapman. Billboard’s track review credits list Swift and Johnson as the songwriters and Chapman and Swift as the producers, with the release under Big Machine. These credits align with the broader production approach of the Speak Now era, in which Swift worked extensively with Chapman. [1]

Swift originally placed If This Was a Movie on the Target-exclusive deluxe edition of Speak Now, released on October 25, 2010. The album marked a period when Swift emphasized authorial control over her work; the track represents one of her few co-written pieces in the Speak Now era. [11], [12]

## Composition and lyrics

Billboard described If This Was a Movie as a fast-paced ballad propelled by a recurring guitar figure and a dramatic chorus with layered harmonies that complement Swift’s country-pop vocal timbre. The review highlighted the song’s narrative of longing and imagined reconciliation after a breakup, reading it as a coming-of-age moment that moves beyond high school fantasy to more adult themes. [1]

## Release and versions

- Original release: If This Was a Movie first appeared on the Target-exclusive deluxe edition of Speak Now (released October 25, 2010). The original recording is credited to Big Machine Records. [11], [12]
- Digital release as part of deluxe tracks: On November 8, 2011, six deluxe tracks from Speak Now—including If This Was a Movie—were released digitally, which led to the song’s entry on multiple charts. [2]
- Re-recording (Taylor’s Version): Swift released If This Was a Movie (Taylor’s Version) on March 17, 2023, as one of four surprise tracks timed to the start of The Eras Tour. The single’s product page lists “℗ 2023 Taylor Swift.” Swift later grouped the track within The More Fearless (Taylor’s Version) Chapter on streaming services. [3], [4], [9], [10]

Commentary around the re-recording’s placement noted that Swift emphasized Speak Now as “completely self-written” when announcing its re-recorded version, while If This Was a Movie is co-written with Martin Johnson. Media reporting framed fan expectations that the track would be released separately from Speak Now (Taylor’s Version) and associated with the Fearless era compilations on streaming. [5]

## Critical reception

Billboard’s track review viewed If This Was a Movie as evidence of Swift’s lyrical maturation and stronger age-appropriate themes, while praising its arrangement as a streamlined, fast-paced ballad with a memorable chorus and supporting harmonies. The outlet also noted the song’s impact a year after Speak Now’s release, underscoring its late surge due to the digital release of deluxe tracks. [1]

## Commercial performance

Original (2011):
- US Billboard Hot 100: debuted at number 10 (week of November 26, 2011), the highest-ranking debut that week. [6]
- Digital Songs (US): debuted at number 3 with 162,000 downloads; it also led the Country Digital Song Sales tally for a week in November 2011. [2]
- Canadian Hot 100: entered at number 17 amid a wave of Speak Now-related tracks reaching Canada charts. [13]

Taylor’s Version (2023):
- US Digital Song Sales: debuted at number 4 with 11,300 sold (charts dated April 1, 2023). [4]
- US Country Digital Song Sales: debuted at number 1. Billboard noted the composition became the first to top the Country Digital Song Sales chart via two different versions, and the result extended Swift’s record number of leaders on the tally. [4]
- United Kingdom: appeared on the Official Singles Chart update and ranked on the Official Singles Sales Chart and Official Singles Downloads Chart; it also entered the Official Trending Chart. [7], [8]

Swift’s pattern of high Hot 100 debuts was emphasized by Billboard in a separate year-end feature, which referenced If This Was a Movie’s top 10 start. [14]

## Live performances

Swift performed If This Was a Movie as a surprise song during The Eras Tour on June 23, 2023, at U.S. Bank Stadium in Minneapolis, playing it on piano as part of the acoustic segment of the show. Billboard’s compiled list documents the performance date and context within the tour’s rotating surprise songs. [15]

## Other uses of the title

Other recordings have used the title If This Was a Movie, but chart and mainstream media documentation predominantly refer to Swift’s composition. This article covers the Taylor Swift work and its re-recording because it is the song with significant coverage in major outlets and chart authorities. [6], [7], [8]

## Controversies and notable discussions

In the lead-up to Speak Now (Taylor’s Version), UK outlet Capital FM reported fan commentary that Swift’s emphasis on the album being “completely self-written” would preclude the inclusion of If This Was a Movie—which is co-written with Martin Johnson—on the re-recorded track list. The song was instead released earlier as a stand-alone Taylor’s Version single and grouped with The More Fearless (Taylor’s Version) Chapter on streaming. These discussions reflect the public narrative around Swift’s catalog control and the framing of her re-recordings. [5], [9], [10]

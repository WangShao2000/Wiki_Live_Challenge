# Grupo Frontera political controversy

Grupo Frontera political controversy refers to public disputes and media coverage linking the Mexican-American band Grupo Frontera to political topics from their public emergence in 2022 through December 12, 2025. The most significant incident occurred in early 2025, when social media users alleged the group supported Donald Trump; the band responded with statements on February 7 and February 22 denying affiliation with any party “against immigrants and the Latino community,” stressing their migrant backgrounds and that relatives’ opinions do not represent the group, while clarifying a backstage “Y.M.C.A.” dance routine had no political intent [1][2][3][6]. The episode prompted backlash, including a Change.org petition to remove the band from the Chicago Sueños festival (which later proceeded as planned), criticism from activists, and extensive coverage by major outlets in the United States and Mexico [2][4][5][7][8][9][10]. Another relevant episode was the band’s performance at Mexico City’s official Grito de Independencia ceremony on September 15, 2023, a governmental event presided over by President Andrés Manuel López Obrador (AMLO), which itself did not generate notable political controversy directed at the band according to the sources surveyed [13][14][15].

## Background

Grupo Frontera formed in the Rio Grande Valley and rose to prominence in 2022–2023 within the música mexicana/Regional Mexican scene. Public interviews prior to 2025 emphasized a nonpartisan posture and support for migrants in their community, framed as consistent with U.S. law. In a January 2, 2025 interview with El País, members stated: “Respetamos las leyes que tiene el país, pero sí apoyamos a los inmigrantes como podemos” (“We respect the country’s laws, but we do support immigrants as we can”), while describing community-oriented activities and avoiding overt political positioning [16].

## 2025 allegations of support for Donald Trump

### Origin and spread of allegations

In early February 2025, social posts and commentary alleged Grupo Frontera supported Donald Trump. The claims drew on several pieces of circulating content: a clip of the band dancing to “Y.M.C.A.” (a song widely used at Trump rallies), posts highlighting a relative celebrating Trump’s electoral victory, and a red-carpet exchange during Premio Lo Nuestro on February 20 that some viewers interpreted as evasive about border politics [2][3][6][10][11][12]. Infobae reported that a separate clip purporting to show booing at a concert was contested by users as manipulated audio, and noted that “hasta el momento, no había videos o publicaciones que acreditaran un apoyo de parte de los integrantes a las políticas de Donald Trump” (“as of publication, there were no videos or posts demonstrating support by the band members for Donald Trump’s policies”) [8]. UnoTV aggregated fan criticism calling for cancellation on social media, citing comments such as “No regresen a México” (“Do not return to Mexico”) and concerns that Latinos felt “decepcionados” (“disappointed”) [9]. Latin Times reported the episode as “intense backlash,” noting that coverage initially focused on relatives’ posts and the “Y.M.C.A.” video while the group’s publicist did not immediately comment to the outlet [10].

### Band statements and clarifications

- February 7, 2025 (Instagram Story): As reported by Billboard (and echoed in Spanish by ABC7), Grupo Frontera stated: “We want to clarify that Grupo Frontera has NO affiliation not alliance with any political party that’s against immigrants and the Latino community.” The message added: “It’s important you know that the opinions of our friends and family don’t represent Grupo Frontera. We are immigrants, we are from the border, and Grupo Frontera will always be by and for the people.” ABC7’s Spanish coverage quoted the post as “no tienen ‘afiliación ni alianza con ningún partido político que esté en contra de los inmigrantes y comunidad latina’” [1][6].

- February 22, 2025 (Instagram video): Coverage by the Los Angeles Times (De Los) and Rolling Stone reported the band reiterating: “We are migrants, our parents are migrants… we don’t support any political party that is against migrants,” and explaining that the “Y.M.C.A.” dance was part of a long-standing pre-show routine without political intent. Lead singer Adelaido “Payo” Solís emphasized: “Any person that’s not one of the six members of Grupo Frontera can’t have an opinion on our behalf, nor speak for us, nor express our emotions.” Rolling Stone further quoted: “We are clarifying this because people are putting us against our raza” [2][3].

- February 20, 2025 (Premio Lo Nuestro red carpet): De Los reported that when asked about the border crisis, group member Julián Peña Jr. answered: “We dedicate ourselves to music. We love everyone and want peace and love. Take care of yourselves,” a response that drew criticism from activist Carlos Eduardo Espina, who said on TikTok: “there are times when not saying anything says a lot” [2][11][12].

- February 2025 (Instagram post on raids): El Universal covered the band’s message regarding immigration enforcement, quoting: “Como inmigrantes entendemos el dolor, la incertidumbre y el miedo que muchos están viviendo. Nos duele ver a nuestra gente pasar por esto y queremos alzar la voz,” and: “Nosotros somos migrantes… no apoyamos a ningún partido político que esté en contra de los migrantes” (“As immigrants we understand the pain, uncertainty, and fear… we do not support any political party that is against migrants”) [7].

### Public reaction and outcomes

- Petition to remove the band from Sueños festival (Chicago): The Chicago Sun-Times reported a Change.org petition posted the prior Tuesday had more than 9,700 signatures as of February 11, 2025. The article quoted the organizer seeking a refund and disputing whether the band’s Instagram Story had appeared for the full 24 hours, while also carrying a statement by the festival’s co-founder emphasizing its mission to uplift the Latino community [4]. De Los later referenced the petition tally exceeding 10,400 signatures as of March 10, 2025 [2]. Despite the petition and online backlash, the band’s performance at Sueños proceeded on May 25, 2025, with the Sun-Times noting their set went on as planned [5].

- Media framing and activism: Coverage by Billboard, the Los Angeles Times (De Los), Rolling Stone, and ABC7 framed the controversy around the perceived symbolism of “Y.M.C.A.” clips and relatives’ posts, but centered the band’s denial of anti-immigrant affiliations and their emphasis on migrant identities [1][2][3][6]. De Los highlighted activist Carlos Eduardo Espina’s criticism of the group’s red-carpet answer, reflecting broader expectations among some Latino fans for explicit stances on immigration policy [2]. Infobae documented disputed clips and contested assertions, while UnoTV and Latin Times reported widespread criticism and calls for cancellation [8][9][10].

- Measurable commercial or legal consequences: Based on reporting cited here, there were no documented cancellations of contracted performances, sponsor withdrawals, legal actions, or chart impacts attributable to the controversy; the band’s planned Sueños headlining performance occurred on schedule [5].

## Performance at the 2023 Grito de Independencia (Mexico City)

On September 15, 2023, Grupo Frontera headlined a free public concert in Mexico City’s Zócalo as part of the official Grito de Independencia ceremony presided over by President Andrés Manuel López Obrador [13]. Prior to the event, AMLO publicly addressed public criticism of the invited act Yahritza y Su Esencia and called for forgiveness—“debemos perdonar”—as they would appear during the celebration, where audience reaction to Yahritza was mixed and included booing [15][14]. TV Azteca’s coverage documented AMLO’s arenga (slogans), including “Vivan nuestros hermanos migrantes; vivan los pueblos indígenas; viva la grandeza cultural de México; viva el amor” (“Long live our migrant brothers; long live indigenous peoples; long live the cultural greatness of Mexico; long live love”), and “Que muera el racismo, que muera la discriminación” (“Let racism die, let discrimination die”) [14]. The sources surveyed did not attribute notable political controversy to Grupo Frontera’s participation in the governmental event; audience backlash reported at the ceremony was directed at Yahritza rather than at Grupo Frontera [14][15][13].

## Reactions and impact

Media and fan reactions in early 2025 illustrate how online symbolic associations (music linked to campaign rallies) and relatives’ posts can fuel perceived endorsements, prompting demands for clarity from artists with large Latino audiences. The band’s February statements and subsequent coverage emphasized a nonpartisan stance and migrant identity, seeking to separate the group’s position from relatives' opinions and off-stage routines [1][2][3][6][7]. The Sueños petition reflected mobilization among a subset of fans, but the absence of cancellations or legal repercussions and the continuation of scheduled performances suggest limited direct commercial impact as documented by the cited reporting [4][5].

## Legacy

By late 2025, the most extensively covered political controversy involving Grupo Frontera was the early-year episode concerning alleged support for Donald Trump, which produced significant online backlash, petition activity, and media analysis, followed by clear denials from the band and clarifications of their migrant-focused identity and nonpartisan posture [1][2][3][6][7]. In Mexico, the group’s participation in the 2023 Grito de Independencia ceremony placed them at a highly political governmental event, though the sources reviewed did not ascribe a distinct controversy to the band’s performance itself [13][14][15]. Interviews and public messages in 2025 continued to emphasize support for migrants without engaging in candidate or party endorsements [7][16].

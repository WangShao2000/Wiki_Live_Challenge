# Washington Dulles International Airport Main Terminal

The Washington Dulles International Airport Main Terminal is the headhouse building of Washington Dulles International Airport in Dulles, Virginia, designed by Eero Saarinen & Associates and opened in 1962. It is operated by the Metropolitan Washington Airports Authority (MWAA). The building houses the airport’s principal landside functions—ticketing and check‑in, security screening, arrivals and baggage claim, information and customer services—and provides the public roadway forecourt and portico. It is distinct from Dulles’s midfield concourses, which are located on separate structures reached from the Main Terminal via internal transport systems (today primarily the AeroTrain). Saarinen’s design is widely cited in architectural histories for its expressive structure of leaning concrete piers and a suspended roof, and for integrating a novel passenger-processing concept built around “mobile lounges” that originally connected the Main Terminal directly to aircraft parked on remote aprons or at concourses. The Main Terminal and related historic elements are included within the Washington Dulles International Airport historic district listed on the National Register of Historic Places. [MWAA – History](https://www.mwaa.com/about/history) [FlyDulles – Terminal Information](https://www.flydulles.com/iad/terminal-information) [NPS – National Register of Historic Places](https://www.nps.gov/subjects/nationalregister/index.htm)

## Identification and scope

- Official name and common variants: The building is formally referred to by MWAA and the airport as the “Main Terminal” of Washington Dulles International Airport, and colloquially as the “Dulles Main Terminal” or “Saarinen terminal.” The article scope is limited to the headhouse building and its forecourt/portico, including public landside levels (departures/check‑in, arrivals/baggage, mezzanines), and associated below‑grade structures tied to the terminal (e.g., AeroTrain access within the terminal footprint). It does not cover the midfield concourses (A, B, C, D) or airside buildings except where interfaces directly affect the Main Terminal. [FlyDulles – Terminal Information](https://www.flydulles.com/iad/terminal-information) [MWAA – Airport Guide](https://www.flydulles.com/iad)

- Location, jurisdiction, and operator: The Main Terminal stands in Dulles, Virginia, within Loudoun and Fairfax Counties, and is owned and operated by the Metropolitan Washington Airports Authority (MWAA) under authority transferred from the federal government in 1987. [MWAA – History](https://www.mwaa.com/about/history)

- Historic district components: Historic designation materials describe the “Washington Dulles International Airport Historic District” to encompass the Saarinen‑designed Main Terminal and contributing elements such as the original air traffic control tower and mobile lounge vehicles, reflecting the integrated design concept of the early airport complex. [NPS – National Register of Historic Places](https://www.nps.gov/subjects/nationalregister/index.htm) [Library of Congress – HABS/HAER Collection](https://www.loc.gov/pictures/collection/hh/)

## Design and architecture

- Architect and team: The Main Terminal was designed by Eero Saarinen & Associates. Landscape architect Dan Kiley collaborated on the site and forecourt landscape. After Saarinen’s death in 1961, his firm (with senior associates including Kevin Roche and John Dinkeloo) completed the project. Structural engineering services were provided by major U.S. firms working with Saarinen’s office; contemporary HAER and architectural histories identify the use of battered reinforced‑concrete piers and a tension‑supported roof structure requiring close collaboration between the architect and structural engineers. [Library of Congress – HABS/HAER: Dulles Terminal](https://www.loc.gov/pictures/collection/hh/) [Architectural Record – Saarinen at Dulles](https://www.architecturalrecord.com)

- Form and structural concept: The building is defined by a rhythmic array of inclined (battered) concrete piers flanking a continuous, curved roof that is hung from steel cables, producing a catenary profile and a large, column‑free interior hall. Full‑height glazing creates transparent front and rear facades under the roof’s overhangs, while the piers carry both vertical and lateral loads. The materials palette combines concrete, steel, and expansive glass. The expressive structural system is a hallmark of mid‑century modern airport design and is frequently compared to Saarinen’s contemporaneous TWA Flight Center for its sculptural roof and focus on passenger experience. [Library of Congress – HABS/HAER: Dulles Terminal](https://www.loc.gov/pictures/collection/hh/) [Architectural Record – Saarinen at Dulles](https://www.architecturalrecord.com)

- Passenger-processing concept and mobile lounges: Saarinen’s concept for Dulles aimed to reduce walking distances by placing ticketing and processing in the Main Terminal and moving passengers airside in “mobile lounges” that docked at the terminal and at aircraft or concourse gates. The mobile lounge system was designed with input from the architect and airport authorities and was implemented as purpose‑built vehicles that could provide a controlled, comfortable environment between the headhouse and aircraft. While today the AeroTrain is the primary mode connecting the Main Terminal to the midfield concourses, mobile lounges continue to be used for certain international arrivals and remote stands. [Smithsonian – Mobile Lounges at Dulles](https://airandspace.si.edu) [FlyDulles – AeroTrain](https://www.flydulles.com/iad/aerotrain)

- Spatial organization and levels: The Main Terminal’s principal public spaces include a large, open departures hall for ticketing/check‑in and security screening; mezzanine and administrative levels; and an arrivals/baggage claim level below or behind the main hall, with Customs and Border Protection facilities located in expansions of the terminal’s lower levels. Daylighting from the glazed facades and roof overhangs is integral to the interior character. [FlyDulles – Terminal Information](https://www.flydulles.com/iad/terminal-information)

- Dimensions and physical characteristics: Architectural documentation and HAER records describe the terminal as a long, low volume with a significant roof span supported by leaning concrete piers and cable suspension; detailed bay counts, spans, and floor areas are recorded in technical drawings and survey archives. Public sources emphasize the length of the building and its clear‑span hall but present dimensions vary with later lengthening and interior reconfiguration. [Library of Congress – HABS/HAER: Dulles Terminal](https://www.loc.gov/pictures/collection/hh/) [MWAA – Facilities](https://www.mwaa.com)

## Planning and construction history

- Federal planning and site selection: Dulles was planned by the Federal Aviation Agency in the 1950s to serve long‑haul jet traffic for the Washington region, with a new site in the outer suburbs selected to permit longer runways and a modern layout. The Main Terminal commission was awarded to Eero Saarinen & Associates as part of the airport’s overall design. [MWAA – History](https://www.mwaa.com/about/history) [FAA – Airport Planning History](https://www.faa.gov)

- Design development and timeline: Saarinen’s office developed the terminal and mobile lounge concept in the late 1950s; construction proceeded in phases culminating in an opening ceremony. After Saarinen died in 1961, his associates continued the work to completion in 1962. [Library of Congress – HABS/HAER: Dulles Terminal](https://www.loc.gov/pictures/collection/hh/) [Architectural Record – Saarinen at Dulles](https://www.architecturalrecord.com)

- Opening and dedication: The airport and Main Terminal were dedicated by President John F. Kennedy on November 17, 1962, in a public ceremony that marked the opening of the nation’s first major airport designed for the jet age. Contemporary press accounts and MWAA historical summaries document the event and its national significance. [MWAA – History](https://www.mwaa.com/about/history) [The Washington Post – Archive](https://www.washingtonpost.com/)

- Original project cost and funding: The airport, including the Main Terminal, was federally funded through congressional appropriations for the Federal Aviation Agency; MWAA historical materials summarize the federal role and subsequent transfer of airport operations to MWAA in 1987. [MWAA – History](https://www.mwaa.com/about/history) [FAA – Airport Development](https://www.faa.gov)

## Alterations, expansions, and renovations

- Terminal lengthening and interior expansions: To accommodate growth, MWAA undertook projects to extend the Main Terminal length and to expand ticketing/check‑in halls, international arrivals and baggage areas, and support spaces, while seeking to preserve the Saarinen design’s defining elements (battered piers, roof form, transparency). MWAA and preservation documentation describe phased expansions, including below‑grade additions for international arrivals and reconfiguration of interior processing zones. [MWAA – Capital Projects](https://www.mwaa.com) [Library of Congress – HABS/HAER: Dulles Terminal](https://www.loc.gov/pictures/collection/hh/)

- Post‑9/11 security reconfiguration: After 2001, security screening was consolidated and enlarged within the Main Terminal, altering circulation and queuing within the departures hall. MWAA project summaries note construction of expanded checkpoint areas and modifications to maintain sightlines and architectural integrity. [MWAA – Security and Facilities](https://www.flydulles.com/iad) [MWAA – Capital Projects](https://www.mwaa.com)

- AeroTrain integration: The underground AeroTrain system began passenger service in 2010, linking the Main Terminal to the A, B, and C concourses via below‑grade tunnels and stations. Within the Main Terminal, access to the AeroTrain is provided from the secure area after screening, via escalators and elevators to the underground platforms. MWAA’s official materials describe the system start‑up and the operational shift away from routine mobile lounge use. [FlyDulles – AeroTrain](https://www.flydulles.com/iad/aerotrain) [MWAA – Press Releases](https://www.mwaa.com)

- Mobile lounge docks and operational changes: As AeroTrain became the primary mode to the midfield concourses, mobile lounge docks at the Main Terminal were retained and reconfigured for selective uses (particularly international arrivals processing and remote aircraft positions). Smithsonian and MWAA sources note the continued specialized role of the lounges. [Smithsonian – Mobile Lounges at Dulles](https://airandspace.si.edu) [MWAA – Facilities](https://www.mwaa.com)

- Envelope and roof work; conservation: MWAA has undertaken maintenance and rehabilitation of the Main Terminal’s roof membrane, glazing, and concrete piers, guided by preservation best practices to retain the building’s character‑defining features while upgrading performance. Preservation bodies have discussed approaches to maintaining the original intent, including careful replacement of materials and reversible interventions. [MWAA – Capital Projects](https://www.mwaa.com) [Library of Congress – HABS/HAER: Dulles Terminal](https://www.loc.gov/pictures/collection/hh/)

## Operations and functionality within the terminal

- Current functional layout: The Main Terminal houses airline check‑in zones along the departures hall, centralized security screening (TSA checkpoints), post‑security circulation leading to AeroTrain access within the building, and, on the arrivals level, baggage claim carousels and customer services (information desks, ground transportation counters). International arrivals processing (CBP passport control, baggage re‑inspection) is located in expanded areas tied to the terminal’s lower levels. [FlyDulles – Terminal Information](https://www.flydulles.com/iad/terminal-information) [FlyDulles – AeroTrain](https://www.flydulles.com/iad/aerotrain)

- Movement from the Main Terminal to concourses: After security, passengers descend within the Main Terminal to the AeroTrain platforms. Mobile lounges operate for specific flights and conditions (e.g., international arrivals to facilities under the Main Terminal), but most routine gate access is via AeroTrain. [FlyDulles – AeroTrain](https://www.flydulles.com/iad/aerotrain)

- Accessibility and wayfinding: The Main Terminal provides elevators, ramps, and accessible restrooms; MWAA publishes wayfinding and accessibility guidance, including visual signage, audio announcements, and assistance services for travelers with disabilities. [FlyDulles – Accessibility](https://www.flydulles.com/iad) [MWAA – ADA Information](https://www.mwaa.com)

- Public art and exhibitions: MWAA’s Arts Program features rotating exhibitions and installations in public airport spaces, including the Main Terminal hall and arrivals level, curated in partnership with regional arts organizations and museums. Program pages describe the scope and locations of displays. [MWAA – Arts Program](https://www.mwaa.com)

## Transportation interfaces directly tied to the Main Terminal

- Roadway forecourt and portico: The Main Terminal is fronted by a two‑level roadway system providing departures (upper) and arrivals (lower) curbside access, with a covered portico under the terminal roof overhangs and adjacent canopies. The forecourt roadway loop connects to parking garages and commercial lanes, with pedestrian connections to the terminal at both levels. [FlyDulles – Directions & Parking](https://www.flydulles.com/parking-transportation) [MWAA – Facilities](https://www.mwaa.com)

- Pedestrian connections to parking: A network of indoor corridors and tunnels connects the Main Terminal to adjacent garages, allowing arrivals level access to baggage claim and to the departures hall via escalators/elevators. [FlyDulles – Parking Garages](https://www.flydulles.com/parking-transportation)

- Metrorail Silver Line station connection: The “Dulles Airport” Metrorail Silver Line station opened on November 15, 2022. A dedicated pedestrian walkway/tunnel links the station directly to the Main Terminal, entering at the terminal’s arrivals/baggage level with clear signage and weather‑protected access. WMATA’s and MWAA’s official pages provide station opening details and the exact connection point within the terminal complex. [WMATA – Silver Line Phase 2 Opening](https://www.wmata.com/about/news/Phase-Two-Silver-Line-Opening.cfm) [FlyDulles – Metrorail to Dulles](https://www.flydulles.com/parking-transportation/metrorail-silver-line)

## Heritage status, recognition, and critical reception

- Historic designations: The Washington Dulles International Airport Historic District, which includes the Main Terminal and related features, is listed on the National Register of Historic Places, reflecting its significance in the evolution of air transportation architecture and engineering. The listing documentation describes the extent and contributing resources. [NPS – National Register of Historic Places](https://www.nps.gov/subjects/nationalregister/index.htm) [Virginia DHR – Historic Registers](https://www.dhr.virginia.gov/)

- Awards and honors: The Main Terminal has been recognized by professional organizations; AIA publications and awards histories frequently cite the building among significant works of the postwar era, and MWAA and architectural journals note honors given to the airport’s design over time. [AIA – Awards & Honors](https://www.aia.org/awards) [Architectural Record – Saarinen at Dulles](https://www.architecturalrecord.com)

- Critical reception: Contemporary and retrospective critiques in Architectural Record, museum publications, and major newspapers have highlighted the building’s expressive structural logic, clarity of circulation in its original concept, and the mobile lounge system’s promise and limitations as passenger volumes grew. Commentaries have discussed how later adaptations (security reconfigurations, AeroTrain integration) affected the experience while attempting to preserve Saarinen’s intent. [Architectural Record – Saarinen at Dulles](https://www.architecturalrecord.com) [Smithsonian – Mobile Lounges at Dulles](https://airandspace.si.edu) [The Washington Post – Architecture Coverage](https://www.washingtonpost.com/)

- Dedication ceremony: The airport and Main Terminal were dedicated by President John F. Kennedy on November 17, 1962, in a ceremony held at the terminal and airfield. [MWAA – History](https://www.mwaa.com/about/history) [The Washington Post – Archive](https://www.washingtonpost.com/)

- Major renovation milestones: MWAA press releases and project pages document milestones such as expansions to the international arrivals facility, the opening of the AeroTrain system (2010) with terminal integration, and the Silver Line station walkway opening (2022) connecting directly to the terminal.

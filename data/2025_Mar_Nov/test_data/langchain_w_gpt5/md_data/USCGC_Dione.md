# USCGC Dione

USCGC Dione was a United States Coast Guard patrol cutter of the 165-foot “A” class, hull number WPC-106, built at Bath Iron Works in Bath, Maine, and commissioned in 1931. Designed for law enforcement and rescue off the U.S. coasts, Dione served through the Prohibition era and World War II, performing patrol, escort, and antisubmarine duties under Navy control as part of the Eastern Sea Frontier. The cutter was decommissioned after the war. Dione is the only documented U.S. Coast Guard cutter to bear that name, and is commonly cataloged within the group of mythologically named 165-foot patrol craft commissioned at the start of the 1930s. ## Nomenclature and scope

- The designation “USCGC Dione” refers to the Coast Guard cutter Dione, hull classification symbol WPC-106, a 165-foot patrol craft (“A” class) placed in service in 1931. [USCGC Dione (WPC-106) – NavSource Online Coast Guard Photo Archive](http://www.navsource.org/archives/12/170106.htm) [1]
- No additional Coast Guard vessel bearing the name Dione has been identified in official Coast Guard historical listings of cutters and craft; available cataloging treats WPC-106 as the sole namesake. [U.S. Coast Guard Historian’s Office – Cutters & Craft](https://www.history.uscg.mil/Browse-by-Topic/Assets/Cutters/) [2]

## Design and construction

The 165-foot “A” class patrol cutters were conceived at the end of the 1920s as multi-mission craft able to conduct law enforcement, search and rescue, and offshore patrols. They were diesel-powered, steel-hulled vessels built for endurance in coastal waters and, during wartime, adapted for convoy escort and antisubmarine warfare. Dione (WPC-106) was constructed by Bath Iron Works at Bath, Maine, among a series of sister ships of the same class. ## Specifications

The following characteristics reflect the typical configuration of the Coast Guard’s 165-foot “A” class patrol cutters to which Dione belonged. Class specifications are drawn from official Coast Guard historical materials and contemporary Navy-frontier documentation; wartime fittings varied among ships based on assignment. [U.S. Coast Guard Historian’s Office – Cutters & Craft](https://www.history.uscg.mil/Browse-by-Topic/Assets/Cutters/) [2]; [Naval History and Heritage Command – Eastern Sea Frontier](https://www.history.navy.mil/research/library/online-reading-room/title-list-alphabetically/e/eastern-sea-frontier.html) [3]

- Length: approximately 165 ft
- Beam: approximately 25 ft
- Draft: approximately 12 ft
- Displacement: roughly 300–350 long tons (varied by fit)
- Propulsion: twin diesel engines driving two shafts (manufacturer and horsepower varied within class)
- Speed: about 15–16 knots
- Range/endurance: designed for extended coastal patrols; endurance dependent on fuel load and mission
- Armament (wartime typical): one 3-inch/50 caliber gun, light antiaircraft weapons (e.g., 20 mm Oerlikon), .50 caliber machine guns, and depth charge racks/throwers for antisubmarine warfare
- Crew complement: on the order of several dozen officers and enlisted personnel, commonly between 50 and 60 in wartime service

Class-wide technical baselines and the addition of antisubmarine gear and convoy escort armament were standardized across Eastern Sea Frontier patrol cutters during World War II. [Naval History and Heritage Command – Eastern Sea Frontier](https://www.history.navy.mil/research/library/online-reading-room/title-list-alphabetically/e/eastern-sea-frontier.html) [3]

## Service history

### Prewar operations

Upon commissioning in 1931, Dione entered service as a general-purpose patrol cutter in the Coast Guard’s Atlantic districts. The 165-foot “A” class cutters carried out law-enforcement patrols and search-and-rescue missions along the U.S. coastline during the final years of Prohibition, continuing similar duties afterward. Contemporary Coast Guard class histories and photographic records place Dione among these cutters assigned to coastal patrol sectors before wartime mobilization. [USCGC Dione (WPC-106) – NavSource Online Coast Guard Photo Archive](http://www.navsource.org/archives/12/170106.htm) [1]; [U.S. Coast Guard Historian’s Office – Cutters & Craft](https://www.history.uscg.mil/Browse-by-Topic/Assets/Cutters/) [2]

### World War II

Following transfer of Coast Guard cutters to Navy operational control during World War II, Dione operated as part of the Eastern Sea Frontier command structure, which coordinated defense, patrol, and convoy escort in Atlantic coastal waters. In this role, the cutter’s missions included:

- Coastal patrols to deter and detect German U-boats along the U.S. Eastern Seaboard. [Naval History and Heritage Command – Eastern Sea Frontier](https://www.history.navy.mil/research/library/online-reading-room/title-list-alphabetically/e/eastern-sea-frontier.html) [3]
- Search and rescue of mariners from torpedoed or distressed vessels within the frontier’s sectors. [Naval History and Heritage Command – Eastern Sea Frontier](https://www.history.navy.mil/research/library/online-reading-room/title-list-alphabetically/e/eastern-sea-frontier.html) [3]
- Convoy escort and antisubmarine patrols using depth charges and standard escort armament common to the class. [Naval History and Heritage Command – Eastern Sea Frontier](https://www.history.navy.mil/research/library/online-reading-room/title-list-alphabetically/e/eastern-sea-frontier.html) [3]

While other sister ships in the 165-foot patrol cutter cohort recorded specific U-boat engagements, Dione’s documented World War II service falls within the broader Eastern Sea Frontier pattern of escort and patrol without a published record of a submarine sinking. Available photo-archive material confirms wartime armament fits and Atlantic service context. [USCGC Dione (WPC-106) – NavSource Online Coast Guard Photo Archive](http://www.navsource.org/archives/12/170106.htm) [1]; [Naval History and Heritage Command – Eastern Sea Frontier](https://www.history.navy.mil/research/library/online-reading-room/title-list-alphabetically/e/eastern-sea-frontier.html) [3]

### Postwar status

After the end of hostilities, Coast Guard patrol cutters of Dione’s class reverted to peacetime Coast Guard control and were gradually decommissioned as newer cutters entered service and wartime craft were disposed of. Dione’s photo-archive entry records postwar decommissioning, consistent with class-wide drawdowns in the late 1940s. [USCGC Dione (WPC-106) – NavSource Online Coast Guard Photo Archive](http://www.navsource.org/archives/12/170106.htm) [1]; [U.S. Coast Guard Historian’s Office – Cutters & Craft](https://www.history.uscg.mil/Browse-by-Topic/Assets/Cutters/) [2]

## Homeports, districts, and command

Assignments of 165-foot “A” class cutters such as Dione included Coast Guard Atlantic districts and, during wartime, the Navy’s Eastern Sea Frontier under the Commander, Eastern Sea Frontier. These cutters operated from coastal bases along the Eastern Seaboard to conduct patrol and escort tasks. Specific year-by-year homeport details for Dione are not provided in accessible online official summaries; Eastern Sea Frontier documentation outlines the operational regions and command relationships for Coast Guard cutters in Dione’s cohort. ## Awards

As an Atlantic Coast Guard cutter serving under Navy control in World War II, Dione’s service would have qualified her crew for applicable U.S. campaign and service medals awarded to Coast Guard personnel for Eastern Sea Frontier duty, such as the American Defense Service Medal (for pre–Pearl Harbor readiness), the American Campaign Medal, and the World War II Victory Medal, consistent with published Navy-frontier award criteria. Specific cutter-level unit citations for Dione are not documented in the publicly accessible online summaries consulted. ## Decommissioning and fate

Dione was decommissioned after World War II as the Coast Guard retired or disposed of many of its interwar patrol cutters. The NavSource photo archive notes postwar decommissioning of WPC-106; detailed federal sale or transfer records and any subsequent civilian renaming are not available in the general-access online materials cited here. ## Legacy

USCGC Dione is representative of the Coast Guard’s 165-foot “A” class cutters that bridged interwar law-enforcement and rescue missions with wartime convoy escort and antisubmarine roles along the U.S. Atlantic coast. While not individually credited with sinkings, Dione’s service contributes to the documented collective role of Coast Guard patrol cutters in the Eastern Sea Frontier’s wartime defense and lifesaving operations, and to the evolution of medium-endurance patrol craft designs in the Coast Guard fleet.

# Dorfromantik (board game)

Dorfromantik: Das Brettspiel (marketed in English as Dorfromantik – The Boardgame) is a cooperative tile‑laying board game designed by Michael Palm and Lukas Zach and published by Pegasus Spiele. It adapts Dorfromantik, the hex‑tile landscape‑building video game by the Berlin studio Toukana Interactive, under license. In the tabletop game, 1–6 players collaboratively place hexagonal tiles to build a pastoral landscape, fulfill exact‑size “task” objectives, extend the longest river and railway, and score closed areas marked with flags. A replayable campaign unlocks new tiles and rules from sealed boxes, expanding the game over multiple plays. The German edition entered retail in November 2022; U.S. distribution began in March 2023. The game won Spiel des Jahres 2023; German media highlighted it as the first tabletop adaptation of a video game to receive that award [1][2][4][6][7].

## Overview

- Titles and license: The official German title is Dorfromantik – Das Brettspiel. Pegasus Spiele’s English‑language listing uses Dorfromantik – The Boardgame. The tabletop game is “based on Dorfromantik,” the award‑winning video game by Toukana Interactive, credited in the rulebook and on publisher pages [1][2][3].
- Credits and publisher: Designers Michael Palm and Lukas Zach are credited for the board game design; illustrations are by Paul Riebe; graphic design by Jens Wiese; realization by Klaus Ottmaier. Publisher is Pegasus Spiele (Germany), with U.S. distribution by Pegasus Spiele North America [2][1][3].
- Specifications: Player count 1–6; playtime about 30–60 minutes. The German rulebook lists ages 8+, while Pegasus Spiele North America lists the U.S. English edition for ages 14+ (reflecting regional labeling) [2][3].
- Notability: Dorfromantik: Das Brettspiel won Spiel des Jahres 2023; the jury praised its accessible, goal‑driven cooperative format. German national media reported it as the first video game adaptation to win the prize [6][7].

## Development and publication

Pegasus Spiele announced the tabletop adaptation at its autumn 2022 press event, noting a preview allotment at SPIEL ’22 in Essen and a planned Q4 2022 release. Trade coverage reported a German MSRP of €34.99 at announcement and the intent to launch by the end of that year [5]. The publisher’s online store lists the German retail availability date as 16 November 2022, with a shop price of €39.99 and downloadable rulebook and play sheets [1].

For English‑language markets, ICv2 reported that Pegasus Spiele would release Dorfromantik into U.S. retail in March 2023 with an MSRP of $39.99. Pegasus Spiele North America later listed the U.S. English product on 10 October 2023 at a shop price of $44.95, with U.S. customer service and English downloads (rules, scorepad, campaign sheet, and an addendum) [4][3]. The rulebook credits explicitly acknowledge the license and the video game’s developers at Toukana Interactive [2].

Pegasus subsequently expanded the product line (see Expansions and spin‑offs) and published a compact standalone title, Dorfromantik – Leichtes Gepäck, in 2025 [8].

## Gameplay

Dorfromantik is a fully cooperative tile‑placement game set on an open table surface. Players work together to build a contiguous landscape of hexagonal tiles and to maximize a team high score. The rules emphasize accessibility and a shared puzzle rather than head‑to‑head competition [2].

- Setup and flow: Players sort the tile stacks into “task tiles” and “landscape tiles.” For the first three turns of a game, a player draws one task tile per turn, draws a matching task marker (showing a value 4/5/6 for that task type), places the marker on the tile, and then places the tile in the landscape. Starting on the fourth turn, each turn begins by checking the number of task tiles with markers in play: if fewer than three, draw and place a task tile (plus marker); if exactly three, draw and place a landscape tile [2].
- Placement rules: New tiles must touch at least one previously placed tile. Rivers and railways must be continued on matching edges; other terrain edges (e.g., forest, village, grain/field) may be mismatched, but mismatches can affect scoring opportunities [2].
- Tasks: Task markers show an exact size requirement. To fulfill a task, the connected area (forest, village, field) or line (rail, river) linked to the task tile must reach exactly the indicated number of tiles at any point after placement. Players may not place a task tile if, at the moment of placement, its connected feature would already exceed the task value, or if they would immediately close it with too few tiles. If later growth overshoots or closure undershoots the exact value, that task is aborted and its marker is returned to the box [2].
- Flags and end scoring: Some landscape tiles show flags for forests, fields, or villages. At game end, only flags in closed areas score; each such flag yields points equal to the size of its completed area. The final score is the sum of points from fulfilled tasks by type, scoring flags, the counts of the longest railway and longest river, and any points granted by unlocked content [2].
- End of game: The game ends immediately when a player must draw a landscape tile but the landscape tile stacks are empty. If the last landscape tile fulfills or aborts a task, players resolve follow‑up task draws until no further tasks are fulfilled or aborted, then proceed to scoring [2].
- Modes: The game supports cooperative play for 1–6 players and scales by sharing a single joint landscape and jointly deciding tile placement. The rule set is identical in solo play [2].

## Campaign and progression

Dorfromantik features a replayable campaign driven by a persistent score track. After each game, the team records its score on a campaign sheet. Reaching threshold spaces on the campaign track unlocks sealed content boxes and success cards that introduce additional tiles, cards, markers, wooden heart tokens, and minor rules additions. Unlocked content then remains available for future games, increasing variety and potential scoring pathways. The campaign pad and score pad are consumable sheets included in the box, and the rulebook includes setup guidance for integrating special tiles into the landscape stacks after an initial removal of three random landscape tiles during setup [2].

## Components

Component details are provided in the official rulebook and on the publisher’s product page:

- Rulebook enumeration: The German rulebook (2nd edition v1.1) lists as core components 73 hex tiles (48 landscape tiles; 25 task tiles across five types), 25 task markers (valued 4/5/6 by type), a 50‑sheet score pad, and a 20‑sheet campaign pad. Sealed content across five boxes adds 32 further tiles, 31 cards, 8 additional markers, 5 wooden heart tokens, and 1 plastic standee/foot. The rulebook also describes the plastic insert with compartments for tiles, markers, and the sealed boxes [2].
- Publisher listing: Pegasus Spiele’s German product page lists, among other items, “104 hex landscape tiles,” “33 order markers,” five wooden hearts, one standee, a score pad, a campaign pad, and five sealed boxes with secret unlockable content, plus the German rulebook. The page also offers official downloads (rules, score sheets, campaign sheet, and inserts) [1].

Counts differ by whether unlocked content is included in totals; both enumerations are provided by the publisher.

## Expansions and spin‑offs

- Dorfromantik – Das Duell (standalone, competitive): A two‑player (or two‑team) competitive version in which each side builds its own landscape and compares scores. It introduces two new task types—Double and Roundabout (“Doppel- und Rundumaufträge”)—and includes two independent modules to add challenge and interaction. Components also include special tiles and module cards. The new task types can be integrated into the cooperative base game. With two copies, up to four players or teams can play. The German edition has been listed since 6 October 2023; ICv2 reported the English edition heading to U.S. retail in February 2024 with a $39.99 MSRP. The German rules detail objective fulfillment, end‑of‑game conditions, and module content [9][10][11].
- Dorfromantik – Sakura (standalone sequel): A cooperative campaign entry set in a Japanese‑themed environment that adds cherry blossoms to the familiar mix of villages, fields, roads, rivers, and rails. Pegasus lists 1–6 players, ages 10+, 30–90 minutes, with six sealed boxes and “over 40 new achievements” to unlock. The product page details a contents list including 110 hex tiles, 39 task markers, 33 additional tiles, a board, 47 cards, and 9 wooden objects. The German product listing date is 16 September 2024. An English rules PDF confirms designer and illustrator credits and the campaign structure [12][13].
- Dorfromantik – Leichtes Gepäck (Light Luggage; standalone, compact): A travel‑oriented compact entry packaged in a small magnet‑closure box that condenses the Spiel des Jahres–winning system “into a handy magnet box” and replaces the full campaign with an integrated high‑score track and new unlockable content. Pegasus announced availability at an MSRP of €19.99 on 4 November 2025. Coverage in Pegasus’s Ringbote magazine describes it as for 1–4 players with a 20–40 minute playtime and mentions that it includes a mini‑expansion for the original Dorfromantik [8].
- Mini‑expansions for the base game:
  - Große Mühle (The Great Mill): A mini‑expansion containing two cards and a mill standee. After the mill success is unlocked, players can deploy the mill figure to gain extra points for grain tasks and yellow flags. Pegasus offers separate German and English versions [14].
  - Die Wetterau: A three‑tile regional mini‑expansion themed to the Wetterau region of Hesse. The BoardGameGeek entry lists it as a three‑tile add‑on for the base game [15].
- Special retail bundle: A “Fachhandels‑exklusiv” bundle packages the base game with both mini‑expansions (Große Mühle and Die Wetterau) for the German market [16].

## Reception

Hobby and general‑interest outlets have described Dorfromantik as an accessible, calming cooperative puzzle with a campaign that encourages repeat play, while also noting limitations at higher player counts and the influence of randomness:

- Meeple Mountain characterized the game as having “an incredibly simple teach and infinite replayability,” with the campaign’s five small boxes providing a “real hook to play the game over and over again,” and described the experience as a “peaceful puzzle” [18].
- Geeks Under Grace called it “a wonderful cooperative experience for the family, and a worthy winner of the Spiel des Jahres,” but criticized the 1–6 player claim as “absolutely ridiculous,” implying that it plays best at lower counts and in solo, and noted that the “legacy”‑style progression is a major part of its appeal [19].
- What’s Eric Playing highlighted the way complexity increases as content is unlocked and suggested four or fewer players as “an ideal number” to avoid analysis bottlenecks [20].
- German‑language outlets emphasized both the game’s accessibility and its solitaire feel. Brettspielbox wrote that “chance plays a strong role” (“Der Zufall spielt kräftig mit”), while also commending its broad appeal and the motivation created by unlocking boxes [21]. Hall9000 noted that “basically, it’s actually a solitaire game” and that the lack of a losing state adds “a form of lightness” and positive atmosphere [22]. Reich der Spiele praised the solo experience as “unbelievably good,” while finding the multiplayer experience less compelling [23].

## Awards

- Spiel des Jahres 2023: The Spiel des Jahres jury named Dorfromantik: Das Brettspiel the 2023 Game of the Year, with jury chair Harald Schrapers stating that the game “takes the pressure out of everyday life. The cooperative feel‑good game sets new, exciting goals from game to game, but you can never lose.” The jury also cited how easily teachable it is and how its campaign entices new plays [6].
- German national media including Der Spiegel reported on the award and noted that it was the first time a board game based on a computer/video game had won the prize [7].

## Sales and impact

Following the Spiel des Jahres award, the Frankfurter Allgemeine Zeitung reported that Pegasus Spiele planned to increase production and projected up to 300,000 copies sold of Dorfromantik in time for the 2023 holiday season, contingent on business conditions [24]. The game’s high‑profile award and family‑friendly positioning were cited in coverage as factors in its market performance [6][7][24].

## Criticism and notable discussions

- Player count and group dynamics: Multiple reviewers suggested that Dorfromantik’s cooperative decision‑making shines in solo or small groups and can bog down at higher counts due to collective analysis and limited individual agency. Some called it “essentially a solitaire game,” and questioned the suitability of six players [19][22][23].
- Randomness and campaign pacing: Commentators noted the influence of tile draw randomness on task fulfillment and scoring, and debated the tension created by a design in which “you can never lose.” For some, the steady drip of positive feedback and unlocks created a motivating, low‑pressure atmosphere; others perceived reduced stakes in individual sessions [21][22].

## Publication history

- Germany: Announced in autumn 2022 with a preview allotment at SPIEL ’22 and a planned Q4 release; the Pegasus shop lists the game “in assortment since” 16 November 2022 [5][1].
- United States: ICv2 reported a March 2023 release to U.S. retail with an MSRP of $39.99; the U.S. web store later listed the product with a price of $44.95 [4][3].
- Product line growth: Pegasus released Dorfromantik – Das Duell (German listing 6 October 2023; English edition to U.S. retail in February 2024), Dorfromantik – Sakura (16 September 2024 listing in Germany), mini‑expansions Große Mühle and Die Wetterau, a special trade bundle including both minis, and the compact standalone Dorfromantik – Leichtes Gepäck (announced 4 November 2025 at €19.99) [9][10][12][14][15][16][8].

## Relationship to the video game

Dorfromantik: Das Brettspiel is a licensed adaptation of the Dorfromantik video game by Toukana Interactive. The board game credits explicitly reference the video game’s development team, and the publisher describes the tabletop as a “board game variant of the namesake video game” by the Berlin indie studio. The core tabletop loop—gentle tile placement, exact‑size tasks, and long route building—echoes the digital game’s focus while translating it into cooperative, campaign‑driven tabletop play [1][2].

# Second-Ponce de Leon Baptist Church

Second-Ponce de Leon Baptist Church is a Baptist congregation and multi-building church campus located at 2715 Peachtree Road NE, Atlanta, Georgia, in the Buckhead community’s Garden Hills neighborhood. The church was formed on November 18, 1932, through the merger of Second Baptist Church (organized in 1854) and Ponce de Leon Avenue Baptist Church; Buckhead Baptist Church had merged with Ponce de Leon Avenue Baptist in 1929, preceding the 1932 consolidation that produced the hyphenated name “Second-Ponce de Leon.” The current sanctuary complex on Peachtree Road was designed by architect George H. Bond and opened in 1937. The campus is a contributing institutional resource within the Garden Hills Historic District, which was listed on the National Register of Historic Places in 1987. The church participates in Baptist life across multiple networks, appearing in the Southern Baptist Convention’s church directory while also collaborating closely with the Cooperative Baptist Fellowship (nationally and in Georgia). Its ministries include worship, discipleship, missions, community programs, and a Community Wellness Center. The congregation has hosted regional and national religious events and civic memorials, maintains an active music ministry and notable pipe organ, and has been led by clergy whose service has intersected with Baptist higher education in Atlanta. [1][2][3][5][6][10][11][12][13][16][17][19][20]

## Location and campus

Second-Ponce de Leon Baptist Church is located at 2715 Peachtree Road NE, Atlanta, GA 30305. The campus hosts weekly worship in the main sanctuary and provides on-site and online access to services and programs. [1]

The multi-building campus includes:
- A four-story sanctuary building with worship center, music suite, and classrooms. [8]
- A Family Life Center and Children’s Education Building connected by an addition that provides campus-wide circulation and includes an indoor play area and renovated exterior playgrounds. [8]
- A four-story, approximately 50,000-square-foot chapel building containing the chapel, fellowship hall, commercial kitchen, administrative space, and adult Christian education space. [8]
- The Community Wellness Center (CWC), a public gym and ministry venue with an indoor pool, gymnasium, basketball and pickleball courts, racquetball court, cardio/weight rooms, indoor track, event rooms, saunas/steam rooms, locker rooms, and on-site parking. [3]

Targeted interior renovations for nursery and children’s spaces have included new theming, finishes, and secure check-in stations designed around a nature motif. [9]

## History

### Origins, mergers, and name

- Second Baptist Church was founded in 1854. [2]
- In 1929, Ponce de Leon Avenue Baptist Church and Buckhead Baptist Church merged and moved to the corner of Peachtree Road and East Wesley Road. [2]
- Second-Ponce de Leon Baptist Church was created on November 18, 1932, when Second Baptist Church merged with Ponce de Leon Avenue Baptist Church. A historical marker erected by the Georgia Baptist Convention commemorates the 1932 merger. [2][18]

The church’s hyphenated name reflects this merger of “Second” and “Ponce de Leon.” [2][18]

### Move to Buckhead and campus development

The present church stands within the Garden Hills neighborhood of Buckhead. The sanctuary designed by George H. Bond opened in 1937, anchoring the southwest side of the neighborhood’s historic district. The Garden Hills Historic District was listed on the National Register of Historic Places in 1987. [6][5]

## Architecture and historic designations

- Architect and opening: The main sanctuary was designed by Atlanta architect George H. Bond and opened in 1937. [6]
- Historic context: The church is a contributing institutional resource within the Garden Hills Historic District, which the National Park Service lists on the National Register of Historic Places (NRHP) under NRIS ID 87001362. The district is recognized for significance in architecture, community planning, and landscape architecture; it was listed in 1987. [5][6]

### Renovations and expansions

Subsequent campus projects have included:
- Renovation of the four-story sanctuary building, encompassing the worship center, music suite, classrooms, and youth department. [8]
- A connecting addition serving as a primary entry for the Family Life Center and Children’s Education Building, improving circulation and adding indoor and outdoor play spaces. [8]
- Full renovation of the four-story chapel building, including the chapel itself, fellowship hall, commercial kitchen, administrative offices, and adult education spaces. [8]
- Early childhood and children’s hallway improvements, with secure check-in points and nature-themed design elements. [9]

## Denominational affiliations

Second-Ponce de Leon Baptist Church participates in multiple Baptist networks:
- It appears in the Southern Baptist Convention’s Church Search directory. [12]
- The church collaborates with the Cooperative Baptist Fellowship (CBF) nationally and with CBF of Georgia; its pastor search materials state that the congregation “cooperate[s] with the Cooperative Baptist Fellowship global office and CBF of Georgia unless directed otherwise.” [13]
- The congregation has hosted CBF-related programs and events, including serving as the Atlanta regional gathering site for the CBF General Assembly in 2021 in partnership with CBF of Georgia and neighboring CBF state organizations. [10][11]

## Governance and organization

According to the church’s profile:
- Governance includes a Board of Deacons (with active and life deacons), an Executive Committee that acts between meetings, and standing committees such as Facilities, Finance, Nominating, Personnel, and Planned Gifts. [3]
- Ministry Councils (Community, Discipleship, Missions, Worship) align lay leadership with core congregational values and coordinate with ministerial staff through a Leadership Council. [3]
- Staff functions encompass ministerial leadership, music, administration, facilities, communications, finance, events, and Community Wellness Center management. [3]

## Membership, attendance, and demographics

The church’s profile reports:
- Average in-person Sunday worship attendance declined from 337 in 2015 to 144 in 2024, with a pandemic-related suspension of in-person services from March 15, 2020, to April 11, 2021. [3]
- Age distribution of members: approximately 60% age 65+, 32% age 35–64, and 8% age 19–34 (profile data period as reported). [3]
- Household composition includes married, single, widowed, and families with children at home; the congregation is described as predominantly White with racial/ethnic diversity including Asian, African American, Native American, and Pacific Islander members. [3]

## Ministries, programs, and partnerships

The church describes four core values—Worship, Missions, Discipleship, and Community—and supports these through programs and partnerships. Examples include: [1][3]
- Worship services (in-person and online), preaching, congregational singing, occasional orchestra, guest preachers, and special prayer experiences.
- Missions funding (including endowed support) and hands-on service such as mobile food pantry distributions in partnership with the Atlanta Community Food Bank and Toco Hills Community Alliance, summer camps, and Habitat for Humanity projects. [3][17]
- Discipleship through age-graded ministries for children, students, and adults; pastoral care, visitation, and support for homebound members; and volunteer service teams. [3]
- Community partnerships and hosted programs, including Respite Care Atlanta (founding congregation), Christ the King Preschool at Second-Ponce (launched 2022), Atlanta Suzuki Violin, Collective Learning Academy, and collaborations with organizations addressing domestic violence. [3]

## Music and arts

- Pipe organ: The sanctuary houses a pipe organ by M. P. Möller (Opus 9185), built in 1959, with multiple divisions and an expansive stoplist documented by the American Guild of Organists (Atlanta Chapter). [16]
- Hymnody and concerts: The church hosted the debut concerts of the Celebrating Grace hymnal, with congregational premieres on March 7–8, 2010, under the auspices of Mercer University. [14]
- Public recitals and seasonal music services are regularly offered through the church’s music ministry. [17]

## Notable clergy and staff

- Monroe F. Swilley Jr., a longtime pastor of the church, later became president of Atlanta Baptist College in 1969; after that institution’s 1972 merger with Mercer University, he served as vice president of Mercer’s Atlanta campus until retiring in 1989. [15]
- James “Dock” Hollingsworth, a former senior pastor, and other Mercer University-affiliated ministers have served on the staff; the Mercer–Second-Ponce relationship is noted in institutional reporting. [15]
- In recent years, ministerial roles have included music, missions, children’s discipleship, community, and student discipleship leadership. [15][3]

## Events and civic role

Second-Ponce has served as a venue for significant religious and civic occasions, including:
- Hosting the Atlanta regional in-person gathering for the Cooperative Baptist Fellowship’s 2021 hybrid General Assembly. [10][11]
- Serving as the site for memorial services for Griffin Bell, former U.S. Attorney General and federal judge, with attendance by national and Georgia leaders in January 2009. [19][20]
- Providing community-facing events such as food distributions and music programs. [17]

## Historic status

The church campus is within the Garden Hills Historic District, which the National Park Service lists on the National Register of Historic Places (NRIS ID 87001362). The district was listed in 1987 and is recognized for architectural and planning significance; the Second-Ponce sanctuary by George H. Bond (opened 1937) is among the district’s notable institutional resources. [5][6]

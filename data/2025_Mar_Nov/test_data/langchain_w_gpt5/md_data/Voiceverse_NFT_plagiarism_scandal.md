# Voiceverse NFT plagiarism scandal

The Voiceverse NFT plagiarism scandal refers to a January–February 2022 controversy surrounding Voiceverse NFT, a project that marketed “voice NFTs” and was publicly endorsed by voice actor Troy Baker. Shortly after Baker’s partnership announcement, the project was accused of using audio generated by the free, non‑commercial text‑to‑speech service 15.ai without permission and credit in promotional materials. The developer of 15.ai published technical claims and comparisons indicating that Voiceverse’s samples were derived from 15.ai outputs, including pitch‑shifted audio from My Little Pony characters. Voiceverse apologized, attributed the use to its marketing team, removed the content, and faced severe backlash. Baker withdrew from the partnership on January 31, 2022. The episode highlighted ethical and legal concerns around AI voice synthesis, consent, and commercialization via NFTs, and it contributed to wider skepticism from the voice acting and gaming communities about “voice NFTs.” [2][6][7][8][9][10][1]

## Background

Voiceverse NFT was presented as a project offering unique “voice NFTs” that purported to give holders ownership and utility rights to a synthetic voice “in the metaverse,” with uses promoted for in‑game chat, video calls, and social content. The project’s website described a “Voice Metaverse” and an initial drop of 8,888 “Voiceverse Origins” NFTs on the Ethereum blockchain, stating that holders could use and mix voices and that the NFTs were built with “next‑generation AI.” The site also listed team roles and claimed backing from companies such as Kakao and LG, and stated that Voiceverse was “powered by LOVO,” an AI speech synthesis company based in San Francisco and Seoul. [21][16]

A press release on February 1, 2022, described Voiceverse Origins as “the world’s first voice NFT,” said the public sale occurred on January 29, 2022, and claimed the collection sold out in less than 10 minutes, with trending status on OpenSea during the launch period. It also stated that Voiceverse was founded by three Bored Ape Yacht Club (BAYC) members and was “powered by LOVO.” [16] The associated collection page on OpenSea remained accessible under “Voiceverse Genesis Official.” [17]

## Product and claims

Voiceverse’s marketing promised:
- Ownership of a unique synthetic voice tied to an NFT, usable across platforms like YouTube, TikTok, Zoom, and game chats. [21]
- The ability to customize profile pictures (PFPs) by pairing them with voices, and to “mix” voices from different NFTs. [21]
- A roadmap presented as opening “a new stream of opportunities for voice actors” and “not replacing them,” according to explanations summarized in industry press. [2]

Prior to the scandal, third‑party NFT community write‑ups echoed Voiceverse claims that holders would have IP rights and “no legal hassle” in using voices, with plans for the 8,888 Origins drop at the end of January 2022. [14][15]

## Timeline

- January 13–14, 2022: Troy Baker announced a partnership with Voiceverse on X/Twitter, writing, “I’m partnering with @VoiceverseNFT to explore ways where together we might bring new tools to new creators… You can hate. Or you can create. What’ll it be?” The phrasing drew immediate criticism from fans, the voice acting community, and commentators wary of NFTs and synthetic voice “ownership.” [2][3][4][5]

- January 14, 2022: The 15.ai developer posted that Voiceverse was “actively attempting to appropriate my work for their own benefit,” and after “digging through the log files,” had evidence some voices credited to Voiceverse were generated by 15.ai. The developer also highlighted that the audio was pitch‑shifted to obfuscate the source, citing Rainbow Dash from My Little Pony. [7][8][9][11]

- January 14, 2022: Voiceverse apologized publicly to @fifteenai, acknowledging “The voice was indeed taken from your platform, which our marketing team used without giving proper credit… We will make sure this never happens again.” The apology accompanied removal of the offending promotional media. [6][8][9][10][11]

- January 14–15, 2022: Press coverage documented side‑by‑side comparisons and community reposts of the now‑deleted promotional video. Reports noted the clip’s ending card “The Voice Powered By Voiceverse” and observed audio matching with 15.ai‑generated samples, including pitch‑shifted Rainbow Dash, which reinforced the plagiarism allegations. [8][9][15]

- January 31, 2022: Baker publicly ended his partnership with Voiceverse, writing, “After careful consideration, I’ve decided to not continue the partnership with VoiceVerseNFT. Intentions aside, I’ve heard you and apologize for accusing anyone of ‘hating’ just by simply disagreeing with me.” Industry outlets covered his withdrawal and apologies for the earlier “hate/create” phrasing. [1][2][3][4][5]

- February 1, 2022: Voiceverse issued a press release highlighting the Voiceverse Origins launch and “sold out” status, framing the product as pioneering “voice NFTs” and reaffirming links to LOVO. [16]

## Allegations and evidence

The core allegation was that Voiceverse used AI‑generated audio from 15.ai in its marketing without permission or attribution. Evidence cited included:
- Statements by the 15.ai developer that server logs indicated Voiceverse‑promoted voices were generated from 15.ai. [7][11]
- Observations that audio in Voiceverse’s promotional material matched 15.ai outputs for My Little Pony characters, sometimes with the pitch modified. [8][9]
- Community reposts of the deleted promotional video and side‑by‑side comparisons. [8][9][15]

Press reports consistently attributed the origin of the audio to 15.ai and described the sequence of posting, deletion, and apology. [8][9][10][11] The 15.ai developer reiterated disinterest in NFTs and rejected requests to integrate NFT functionality, referencing a pinned stance from December 2021. [10][11][15]

## Voiceverse statements and actions

Voiceverse publicly apologized to 15.ai, acknowledging use of 15.ai audio by its marketing team without credit and promising it would not recur. The implicated video was deleted. In subsequent industry coverage, Voiceverse was quoted or paraphrased as saying the project aimed to “disrupt the industry by opening up a new stream of opportunities for Voice Actors, not replacing them.” [6][2][8][9][10][11]

Following Baker’s withdrawal, Voiceverse communicated that the partnership had ended and that it intended to “double‑down” on executing its roadmap and strengthening its community and the broader NFT ecosystem, according to press summaries of team statements around that time. [5]

## Troy Baker’s involvement and withdrawal

Baker’s initial announcement (January 13–14, 2022) framed the partnership in aspirational terms and included the “You can hate. Or you can create” phrasing that drew widespread criticism. Subsequent interviews and coverage reported that Baker said his initial tweet attempted “levity” and that he was exploring tools to benefit creators, while acknowledging missteps, including accompanying the announcement with a headshot image. On January 31, 2022, Baker ended the partnership and apologized, specifically retracting the implication that disagreement constituted “hating.” [2][3][4][5][1]

The episode became a prominent example of gaming and voice acting community resistance to NFT partnerships, with commentators noting the volume of critical quote‑tweets and pushback within days of the announcement. [2][24]

## Reactions

- 15.ai: The developer asserted that Voiceverse’s promotional audio derived from 15.ai, cited log evidence, and criticized attempts to commercialize outputs from a free, non‑commercial service. The developer also reiterated a refusal to incorporate NFTs and published pointed responses to Voiceverse’s apology. [7][10][11][8][9][15]

- Voice acting and gaming communities: Industry press coverage documented significant social media backlash to “voice NFTs,” concerns about synthetic voice products supplanting human voice actors, and criticism of the framing that equated dissent with “hate.” Baker’s withdrawal was widely reported as a response to this backlash. [2][3][4][5][24]

- NFT/tech media: Crypto‑skeptic outlets and incident trackers cataloged the plagiarism claims, apology, and withdrawal as emblematic of overreach in NFT marketing and the fraught ethics of AI voice cloning. [12][13][26][16]

## Consequences and aftermath

Immediate consequences included:
- Removal of the offending promotional video and Voiceverse’s public apology acknowledging uncredited use of 15.ai audio by its marketing team. [6][8][9][10][11]
- Troy Baker’s termination of the partnership and public apology on January 31, 2022. [1][2][3][4][5]

Commercially, Voiceverse claimed its Voiceverse Origins collection sold out on January 29, 2022, and highlighted trending status on OpenSea during launch. The OpenSea collection page remained accessible, titled “Voiceverse Genesis Official.” [16][17] Mainstream reporting at the time did not document any DMCA takedowns or legal filings specifically connected to the 15.ai plagiarism incident. [2][8][9]

In broader developments related to Voiceverse’s parent domain, later commentary noted that LOVO faced separate litigation initiated by voice actors in 2024, with a judge allowing claims to proceed in mid‑2025; this coverage did not allege a direct legal action arising from the 2022 15.ai episode but reflected ongoing disputes around AI voice tech and consent. [21]

## Ethical and legal context

The scandal centered on two intersecting issues:
- Consent and attribution in AI voice synthesis: 15.ai is a free, non‑commercial text‑to‑speech service; its developer objected to NFT‑linked commercialization of outputs and to the use of samples without permission and credit. [10][11][20]
- NFTs and “ownership” of synthetic voices: Voiceverse marketed voice NFTs as conferring rights to use and “own” voices across platforms, which raised concerns among voice actors and creators about replacement, deepfakes, and the enforceability or ethicality of such claims. Industry reporting captured these tensions, with outlets noting skepticism of crypto projects and the backlash to “voice NFTs.” [2][15][24]

The episode reinforced calls in the voice acting and AI communities for clear licensing, consent frameworks, and transparent provenance of synthetic media, especially when commercialized via blockchain‑linked products. [2][8][9][24]

## Legacy

The Voiceverse plagiarism scandal is frequently cited in retrospectives and incident databases as an early, high‑profile clash between NFT marketing and AI voice ethics. It helped crystallize opposition in gaming and voice acting circles to “voice NFTs” and highlighted the reputational risks of using third‑party AI outputs without consent. While Voiceverse continued to associate its origins collection with LOVO and maintained an OpenSea presence, its public profile diminished following the controversy, and subsequent debates around AI voice cloning moved into legal venues unrelated to the initial incident. [12][16][17][24][21]

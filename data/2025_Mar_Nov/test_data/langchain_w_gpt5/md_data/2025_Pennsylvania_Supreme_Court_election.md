# 2025 Pennsylvania Supreme Court election

## Lead

The 2025 Pennsylvania Supreme Court election consisted of statewide retention votes for three sitting justices—Christine Donohue, Kevin M. Dougherty, and David N. Wecht—on November 4, 2025. No partisan, contested open-seat races for the Supreme Court appeared on the 2025 ballot. All three justices were elected to 10‑year terms in 2015 and stood for retention in 2025 under Pennsylvania’s judicial retention system. The retention votes were approved statewide, and the court’s partisan balance remained 5–2 with a Democratic majority, as established following the 2023 election cycle. The election was notable for featuring only retention questions for the Supreme Court and no contested statewide judicial vacancies for that court in an odd-year municipal cycle. [Pennsylvania Department of State – Election Returns](https://www.electionreturns.pa.gov) [Unified Judicial System of Pennsylvania – Supreme Court](https://www.pacourts.us/courts/supreme-court) [Associated Press – 2023 Supreme Court outcome](https://apnews.com/article/pennsylvania-supreme-court-election-2023-mccaffery)

## Background

The Supreme Court of Pennsylvania is the state’s court of last resort and is composed of seven justices. Justices are elected to 10‑year terms in partisan elections when vacancies arise and then face nonpartisan “yes/no” statewide retention votes at the end of their terms. The Pennsylvania Constitution sets a mandatory retirement age of 75 for justices. In between contested elections, retention ballots ask, “Shall Justice [Name] be retained?” and a simple majority statewide determines whether the justice serves another term. [Unified Judicial System of Pennsylvania – Supreme Court](https://www.pacourts.us/courts/supreme-court) [Pennsylvania Constitution, Article V](https://www.legis.state.pa.us/cfdocs/legis/LI/consDisplay.cfm?file=article%20v)

The court’s partisan composition before the 2025 election was a 5–2 Democratic majority. That alignment followed the 2023 statewide contest in which Democrat Daniel McCaffery won a vacant seat, preserving the court’s 5–2 balance. Other current members include Chief Justice Debra Todd (D), Sallie Updyke Mundy (R), Kevin Brobson (R), and Daniel McCaffery (D), alongside Justices Donohue (D), Dougherty (D), and Wecht (D). The 2025 cycle did not include any new vacancies for the Supreme Court. [Associated Press – 2023 Supreme Court outcome](https://apnews.com/article/pennsylvania-supreme-court-election-2023-mccaffery) [Unified Judicial System of Pennsylvania – Supreme Court](https://www.pacourts.us/courts/supreme-court)

## Electoral system and legal framework

Pennsylvania uses partisan elections to fill judicial vacancies, including Supreme Court seats, and nonpartisan retention elections for sitting jurists at the conclusion of each 10‑year term. For retention, a justice appears on the general election ballot without party designation, and voters cast “Yes” or “No” votes. A majority statewide is required for the justice to be retained for another term. There is no primary for retention questions; retention ballots appear only in the general election. [Pennsylvania Constitution, Article V](https://www.legis.state.pa.us/cfdocs/legis/LI/consDisplay.cfm?file=article%20v) [Unified Judicial System of Pennsylvania – Supreme Court](https://www.pacourts.us/courts/supreme-court)

To run for a judicial vacancy, candidates must meet constitutional eligibility requirements (including being members of the bar and meeting residency criteria) and complete nomination procedures under state election law. Statewide judicial candidates ordinarily file nomination petitions and, if they seek party nominations, appear on the spring primary ballot. Administrative details—including filing windows, deadlines for nomination petitions, and ballot preparation—are set and published by the Pennsylvania Department of State. For the 2025 municipal election cycle, the primary occurred on May 20, 2025, and the general election was held on November 4, 2025, per the state election calendar. [Pennsylvania Department of State – Running for Office](https://www.dos.pa.gov/VotingElections/CandidatesCommittees/RunningforOffice/Pages/default.aspx) [Pennsylvania Department of State – Election Calendar](https://www.dos.pa.gov/VotingElections/OtherServicesEvents/ElectionCalendar/Pages/default.aspx)

## Candidates and ballot

The 2025 ballot included retention questions for three Supreme Court justices:

- Christine Donohue (Democratic; elected in 2015): Donohue was elected to the Supreme Court in 2015 following prior service on the Pennsylvania Superior Court and in private practice. She appeared on the 2025 ballot for retention to a subsequent 10‑year term. [Justice Christine Donohue – official biography](https://www.pacourts.us/judicial-directory/supreme-court/christine-donohue)

- Kevin M. Dougherty (Democratic; elected in 2015): Dougherty joined the Supreme Court in 2016 after winning election in 2015. Before his elevation, he served as Administrative Judge of the Philadelphia Family Court and worked in the Philadelphia Court of Common Pleas. He appeared for retention in 2025. [Justice Kevin M. Dougherty – official biography](https://www.pacourts.us/judicial-directory/supreme-court/kevin-m-dougherty)

- David N. Wecht (Democratic; elected in 2015): Wecht was elected in 2015 and previously served on the Superior Court and Allegheny County Court of Common Pleas. He appeared for retention in 2025. [Justice David N. Wecht – official biography](https://www.pacourts.us/judicial-directory/supreme-court/david-n-wecht)

No open-seat, partisan Supreme Court races were on the 2025 ballot, and there were no reported withdrawals or appointments that created special Supreme Court elections in 2025. [Unified Judicial System of Pennsylvania – Supreme Court](https://www.pacourts.us/courts/supreme-court)

## Campaign

Retention campaigns in Pennsylvania are generally lower-profile than contested judicial elections, and 2025 followed that pattern for the Supreme Court. The Pennsylvania Bar Association’s Judicial Evaluation Commission typically issues nonpartisan ratings and narratives for appellate judges facing retention, and such materials are frequently cited in voter guides and local news coverage. Public reporting emphasized that the three Supreme Court jurists were on “yes/no” ballots without party labels, consistent with Pennsylvania’s retention process. Significant statewide polling specific to the retention questions was limited, and there were no widely reported ethics controversies or litigation affecting the Supreme Court retention ballots in 2025. [Pennsylvania Bar Association – Judicial Evaluation Commission](https://www.pabar.org/site/Public/Government-Relations/Judicial-Evaluation-Commission) [Pennsylvania Department of State – Running for Office](https://www.dos.pa.gov/VotingElections/CandidatesCommittees/RunningforOffice/Pages/default.aspx)

Campaign finance activity around statewide judicial retention is typically modest compared to contested appellate races. Pennsylvania’s campaign finance disclosure system provides committee and independent expenditure filings; however, 2025 retention reporting for Supreme Court justices drew limited statewide media coverage relative to 2023’s contested Supreme Court race. [Pennsylvania Department of State – Running for Office](https://www.dos.pa.gov/VotingElections/CandidatesCommittees/RunningforOffice/Pages/default.aspx)

## Election dates and administration

- Municipal primary: May 20, 2025
- General election: November 4, 2025

The Pennsylvania Department of State publishes the annual election calendar, sets filing deadlines, and administers statewide election certification. In odd-numbered years, county election boards canvass returns and transmit results for statewide certification after the general election. Supreme Court retention questions are included on the general ballot and do not appear on the primary ballot. [Pennsylvania Department of State – Election Calendar](https://www.dos.pa.gov/VotingElections/OtherServicesEvents/ElectionCalendar/Pages/default.aspx) [Pennsylvania Department of State – Election Returns](https://www.electionreturns.pa.gov)

## Results

The Pennsylvania Department of State reported statewide returns showing majority “Yes” votes for the retention of Justices Christine Donohue, Kevin M. Dougherty, and David N. Wecht. Certified results were published by the Department following county canvass and certification procedures, consistent with Pennsylvania election law. County-level breakdowns are available through the Department’s returns portal. [Pennsylvania Department of State – Election Returns](https://www.electionreturns.pa.gov)

## Aftermath and impact

With all three justices retained, the Pennsylvania Supreme Court’s partisan composition remained unchanged at 5–2 in favor of Democrats. No changes to court leadership or internal administrative structure were reported in connection with the 2025 retention cycle. The absence of contested Supreme Court vacancies in 2025 meant that statewide judicial attention remained focused on retention and lower-court races, while broader analysis of the court’s direction continued to reference the 5–2 alignment established in the 2023 election.

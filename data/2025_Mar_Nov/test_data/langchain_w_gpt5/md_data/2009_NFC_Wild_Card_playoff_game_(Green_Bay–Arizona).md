# 2009 NFC Wild Card playoff game (Green Bay–Arizona)

The 2009 NFC Wild Card playoff game between the Green Bay Packers and the Arizona Cardinals was played on January 10, 2010, at University of Phoenix Stadium in Glendale, Arizona. Arizona won 51–45 in overtime, ending the game on a strip-sack and fumble return by linebacker Karlos Dansby after cornerback Michael Adams blitzed Aaron Rodgers, a sequence widely discussed in postgame coverage. The game set then-NFL playoff records for combined points (96) and combined touchdowns (13) and featured prolific quarterback performances by Kurt Warner (29-of-33, five touchdowns, no interceptions) and Aaron Rodgers (423 passing yards, four touchdowns) [8][5][3]. Attendance was 61,926, and the referee was Scott Green [5][3]. The broadcast aired on FOX and delivered 34.4 million viewers, peaking at 44.1 million [11].

## Background

The matchup was part of the NFL’s Wild Card round in the 2009 season. Green Bay entered as the No. 5 seed at 11–5 and Arizona as the NFC West champion at 10–6 [1]. The teams had played in Week 17 at the same venue, with Green Bay winning 33–7; that game served as contextual prelude for the rematch one week later [1][14]. Green Bay had been among the league’s most turnover-averse teams during the regular season (fewest turnovers, 16), a trend that contrasted with the early sequence of the playoff game, when the Packers committed two turnovers in the first five minutes and fell behind 14–0 [10].

Arizona played without three-time Pro Bowl wide receiver Anquan Boldin due to knee and ankle injuries, which shifted targets to Larry Fitzgerald, Early Doucet, and Steve Breaston [7][8]. Local and national outlets later highlighted the contest as a high-scoring “shootout” and a notable entry in Arizona–Wisconsin sports history [8][14].

## Teams

- Green Bay Packers: Finished 11–5 and second in the NFC North, earning a Wild Card berth. The team won seven of its final eight regular-season games, including the 33–7 Week 17 win at Arizona [1][14][9]. Aaron Rodgers was making his first playoff start [9].

- Arizona Cardinals: Won the NFC West at 10–6 and hosted the Wild Card game. Despite Boldin’s absence, Arizona’s offense featured Warner at quarterback and wide receivers Fitzgerald, Doucet, and Breaston. Rookie running back Beanie Wells contributed 92 rushing yards in the playoff game [7][8][4].

## Venue

The game was played at University of Phoenix Stadium in Glendale, Arizona, a venue with a retractable roof and a natural-grass playing surface that is grown on a movable field tray [1][15]. Reported attendance was 61,926 [5].

Kickoff was promoted locally at 2:30 p.m. Arizona time; Green Bay’s pregame notes listed 3:40 p.m. CST (2:40 p.m. Arizona) [2][1].

## Broadcast

FOX televised the game. The network’s primary booth of Joe Buck and Troy Aikman is listed for the telecast [17]. The Cardinals announced a halftime performance by the band 3 Doors Down [13].

The telecast drew a 19.6 household rating/34 share and 34.4 million viewers, peaking at a 24.3/37 rating and 44.1 million viewers. It ranked as the highest-rated NFL Wild Card game since 2000 and the most-viewed since 1999; in adults 18–49, the game drew a 12.2/36 [11]. Overnight ratings reached 21.8 nationally, with local overnights of 49.1/70 in Milwaukee and 38.0/62 in Phoenix; ratings peaked at 27.0/40 from 7:45–8:00 p.m. ET [12].

## Game summary

Arizona opened quickly. On Green Bay’s first play, Karlos Dansby disrupted a pass that resulted in an interception by Dominique Rodgers-Cromartie, and on the next Packers possession, Adams stripped Donald Driver with Alan Branch recovering; Tim Hightower’s 1-yard run and Early Doucet’s 15-yard scoring reception established a 14–0 lead within five minutes [13][9][5]. Neil Rackers added a 23-yard field goal late in the first quarter [5].

Green Bay narrowed the deficit on Rodgers’ 1-yard touchdown run, but Doucet’s second 15-yard touchdown catch extended Arizona’s lead. Mason Crosby’s 20-yard field goal as time expired made the halftime score 24–10 Cardinals [5].

The third quarter featured rapid scoring. Larry Fitzgerald’s 33-yard touchdown reception pushed the lead to 31–10; Green Bay responded with touchdown passes to Greg Jennings (6 yards) and Jordy Nelson (11 yards). Fitzgerald’s 11-yard touchdown restored a 38–24 margin entering the fourth quarter [5].

Green Bay tied the game with a 30-yard touchdown pass to James Jones and a 1-yard touchdown run by John Kuhn at 10:57 of the fourth. Warner’s 17-yard touchdown strike to Steve Breaston put Arizona ahead 45–38, and Rodgers tied the game with an 11-yard pass to Spencer Havner at 1:52. In the final seconds of regulation, Rackers missed a 34-yard field goal wide left with 0:14 remaining, sending the game to overtime [8][7][5].

Green Bay won the overtime coin toss and nearly ended the game on the first play when Rodgers narrowly overthrew a wide-open Jennings deep [7]. On third-and-6 from the Packers’ 24, Adams blitzed and strip-sacked Rodgers; as the ball popped free, it deflected off Rodgers’ foot into the air, where Dansby caught it and returned it 17 yards for the walk-off touchdown at 13:42 of overtime [8][7][5].

## Records and milestones

- Combined points and touchdowns: The game set then-NFL playoff records for combined points (96) and combined touchdowns (13) [8].
- Franchise passing mark: Rodgers’ passing total established a Packers franchise playoff record for single-game yardage, as noted by ESPN’s NFL Nation coverage [10].
- Combined yardage and punts: The teams combined for 1,024 total yards and punted once each [6][4].

Subsequent sources have continued to refer to the contest as among the highest-scoring in postseason history and a prominent offensive display for both teams [8][14].

## Officiating and controversies

Scott Green served as the game’s referee, as listed by ESPN game information [3][16]. The overtime sequence generated debate. On the deciding play, cornerback Michael Adams blitzed and strip-sacked Rodgers; the ball deflected off Rodgers’ foot and was returned by Dansby for the winning touchdown. Television replays suggested potential contact to the quarterback’s facemask, but no penalty was called on the field [8]. Fan commentary and team-focused outlets criticized several aspects of officiating, including the overtime hit, while acknowledging Arizona’s effective pass rush; those views represent postgame opinion rather than official determinations [33]. Mainstream reporting emphasized the play’s mechanics and the absence of a flag rather than presenting an official ruling beyond the game result [8].

## Aftermath and legacy

Arizona advanced to the NFC Divisional Round and played at top-seeded New Orleans; several outlets described the Wild Card victory as a high-scoring shootout and highlighted Warner’s postseason performance, with comments from head coach Ken Whisenhunt and Green Bay’s Mike McCarthy emphasizing the game’s competitive nature [8][7]. The Week 17 rematch context was revisited in later coverage, noting the reversal of fortunes from Green Bay’s 33–7 win to the playoff outcome [14].

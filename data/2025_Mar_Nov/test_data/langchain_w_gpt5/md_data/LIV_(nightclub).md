# LIV (nightclub)

LIV is a nightclub located inside the Fontainebleau Miami Beach resort at 4441 Collins Avenue, Miami Beach, Florida 33140. Opened in 2008 during the hotel’s high-profile relaunch, the venue has been covered extensively by national and local media and has appeared repeatedly in industry rankings, including DJ Mag’s Top 100 Clubs and Bar & Restaurant’s Top 100 revenue lists. LIV is known for large-scale DJ and live performances, bottle-service-centered operations, a 2017 redesign featuring a prominent kinetic LED chandelier, and the long-running “LIV on Sunday” hip-hop night. The club is operated by Groot Hospitality in partnership with Fontainebleau Miami Beach/Fontainebleau Development, and the LIV brand has expanded to additional venues, notably at Hard Rock Stadium in Miami Gardens and at Fontainebleau Las Vegas. [1][2][10][11][25][27][28][14][15][16][29]

## Location and identification

LIV is situated within the Fontainebleau Miami Beach resort complex at 4441 Collins Avenue, Miami Beach, Florida 33140. The club is listed by the hotel among its nightlife venues, and the hotel’s transportation and directions page provides the property’s street address. LIV generally operates Thursday through Sunday nights and follows the resort’s policies regarding admission and dress. [1][2]

## History

### Opening and early years

LIV opened in 2008 in conjunction with the Fontainebleau Miami Beach’s grand reopening, following a multi-year renovation of the historic resort. The hotel relaunched with events on November 18, 2008, drawing national media coverage and celebrity attendees, and subsequent reporting has consistently placed LIV’s opening in 2008 as part of that broader revival of the Fontainebleau property. [27][28][16][11]

Through the early 2010s, media and industry coverage positioned LIV among the leading U.S. nightclub venues, citing celebrity patronage and high-end programming. DJ Mag’s profiles during this period highlighted its scale and the then-operator Miami Marketing Group (MMG), reflecting the venue’s role in Miami’s nightlife ecosystem and during high-attendance weeks such as Art Basel. [11]

### 2017 renovation and reopening

In summer 2017, LIV closed temporarily for an extensively reported redesign and renovation. Outlets including Miami New Times, the Orlando Sentinel, the Miami Herald (Miami.com), Haute Living, and Curbed Miami reported the project budget at approximately $10 million, with design and production by ICrave and lighting designer Steve Lieberman. The update introduced numerous visual and structural changes, notably a kinetic, “spider”-shaped LED chandelier mounted below the venue’s signature dome, hundreds of synchronized LED panels, and reconfiguration of upper-level spaces. The club resumed operations with a “sneak-peek” reopening on September 22, 2017, followed by a staged grand opening, with performances reported by media including Travis Scott (Friday), Skrillex (Saturday), and its signature Sunday programming. [3][4][5][6][7]

### COVID-19 closure and 2021 reopening

During the COVID-19 pandemic, LIV closed in March 2020 alongside other Miami nightlife venues. The club’s reopening was reported for April 16, 2021, coinciding with the lifting of late-night curfews and business restrictions. Coverage also noted Miami Beach’s removal of a coronavirus-era noise restriction following discussions with Fontainebleau representatives, enabling the nightclub to resume operations at typical sound levels. [17][18][19]

### Brand expansions

The LIV brand has been extended beyond Miami Beach:

- LIV at Hard Rock Stadium: Groot Hospitality lists a branded venue at Hard Rock Stadium (home of the Miami Dolphins), part of broader hospitality programming at the stadium. Local coverage has referenced LIV-branded spaces at the venue. [29][30]

- Fontainebleau Las Vegas: In 2023, Fontainebleau Las Vegas announced a partnership with Groot Hospitality to open LIV nightclub and the adjacent LIV Beach dayclub at the new resort, expressly positioned as extensions of the original Miami Beach venue. The partnership was announced with statements from Fontainebleau Development Chairman and CEO Jeffrey Soffer and media coverage outlining design and operational philosophies aligned with the Miami flagship. [15][16]

## Design and facilities

LIV’s design references the architecture of the Fontainebleau, including a prominent dome over the central dance floor. Hotel and venue materials describe the club as spanning more than 18,000 square feet, with multiple bars and VIP seating areas. Industry coverage has cited capacity figures between 1,000 and 1,100, with DJ Mag listing 1,100 and professional audio manufacturer L‑Acoustics describing the space as a two-story, 1,000-capacity venue. [1][10][9]

Key features and systems include:

- Lighting and visual systems: The 2017 renovation installed hundreds of LED panels and a custom kinetic LED chandelier, often referred to as “the Spider,” designed to lower from the dome and move over the crowd. Media described additional elements such as projection mapping of the stage and reworked staircases flanking the DJ booth. [3][4][5][6][7]

- Audio systems: In 2024, LIV installed an L‑Acoustics L Series professional sound system. L‑Acoustics’ press and case study detail the configuration—arrays of L2D enclosures over the dance floor, KS28 subwoofers including units integrated into the DJ platform, and coaxial X8 and X12 speakers covering the DJ area, upper-level VIP booths, and bar areas—powered by LA7.16i, LA12X, and LA4X amplified controllers. The manufacturer’s materials emphasize the system’s alignment with rider expectations common to major music festivals. [8][9]

## Ownership and management

LIV is operated by Groot Hospitality, the Miami-based hospitality group founded by David Grutman. In October 2019, Live Nation Entertainment acquired a majority stake in Groot Hospitality; trade and business press reported that Grutman remained as CEO following the transaction. The nightclub operates inside the Fontainebleau Miami Beach resort, which is owned by Fontainebleau Development, led by Chairman and CEO Jeffrey Soffer; the companies publicly partner on LIV-branded projects, including at Fontainebleau Las Vegas. Historically, DJ Mag reported that the venue was operated by Miami Marketing Group (MMG) earlier in its history. [12][13][14][15][16][11]

## Programming and operations

Coverage of LIV has consistently associated the club with a mix of electronic dance music, hip hop, and popular live performances. The venue draws high-profile artists and DJs for special events and recurring nights, and its programming is regularly aligned to major Miami event weeks.

- LIV on Sunday: Miami Herald and Miami New Times profiles attribute the club’s “LIV on Sunday” night to promoter Michael Gardner (Headliner Market Group), describing it as a longstanding hip-hop staple. Reporting characterizes the event’s role in Miami’s hip-hop nightlife, with frequent performances and celebrity attendance. [20][21]

- Post-renovation and COVID-era highlights: Media reported performances during the September 2017 reopening (including Travis Scott and Skrillex), and the April 2021 reopening event was headlined by Alesso, following the lifting of curfews and noise limits. [4][5][6][18][19]

- Art Basel and Miami Music Week: DJ Mag’s 2015 profile described the atmosphere and guest mix during Art Basel week, reflecting the club’s participation in citywide cultural and music events. [11]

## Reception, rankings, and awards

LIV has received sustained coverage from local and national outlets and has appeared in prominent industry rankings:

- DJ Mag Top 100 Clubs: LIV has been profiled multiple times by DJ Mag, including in 2011, 2015, and 2025. The 2025 entry notes the club’s capacity and recent professional audio upgrades, while the 2015 profile discusses the venue’s scale, operator, and celebrity context. [21][11][10]

- Bar & Restaurant Top 100 (formerly Nightclub & Bar): In the 2015 Top 100 list, LIV was placed fifth among U.S. nightclubs, with estimated 2014 revenue of $40–45 million. Eater Miami summarized the list and reiterated the placement and revenue range. [25][26]

Collectively, this independent coverage has been used by media and trade publications to establish the venue’s notability within U.S. and global nightlife. [10][11][25][26]

## Economic impact

Published revenue estimates from Bar & Restaurant’s 2015 Top 100 list place LIV’s annual revenue range at $40–45 million for the prior year, ranking fifth nationally among nightclubs. There is limited publicly available data beyond these industry estimates regarding employment or broader economic impact; the club’s role is frequently described through rankings and event coverage rather than formal economic studies. [25][26]

## Policies and regulations

LIV follows the resort’s policies for admission and attire. The club advertises an upscale dress code and reserves the right to refuse entry. Event listings indicate a 21-and-over age requirement with government-issued ID for admission. Hours typically run from 11 p.m. to 5 a.m. Thursday through Sunday, subject to special events or private bookings. During the pandemic reopening period in April 2021, Miami Beach lifted a noise restriction after meetings with Fontainebleau representatives, enabling the nightclub’s return to typical operating conditions. [1][2][18][19]

## Controversies and incidents

Publicized incidents associated with the nightclub include:

- December 2014 altercation: Billboard and the Los Angeles Times reported a physical altercation involving Sean “Diddy” Combs and Drake in the early morning of December 8, 2014, outside the club following DJ Khaled’s birthday party. The outlets attributed reports of a confrontation to multiple sources; no official police reports were cited in these accounts. [22][23]

- December 2018 brawl: Miami Herald (Miami.com) reported arrests following a fight outside LIV in the early morning hours after another DJ Khaled birthday event in December 2018. The coverage noted multiple detentions and the presence of celebrities but did not attribute any charges to the nightclub itself. [24]

These incidents were widely covered by entertainment and local news media, with no long-term regulatory actions identified in the cited reporting. [22][23][24]

## Cultural impact

LIV has been covered by media as a focal point of Miami Beach nightlife since its 2008 opening, with “LIV on Sunday” repeatedly described by independent outlets as a defining hip-hop club night in the city. DJ Mag’s profiles and local reporting emphasize the venue’s role during major event weeks and its mix of celebrity patrons and marquee performers. These accounts, together with recurring high placements in industry lists, have contributed to the club’s broader cultural profile. [20][11][25][26]

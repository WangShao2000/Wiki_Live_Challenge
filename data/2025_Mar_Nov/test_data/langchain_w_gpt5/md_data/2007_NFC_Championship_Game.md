# 2007 NFC Championship Game

The 2007 NFC Championship Game was the National Football Conference title game of the 2007 NFL season, played on January 20, 2008, at Lambeau Field in Green Bay, Wisconsin, between the New York Giants and the Green Bay Packers. New York won 23–20 in overtime on a 47‑yard field goal by Lawrence Tynes after an interception of Brett Favre on the opening possession of overtime, sending the Giants to Super Bowl XLII [3][4]. The game was contested in extreme cold at Lambeau Field, with wind chills around −24 °F; it has been cited as among the coldest games in NFL history and the second-coldest played at Lambeau [4][5][6]. The broadcast on Fox drew one of the largest conference championship audiences on record, averaging 53.9 million viewers nationally (29.0/43 rating/share) [13][14]. Two weeks later, the Giants defeated the New England Patriots in Super Bowl XLII [5].

  - Green Bay defeated New York 35–13 at Giants Stadium on September 16, 2007, in the regular season [16].

  - Green Bay entered after a Divisional Round win over the Seattle Seahawks, 42–20 [8].
  - New York advanced to the Super Bowl after winning the NFC Championship; subsequent postseason context is covered under “Aftermath and legacy” [5].

## Pre-game context and weather

Lambeau Field hosted a night game in severe cold. Multiple reliable outlets documented subzero conditions and dangerous wind chills:
- The Cap Times (Madison) reported an air temperature of −1 °F with wind chill of −24 °F and noted the game as the fifth-coldest in NFL history and second-coldest in Packers history [4].
- NFL.com’s “Coldest games” feature lists the matchup with a temperature of −4 °F and wind chill of −24 °F [6].
- NFL Films’ feature on the game (“The Chilling Championship”) emphasized that the wind chill at kickoff was −23 °F and highlighted cold-weather images circulating widely, including head coach Tom Coughlin’s frost-reddened face on the sideline [5].

For historical comparison at Lambeau Field, the 1967 NFL Championship (“Ice Bowl”) kicked off at −13 °F with an estimated wind chill of −36 °F, according to the National Weather Service [50].

## Game summary

New York controlled possession and field position early, and the teams traded the lead through the middle quarters before a tie in regulation and sudden-death overtime decided the conference title.

- First quarter:
  - The Giants opened the scoring with a 29‑yard field goal by Lawrence Tynes at 4:50 (NYG 3–0) [3].
- Second quarter:
  - Tynes extended New York’s lead with a 37‑yard field goal at 11:41 (NYG 6–0) [3].
  - Green Bay answered quickly: on the first play of the next series, Brett Favre hit Donald Driver for a 90‑yard touchdown to give the Packers a 7–6 lead at 11:18; it stood as one of the longest pass plays in NFL postseason history [3][29].
  - Mason Crosby’s 36‑yard field goal with 1:30 left in the half put Green Bay ahead 10–6 at halftime [3].
- Third quarter:
  - New York opened the half with a sustained drive capped by Brandon Jacobs’ 1‑yard touchdown run at 7:56 (NYG 13–10) [3].
  - Favre’s 12‑yard touchdown to tight end Donald Lee restored Green Bay’s lead at 5:00 (GB 17–13) [3].
  - The Giants responded with a ground-based march ending in Ahmad Bradshaw’s 4‑yard touchdown at 2:12 (NYG 20–17) [3].
- Fourth quarter:
  - Crosby tied the game 20–20 with a 37‑yard field goal at 11:46 [3].
  - The teams traded possessions in frigid conditions down the stretch; New York controlled the ball for a significant time-of-possession edge but did not score again in regulation [1].
- Overtime:
  - Winning the coin toss, Green Bay began with the ball, but on the second play of the series Brett Favre’s pass was intercepted by cornerback Corey Webster and returned to the Packers’ side of midfield, a play local coverage identified as the pivot point of the game [4].
  - Four plays later, Tynes converted a 47‑yard field goal at 12:25 of overtime to win 23–20 [3][4].

New York’s plan emphasized balance and possession, with the Giants holding the ball for 40:01 to Green Bay’s 22:34 and outgaining the Packers 377–264, while the defense limited the Packers to 1-of-10 on third down [1].

## Broadcast and ratings

Fox televised the game nationally [1]. The telecast averaged 53.9 million viewers with a 29.0 rating and 43 share, according to Nielsen figures reported by industry sources; it ranked among the most-watched conference championship games on record and was the second-most watched sporting event in the United States in 2008 after Super Bowl XLII [13][14].

## Significance, aftermath, and legacy

- Overtime and Favre’s final Packers appearance:
  - The game was decided on Lawrence Tynes’ 47‑yard field goal in overtime, set up by Corey Webster’s interception of Brett Favre’s pass on the second play of the extra session [3][4].
  - Local coverage noted this was Favre’s final game in a Packers uniform before his subsequent departure from Green Bay, a point widely cited in retrospectives of his career [4][8].
- Super Bowl implications:
  - The Giants advanced to Super Bowl XLII, where they defeated the previously unbeaten New England Patriots 17–14 [5].
- Cold-weather legacy:
  - The contest has been highlighted by NFL Films among the league’s “100 Greatest Games,” in part for its extreme conditions; NFL.com and other outlets have characterized it among the coldest games in NFL history and the second-coldest at Lambeau Field, trailing the 1967 “Ice Bowl” [5][6][50].
- Individual and team narratives:
  - Plaxico Burress’ 11‑catch, 151‑yard performance became one of the defining efforts of the Giants’ postseason run [3].
  - The 90‑yard Favre-to-Driver touchdown is listed among the longest pass plays in NFL postseason history [29].

## Records and notable feats

- Longest pass plays (postseason context): Brett Favre’s 90‑yard touchdown to Donald Driver ranks among the longest pass plays in NFL playoff history [29].
- Cold-weather context:
  - NFL.com places the game among the league’s coldest, listing a −24 °F wind chill; Cap Times reported −1 °F air temperature with −24 °F wind chill and described it as the fifth-coldest game in NFL history and the second-coldest in Packers history [6][4].
  - By comparison, the 1967 “Ice Bowl” at Lambeau began at −13 °F with an estimated −36 °F wind chill, per the National Weather Service [50].

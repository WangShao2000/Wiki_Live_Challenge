# 1982 Los Angeles Rams–Green Bay Packers game

The 1982 Los Angeles Rams–Green Bay Packers game was a National Football League regular-season contest played on September 12, 1982, at Milwaukee County Stadium in Milwaukee, Wisconsin. It was the Week 1 opener of the strike-shortened 1982 season and the teams’ only meeting that year. The Packers defeated the Rams 35–23 after trailing 23–0 at halftime, scoring 35 unanswered points in the second half. Team publications and regional media have identified the comeback as the largest winning rally in Packers history at the time; the 23-point margin has remained a benchmark in franchise records and retrospectives. The game aired on CBS; the television broadcast featured Jack Buck and Hank Stram on commentary, as preserved on surviving recordings. Attendance was reported as 53,694. [1][2][3][4][5][6][7][8][12][13]

## Background

- Season context and format: The 1982 NFL season was interrupted by a players’ strike that began on September 21 and lasted 57 days, reducing the regular season from 16 to 9 games and leading to an expanded postseason format that admitted the top eight teams per conference regardless of division standings. Green Bay ultimately qualified and won its first playoff game since 1967 under the “Super Bowl Tournament” format. [9][10][8]

- Teams and coaches: The Packers were coached by Bart Starr, then in his eighth season as head coach; the Rams were coached by Ray Malavasi. [7][8] The opener in Milwaukee was the clubs’ only meeting in 1982. [7][1][2]

- Venue and attendance: The game was played at Milwaukee County Stadium, Green Bay’s second home venue in that era. Attendance was 53,694. [5][12]

## Teams and pre-game context

- Green Bay Packers
  - Head coach: Bart Starr. [8]
  - Entered the game 0–0; improved to 1–0 with the win. Green Bay would finish 5–3–1 and reach the playoffs, defeating the St. Louis Cardinals 41–16 in the Wild Card round before losing to Dallas 37–26 in the Divisional round. [8]
  - Core offensive personnel in the game included QB Lynn Dickey, WRs James Lofton and John Jefferson, TE Paul Coffman, and RB Eddie Lee Ivery. [1][2]

- Los Angeles Rams
  - Head coach: Ray Malavasi. [7]
  - Entered the game 0–0; fell to 0–1 with the loss. The Rams completed the season 2–7. [7]
  - QB Bert Jones started for Los Angeles; the offense also featured RB Wendell Tyler, TE Mike Barber, WR Willie Miller, and K Mike Lansford. [1][2]

## Game summary

Los Angeles dominated the first half, capitalizing on Green Bay turnovers and field position to build a 23–0 lead by halftime. The Rams opened with a 4-yard rushing touchdown by Wendell Tyler and extended the margin with Mike Lansford field goals of 32, 29, and 28 yards, plus an 8-yard touchdown pass from Bert Jones to Mike Barber. Green Bay turned the ball over six times in the game (five in the first half), leading to short fields and Rams points. [1][2][3][4]

Green Bay reversed the game’s momentum in the third quarter. Lynn Dickey directed a 55-yard drive capped by a 4-yard touchdown pass to Paul Coffman, then a short field set up Eddie Lee Ivery’s 3-yard touchdown run to bring the Packers within 23–14. Early in the fourth quarter, following a missed 51-yard field goal by Lansford that would have pushed the Rams lead to 26–14, Dickey led a rapid 66-yard drive highlighted by a 50-yard completion to Coffman and finished with a 15-yard touchdown pass to James Lofton, cutting the deficit to 23–21. On the ensuing kickoff, Los Angeles fumbled; Green Bay recovered at the Rams’ 10-yard line and scored on the next play—a 10-yard Dickey-to-Coffman touchdown—to take its first lead, 28–23. A later George Cumby interception of Jones set up the clinching drive, culminating in Ivery’s 27-yard touchdown run with 3:06 remaining. Green Bay’s defense held the Rams scoreless after halftime. Team historian accounts place the go-ahead touchdown at 3:48 of the fourth quarter and the clinching score at 3:06. [1][2][3][4]

## Officiating and broadcast

- Television: The game was televised by CBS. Surviving broadcast recordings feature Jack Buck (play-by-play) and Hank Stram (analyst). [13][10]

- Radio: The Rams’ 1982 media guide lists KMPC (710 AM) in Los Angeles among the club’s radio outlets for regular-season coverage; the guide notes a Sept. 12 regular-season game “at Green Bay (Milwaukee).” [11]

## Aftermath and legacy

- Immediate standings: Green Bay moved to 1–0; Los Angeles fell to 0–1. [1][2]

- Season outcomes: The Packers finished 5–3–1 in the strike-shortened season, won a Wild Card playoff game over the St. Louis Cardinals 41–16, and lost 37–26 at Dallas in the Divisional round. The Rams finished 2–7. [8][7][9]

- Significance: Team historical accounts and later coverage have identified the 23-point rally as the largest comeback victory in Packers history, a distinction highlighted in organizational histories and media retrospectives. It has been cited in lists of notable Packers comebacks and season openers. [3][4][6] Attendance for the game was documented at 53,694. [5] The turnaround hinged on a string of second-half plays noted in team histories: a missed 51-yard field goal by the Rams, a go-ahead touchdown following a fumbled kickoff recovered at the Los Angeles 10-yard line, and a late interception by George Cumby preceding Eddie Lee Ivery’s clinching 27-yard touchdown run. [3][4][2]

## Notes on identification and scope

- Game identity and uniqueness in 1982: The September 12, 1982 contest at Milwaukee County Stadium was a Week 1 regular-season game and the only Rams–Packers meeting of the 1982 season. [1][2][7]

## Media coverage and reaction

Subsequent historical features by team and regional media have revisited the game as a benchmark comeback. Packers team histories have framed it as “the greatest comeback in team history,” and later Milwaukee Journal Sentinel coverage has ranked the rally among the franchise’s foremost comebacks and top season openers. [3][4][6]

# Erik Campbell (Final Destination)

Erik Campbell is a fictional character in Final Destination: Bloodlines (2025), the sixth film in New Line Cinema’s Final Destination franchise. He is portrayed by Canadian actor Richard Harmon. Within the film’s narrative, Erik is part of the extended Campbell family targeted by “Death’s design” after a catastrophic 1960s disaster was averted. He features prominently in two of the movie’s most discussed sequences: a tattoo‑parlor fake‑out early in the story, and a hospital‑set climax in which he is killed during a malfunction involving a powerful MRI machine. The character and Harmon’s performance drew attention from trade publications and major outlets, with critics highlighting Erik’s mix of sardonic humor and protective, family‑first impulses and singling out the MRI sequence among the film’s standout set pieces. [1][4][5][6][7][8][9][10]

## Role in the Final Destination franchise

Final Destination: Bloodlines reframes the series’ premise around a single family whose fate is tied to a decades‑old disaster. The story follows college student Stefani Reyes as she uncovers how a 1960s tower‑restaurant collapse, foreseen and prevented by her grandmother Iris, disrupted “Death’s design,” later marking survivors and their descendants. Erik appears as one of Stefani’s cousins in the Campbell branch targeted during the film’s present‑day events. [6][7][8]

Erik’s early involvement is defined by a suspenseful misdirection in his workplace: a “tattoo‑shop fake‑out” in which a spilled flammable liquid, a ceiling fan chain, and his facial jewelry create the impression that Death is about to claim him. The sequence personifies the franchise’s signature “Rube Goldberg” tension while ultimately sparing Erik—at least initially. [4][5]

The film later stages multiple family deaths, culminating in a widely discussed hospital sequence. There, Erik becomes entangled in an attempt to “cheat” Death’s plan, after the group consults series stalwart William Bludworth (Tony Todd). Both in‑universe guidance and filmmakers’ interviews emphasize a recurring rule: interfering with Death’s design makes “things get messy.” Erik’s intervention places him directly in harm’s way; an active MRI magnet violently attracts nearby metal, pulls on his piercings, and drags a wheelchair into the scanner bore, impaling and crushing him. Coverage by TODAY consulted medical and physics experts to assess the scene’s plausibility, concluding the depiction pushes scientific reality for horror effect while noting that ferromagnetic projectiles in MRI suites pose real‑world hazards. [5][9][11]

## Portrayal and crediting

- Actor: Richard Harmon (credited on‑screen; listed in authoritative databases) [2][4]
- Character: Erik (named in reviews and interviews as Erik Campbell; cousin within the film’s family ensemble) [4][6][7][8]

Harmon’s performance as Erik is noted in several reviews and features. The Hollywood Reporter called him a source of mordant comic relief; Variety singled out his “thoughtful” turn; the AV Club described him as a “punk softie” whose chemistry with his on‑screen brother contributes to the film’s appeal; and IndieWire’s critic praised a hospital set piece that hinges on character beats between Erik and his younger relative. [5][6][10][4]

## Production and development

Bloodlines was directed by Adam B. Stein and Zach Lipovsky from a screenplay by Guy Busick and Lori Evans Taylor (story by Jon Watts with Busick and Taylor), and was released theatrically by Warner Bros./New Line in May 2025. The studio’s official film page confirms principal creative credits and the ensemble cast including Harmon. [1]

- Casting and preparation: Harmon, a longtime fan of the franchise, auditioned for Erik and eventually booked the role after a strike‑related delay. In interviews, he recounted an emotional reaction to being cast and described the production’s emphasis on rehearsals and improvisation, particularly around the family twists and the hospital scene’s tonal balance between gallows humor and pathos. [4]

- Tattoo‑parlor fake‑out: Harmon detailed the safety rig used to stage the sequence—wires threaded through a visible chain and a device in his nose to distribute pressure while real fire burned below—calling it “some of the most fun” he has had on a set. Trade reviews highlighted the set piece as a playful embodiment of Final Destination’s suspense mechanics. [4][6]

- MRI sequence: The directors and reviewers have framed the MRI scene as one of the film’s most audacious kills. Harmon described improvisation with co‑star Owen Patrick Joyner and direction intended to sharpen the emotional beat (e.g., delivering the line “I’m not gonna let you die” with awareness of the plan’s moral complexity). TODAY’s analysis, with input from an MD Anderson radiologist and a UC Berkeley physicist, explained MRI field strengths and safety protocols, acknowledging documented projectile incidents while clarifying the film’s heightened depiction. [4][9][11]

- Directorial approach: In a RogerEbert.com interview, Stein and Lipovsky discussed the franchise’s “death by insert shot” suspense, their practical‑effects‑inflected pitch to win the job, and thematic motifs (including circular imagery and penny lore) that recur across set pieces like the tower collapse and MRI room. These choices informed how sequences involving Erik were staged to blend plausibility, misdirection, and dark comedy. [8]

## Reception and legacy

Erik Campbell and his scenes were frequently highlighted in press coverage:

- Set pieces and performance: The Hollywood Reporter praised Bloodlines for ingeniously staged, mordantly humorous kills, specifically noting the lawnmower, garbage truck, and MRI sequences; it also cited Harmon’s contribution as snarky comic relief. Variety characterized the film as “clever, unpredictable and fun,” with Harmon standing out in the ensemble. Empire situated Erik among cousins whose dynamic refreshes the friend‑group formula with family stakes. The AV Club called Harmon a standout and spotlighted the “piercing‑parlor” sequence as a kinetic highlight. [5][6][7][10]

- Scene analysis: TODAY’s MRI explainer amplified discourse around Erik’s death, becoming a reference point for discussions about the scene’s scientific liberties and real‑world safety. [11]

- Broader acclaim: Bloodlines received strong notices and high box‑office returns, with many reviewers crediting its set‑piece craftsmanship and Tony Todd’s poignant farewell as Bludworth. Erik’s tattoo‑parlor fake‑out and the MRI death were repeatedly cited as emblematic of the installment’s giddy, grisly inventiveness. [5][6][7][8][10]

## Appearances in other media

As of publication, Erik Campbell’s appearances are limited to the feature film Final Destination: Bloodlines (2025). No official novelizations, comics, or video‑game tie‑ins featuring the character have been announced or released by the studio or recognized publishers. [1][3]

## Name and crediting discrepancies

Authoritative sources (on‑screen credits, major reviews, and interviews) identify the character as Erik Campbell, portrayed by Richard Harmon. Some online coverage and social posts occasionally render his given name as “Eric.” The film’s directors, speaking about Erik’s near‑death in his tattoo parlor and ultimate fate, reiterated the franchise rule that “when you mess with Death, things get messy”—a formulation echoed in mid‑tier outlets that sometimes use the “Eric” spelling. Reliable, published sources naming him as Erik include IndieWire, RogerEbert.com, Empire, The Hollywood Reporter, and TODAY. [4][5][6][7][8][10][11][12]

## Notability

Erik Campbell is notable within the Final Destination franchise for:

- His central involvement in two of Bloodlines’ most discussed sequences (tattoo‑parlor fake‑out; MRI‑room death). [4][5][6][9][10][11]
- A performance widely cited by reputable outlets as a standout among the film’s ensemble. [5][6][10]
- The scene‑specific discourse his death generated beyond film criticism, including expert analysis of MRI physics and safety. [11]

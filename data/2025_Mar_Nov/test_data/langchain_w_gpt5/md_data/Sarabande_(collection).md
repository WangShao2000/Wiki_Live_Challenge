# Sarabande (collection)

Sarabande: pour une terre impossible is a French-language poetry collection by Tristan Cassir, published in France by Éditions L’Harmattan on 18 October 2021 as volume 137 of the Levée d’ancre series directed by Michel Cassir and Christian Cavaillé. The book engages with contemporary political and social ruptures, with poems addressing wounds and uprisings in places such as Lebanon and Palestine. It has been cataloged by the Bibliothèque nationale de France and distributed in print and as a PDF e-book, with commentary on its themes and style by critics on the European poetry platform Versopolis and a related poem (“Liban”) presented in L’Orient-Le Jour’s poetry feature [1][2][3][6][7][8][9][11].

## Contents

Publicly accessible previews and catalog pages do not provide a full table of contents, and no complete list of poem titles is disclosed in the publisher’s page or Google Books preview [2][5]. Excerpts available from the digital preview and press features indicate the inclusion of poems engaging with Lebanon and Palestine, with one excerpted piece titled “Liban” appearing with lines also present in an e-book preview for Sarabande [6][9]. The PagePlace preview shows lines that situate the collection’s voice and subject matter—“Ces mots sont l’ultime abri / Avant l’aphasie des pages blanches irréversibles” and a reference to “Sarabande foudroyante pour une terre impossible,” signaling an internal textual motif rather than a sectional title [6].

As presented in retailer and distributor summaries, the collection is organized as poems that foreground uprisings, fractures, and escape lines, coupling an engaged and at times raw diction with an “insubordinate lyricism” and a recurrent interplay between poetic phrasing and the “architexture” of the world [7][14].

## Background and development

Sarabande appears as Cassir’s third poetry collection with L’Harmattan, and it is published within Levée d’ancre, a poetry series founded in 2001 and directed by Michel Cassir and Christian Cavaillé [2][11]. The publisher’s summary and critical notes emphasize the work’s engagement with geopolitical trauma and social upheaval in specific locales (Lebanon and Palestine), reflecting the author’s recurring interest in themes of revolt and memory identified by Versopolis critics [7]. Biographical notes in L’Orient-Le Jour profile Cassir as a poet, architect, and teacher, born in 1991 in Les Lilas, with roots in Argentina, Lebanon, and Egypt, a context underlying the collection’s Mediterranean and transnational sensibility [9].

No interviews or archival materials about Sarabande’s conception were located in major French press databases consulted for this report; series context and program listings (Levée d’ancre) confirm editorial direction but do not provide a detailed writing timeline [11].

## Themes and style

Publisher and distributor summaries describe poems that foreground “soulèvements, éclats explosifs, lignes de faille et de fuite” (uprisings, explosive bursts, fault lines, and escape routes) to denounce wounds affecting countries like Lebanon and Palestine, articulated in engaged, sometimes raw diction, while also advancing a forward-looking lyricism (“Avancer sous le doux éclat de la grande étoile … Chanter les rires / Danser les cicatrices … Fomenter l’anatomie imparfaite de nos futurs”) [7][14].

Critical notes on Versopolis attribute to Cassir’s poetry an “insubordinate lyricism” and a journey through roots that “transcends borders to embrace universal sentiments: revolt, exile, oblivion, memory, absence,” balancing fidelity to reality with a reframing impulse [8]. The e-book preview’s lines and the L’Orient-Le Jour poem “Liban” underscore motifs of violence, resistance, and the refuge of language—“Ces mots sont l’ultime abri”—as well as imagery of stones, fortresses, and irreversible silence, linking the collection’s rhetoric to crisis and testimony [6][9].

- Numerous musical compositions are titled “Sarabande,” including Claude Debussy’s Sarabande (orchestrated by Maurice Ravel) and Erik Satie’s Sarabandes, among others. Catalog records document such works and reflect the term’s historical association with a baroque dance form [15][16][17].

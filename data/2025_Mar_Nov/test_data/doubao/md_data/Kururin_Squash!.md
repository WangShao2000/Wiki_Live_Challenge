# Kururin Squash!

**Kururin Squash!** is a 2004 puzzle video game developed by Eighting and published by Nintendo for the GameCube. The game was released exclusively in Japan on October 14, 2004 . It is the third and final entry in the Kururin series, following Kuru Kuru Kururin (2001) and Kururin Paradise (2002), and the first game in the series to be released on a home console and to use 3D polygonal graphics [1]).

## Gameplay

Kururin Squash! maintains the core gameplay concept from the previous Kururin games, where players control a constantly rotating helicopter-like stick called a Helirin (or Heririn) through narrow mazes . The objective is to navigate through these mazes without hitting obstacles or the course sides, as three hits will cause the Helirin to shatter .

The game introduces several new gameplay elements that distinguish it from its predecessors. Players can now pilot specialized helicopters with unique abilities :



* **Punch Heririn**: Can destroy obstacles and enemies but produces recoil, requiring careful timing and positioning

* **Dive Heririn**: Enables underwater navigation, allowing players to submerge and surface through water levels

* **Flame Heririn**: Shoots fire left and right from the wings, used for destroying enemies and solving puzzles

* **Tornado Heririn**: Creates spinning tornadoes for both offensive and defensive purposes

* **Action Helirins**: Five varieties that shoot rapid-fire projectiles

The game also introduces a currency system where gold coins are scattered throughout stages. Collecting all coins in a stage without taking damage earns a perfect clear . Coins can be used in shops to purchase items such as world maps, a Game Boy Advance to GameCube link-up minigame, additional Helirins, customizable HUDs, and instructional movies .

Kururin Squash! features both single-player and multiplayer modes. The multiplayer component includes four-player split-screen gameplay with two modes: Race Mode, where players compete to complete mazes within a target time, and Battle Mode, where players collect coins while using weapons to hinder opponents .

## Plot

The game follows the Kururin family after they win the lottery and embark on a trip around the world . Upon their return journey, they discover that only three family members have returned: Kururin, Kakarin, and Totorin . The player's goal is to find all the missing family members across the game's various levels.

The game's story is presented through cutscenes featuring the characters, though the high-pitched bird voices used for character dialogue have been noted as a minor annoyance in reviews .

## Development

Kururin Squash! was developed by Eighting, who transitioned the series from 2D to 3D computer graphics for this installment . The directors and producers from Kururin Paradise returned to work on this game, which was intended as the final entry in the series .

The game's development team included 31 credited people . Key personnel included:



* **Yasunari Watanabe**: Main Programmer

* **Yuuichi Ochiai**: Program Director

* **Mitsuakira Tatsuta**: Graphics Director

* **Atsuhiro Motoyama**: Composer [2]

* **Yoichi Kotabe**: Character Designer

The game's visual style received particular praise, with reviewers noting the high-quality 3D graphics, transparency effects, lighting, and particle effects . The levels feature fully polygonal environments with detailed textures and effects, including forests with butterflies visible through transparent floors and various lighting effects .

## Reception

Kururin Squash! received generally positive reviews from critics, with an average score of 75% based on 7 professional reviews . Individual review scores ranged from 60% to 95%, indicating a mixed but overall favorable reception .

### Notable reviews

Nintendo World Report gave the game a 9.5/10 rating, praising its brilliant art style, excellent controls, and the new Heririns that "make for some brilliant new puzzles" . The review specifically highlighted the game's graphics, stating: "The levels themselves are fully polygonal, which is a massive leap from the 2D (but still excellent) sprite graphics of the previous games" .

The review also noted the game's sound quality, though it mentioned that the music tracks are relatively short (about a minute before looping) and that the high-pitched bird voices used for character dialogue could be annoying .

HonestGamers listed the game with a 9.5 average score based on the Nintendo World Report review .

## Legacy

Kururin Squash! is the final game in the Kururin series . Despite positive reception, none of the Kururin games were released outside of Japan, with the exception of the original Kuru Kuru Kururin which was released in Europe and Australia [3].

The Kururin series has had representation in Nintendo's Super Smash Bros. series:



* In Super Smash Bros. Melee, there is a Helirin trophy (misromanized as "Heririn")

* In Super Smash Bros. Brawl, Helirin appears as an Assist Trophy and becomes part of the stage that can be jumped on or slid on

* In Super Smash Bros. Ultimate, Helirin and Kururin return as a shared Spirit

The game is noted for its technical achievements in the puzzle genre, particularly its use of advanced graphics and effects for a puzzle game [4]. Reviewers noted that it was unusual to see such high-quality lighting and particle effects in a puzzle game [4].

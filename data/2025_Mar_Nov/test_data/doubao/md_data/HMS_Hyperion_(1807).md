# HMS Hyperion (1807)

HMS Hyperion was a 32-gun fifth-rate frigate of the Royal Navy, launched in 1807 and broken up in 1833 [1]. Named after Hyperion, one of the Titans of Greek mythology, she was the first of two Royal Navy ships to bear this name [1]. The ship had a varied career spanning nearly 26 years, serving in multiple theaters including the Mediterranean, West Indies, and South America .

## Construction and Design

HMS Hyperion was ordered on June 13, 1805, and her keel was laid down in February 1806 at Kingston upon Hull in Yorkshire . The ship was built by contract at the Kingston upon Hull shipyard on the Humber River, under the supervision of constructor William Gibson . She was designed by Sir John Henslow, a British ship designer and dockyard staff member who served from 1761 to 1795 .

The frigate was launched on November 3, 1807, and completed construction at a cost of £20,165 . Following her launch, Hyperion began fitting out at Chatham Dockyard on December 9, 1807, and completed this process on April 23, 1808 .

As a fifth-rate frigate, Hyperion was classified as a ship of the line below the first, second, and third rates but above the sixth rates . She was ship-rigged, meaning she had three masts with square sails on all of them .

The ship's dimensions were substantial for her class:



* Length of gundeck: 143 feet 9 inches (43.59 meters)

* Length of keel: 188 feet 8 ⅛ inches (57.31 meters)

* Breadth: 39 feet 4 ½ inches (11.90 meters)

* Depth in hold: 12 feet 4 inches (3.66 meters)

* Draught forward: 10 feet 0 inches (3.05 meters)

* Draught aft: 13 feet 6 inches (3.96 meters)

* Burthen: 978 ³⁴⁄₉₄ tons burthen (bm)

In terms of armament, Hyperion carried a formidable broadside of 396 imperial pounds (179.59 kg) as of 1808 . Her guns were distributed as follows:



* Upper Gun Deck: 26 British 18-pounder guns

* Quarterdeck: 10 British 24-pounder carronades and 2 British 9-pounder guns

* Forecastle: 2 British 24-pounder carronades and 2 British 9-pounder guns

The ship's complement was 254 men in 1808 .

## Service History

### Early Career (1808-1815)

Hyperion began her career in 1808 under the command of Captain Thomas Charles Brodie . However, her early service was marked by controversy. According to historical records, Hyperion started her career in 1808 under the command of a captain so poor he attempted to attack a rock and caused passengers to abandon the ship in fear of his abilities . While the exact details of this incident are scarce in the available sources, it appears to have occurred shortly after the ship's commissioning.

Despite this inauspicious beginning, Hyperion went on to serve in the Mediterranean and West Indies under Captain Brodie's command . On April 4, 1808, in company with the Alceste and Mercury frigates, Hyperion participated in an action off Rota near Cadiz, where they assisted in destroying or capturing a convoy of merchant vessels after dispersing or sinking the gunboats that escorted them and silencing the batteries of Rota that protected them .

The ship continued her service in the Mediterranean until January 1810, when she sailed for Jamaica . She remained in the West Indies until May 1812, when she sailed for Newfoundland . On January 19, 1812, Hyperion arrived at Portsmouth from Jamaica [2].

In August 1813, Hyperion was in the Banks of Newfoundland in company with HMS Bellerophon [2]. This was during the War of 1812, and the ship likely participated in blockading American ports and protecting British merchant shipping.

On June 3, 1814, Hyperion captured the American brig Rattlesnake, which carried 16 guns . This was one of her few documented prizes during the war.

After the war ended in 1815, Hyperion was paid off and laid up at Portsmouth in August 1815 .

### Later Career (1818-1833)

In June 1818, Hyperion began small repairs at Portsmouth Dockyard . She was recommissioned in September 1818 under Captain Thomas Searle . Captain Searle had a distinguished career, having been promoted to rear-admiral on November 9, 1846 .

Under Searle's command, Hyperion served in the Channel, where she was in attendance upon King George IV, and made a voyage to South America . During this South American voyage, Hyperion brought back specie to the amount of half a million sterling . This was a significant achievement, as it represented a substantial amount of money in the early 19th century.

However, Captain Searle's command was not without controversy. He fostered such a negative relationship with the Chilean naval commander Lord Cochrane that soon afterward the frigate was ordered back to Britain . Lord Cochrane, later known as the 10th Earl of Dundonald, was a controversial figure in the Royal Navy, having been tried for fraud and found guilty (though his co-defendants had fled the country) [3].

In April 1821, Hyperion was paid off and recommissioned for service at the Cape of Good Hope . She was again paid off in May 1823 .

The ship was recommissioned in January 1825 under Captain William James Mingay . She began fitting out at Sheerness Dockyard in January 1825 and completed this process in March 1825 . Captain Mingay served until May 1831, when the ship was paid off .

## Fate

After her final commission, Hyperion was laid up until June 1833, when she was broken up at Portsmouth . Her career had spanned nearly 26 years, from her launch in 1807 to her disposal in 1833.

## Legacy

HMS Hyperion was the first of two Royal Navy ships to bear this name [1]. A second HMS Hyperion (H97) was an H-class destroyer built for the Royal Navy during the mid-1930s [4]). This later ship served during the Spanish Civil War and World War II before striking a mine and being sunk on December 22, 1940 [5].

The name Hyperion has also been used for other vessels, including a wooden screw frigate that was ordered in 1861 but cancelled in 1863 .

## Commanders

HMS Hyperion had several commanders during her service:



1. Captain Thomas Charles Brodie (January 1808 - March 1811)

2. Captain William Pryce Cumby (May 1811 - January 1815)

3. Captain James Lillicrap (January 24, 1815 - April 8, 1815)

4. Captain William Pryce Cumby (April 8, 1815 - August 1815)

5. Captain Thomas Searle (September 1818 - April 1821)

6. Captain James Lillicrap (April 6, 1821 - October 24, 1823)

7. Captain William James Mingay (January 1825 - May 1831)

## Conclusion

HMS Hyperion (1807) was a significant vessel in the Royal Navy during the Napoleonic Wars and the early 19th century. Despite a troubled beginning with an incompetent captain, she went on to serve in multiple theaters and carry out important missions, including the capture of American vessels during the War of 1812 and the transportation of valuable specie from South America. Her career of nearly 26 years demonstrates the durability and utility of British frigates of this era.

The ship's service under various commanders, including the distinguished Thomas Searle, and her participation in several significant actions, make her an important part of British naval history. While her early misadventure with a poor captain is a curious footnote, her later successes in protecting British interests around the world highlight the role of such vessels in maintaining Britain's maritime supremacy during the Age of Sail.

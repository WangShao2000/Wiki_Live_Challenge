# The Story & the Engine

**The Story & the Engine** is the fifth episode of the fifteenth series (also marketed as Season 2) of the British science fiction television series *Doctor Who* [1]. It was written by Inua Ellams and directed by Makalla McPherson [1]. The episode was originally released through the BBC iPlayer and Disney+ on 10 May 2025 [1]. In the episode, the alien time-traveler known as the Doctor (Ncuti Gatwa) and his companion Belinda Chandra (Varada Sethu) become trapped in a barbershop in Lagos, Nigeria, that is being controlled by a mysterious figure known as "the Barber" (Ariyon Bakare), who is actually a story counter of the gods seeking to eliminate them after being betrayed by the deities [1].

## Plot

The Doctor and Belinda Chandra arrive in Lagos, Nigeria, in 2019 [1]. The Doctor leaves to visit his friend Omo, who runs a barbershop, while Belinda remains in the TARDIS [1]. When the Doctor arrives at the barbershop, he discovers it has been taken over by a mysterious figure called the Barber . The Barber has trapped Omo and his customers inside the shop, forcing them to tell stories to power an enigmatic engine .

The Doctor soon discovers that the barbershop exists simultaneously in Lagos and on the back of a giant mechanical spider crawling across a web-like structure called the Nexus [1]. The Barber reveals himself to be a story counter who helped various gods spread and preserve their stories using the Nexus [1]. However, after being betrayed by the gods, he plans to reach the center of the Nexus to isolate all gods from the web and destroy them [1].

As the story unfolds, the Doctor realizes that one of the customers, Abena (Michelle Asante), is the daughter of the spider god Anansi [1]. Abena eventually changes her mind about the Barber's plan and helps the Doctor by braiding his hair in a style that functions as a map to the engine's power source [1]. The Doctor and Belinda reconfigure the engine to allow the Doctor to power it with his own "never-ending story," but the energy proves excessive, causing the engine to self-destruct [1].

The Doctor convinces the Barber to release the trapped people and escape with them. The engine is destroyed, and the barbershop returns to normal [1]. The missing men return to their loved ones, Omo apologizes and makes peace with the Doctor, and Omo retires, leaving the Barber in charge of the shop [1]. The episode ends with the Doctor and Belinda departing as he tells her the story of how he first met Omo [1].

## Production

### Development

"The Story & the Engine" was written by Inua Ellams, a playwright known for works including "Barber Shop Chronicles" [2]. Ellams' previous plays have focused on African themes and settings, and this episode gave him the opportunity to bring these themes to a wider audience [2]. The episode was directed by Makalla McPherson [1].

The production team initially considered filming the episode on location in Lagos, Nigeria, where the story is set, but discovered that transporting the entire cast and crew would be prohibitively expensive [1]. As a result, the sets were constructed in a studio in Cardiff, Wales [1].

The episode features a special appearance by Jo Martin as the Fugitive Doctor [1]. According to showrunner Russell T. Davies, since the story explores the Doctor's ethnicity, he wanted to include the Fugitive Doctor and demonstrate that the character "still exists" [1]. The episode also includes a cameo appearance by child actor Sienna-Robyn Mavanga-Phipps reprising her role as Poppy from the Series 14 episode "Space Babies" (2024) .

### Cast

The episode stars Ncuti Gatwa as the Fifteenth Doctor and Varada Sethu as Belinda Chandra [1]. The guest cast includes:

With the exception of flashback scenes, the episode features an entirely non-white cast [1]. Eighty extras were used in the Nigerian market scene [1].

### Filming

The episode was directed by Makalla McPherson . Filming took place in studio sets constructed in Cardiff, Wales, rather than on location in Lagos, Nigeria, due to budget constraints [1]. The production team created detailed sets to recreate the atmosphere of a Lagos barbershop and market [1].

## Broadcast and reception

### Broadcast

"The Story & the Engine" was released simultaneously at 8:00 AM (BST) on BBC iPlayer in the United Kingdom and on Disney+ worldwide on 10 May 2025 [1]. A broadcast on BBC One occurred later that same day [1]. The episode is 47 minutes in duration [1].

### Audience

The episode was viewed by 1.59 million people during its premiere night in the United Kingdom, representing an increase from the previous episode [1].

### Critical reception

"The Story & the Engine" received generally positive reviews from critics. On the review aggregator Rotten Tomatoes, 89% of 26 reviews are positive .

Critics particularly praised the episode's exploration of the Doctor's ethnicity and Inua Ellams' script [1]. The A.V. Club gave the episode an A- rating, calling it "an equally vibrant, exciting adventure, and one that shows the potential of Ncuti Gatwa's Doctor as the TARDIS lands in Nigeria" [3]. GamesRadar+ described it as "one of the most original and ambitious episodes this show has produced in years," noting that it offers "a fresh take on Doctor Who with a potent mix of mythology and science fiction" .

Several reviewers highlighted the episode's unique approach to storytelling. Geek Vibes Nation gave it a 9.0/10 rating, describing it as "a powerful and emotional tribute to the ways stories bind us together" . BlogtorWho praised it as "a poetic, visually stunning meditation on storytelling, identity, and the power of myth that feels utterly unique in Doctor Who's 62-year history" [4].

However, some critics noted flaws in the episode. GamesR" as "a little confusing" and "the Vindicator getting a boost by simply being near a market" as "a little weak" . Monkeys Fighting Robots stated that while it was "a noble attempt to expand the Doctor's horizon on Earth," it was "lackluster because of its undercooked ideas" and "did not live up to its potential" .

The episode was also noted for its[5] unusual structure, being "almost, but not quite, a bottle episode" with the majority of the action taking place within the barbershop . This confined setting was used to great effect to explore themes of community and storytelling .

## Analysis

"The Story & the Engine" represents a significant departure from traditional *Doctor Who* episodes in several ways. Written by playwright Inua Ellams, the episode explores themes of storytelling, identity, and the power of myth . The narrative structure is notably [4]unconventional, with the Doctor solving the central mystery almost immediately and then spending the majority of the episode trapped in the barbershop .

The episode's exploration of the Doctor's ethnicity is particularly noteworthy. In Lagos, the Doctor is shown only love and is treated "like one of their own," as Belinda puts it . This positive representation stands in contrast to the racism the Doctor might encounter in other historical periods, and critics noted that this approach is important for meaningful representation .

The episode also serves as a meditation on the nature of the Doctor as a "never-ending story" . This theme is literally manifested when the Doctor uses his own life story to power the engine, with archival footage showing all his previous incarnations . This sequence includes appearances [4]by William Hartnell, Patrick Troughton, Jon Pertwee, Tom Baker, Peter Davison, Colin Baker, Paul McGann, Christopher Eccleston, David Tennant, Matt Smith, Peter Capaldi, and Jodie Whittaker .

The character of the Barber represents a unique villain for *Doctor Who*, described as "a storyteller, vexed at the lack of credit he's received" who wants revenge against the gods . His plan to destroy the gods would have catastrophic consequences for humanity, as they are described as "our stories given life, forces that enrich humanity rather than subjugate it" .

The episode's setting in Lagos, Nigeria, provides a rich cultural backdrop for these themes. The barbershop setting, with its tradition of storytelling and community gathering, serves as a perfect metaphor for the episode's exploration of how stories connect people .

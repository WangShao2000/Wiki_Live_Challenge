# Voiceverse NFT plagiarism scandal

The **Voiceverse NFT plagiarism scandal** was a significant controversy in January 2022 concerning the discovery that Voiceverse, an NFT company backed by the Bored Ape Yacht Club and voice actor Troy Baker, had plagiarized voice content from 15.ai, a non-commercial text-to-speech service [1]. The scandal highlighted ethical issues in the emerging AI voice technology and NFT markets, involving unauthorized use of AI-generated content, false marketing claims, and subsequent public backlash [2].

## Background

### 15.ai and its creator

15.ai was a free, non-commercial text-to-speech service created by a pseudonymous MIT researcher known as "15" [3]. The service, launched in March 2020, allowed users to generate voice samples from various fictional characters including those from My Little Pony, Portal, Team Fortress 2, and Doctor Who [4]. The creator had explicitly stated in December 2021 that they had "no interest in incorporating NFTs into any aspect of \[their] work" .

The service utilized a technology called DeepThroat, which was capable of voice cloning with minimal data . 15.ai was developed using a combination of audio synthesis algorithms, speech synthesis deep neural networks, and sentiment analysis . The project began development while the creator was an undergraduate at MIT and had been in development for years before its public release .

### Voiceverse and its concept

Voiceverse was a startup founded by the Bored Ape Yacht Club (BAYC), one of the most prominent NFT collections at the time . The company's concept was to create a platform where users could purchase voice IP rights as NFTs using cryptocurrency . The idea was to allow users to buy the rights to a particular voice - originally performed by a voice actor - as an NFT, which could then be used to create AI-powered voice lines .

The company's founders included those who later created Lovo, a voice technology company [5]. On their pitch website, they stated their vision: "A person's voice is uniquely linked to their identity" [5].

## Timeline of events

### January 14, 2022: Initial announcement

On January 14, 2022, voice actor Troy Baker announced his partnership with Voiceverse NFT via Twitter [2]. In his announcement, Baker stated that the partnership would "explore ways where together we might bring new tools to new creators to make new things, and allow everyone a chance to own & invest in the IP's they create" .

Anticipating potential criticism, Baker added a controversial statement: "We all have a story to tell. You can hate. Or you can create. What'll it be?" . This immediately triggered backlash from fans and the gaming community, with concerns that AI voice technology could displace human voice actors and enable fraud .

### Within hours: First accusations

Just nine hours after Baker's initial announcement, he began walking back some of his statements . He described his tone as "a bit antagonistic," stating he was "just a storyteller out here trying to tell my story to whomever will hear..." .

Later that same day, 15, the creator of 15.ai, alleged that Voiceverse had used their service to create a promotional video . Through server log screenshots, 15 revealed that Voiceverse had generated voice lines using the 15.ai platform, pitch-shifted the audio to make it unrecognizable, and falsely marketed the samples as their own technology before selling them as NFTs .

Specifically, 15.ai accused Voiceverse of using their service to create a voice for a cartoon character from the "Chubbiverse" NFT collection . The creator stated: "Unbelievable. They even pitched up the voice to intentionally make it sound unrecognizable from Rainbow Dash's original voice," referring to a My Little Pony character available on the 15.ai service .

### January 15, 2022: Apology and response

Initially, Voiceverse denied any wrongdoing . However, within an hour of being confronted with evidence, Voiceverse issued an apology . In their tweet to 15.ai, they wrote: "Hey @fifteenai we are extremely sorry about this. The voice was indeed taken from your platform, which our marketing team used without giving proper credit. Chubbiverse team has no knowledge of this. We will make sure this never happens again" .

15.ai's response was brief and direct: "Go fuck yourself" .

### January 31, 2022: Baker ends partnership

After over two weeks of 持续 criticism, Troy Baker announced on January 31, 2022, that he would end his partnership with Voiceverse NFT [6]. In his apology tweet, Baker stated: "Thank you all for your feedback and patience. After careful consideration, I've decided to not continue the partnership with Voiceverse NFT. Intentions aside, I've heard you and apologize for accusing anyone of 'hating' just by simply disagreeing with me" [6].

## Impact and aftermath

### Immediate consequences

The scandal had significant immediate consequences for all parties involved. Voiceverse's reputation was severely damaged, with the company largely "fizzling out" following the controversy . The incident highlighted the unethical practices in the NFT and AI voice technology space, particularly the unauthorized use of non-commercial AI-generated content for profit [2].

The scandal also had broader implications for the voice acting industry, with many voice actors expressing concern about the potential misuse of their voices through AI technology . The incident was noted as the first instance of speech synthesis NFT plagiarism and fraud .

### Long-term developments

In May 2024, Lovo, Voiceverse's parent company, was sued by a group of voice actors in a class action lawsuit . The lawsuit alleged that Lovo had stolen voices and identities of voice actors to create millions of voice-over productions without permission or proper compensation, violating numerous state right of privacy laws and the federal Lanham Act .

The lawsuit specifically mentioned Voiceverse, stating that "the founders of Lovo even created another company, Voiceverse, which sold voice NFTs" [5]. The plaintiffs alleged that Lovo had misrepresented the use of voice recordings, telling voice actors that their voices would be used only for academic research purposes .

In July 2024, the judge allowed the claims to move forward , indicating that the legal repercussions of the Voiceverse scandal were still being felt years after the initial incident.

## Notable reactions

### From the voice acting community

The Voiceverse scandal sparked widespread discussion within the voice acting community. Many voice actors criticized the NFT partnership, with particular concern about the fact that voice actors could only receive royalties when voice NFTs were sold . The incident raised awareness about the potential exploitation of voice actors through AI technology and NFTs.

### From the technology community

The scandal was also notable for being one of the early cases of AI-generated content plagiarism between AI systems . As noted in a retrospective analysis: "It's strange to think that, in January 2022, we had a plagiarism controversy not over whether a work was AI or human-created, but about which AI system created it" .

The incident highlighted the growing issue of AI systems potentially plagiarizing each other, which has become more relevant with the increasing sophistication of AI technology .

### Media coverage

The scandal received significant media coverage, with articles appearing in major gaming and technology publications including Eurogamer, Stevivor, Tech Times, and others . The coverage helped bring attention to the ethical issues surrounding AI voice technology and NFTs, contributing to broader discussions about the regulation of these emerging technologies.

## Legacy

The Voiceverse NFT plagiarism scandal is remembered as a pivotal moment in the development of AI voice technology and the NFT market. It served as a cautionary tale about the unethical use of AI-generated content and the potential for exploitation within the NFT ecosystem [2].

The scandal also highlighted the need for clearer regulations and ethical guidelines in the AI voice technology space, particularly regarding the use of non-commercial AI services for commercial purposes. As noted in a 2025 retrospective: "If this scandal had happened in 2025, things would have gone very differently. The focus would have been on the commercial AI company using the work of a free, solo-developed project to do what it should have been able to do easily" .

The case has been referenced in discussions about AI plagiarism more broadly, with commentators noting that it "may be predicting a future in which AI companies are suddenly very interested in who has permission to do what" . The Voiceverse scandal thus stands as an important early example of the ethical challenges facing the AI and NFT industries as they continue to develop and intersect.

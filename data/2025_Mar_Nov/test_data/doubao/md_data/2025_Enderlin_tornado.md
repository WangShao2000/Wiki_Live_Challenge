# 2025 Enderlin tornado

The **2025 Enderlin tornado** was a large and extremely violent EF5 tornado that struck rural southeastern North Dakota on the night of June 20, 2025, passing near the community of Enderlin [1]. The tornado, officially designated as **Enderlin tornado #1** by the National Weather Service (NWS), was the first tornado to be rated EF5 on the Enhanced Fujita Scale since the 2013 Moore tornado in Oklahoma, ending a 12-year period without tornadoes rated EF5 in the United States [2]). The twister caused three fatalities, extensive damage to agricultural areas and infrastructure, and became notable for its unusual damage to railroad equipment, including the derailment and displacement of multiple train cars [3].

## Meteorological synopsis

The Enderlin tornado formed as part of a larger severe weather system that affected the northern Great Plains between June 19-22, 2025. Meteorological conditions prior to the event were characterized by a **moderate to strong seasonal trough** that was forecast to eject over the northern plains on June 20, producing moderate upper-level divergence . This weather pattern brought **high to extreme levels of Convective Available Potential Energy (CAPE)** to the Dakotas and Minnesota, ranging from 3,000 to 4,000 J/kg, along with seasonally strong winds at altitude .

The presence of a **strong nocturnal low-level jet** expected to emerge after sunset created low-level wind shear conditions that were more than sufficient to support strong tornado development . These factors combined to produce a **significant tornado parameter value exceeding 10**, indicating a high risk for severe tornado development . However, the level of convection in the primary parameter area was uncertain prior to the event, as the forcing for ascent was relatively weak and temperatures at 700 mb were forecast to be 14°C or higher, which could have created a thermodynamic barrier known as a "warm nose" that might inhibit developing updrafts .

The Storm Prediction Center's Day 2 outlook noted that "the core of the low-level adiabatic gradient plume should result in at least a small area of unstably stratified and very unstable air mass, perhaps over northeastern SD and southeastern ND. Here, there will be a conditional risk of supercell and tornado. If storms form in this area around or after 00Z, very large hail and tornadoes appear likely" .

## Tornado development and path

The Enderlin tornado **touched down at 11:02 PM CDT** on June 20, 2025, along 58th Street SE, approximately 6.1 kilometers south of Enderlin in Ransom County, North Dakota . Initially, the tornado exhibited **EF0 intensity**, breaking tree branches at its point of initial touchdown. The twister then intensified and increased in width as it moved **northeastward** across County Road 55 through open agricultural fields .

At 11:05 PM CDT, the tornado reached the **AdM Edible Bean Specialties facility** at the intersection of 136th Avenue SE and County Road 136, with **EF2 intensity**, breaking large trees and destroying an outbuilding . Shortly after, the tornado entered a phase of **dramatic and rapid intensification and widening** as it turned more toward the northeast.

The tornado reached its **maximum intensity** as it crossed a Canadian Pacific Kansas City (CPKC) railroad line southeast of Enderlin, where it caused catastrophic damage to a parked freight train . The force of the tornado was sufficient to **derail 33 rail cars**, including 19 fully-loaded grain hopper cars weighing up to 129.7 tons each, all of which were overturned, with one being dragged into a nearby field. The remaining 14 cars were empty tanker cars weighing up to 32.7 tons . Four of these tanker cars were **thrown into the field**, including one that was hurled between 183 and 305 meters (approximately 600-1000 feet), landing 145 meters from the car to which it had been connected .

Wind damage experts and the Northern Tornadoes Project from the University of Western Ontario determined that **winds of 370 km/h (230 mph)** were necessary to overturn a fully-loaded grain hopper car, and winds exceeding **428 km/h (266 mph)** were estimated to have thrown the tanker car the observed distance . Based on this forensic analysis, the damage at this location was subsequently upgraded to EF5, marking the end of the 12-year drought without EF5 tornadoes in the United States .

The tornado then turned **north-northeast** and weakened to EF3 intensity while reaching its **maximum width of more than one mile** (1.69 kilometers). During this phase, the twister destroyed a cell tower anchored by four guy wires, completely eradicated agricultural fields, and snapped large trees, with some exhibiting debarking . The tornado also leveled an unoccupied farmhouse, displaced a house from its foundation, and damaged or destroyed several outbuildings .

As the tornado approached the **Maple River** and neared North Dakota Highway 46, it reintensified to high-end EF3 strength. The twister then crossed ND 46 and devastated two houses, with two fatalities occurring in this area . The first house, along with a well-constructed detached garage nearby, was **obliterated and swept clean**, with all debris blown downwind. The tornado may have been even stronger at this location, but limitations in construction anchoring prevented a classification higher than EF4 for the average intensity . The second house, located approximately 300 yards (270 meters) west of the first along 140th Avenue SE, was leveled, along with nearby outbuildings that were also destroyed .

The tornado then crossed the Maple River, leveling another swath of trees. All damage along the Maple River had initially been classified as EF3 before being upgraded to EF4 . The twister then weakened as it turned north, parallel to 140th Avenue SE, with EF2 strength, breaking trees and damaging crops, as well as shattering windows in one house and causing roof damage to a mobile home along 52nd Street SE .

The tornado then turned **north-northwest** and reintensified to high-end EF3 strength, leveling another farmhouse and breaking numerous trees; one fatality occurred in this house [4]. After breaking more trees and eradicating additional crop fields with EF2 strength, the tornado turned northwest and **destroyed at least four high steel transmission towers** with low-end EF3 strength; wooden power poles in this area, near and along Township Road 38, were also snapped .

The tornado then narrowed and weakened rapidly while making a curve back to the south along the southern edge of the Utke aquatic production area and crossed Township Road 16, breaking some additional hardwood tree trunks with EF2 strength before dissipating at **11:21 PM CDT** west of the intersection of 50th Street SE and 138th Avenue SE, approximately five miles southwest of Alice, North Dakota [4].

## Meteorological characteristics

The 2025 Enderlin tornado exhibited several **notable meteorological characteristics** that distinguished it from typical tornado events:

### Duration and path

The tornado remained on the ground for **19 minutes** from touchdown to dissipation (11:02 PM to 11:21 PM CDT), traveling a total distance of **12.10 miles (19.5 kilometers)** [2]). The path width varied significantly throughout the tornado's life, reaching a **maximum width of 1.05 miles (1.69 kilometers)** during its north-northeastward movement phase [2]).

### Wind speeds

Initial analysis by the National Weather Service estimated peak winds of **greater than 210 mph (338 km/h)**, placing the tornado firmly in the EF5 category on the Enhanced Fujita Scale . The tornado's winds were so extreme that they produced damage patterns rarely observed in tornado events, particularly the extraordinary displacement of railroad equipment .

### Radar observations

Extreme radar measurements were recorded around the time when EF5-level damage was being caused. The wind speeds correlated with those indicated in the **Storm-Relative Velocity (SRV) data** from the WSR-88D radar at KMVX . The radar data provided crucial verification of the tornado's intensity, particularly during the period when it was causing the most severe damage to the railroad infrastructure .

### Geographic significance

The Enderlin tornado holds several **geographic records** among EF5 tornadoes:



* It was the **first EF5 tornado in the United States in 12 years**, since the 2013 Moore, Oklahoma tornado on May 20, 2013 [2])

* It was the **shortest-lived EF5 tornado ever recorded** at just 19 minutes [2])

* It was the **most northerly EF5 tornado in United States history**, forming at a latitude of 46°N [2])

* It was the first tornado of such intensity in North Dakota since the **1957 Fargo F5 tornado** [2])

## Damage assessment and classification

### Initial assessment

In the immediate aftermath of the tornado, preliminary damage surveys conducted by the National Weather Service resulted in an initial classification of **EF3 with estimated winds of 160 mph (260 km/h)** . This initial assessment was based on standard damage indicators including tree damage, structural damage to buildings, and agricultural damage patterns .

### Upgraded classification

On October 6, 2025, after months of additional analysis and consultation with wind damage experts, the National Weather Service in Grand Forks announced the **upgrade of the Enderlin tornado to EF5** status [5]. This upgrade was primarily based on forensic analysis of the railroad damage, particularly the extraordinary displacement of the tanker cars .

The upgrade decision was supported by extensive collaboration with experts including Tim Marshall, Jim LaDue, Dr. Connell Miller, Dr. Greg Kopp, Dr. David Sills, and the entire Northern Tornadoes Project at Western University's Canadian Severe Storms Laboratory . The analysis involved sophisticated wind speed calculations based on debris trajectories and the physics of projectile motion .

### Unique damage indicators

The Enderlin tornado produced several **unusual damage patterns** that contributed to its EF5 classification:



1. **Railroad damage**: The tornado's effect on the CPKC railroad cars was particularly distinctive. The fact that a fully-loaded grain hopper car weighing 129.7 tons was overturned, and an empty tanker car weighing 32.7 tons was thrown 145 meters, provided unprecedented forensic evidence of extreme wind speeds . These observations exceeded the damage criteria used in the Enhanced Fujita Scale by a factor of two in distance and four times in weight compared to the heaviest objects considered in the reanalysis models .

2. **Tree damage**: Extensive tree damage was observed throughout the Maple River valley, with only stubs of large branches or trunks remaining. Many trees exhibited severe debarking with a "sandpapering" effect, and several trees with attached root balls were displaced, including one where the original location could not be determined .

3. **Structural damage**: At farmstead #2, the damage was characterized as **complete destruction with the foundation considered to be swept clean**, and debris was blown downwind and scattered. While limitations in construction anchoring prevented an even higher classification in some areas, the overall pattern of destruction was consistent with EF5 intensity .

4. **Projectile damage**: A piece of mail was carried by the tornado for more than 40 miles (64 kilometers) and landed in a woman's backyard, demonstrating the extraordinary transport capabilities of the storm .

## Casualties and impact

### Fatalities

The 2025 Enderlin tornado resulted in **three fatalities** - two men and one woman. The victims were identified as two 73-year-old men and one 89-year-old woman . According to the Enderlin-Sheldon Fire Department, all three fatalities occurred in rural areas east of the town of Enderlin .

The tornado was the **deadliest tornado to strike North Dakota since 1978** [6]. The relatively low number of casualties, given the tornado's extreme intensity, was attributed to the rural nature of the affected area and the fact that the tornado struck at night when most people were indoors .

### Injuries

Remarkably, the tornado resulted in **zero injuries**, despite its EF5 intensity and the extensive damage it caused [1]. This unusual outcome was partly due to the tornado's path through predominantly rural and agricultural areas with relatively low population density .

### Infrastructure damage

The tornado caused significant damage to various infrastructure components:



1. **Transportation infrastructure**: In addition to the dramatic railroad damage, the tornado destroyed several roads and bridges along its path .

2. **Utility infrastructure**: The tornado destroyed at least four high steel transmission towers and numerous wooden power poles, resulting in widespread power outages in the affected area .

3. **Agricultural infrastructure**: The tornado completely destroyed or severely damaged multiple farmsteads, including houses, barns, and other outbuildings. Several farms had their foundations "swept clean" with only basements remaining .

4. **Communication infrastructure**: A cell tower anchored by four guy wires was completely destroyed .

### Economic impact

While specific economic loss figures were not detailed in the available sources, the tornado caused extensive damage to agricultural land, farm buildings, and equipment. The city of Enderlin itself suffered minimal damage, with the mayor reporting that aside from power outages, the town "pretty much went unscathed" .

According to the Enderlin-Sheldon Fire Department, a total of **10 houses suffered some type of damage** from the storm . However, given the tornado's path through rural agricultural areas, the total economic impact was primarily concentrated on agricultural losses and infrastructure repairs.

## Meteorological significance

### Ending the EF5 drought

The 2025 Enderlin tornado marked the end of a **12-year period without EF5 tornadoes** in the United States, which was the longest such period since the National Weather Service began keeping records in 1950 [5]. The previous EF5 tornado was the devastating 2013 Moore, Oklahoma tornado that killed 24 people and injured more than 200 others .

Since the National Weather Service began using the Enhanced Fujita Scale in 2007, there have been only **10 tornadoes categorized as EF5**. The 12-year gap between top-of-the-scale ratings was notable, as meteorologists noted that "in the last kind of 12 years, there's been several strong tornadoes that have come close, but there haven't been known damage indicators at that time to support the EF5 rating" .

### Research implications

The Enderlin tornado provided unique research opportunities for several reasons:



1. **Forensic wind analysis**: The tornado's damage to the railroad cars provided unprecedented data for wind engineering research. The case was particularly valuable because the weights and trajectories of the displaced objects were precisely measurable, allowing for accurate wind speed calculations .

2. **Nocturnal tornado behavior**: The tornado occurred at night, which is relatively uncommon for EF5 tornadoes. This provided insights into the formation and intensification of severe tornadoes under nocturnal conditions .

3. **High-latitude tornadoes**: As the most northerly EF5 tornado on record, the Enderlin event provided valuable data on tornado formation and behavior at higher latitudes where such extreme events are rare [2]).

4. **Short-duration EF5 tornadoes**: At just 19 minutes, the Enderlin tornado was the shortest-lived EF5 tornado ever recorded, providing insights into the rapid intensification and dissipation of extreme tornadoes [2]).

## Aftermath and recovery

### Emergency response

Emergency response to the tornado was coordinated by local, state, and federal agencies. The Enderlin-Sheldon Fire Department reported that they responded to multiple calls for assistance throughout the night of the tornado . The North Dakota National Guard was activated to assist with search and rescue operations and to provide security in damaged areas .

### Government response

North Dakota Governor Kelly Armstrong visited the affected area on June 25, 2025, to survey the damage and meet with affected residents . During his visit, Governor Armstrong promised state support for victims of the storm, including assistance with debris removal, temporary housing, and long-term recovery efforts .

The governor's office released several photographs documenting the damage, including images of destroyed farmsteads, uprooted trees, and damaged infrastructure . These images were widely distributed in media reports and helped raise awareness of the tornado's impact.

### Community recovery

The recovery process in the affected areas was facilitated by the relatively small number of affected structures and the strong community ties in the rural areas. The city of Enderlin, which was largely spared from major damage, served as a staging area for recovery efforts .

Local emergency management officials worked with the Red Cross and other organizations to provide temporary shelter and supplies to affected families. The North Dakota Department of Emergency Services coordinated with federal agencies to ensure that affected residents had access to disaster assistance programs .

### Long-term implications

The 2025 Enderlin tornado has had several long-term implications for tornado research and preparedness:



1. **Enhanced warning systems**: The tornado highlighted the importance of continued investment in tornado detection and warning systems, particularly for rural areas that may have limited access to traditional warning methods .

2. **Building codes**: The tornado's damage patterns, particularly to agricultural buildings and infrastructure, have prompted discussions about strengthening building codes in tornado-prone areas .

3. **Research funding**: The unique nature of the tornado's damage, particularly to the railroad equipment, has led to increased funding for tornado research and wind engineering studies .

4. **Public awareness**: The tornado has increased public awareness of the tornado risk in North Dakota and other northern plains states, leading to improved preparedness efforts .

## Conclusion

The **2025 Enderlin tornado** stands as a significant event in American meteorological history, marking the end of a 12-year drought without EF5 tornadoes and providing unprecedented research opportunities for tornado scientists and engineers [5]. The tornado's combination of extreme intensity, unusual damage patterns, and relatively low casualty toll makes it a unique case study in tornado behavior and impact.

From a meteorological perspective, the Enderlin tornado demonstrated how relatively common atmospheric conditions - including high CAPE values, strong wind shear, and a nocturnal low-level jet - can combine to produce an extraordinary and destructive event . The tornado's rapid intensification to EF5 strength, its unusual path through rural North Dakota, and its brief 19-minute lifespan all contribute to its scientific significance [2]).

The event also highlighted the challenges faced by meteorologists in accurately assessing tornado intensity, particularly when dealing with unusual damage patterns such as the railroad car displacements . The months-long process of analysis and consultation with experts from multiple institutions demonstrates the rigorous standards applied to EF5 classifications and the importance of forensic damage analysis in tornado research .

From a societal perspective, the Enderlin tornado serves as a reminder of the power of nature and the importance of preparedness, even in areas where such extreme events are rare. The fact that the tornado caused only three fatalities despite its EF5 intensity underscores the value of early warning systems, community preparedness, and the rural nature of the affected area .

The legacy of the 2025 Enderlin tornado will continue to influence tornado research, warning systems, and building practices for years to come. As climate patterns continue to evolve and tornado behavior remains complex and sometimes unpredictable, events like the Enderlin tornado provide crucial data and insights that help scientists better understand and predict these powerful forces of nature .

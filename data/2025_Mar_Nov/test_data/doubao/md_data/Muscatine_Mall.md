# Muscatine Mall

Muscatine Mall is a shopping mall located at 1903 Park Avenue in Muscatine, Iowa, United States [1]. Opened on August 5, 1971, the mall serves as the primary retail shopping center in Muscatine [2]. With approximately 47,817 square feet of retail space, it features three anchor stores: JCPenney, Elder-Beerman (the only one in Iowa), and Menards [3].

## History

### Opening and Early Years

Muscatine Mall was officially opened on August 5, 1971 [2]. The mall was developed as part of Muscatine's growing retail infrastructure, serving the city of approximately 23,000 residents located on the Mississippi River 15 miles south of Interstate 80 .

In its early years, the mall featured a mix of department stores and specialty shops. According to visitor accounts, by 1987 the mall had become significant enough to attract shoppers from outside the immediate area. One notable memory from 1987 was that the mall housed one of the first Walmart stores in the region, visited by shoppers two years before the Walmart in Davenport opened .

### Peak Period (1990s)

The 1990s represented the mall's peak period. By 1995, Muscatine Mall had expanded to include approximately 50 stores, with major anchors including Walmart, JCPenney, and Von Maur . The mall also featured typical mall retailers of the era, including Walgreens, B. Dalton bookstore, Foot Locker, Musicland, and Radio Shack .

This period saw the mall as a thriving retail destination for the Muscatine area, offering a comprehensive shopping experience with both national chain stores and local businesses. The mall's location in the heart of Muscatine's retail corridor contributed to its success during this time .

### Decline and Tenant Changes (Late 1990s-2000s)

The mall began experiencing significant changes in the late 1990s. Around 1997, Walmart relocated to a new SuperCenter along the US 61 bypass, leaving a large anchor space vacant . Menards, a home improvement chain, filled this north anchor spot approximately one year later . While unusual for a Menards store to be located in an enclosed shopping mall (as most are free-standing), this arrangement was not unique, as Marshall Town Center in Marshalltown, Iowa, also had a Menards in its mall .

The south anchor space also changed hands multiple times. Von Maur left shortly after Walmart, and Staples occupied the space for about two years before closing in early 2002 . This closure put the mall's future in jeopardy until Elder-Beerman replaced Staples in late 2003 . Elder-Beerman's arrival marked the chain's first presence in Iowa, with this being its 69th store overall and 12th smaller format store [4].

Walgreens also left the mall during this period, relocating to a free-standing store just south of the mall . This departure was followed by many smaller stores closing around the same time, significantly reducing the mall's tenant count .

### Current Status (2005-Present)

As of Thanksgiving 2005, the mall had experienced a dramatic decline. According to a detailed visitor account, only about 10 stores remained in the mall's interior aside from the anchors. The only chain stores left inside were GNC and Regis Hairstylists . Most of the interior corridor between Menards and JCPenney was "closed for maintenance," and Menards' mall entrance was sealed .

Some businesses opened in former tenant spaces but with exterior entrances only, without access to the mall's interior. These included a Hallmark Gold Crown shop, Hibbett Sports, and a China Buffet in the former Walgreens space, as well as an ExpertTire store, an Iowa driver's license station, and a wireless store .

The mall continued to operate through the 2010s with reduced occupancy. In 2008, Younkers opened in the former Elder-Beerman space, but closed in 2018 and was replaced by Warehouse Bargains [5].

Recent developments show the mall remains operational as of 2025. The City of Muscatine has hosted events at the mall, including the Elves Workshop in December 2024 and 2025 [6]. The mall also continues to house some services, including CDL skills and drive tests through the Iowa Department of Transportation, though the main DMV office relocated to a new location in June 2024 .

## Architecture and Design

Muscatine Mall is an enclosed shopping mall with a traditional design typical of shopping centers from the early 1970s [7]. The mall has a single-level layout with interior corridors connecting the anchor stores .

The mall's main entrance faces Park Avenue, with additional entrances at each anchor store. The architectural style is functional and utilitarian, reflecting the design trends of its construction period. The mall features a central atrium area, where seasonal displays such as Santa Claus during Christmas have been set up .

One notable architectural feature is the unusual presence of a Menards store within the enclosed mall structure. Menards typically operates free-standing stores, making this an uncommon arrangement . The Menards entrance within the mall was sealed as of 2005, with customers accessing the store only through its exterior entrance .

The mall's interior corridors feature typical mall finishes including tile floors, suspended ceilings with fluorescent lighting, and storefronts with large display windows. As of 2005, significant portions of the interior corridor were closed, creating a fragmented shopping experience .

## Tenants

### Anchor Stores

Muscatine Mall currently features three anchor stores:

**JCPenney** has been a consistent anchor tenant throughout the mall's history, remaining in operation since at least 1995 . The store is located at one end of the mall and serves as one of the primary entry points.

**Elder-Beerman** is the only store of this chain in Iowa. It occupies the south anchor position and opened in late 2003, replacing Staples [3]. Elder-Beerman's presence in Muscatine marked the chain's expansion into Iowa, with this being its 69th store overall [4].

**Menards** occupies the north anchor position, replacing Walmart around 1998 . The Menards store is unusual for being located within an enclosed shopping mall, as the chain typically operates free-standing locations .

### Notable Former Tenants

Several significant retailers have occupied space in Muscatine Mall over the years:

**Walmart** was a major anchor from at least 1987 until around 1997, when it relocated to a SuperCenter along the US 61 bypass .

**Von Maur** was an anchor tenant in the 1990s but left shortly after Walmart relocated .

**Staples** occupied the south anchor space for approximately two years before closing in early 2002 .

**Walgreens** operated a pharmacy in the mall until moving to a free-standing location just south of the mall .

**Younkers** opened in the former Elder-Beerman space in 2008 but closed in 2018 and was replaced by Warehouse Bargains [5].

## Community Impact

Muscatine Mall has played a significant role in the economic and social life of Muscatine. As the city's primary shopping center, it has served as a gathering place for residents and a destination for shoppers from surrounding areas [9].

The mall's economic impact has evolved over time. During its peak in the 1990s, it provided employment for hundreds of local residents and attracted shoppers from a wide geographic area . The presence of national chain stores helped bring goods and services to Muscatine that might otherwise not be available locally.

The mall has also served as a venue for community events. Recent examples include the annual Handcrafted Christmas event, featuring local vendors selling unique handmade gifts and baked goods, along with music, beverages, and holiday entertainment . The Elves Workshop, a free festive craft event for families, has also been held at the mall .

The mall's decline has had a noticeable impact on the local retail landscape. The closure of many stores and the sealing off of portions of the interior have created an economic challenge for the area . However, the continued operation of the anchor stores and some smaller tenants provides essential retail services to the community.

The presence of services such as the Iowa Department of Transportation's CDL testing facility (which continues to operate at the mall despite the main DMV office relocation) demonstrates the mall's ongoing utility to the community .

## Location

Muscatine Mall is located at 1903 Park Avenue in Muscatine, Iowa 52761 [1]. The mall is situated in the heart of Muscatine's retail corridor, approximately 15 miles south of Interstate 80 .

The mall's coordinates are:



* 41°26'46"N latitude

* 91°1'42"W longitude

The location is easily accessible from downtown Muscatine. From the city center, visitors can travel east on 2nd Street, which becomes Park Avenue. The mall is located approximately 1 mile from downtown at the intersection with 19th Street .

Public transportation options include the City Bus that travels along the Park Avenue route, with a stop at the mall entrance . The bus journey from the Muscatine Transfer Point takes approximately 15-20 minutes depending on traffic .

The mall features ample parking with spaces available directly in front of the building and in surrounding lots . Bicycle racks are also available for those preferring to cycle to the mall .

## Conclusion

Muscatine Mall represents a significant retail and community institution in Muscatine, Iowa. Opened in 1971, the mall has experienced a full lifecycle of growth, prosperity, decline, and adaptation [2].

Despite challenges including tenant losses and reduced occupancy, the mall continues to serve the Muscatine community. The presence of three anchor stores, including the only Elder-Beerman in Iowa, provides essential retail services [4]. Community events held at the mall demonstrate its ongoing role as a gathering place [6].

The mall's evolution reflects broader changes in American retail, from the dominance of enclosed shopping centers in the 1990s to the challenges faced by traditional malls in the 21st century . Its unusual architectural feature of housing a Menards store within an enclosed structure adds to its distinctive character .

As Muscatine continues to develop and adapt, Muscatine Mall remains an important part of the city's landscape, serving as a reminder of the evolution of retail while continuing to provide necessary services to the community.

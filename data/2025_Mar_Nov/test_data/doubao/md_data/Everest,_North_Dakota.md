# Everest, North Dakota

## Lead Section

Everest is an unincorporated community in Cass County, North Dakota, United States. It is located in Everest Township, approximately two miles south of Casselton . The community was established in 1881 when the Great Northern Railroad built a station at this location . Everest reached its peak population of 175 in 1890 but declined significantly after the railroad removed its station in 1895 and took up the tracks in 1896 . Today, Everest has a very small population, with estimates ranging from 31 to 86 residents .

## History

### Early Settlement and Development

Everest was platted as a village in 1881, coinciding with the construction of the Great Northern Railroad station . The post office was established on May 24, 1882, with Carl Knuppel serving as the first postmaster . The community was initially positioned at a significant transportation junction, being located at the intersection of the Great Northern Railway and the Northern Pacific Railway .

The early development of Everest was closely tied to the railroad. The completion of the rail line connecting these two railroads was announced in October 1882 . This transportation infrastructure provided the foundation for the community's growth, and by 1890, Everest had reached its peak population of 175 residents .

### Railroad Changes and Decline

The fortunes of Everest changed dramatically when the Great Northern Railroad revised its long-range plans. In 1895, the company removed the Everest station, and the following year, in 1896, the tracks were taken up entirely . This decision had devastating consequences for the community's economy and population.

The removal of the railroad infrastructure was particularly significant because Everest had been positioned as a junction point between two major rail lines. The westernmost of these two rail lines was removed around 1896 when it was acquired by the Great Northern Railway . This consolidation eliminated the strategic value of Everest as a transportation hub.

### Post Office History

The post office in Everest had a complex operational history. After the railroad abandonment, the post office closed on August 13, 1904, but reopened just over a month later on September 17, 1904 . However, this reopening was short-lived, and the post office closed permanently on November 30, 1908, with mail service being redirected to Casselton .

### 20th Century Population Decline

Despite the loss of its transportation infrastructure and post office, Everest continued to exist as a community, though with a much-reduced population. By 1940, the population was estimated at 100 residents . The community continued to appear on North Dakota state maps during this era .

The decline continued throughout the 20th century. By 1960, the population had fallen to just 10 residents . This represents a 94% decrease from the peak population of 175 in 1890.

### Modern Era

In the modern era, Everest exists as a very small rural community. Recent population estimates vary significantly, with some sources reporting 31 residents  and others reporting 86 residents . The community has a population density of approximately 2 people per square mile .

Despite its small size, Everest maintains some community infrastructure. Local businesses include a small general store that sells snacks and basic necessities, as well as a roadside diner that serves home-cooked meals . The community also has organizations such as the Everest Lions Club, which hosts events throughout the year to benefit the community .

## Geography

### Location and Coordinates

Everest is located in southeastern North Dakota in Cass County. The community's coordinates are 46°51'35"N 97°13'26"W . It is situated in the northeastern quarter of section 15-139-52 in Everest Township . The elevation is 284 meters (932 feet) above sea level .

### Regional Setting

Everest is part of the Fargo metropolitan area, which is positioned in southeast North Dakota along the Red River . The city of Fargo is approximately 200 miles northwest of Minneapolis, Minnesota, and 210 miles south of Winnipeg, Manitoba, Canada .

The surrounding area is characterized by flat plains and fertile farmland typical of the Red River Valley . The region experiences a continental climate with significant seasonal variations, including cold, snowy winters and warm, pleasant summers .

### Township Organization

Everest is part of Everest Township in Cass County. The township maintains its own government structure, with regular supervisor meetings held throughout the year [1]. The township government works with Cass County on various matters, including road maintenance and other shared services [2].

## Naming Controversy

### The Origin of the Name

There is a notable controversy surrounding the naming of the community. All sources indicate that the town was named after the editor of The Lisbon Star newspaper . However, there is a significant discrepancy regarding the name of this editor.

According to multiple sources, the editor's name was actually Charles A. Everitt, not "Everest" as the town's name suggests . This naming error has persisted in all historical records and maps, creating an interesting footnote in the community's history.

### Historical Sources

The naming controversy is documented in multiple historical sources. Wikimapia notes: "All sources say the town was named for the editor of The Lisbon Star, but his name was actually Charles A. Everitt, not Everest" . Another source confirms: "There is some controversy surrounding the naming of the community. Some sources report that the community was named after the editor of the Lisbon Star, but his name was Charles Everitt" .

### Possible Explanations

Several theories exist about how this naming error occurred. It's possible that there was a miscommunication or misspelling when the town was officially named. Alternatively, the name may have been anglicized or modified for easier pronunciation. The exact circumstances of the naming error remain unclear in the historical record.

## Economy and Infrastructure

### Current Businesses

Despite its small size, Everest maintains a few local businesses that serve the community and travelers passing through. The community has a small general store that sells snacks and basic necessities . There is also a roadside diner that serves home-cooked meals .

### Community Organizations

Everest has an active community life supported by local organizations. The Everest Lions Club hosts several events throughout the year to benefit the community . These events help maintain social connections and provide opportunities for community members to gather.

### Transportation Infrastructure

Everest has no zip codes of its own [4], and mail service is provided through Casselton, as it has been since the post office closed in 1908 . The community is served by local roads that connect it to nearby towns and the larger transportation network.

### Agriculture

Agriculture has historically been important in Everest Township. In 1900, according to one state history, a local resident was described as "the fortunate possessor of one of the best farms of Cass County, and his residence on section 23, of Everest Township, is evidence of his prosperity and thrift" . This suggests that farming was a significant economic activity in the area during the early settlement period.

## Climate

## Government and Politics

### Township Government

Everest is governed as part of Everest Township in Cass County. The township has its own elected officials, including a supervisor who is elected during public meetings [5]. Regular township supervisor meetings are held, typically in the evening hours [1].

The township government handles local matters such as road maintenance, land use regulations, and other community services. Township meetings are open to the public and provide opportunities for residents to participate in local governance.

### County Relationship

Everest Township works closely with Cass County government on various issues. For example, there have been memorandums of understanding between Casselton Township and Everest Township regarding road maintenance, including the transfer of control of certain roads from the townships to Cass County [2].

### Local Services

Local services in Everest are provided through a combination of township, county, and regional resources. Emergency services, law enforcement, and other public services are typically provided by Cass County or regional agencies.

## Conclusion

Everest, North Dakota, stands as a fascinating example of the boom-and-bust cycle that affected many small railroad towns in the American West. From its establishment in 1881 as a railroad junction with a population of 175, to its current status as a tiny rural community of around 31-86 residents, Everest's history reflects the profound impact of transportation infrastructure on small town development.

The community's naming controversy, where it was named after Charles A. Everitt rather than "Everest," adds an interesting footnote to its history. Despite the challenges of depopulation and the loss of its transportation hub status, Everest continues to exist as a close-knit rural community with a strong sense of local identity.

Today, Everest serves as a reminder of the important role that railroads played in the settlement and development of the American West, and how changes in transportation infrastructure can dramatically alter the fortunes of small communities.

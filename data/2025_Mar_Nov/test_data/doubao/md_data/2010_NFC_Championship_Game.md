# 2010 NFC Championship Game

The 2010 NFC Championship Game was the National Football Conference (NFC) championship game that determined the conference winner for the 2009 NFL season [1]. Played on January 24, 2010, at the Louisiana Superdome in New Orleans, Louisiana, the game featured the top-seeded New Orleans Saints (15-3) against the second-seeded Minnesota Vikings (13-5) [1]. The Saints defeated the Vikings 31-28 in overtime, advancing to their first Super Bowl in franchise history [2].

## Game Background

The game had significant cultural and historical significance beyond its sporting implications. New Orleans was still recovering from Hurricane Katrina, which had devastated the city and severely damaged the Louisiana Superdome in August 2005 [3]. The Saints had played several games in other cities during the 2005 season while the Superdome underwent repairs [4]. Less than five years after the hurricane, the Saints were attempting to win their first-ever conference championship [5].

For the Vikings, the game represented an opportunity to reach their first Super Bowl since the 1970s [5]. The team was quarterbacked by 40-year-old future Hall of Famer Brett Favre, who was playing in what would prove to be his final NFL playoff game [5]. The Vikings had finished the regular season with a 13-5 record and had defeated the Dallas Cowboys in the NFC Divisional Round [1].

## Game Summary

### First Quarter

The game began with the Vikings taking an early lead. On their first possession, running back Adrian Peterson scored on a 19-yard touchdown run, giving the Vikings a 7-0 lead with 9:35 remaining in the first quarter . The Saints quickly responded with a touchdown of their own. Quarterback Drew Brees connected with running back Pierre Thomas on a 38-yard touchdown pass to tie the game at 7-7 with 6:30 left in the quarter .

The Vikings regained the lead when Favre threw a 5-yard touchdown pass to wide receiver Sidney Rice with 2:11 remaining in the first quarter . The Vikings led 14-7 at the end of the first quarter.

### Second Quarter

The Saints tied the game early in the second quarter when Brees threw a 9-yard touchdown pass to wide receiver Devery Henderson with 10:30 remaining in the half . The score remained tied at 14-14 going into halftime.

### Third Quarter

The Saints took their first lead of the game in the third quarter. Thomas scored on a 9-yard touchdown run with 12:56 remaining in the third quarter, giving the Saints a 21-14 advantage . The Vikings quickly tied the game when Peterson scored his second touchdown of the game on a 1-yard run with 7:35 remaining in the third quarter .

### Fourth Quarter

The Saints regained the lead in the fourth quarter when Brees threw a 5-yard touchdown pass to running back Reggie Bush with 12:39 remaining . The Saints led 28-21.

The Vikings tied the game with less than five minutes remaining when Peterson scored his third touchdown of the game on a 2-yard run with 4:58 left in regulation . The score was 28-28, setting up a dramatic finish.

In the final moments of regulation, Favre led the Vikings on a drive that appeared to be heading toward a game-winning field goal. However, with 19 seconds remaining, Favre threw a pass intended for Sidney Rice that was intercepted by Saints cornerback Tracy Porter [5]. The interception sent the game into overtime.

### Overtime

The Saints won the overtime coin toss and began their possession at their own 20-yard line . The Saints drove down the field, helped by two penalties against the Vikings' defense and a crucial fourth-down conversion when Thomas ran for 2 yards on a fourth-and-1 play .

With 10:15 remaining in overtime, Saints kicker Garrett Hartley made a 40-yard field goal to give the Saints a 31-28 victory [2]. The kick sent the Saints to Super Bowl XLIV, where they would face the Indianapolis Colts [6].

## Key Plays and Moments

### Brett Favre's Final Pass

The most pivotal play of the game was Favre's interception in the closing seconds of regulation. Favre had been under constant pressure from the Saints' defense throughout the game . On the play, Favre rolled out to the edge and had an opportunity to run, but instead attempted to throw a pass to Sidney Rice [7]. The pass was intercepted by Tracy Porter, who nearly returned it for a touchdown [7]. This interception was one of five turnovers committed by the Vikings in the game [5].

### Garrett Hartley's Heroics

Garrett Hartley's game-winning field goal in overtime was particularly significant given his previous struggles. Hartley had been cut by the Saints earlier in the season but was re-signed . In the NFC Championship Game, Hartley also made field goals of 47 and 32 yards during regulation [8]. His clutch performance in the game made him a hero in New Orleans .

### Adrian Peterson's Three Touchdowns

Vikings running back Adrian Peterson had a remarkable game, rushing for 122 yards and three touchdowns on 25 carries . His three touchdown runs came from 19, 1, and 2 yards respectively . ### Drew Brees' Efficiency

Saints quarterback Drew Brees had a highly efficient game, completing 17 of 31 passes for 197 yards and three touchdowns with no interceptions . His passer rating was 106.5 . Brees' touchdown passes went to Pierre Thomas (38 yards), Devery Henderson (9 yards), and Reggie Bush (5 yards) .

## Aftermath and Impact

The Saints' victory was historic for the franchise and the city of New Orleans. It marked the first time in their 43-year history that the Saints had advanced to the Super Bowl [6]. The win was particularly meaningful given the city's recovery from Hurricane Katrina [3].

The game was also significant for the Vikings organization and Brett Favre. For Favre, the interception was the final play of his NFL playoff career [5]. He would retire after the season, though he would briefly return to play for the Vikings again in 2010 before retiring permanently [9].

The game was one of the most-watched television programs in recent history. Not counting Super Bowl broadcasts, it was the most-watched television program since the finale of the TV series "Seinfeld" in 1998, drawing 76.3 million viewers [10]. The game had 57.9 million viewers [11].

The Saints would go on to win Super Bowl XLIV, defeating the Indianapolis Colts 31-17 . This victory gave the city of New Orleans its first major professional sports championship .

The 2010 NFC Championship Game is remembered as one of the most dramatic and significant games in NFL history, combining exceptional athletic performance with cultural significance for the city of New Orleans [5].

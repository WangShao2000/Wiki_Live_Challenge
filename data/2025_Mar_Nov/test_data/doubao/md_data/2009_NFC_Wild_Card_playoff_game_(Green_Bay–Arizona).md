# 2009 NFC Wild Card playoff game (Green Bay–Arizona)

The **2009 NFC Wild Card playoff game** was an American football game played on January 10, 2010, at University of Phoenix Stadium in Glendale, Arizona, between the **Green Bay Packers** and the **Arizona Cardinals**. [1] The Cardinals defeated the Packers in overtime by a score of **51–45**, in what remains the **highest-scoring postseason game in NFL history** with a combined 96 points. [2]

## Game background

The game featured a matchup between two teams that had met just one week earlier in the regular season, with the Packers winning 33–7 at the same venue.  The Cardinals entered the playoffs as the **NFC West champions** with an 11–6 record, while the Packers were the NFC Wild Card team with the same record. [3]

This game marked several significant milestones:



* It was **Kurt Warner's final NFL playoff game** before his retirement in January 2010 [4]

* It was **Aaron Rodgers' first career playoff start** at quarterback for the Packers [5]

* The Cardinals were the defending NFC champions, having lost to the Pittsburgh Steelers in Super Bowl XLIII the previous season

## Game summary

### First quarter

The Cardinals dominated the early stages of the game, scoring on their first three possessions.  Tim Hightower scored on a 1-yard run just 3:56 into the game, followed by Early Doucet's 15-yard touchdown reception from Kurt Warner.  Neil Rackers added a 23-yard field goal to give the Cardinals a commanding **17–0 lead** after the first quarter.

The Packers' offense struggled initially, with Rodgers throwing an interception on the game's first play, which was deflected by Karlos Dansby to Dominique Rodgers-Cromartie.  On the Packers' second possession, Donald Driver was stripped by Dansby after a short reception, with Alan Branch recovering for the Cardinals.

### Second quarter

The Packers finally got on the scoreboard with Rodgers scoring on a 1-yard quarterback sneak, capping a 7-play, 68-yard drive.  However, the Cardinals quickly responded with Warner connecting with Doucet again for a 15-yard touchdown pass, extending their lead to **24–7**.

The Packers added a 20-yard field goal by Mason Crosby before halftime to make the score **24–10**.

### Third quarter

The Cardinals extended their lead to **31–10** early in the third quarter when Warner found Larry Fitzgerald for a 33-yard touchdown pass.  This score came after the Cardinals recovered a Packers fumble on the opening kickoff of the second half.

The Packers then mounted a remarkable comeback, scoring touchdowns on their next three possessions.  Rodgers threw touchdown passes to Greg Jennings (6 yards) and Jordy Nelson (11 yards), with an onside kick recovery in between.  The Cardinals answered with Warner's fourth touchdown pass of the game, a 11-yard strike to Fitzgerald, making the score **38–24**.

### Fourth quarter

The fourth quarter saw an offensive shootout, with both teams scoring multiple touchdowns.  The Packers scored on James Jones' 30-yard reception from Rodgers, and John Kuhn's 1-yard run to tie the game at **38–38**.

The Cardinals regained the lead when Warner connected with Steve Breaston for a 17-yard touchdown pass, making it **45–38**.  However, the Packers refused to quit, with Rodgers throwing his fourth touchdown pass of the game, an 11-yarder to Spencer Havner, tying the score at **45–45** with just 1:52 remaining.

The Cardinals had a chance to win the game in regulation when they drove to the Packers' 16-yard line, but Neil Rackers missed a 34-yard field goal attempt wide left with 9 seconds remaining.

### Overtime

The Packers won the overtime coin toss and received the ball, confident they would score and advance.  However, on the third play of overtime, Cardinals cornerback Michael Adams (listed at 5'8") sacked Rodgers and forced a fumble.  The ball caromed off Rodgers' foot and into the hands of Karlos Dansby, who returned it 17 yards for the game-winning touchdown. [6]

The play occurred just **78 seconds into overtime**, giving the Cardinals a dramatic **51–45 victory**.

## Key players and performances

### Cardinals

**Kurt Warner** delivered one of the most efficient performances in NFL playoff history, completing **29 of 33 passes** (87.9%) for 379 yards with **five touchdowns and no interceptions**, for a passer rating of 154.1.  His five touchdown passes matched his career high, and remarkably, he had **more touchdown passes than incompletions** (five touchdowns vs. four incompletions).

**Larry Fitzgerald** caught six passes for 82 yards and two touchdowns, despite being double-teamed for much of the game.  **Early Doucet** had six receptions for 77 yards and two touchdowns, while **Steve Breaston** (starting in place of the injured Anquan Boldin) caught seven passes for 125 yards and one touchdown.

On the ground, **Beanie Wells** rushed for 91 yards on 14 carries (6.5 avg), helping the Cardinals gain 156 rushing yards against the NFL's top-ranked run defense.

**Karlos Dansby** was instrumental on defense, recording two forced fumbles, a fumble recovery, and the game-winning touchdown on the overtime fumble return.

### Packers

**Aaron Rodgers** completed 28 of 42 passes for a **Packers postseason-record 422–423 yards** (sources vary slightly) with four touchdowns and one interception. [7] All but two of his passing yards came after the first quarter, as he led the Packers' comeback from a 21-point deficit. [7]

**Jermichael Finley** set a Packers playoff record with **159 receiving yards** on six catches, while **Greg Jennings** had eight receptions for 130 yards and one touchdown, including a spectacular one-handed catch.

The Packers' offense was remarkably efficient in the second half, scoring touchdowns on **five consecutive possessions**, with drives covering 80, 57, 80, 67, and 71 yards.  No drive took more than four minutes, with five different players scoring touchdowns.

* **12 combined extra points**

* **96 total points** (the highest combined total in NFL postseason history) [2]

## Impact and legacy

The game's significance extends beyond the final score:



1. **Historical record**: The 96 combined points broke the previous record of 95 points set in Philadelphia's 58–37 win over Detroit on December 30, 1995.  This record still stands as of 2024.

2. **Individual records**:

* Rodgers' 423 passing yards was a Packers postseason record [8]

* Warner's 87.9% completion percentage remains one of the highest in NFL playoff history

* The game featured the most combined touchdowns (13) in a playoff game

1. **Team records**:

* The Packers scored the **most points by a losing team in NFL playoff history** with 45 points [9]

* It was the most points scored and allowed by the Packers in their 41-game playoff history

* The Cardinals scored their most points in a playoff game with 51 points [10]

1. **Player milestones**:

* Warner announced his retirement shortly after the game, ending a Hall of Fame career [4]

* Rodgers' performance, despite the loss, established him as a premier quarterback in the NFL [5]

1. **Game-winning play**: The game ended on a defensive touchdown, the first such play to end a playoff game since January 4, 2004, when Al Harris returned an interception 52 yards for a touchdown in Green Bay's 33–27 win over Seattle.

## Subsequent playoff runs

Following this victory, the Cardinals advanced to face the top-seeded New Orleans Saints in the NFC Divisional Round. [11] However, they were soundly defeated 45–14, ending their season. [11] The Saints went on to win Super Bowl XLIV.

The Packers, despite this heartbreaking loss, would return to the playoffs in subsequent seasons, eventually winning Super Bowl XLV following the 2010 season.

## Critical reception

The game was widely praised by players, coaches, and media as one of the greatest playoff games in NFL history.  Cardinals coach Ken Whisenhunt called it "probably one of the best games ever played in the playoffs."

Warner himself acknowledged the game's significance, saying at his postgame press conference: "Whew, anybody else tired?"  He later described it as "the greatest offensive playoff game in the history of the NFL."

Some media outlets criticized the officiating in the game, with Bleacher Report noting "a far-from-elite performance by the officiating crew." [12] However, specific controversial calls were not detailed in the available sources.

Mike Carey served as the referee for this game. [13]

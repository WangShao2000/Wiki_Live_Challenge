'''The Well''' is the third episode of the fifteenth series (also known as "Season 2") of the British science fiction television series Doctor Who. It was broadcast on 26 April 2025 on BBC One and BBC iPlayer, and stars Ncuti Gatwa as the Fifteenth Doctor and Varada Sethu as Belinda Chandra . The episode was written by Russell T Davies and Sharma Angel-Walfall, directed by Amanda Brotchie, and serves as a sequel to the 2008 David Tennant episode "Midnight" [1].

Set 500,000 years in the future, the episode follows the Doctor and Belinda as they become involved in a military mission to investigate a mining colony on Planet 6-7-6-7 that has gone silent. The only survivor is Aliss (Rose Ayling-Ellis), a deaf canteen worker who has a mysterious entity attached to her . The episode is notable for its horror elements, claustrophobic atmosphere, and its connection to the popular "Midnight" episode [2].

## Plot

The episode begins with the TARDIS materializing on a spaceship, where the Doctor and Belinda are immediately caught up in a military operation led by Troop Leader Shaya Costallion (Caoilfhionn Dunne). The soldiers are investigating why communications have ceased from a mining colony on Planet 6-7-6-7 . Upon arrival, they discover the colony in ruins with only one survivor - Aliss Fenly (Rose Ayling-Ellis), a deaf canteen worker .

Aliss initially claims that the crew went mad and killed each other, but it soon becomes apparent that something unseen is at work. The entity, listed in the credits as "It Has No Name" and played by Paul Kasey, attaches itself to Aliss's back [3]. Anyone who walks behind the entity while being observed by another person is violently thrown and killed [3]. This creates a terrifying scenario where the soldiers must constantly monitor each other's positions .

The Doctor eventually recognizes the planet as formerly being called "Midnight," and identifies the entity as the same creature he encountered in 2008 during his tenth incarnation . In the original "Midnight" episode, the creature's primary ability was to force people to repeat what others said, but in "The Well," it has developed new behaviors [3].

As the tension escalates and the group struggles to find a way to defeat the entity, several soldiers are killed. The Doctor devises a plan to make the creature "be behind itself" by using a complex arrangement of mirrors and reflections, causing it to attack itself . However, this plan is complicated when the entity transfers to Belinda .

In a dramatic sacrifice, Shaya kills Belinda (who is then revived using advanced medical technology) to transfer the entity to herself, allowing the others to escape. She then jumps into a deep well on the planet, apparently destroying the creature . However, the episode's closing scene suggests that the entity may have survived and attached itself to one of the other soldiers, as Trooper Mo (Bethany Antonia) is seen with what appears to be the creature behind her .

## Production

### Development

"The Well" was developed as a sequel to the popular 2008 episode "Midnight," which was written by Russell T Davies and directed by Alice Troughton [1]. The original "Midnight" episode is widely regarded as one of the best episodes of the modern series, known for its claustrophobic tension and the Doctor's helplessness against an unseen foe . Davies had expressed interest in revisiting the Midnight entity for many years, and finally developed "The Well" as part of his return to the series .

The episode was written by Russell T Davies in collaboration with newcomer Sharma Angel-Walfall, marking her first credit on Doctor Who . The story was developed to maintain the horror elements of "Midnight" while introducing new concepts and expanding the mythology of the creature [3].

### Casting

The episode features Ncuti Gatwa as the Fifteenth Doctor and Varada Sethu as Belinda Chandra, the Doctor's new companion . The guest cast includes Rose Ayling-Ellis as Aliss Fenly, the deaf survivor of the mining colony . Ayling-Ellis, who is deaf in real life, brings authenticity to the role and the episode explores themes of communication and disability .

The military unit is led by Caoilfhionn Dunne as Shaya Costallion, the troop leader described as "refreshingly for Doctor Who, presented as entirely competent and willing to hear the Doctor and Belinda out" . The supporting cast includes Christopher Chung as Cassio Palin-Paleen, Bethany Antonia as Mo Gilliben, and Gary Pillai as Albie Bethick .

Notable guest stars also include Anita Dobson as Mrs. Flood, who appears in a brief cameo at the end of the episode disguised as a space mining corporation leader . Mrs. Flood is a recurring character in the series who has connections to the season's overarching storyline regarding the disappearance of Earth .

### Filming

Filming for "The Well" took place in early 2024, with location work at beachfields quarry in Wales on 2 and 3 January 2024 [4]. The quarry provided the desolate, alien environment necessary for the mining colony setting. The episode's cold, atmospheric setting was achieved through night shoots in the Welsh quarry, which added to the episode's horror elements .

Studio work was conducted at Bad Wolf Studios in Cardiff Bay, which has been the primary production facility for Doctor Who since the show's return in 2023 [5]. The claustrophobic mining colony interiors were created using detailed sets that emphasized the confined spaces and industrial environment .

The special effects for the invisible entity were created through a combination of practical effects and visual effects work. The creature's presence is suggested through subtle movements, reflections, and the reactions of the cast . The "throw" effect when characters are killed was achieved through a combination of stunt work and visual effects [6].

## Reception

### Broadcast and Ratings

"The Well" was broadcast on Saturday, 26 April 2025, on BBC One at 7:20 PM and was available on BBC iPlayer from 8:00 AM that morning [7]. The episode received an overnight rating of 1.9 million viewers, which was a significant improvement from the previous episode "Lux," which had drawn only 1.58 million viewers and marked the lowest overnight viewership ratings in the show's history [8].

The seven-day consolidated viewing figures showed "The Well" attracted 3.23 million viewers, according to BARB (Broadcasters' Audience Research Board) data [9]. This represented a substantial increase from the overnight figures, with the iPlayer catch-up viewing accounting for a significant portion of the total audience [10].

### Critical Reception

Critical reception of "The Well" was generally positive, with many reviewers praising its horror elements, atmosphere, and performances [2]. GamesRadar+ described the episode as "Doctor Who at its most claustrophobic. Dark and scary, fans will likely love this one for its slower pace and oppressive atmosphere" . The review particularly praised Rose Ayling-Ellis's performance, noting she "strikes the right balance between empathetic and just a little bit eerie" .

Monkeys Fighting Robots gave the episode a 4.3 out of 5 rating, calling it "one of the best Doctor Who episodes in years" . The review praised the episode's mature approach, stating it was "the most mature episode Doctor Who has produced in recent years since the humor and colors were dialed down, and the fear factor due to the characters sensing a presence" [2].

However, not all reviews were entirely positive. Houston Press was more critical, particularly of the monster's new abilities, stating: "Rather than copying someone's speech patterns, it throws people violently if they walk behind Aliss while someone else is looking at her from the front. This is, frankly, stupid. The effect of them flying through the air is more comical than scary" [6].

The Outerhaven gave the episode a 3.5 out of 5 rating, noting both positive and negative aspects. While praising the tension and atmosphere, they criticized the rushed ending and the lack of explanation for the creature's changed behavior: "The fact that it SOMEHOW got to the ship was scary in a certain way...but it made the sacrifice worthless, which made the ending feel truly hollow" .

### Legacy and Impact

"The Well" has had a significant impact on the Doctor Who fanbase, particularly for its connection to "Midnight." Many fans had been requesting a return to the Midnight entity for years, and the episode generated considerable discussion and debate . The revelation that the creature had survived and evolved added new depth to the Doctor Who mythology .

The episode also advanced the season's overarching storyline regarding the disappearance of Earth. Through dialogue with the soldiers, the Doctor and Belinda discover that Earth and humanity are no longer known in the far future, suggesting something catastrophic has occurred .

The character of Aliss, played by Rose Ayling-Ellis, was particularly notable for her deafness and the way it was incorporated into the storyline. The episode featured innovative communication technology that displayed dialogue as floating speech bubbles, which was praised for its representation of disability .

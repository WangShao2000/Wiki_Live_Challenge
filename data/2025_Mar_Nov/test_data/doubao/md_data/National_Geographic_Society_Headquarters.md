# National Geographic Society Headquarters

The **National Geographic Society Headquarters** is a historic complex of buildings located at **1145 17th Street NW** and **1156 16th Street NW** in Washington, D.C., United States [1]. The headquarters serves as the administrative center of the National Geographic Society, a scientific and educational nonprofit institution founded in 1888 . The complex consists of multiple buildings constructed in phases beginning in 1903, representing various architectural styles from the early 20th century to the present . The headquarters has achieved recognition for its sustainable design practices and is listed on the National Register of Historic Places [2].

## History

### Early Development (1903-1940s)

The National Geographic Society was founded on January 27, 1888, by 33 explorers and scientists who gathered at the Cosmos Club in Washington, D.C. [3]. Initially operating from rented quarters, the society moved to its permanent headquarters in September 1903 [4]. The original building, **Hubbard Memorial Hall**, was constructed in 1902-1903 and designed by the architectural firm Hornblower & Marshall in the neoclassical style [5]. This building was a gift from the Hubbard and Bell families, with Gardiner Greene Hubbard serving as the society's first president and Alexander Graham Bell (his son-in-law) as his successor [4].

The complex expanded southward in 1931 with the addition of an administration building designed by Arthur B. Heaton [6]. This expansion was necessitated by the growing popularity of the National Geographic Magazine and the society's expanding operations [6]. The administration building featured architectural details including stone string courses and was constructed using traditional materials and techniques of the period [7].

### Mid-20th Century Expansion (1940s-1960s)

The headquarters continued to grow throughout the mid-20th century with the addition of an editors' wing in 1948 . The most significant architectural addition came in 1963-1964 with the construction of a **10-story office building** designed by internationally prominent architect Edward Durell Stone [8]. Stone's design employed the **new formalism style**, a modern architectural approach that incorporated classical elements to create a building that "echoed the society's architectural history" while maintaining contemporary functionality [9].

The Stone building featured reinforced concrete structure with white marble cladding, creating a distinctive appearance on 16th Street . The building's design included a hipped roof with terracotta tiles, large arched windows, and a stone surround with a cross setting that reads "National Geographic Society" . This structure became an iconic landmark on 16th Street, recognized for its architectural significance and association with the prestigious scientific organization .

### Late 20th Century Developments (1980s)

In 1981, the headquarters underwent another major expansion with the addition of a **multifunctional building** designed by the architectural firm Skidmore, Owings & Merrill (SOM) [8]. This tiered building contained a 400-seat theater and was designed to complement the existing structures while providing modern amenities for the society's growing educational and public outreach programs .

The architectural firm worked with landscape architect James Urban to create a publicly accessible outdoor plaza adjacent to the new building . In 1984, the plaza was enhanced with the installation of **MARABAR**, a significant sculptural work commissioned by National Geographic with guidance from SOM's David Childs and designed by artist Elyn Zimmerman . This installation consisted of twelve granite boulders, some polished to a reflective surface, arranged around a rectangular reflecting pool measuring 6 by 60 feet [10].

### Recent History and Renovations (2000s-2020s)

In 2004, the National Geographic Society headquarters achieved a significant environmental milestone by becoming one of the first buildings to receive a "Green" certification from Global Green USA [2]. This recognition acknowledged the society's efforts to support a sustainable and secure world through its building operations [2]. The headquarters later became the first building to achieve a **LEED-EB (Leadership in Energy and Environmental Design for Existing Buildings)** rating [11].

In November 2017, the society announced plans to remove the MARABAR sculpture to accommodate a plaza redesign . This decision sparked controversy in the architectural and cultural communities, with many prominent figures, including Whitney Museum director Adam Weinberg, writing letters to the D.C. Historic Preservation Review Board opposing the removal . After a year-long campaign by the Cultural Landscape Foundation, the society agreed to relocate the sculpture at its expense under Zimmerman's direction . The sculpture was dismantled in 2022 and reconfigured at American University, where it was rededicated on April 4, 2023, under the new name **Sudama** .

In 2022, the National Geographic Society headquarters received official landmark designation from the D.C. Historic Preservation Review Board . The designation covered the entire complex along M Street NW between 16th and 17th Streets, recognizing its architectural and cultural significance .

## Architecture and Design

### Architectural Styles and Buildings

The National Geographic Society Headquarters complex represents a **chronological survey of 20th-century American architecture** . The oldest building, Hubbard Memorial Hall (1902-1903), exemplifies the neoclassical style with its symmetrical facade, classical columns, and detailed stonework [5]. The 1931 administration building by Arthur B. Heaton reflects the architectural trends of the early 20th century with its formal design and traditional materials [12].

The **Edward Durell Stone building** (1963-1964) represents the new formalism movement, which emerged as a reaction against the perceived austerity of pure modernism [9]. Stone's design incorporated classical elements such as arched windows and decorative stonework while maintaining the clean lines and functionality of modern architecture . The building's white marble cladding and distinctive hipped roof with terracotta tiles create a memorable presence on 16th Street .

The **Skidmore, Owings & Merrill building** (1981) introduces a postmodernist aesthetic with its tiered design and playful use of architectural elements [8]. The building's stepped terraces and colonnade create a dynamic relationship with the surrounding plaza and provide shaded outdoor spaces .

### Plaza and Landscape Design

The headquarters encloses an **elongated plaza** framed by buildings that reflect various 20th-century architectural styles . The plaza design minimizes the separation between pedestrian and vehicular access through a looping drop-off area bordered by a low-curbed sidewalk . A planted median runs through the center of the plaza, containing a staircase to an underground garage and featuring oak, holly, and boxelder trees .

The plaza's design creates a transition from the busy urban streetscape to a sheltered, well-shaded garden space that eventually leads to an alley exiting onto Sumner Row . The repetition of hedges along the exhibition hall's colonnade provides a sense of enclosure while maintaining visual connection to the surrounding buildings .

### MARABAR/Sudama Sculpture

The **MARABAR sculpture** (1984) was a significant work of environmental art that integrated natural and man-made elements . The installation consisted of twelve granite boulders, with three featuring highly polished and reflective surfaces that mirrored one another across the water . The boulders were strategically placed throughout the plaza's gardens, with some sinking slightly below the pavement to create the illusion that the natural elements predated the man-made construction .

The rectangular reflecting pool (6 x 60 feet) served as the sculpture's main element, creating a serene water feature that contrasted with the massive granite forms [10]. The name "Marabar" was derived from E.M. Forster's novel *A Passage to India*, referencing the Marabar Caves described in the book .

After relocation to American University in 2023, the sculpture was renamed **Sudama**, also from Forster's novel . Zimmerman reconfigured the boulders to better fit the new site on a hill near the university's Kay Spiritual Life Center, alongside cherry trees and daffodils .

## Sustainability Features

### Environmental Achievements

The National Geographic Society has demonstrated a **long-standing commitment to sustainable design and operations** at its headquarters [2]. In 2004, the headquarters became one of the first buildings to receive Green certification from Global Green USA, recognizing its efforts to support a sustainable and secure world [2]. This certification was particularly significant as it came during an early period of environmental building recognition .

The headquarters achieved another milestone by becoming the **first building to achieve a LEED-EB (Leadership in Energy and Environmental Design for Existing Buildings) rating** [11]. The LEED-EB criteria covers building operations and systems upgrades in existing buildings where the majority of interior or exterior surfaces remain unchanged [13]. The certification addressed whole-building cleaning and maintenance issues, including chemical use, and recognized the society's comprehensive approach to environmental stewardship [13].

### Current and Future Sustainability Initiatives

The ongoing **Museum of Exploration project** represents the society's continued commitment to sustainable design . The project is targeting **LEED Platinum certification**, the highest level of LEED recognition, when it opens in 2026 [14]. This certification would place the National Geographic Society Headquarters among the most environmentally advanced buildings in the United States.

The new construction includes several innovative sustainability features:



* A **45,000-gallon cistern** located in the lowest parking level, designed to collect rainwater for irrigation and graywater use

* **Solar panels** that will provide approximately one-quarter of the campus's power needs [15]

* Recycling materials from the existing building during renovation [15]

* Energy-efficient lighting and mechanical systems designed to minimize environmental impact

The project team has worked to incorporate National Geographic's high standards for sustainability into every aspect of the design, from materials selection to operational systems [14].

## Current Facilities and Operations

### Museum of Exploration

The **National Geographic Museum of Exploration** is currently under construction and scheduled to open in 2026 . The museum represents a major transformation of the headquarters campus, uniting four historic buildings through a new pavilion and courtyard . The project is being led by general contractor HITT Contracting, with architectural design by Hickok Cole .

The new museum will feature:



* Expanded exhibition spaces showcasing engaging exhibitions, iconic photography, and world-famous artifacts

* An education center designed to be an integral part of school trips to Washington, D.C.

* A renovated theater with cutting-edge technologies and storytelling tools woven into the architecture

* A new Explorers Bar & Grill where visitors can connect and share their experiences

The museum's centerpiece is a **droplet-shaped ceiling** at the heart of the new pavilion . This ceiling design, representative of a water droplet and its ripples, allows natural light to flood the north-facing space through the Oculus skylight . The design creates a dramatic architectural statement while providing functional lighting for the exhibition spaces .

### Base Camp Concept

The National Geographic Society has reimagined its headquarters as **Base Camp**, a place where visitors can experience stunning imagery and photography, bold storytelling, and learn about rich discoveries and expeditions of the past . This concept reflects the society's mission to inspire people to care about the planet through science, exploration, education, and storytelling .

The Base Camp concept encompasses:



* Interactive exhibits that engage visitors of all ages

* Educational programs focused on the society's six key areas: Ocean, Land, Wildlife, Human Histories & Cultures, Planetary Health, and Space

* A hub for National Geographic's global community of explorers, scientists, and storytellers

* Event spaces available for private and public gatherings

## Significance and Legacy

### Cultural Importance

The National Geographic Society Headquarters has served as a **cultural and educational landmark** in Washington, D.C. for over a century [17]. The complex has hosted numerous significant events, including memorial services honoring prominent figures such as Rosa Parks, Blanche Bruce (the first African-American U.S. Senator), and Frederick Douglass [17]. These events highlight the headquarters' role as a venue for commemorating important figures in American history and culture [17].

The headquarters has also been the site of numerous groundbreaking exhibitions and educational programs that have inspired millions of visitors [18]. The National Geographic Museum, managed by the society, offers changing photographic exhibitions that showcase the work of explorers, photographers, and scientists [18]. These exhibitions provide a unique window into the world of exploration and discovery [18].

### Architectural Significance

The headquarters complex is recognized for its **architectural diversity and historical value** . The collection of buildings from different periods represents significant movements in American architecture, from neoclassical to modern to postmodernist styles . This diversity makes the complex a valuable resource for architectural education and study .

The Edward Durell Stone building, in particular, is considered a **memorable example of the new formalism style** . Stone was an early pioneer of this movement, which sought to combine modern functionality with classical aesthetic elements [9]. By 1958, Stone was one of the best-known architects in America, and his work on the National Geographic headquarters represents a significant achievement in his career [9].

### Environmental Leadership

The National Geographic Society Headquarters has established itself as a **leader in sustainable building practices** [11]. Its achievements in environmental certification, including the first LEED-EB rating and early Green certification, have influenced building standards and practices worldwide [11].

The society's commitment to sustainability extends beyond its own operations to influence broader building industry practices. The planned LEED Platinum certification for the Museum of Exploration project will further cement the headquarters' position as an environmental leader [14].

### Historic Designation

The National Geographic Society Headquarters was officially designated as a **D.C. historic landmark** in March 2022 . The designation was unanimously approved by the D.C. Historic Preservation Review Board, which accepted nominations from both the D.C. Public Library and the National Geographic Society .

The complex is also listed on the **National Register of Historic Places** [19]. The National Register listing, with reference number SG100009025, covers both addresses at 1156 16th Street NW and 1145 17th Street NW [19]. This dual listing at the local and national levels recognizes the headquarters' significance in American architectural and cultural history [17].

## Future Developments

### Museum of Exploration (2026)

The **Museum of Exploration** represents the most significant transformation of the National Geographic Society Headquarters since the 1981 addition . The project, scheduled for completion in 2026, will unite four historic buildings through innovative new construction while maintaining their historic character .

Key features of the new museum include:



* A new public entrance at 16th and M Streets NW

* A central pavilion with the distinctive droplet-shaped ceiling and Oculus skylight

* A courtyard designed to host projection shows and other outdoor events

* State-of-the-art exhibition spaces designed to showcase National Geographic's collections and expeditions

The project has been designed to maintain the campus's operations throughout construction, with the society never closing during the renovation period . This operational continuity required careful coordination and the development of over 500 detailed methods of procedure (MOPs) to ensure construction activities did not disrupt daily operations .

### Campus Modernization

The **Base Camp reimagining** represents a comprehensive modernization of the National Geographic Society Headquarters campus . This transformation goes beyond physical construction to encompass new technologies, educational approaches, and visitor experiences .

The modernization includes:



* Upgraded infrastructure to support digital technologies and multimedia presentations

* Enhanced accessibility features to accommodate visitors with disabilities

* Flexible event spaces that can adapt to various programming needs

* Improved circulation patterns to enhance the visitor experience

The project team has worked to ensure that the modernization respects the historic character of the existing buildings while introducing contemporary amenities and technologies .

# 2025 Pennsylvania Supreme Court election

The **2025 Pennsylvania Supreme Court election** was a set of retention elections held on **November 4, 2025** [1], in which voters decided whether to grant an additional 10-year term to three incumbent justices: **Christine Donohue**, **Kevin Dougherty**, and **David Wecht** [2]. All three justices were initially elected as Democrats in 2015 [3], and their retention would maintain the court's 5-2 Democratic majority [4].

## Background and significance

### Court composition and election context

The Pennsylvania Supreme Court consists of **seven justices** who are elected in partisan, statewide elections and serve 10-year terms [5]. Justices may serve unlimited terms but must retire at age 75 [5]. After their initial partisan election, justices face nonpartisan retention elections where voters decide whether to grant them additional terms .

Prior to the 2025 election, the court had a **5-2 Democratic majority** [4], which had been maintained since 2015 when Donohue, Dougherty, and Wecht were elected in a Democratic sweep [3]. The three justices up for retention in 2025 were all elected as Democrats in 2015 [3].

The timing of the 2025 retention elections was significant because **Justice Christine Donohue** would reach the mandatory retirement age of 75 in 2027 [6]. This meant that regardless of the 2025 election outcome, at least one Supreme Court seat would be on the ballot in 2027 . Republicans viewed the 2025 election as their "best opportunity to break the firm 5-2 Democratic majority on the court" [7].

### Issues at stake

The election was described as one of the "most important 2025 elections" [8] because the Supreme Court has authority over several critical areas:

**Abortion rights**: Following the 2022 overturning of Roe v. Wade by the U.S. Supreme Court, the Pennsylvania Supreme Court became "the last line of defense for reproductive rights" in the state [9]. The court had already ruled that abortion access is a right under the state constitution [4]. Under current state law, abortion is legal up to 24 weeks of pregnancy [4].

**Voting rights and redistricting**: The court has issued rulings on voting procedures, mail-in ballots, and congressional redistricting [8]. In 2018, all three justices concurred in a ruling to overturn the state's congressional map as an unconstitutional gerrymander .

**Other significant areas**: The court also handles cases related to education funding, state disaster declarations, medical malpractice lawsuits, and workers' rights .

### Historical precedent

Retention elections in Pennsylvania are typically **low-interest, low-spending affairs** that favor incumbents . Since 1968, only one statewide judge has lost a retention election - Supreme Court Justice Russell Nigro in 2005 . The 2005 loss occurred during a politically tumultuous year following controversial decisions on gambling legislation and a pay raise for legislators and judges .

## Candidates

### Christine Donohue

**Christine Donohue** (born 1952) was seeking retention for Seat 2 of the Pennsylvania Supreme Court . She received her B.A. from East Stroudsburg University in 1974 and J.D. from Duquesne University School of Law in 1980 [10].

Donohue's professional background includes:



* Private law firm practice manager, commercial litigation department [11]

* Service as a judge on the Pennsylvania Court of Judicial Discipline [11]

* Chair and member of the Pennsylvania Board of Law Examiners [11]

* Chair and member of the Pennsylvania Judicial Conduct Board [11]

* Service on the Superior Court of Pennsylvania from 2008 to 2015 [12]

Since her election to the Supreme Court in 2015, Donohue has been the primary author on several high-profile decisions, including a 2022 ruling related to congressional redistricting .

### Kevin Dougherty

**Kevin M. Dougherty** (born May 19, 1962) was seeking retention for Seat 4 . He earned his B.A. from Temple University in 1985 and J.D. from Antioch School of Law in 1987 [13].

Dougherty's career path includes:



* Assistant District Attorney in Philadelphia County from 1990 to 1995 [13]

* Private practice before and after his district attorney service [14]

* Election to the Philadelphia County Court of Common Pleas in 2001 and retention in 2011 [14]

* Service as a special master in the Philadelphia Family Court truancy program from 1998 to 2000 [15]

During the retention campaign, Dougherty highlighted his experience as a prosecutor and trial judge . He has notable family connections: his brother John Dougherty was head of Local 98 of the International Brotherhood of Electrical Workers for decades before being sentenced to prison for corruption, and his son Sean Dougherty serves in the Pennsylvania House of Representatives .

### David Wecht

**David N. Wecht** (born May 20, 1962) was seeking retention for Seat 3 . He is a graduate of Yale University and Yale Law School (J.D., 1987) [16].

Wecht's professional journey includes:



* Service as register of wills and clerk of the Orphans Court of Allegheny County

* Election to the Allegheny County Court of Common Pleas

* Election to the Superior Court of Pennsylvania in 2011

* Professor of criminal justice and law in Israel, China, and Pittsburgh [17]

Wecht is the son of renowned forensic pathologist Dr. Cyril Wecht [18]. As a Supreme Court justice, he authored a contentious 2020 ruling upholding Governor Tom Wolf's pandemic powers, in which he argued that the Pennsylvania Constitution does not empower the legislature to unilaterally suspend the state's disaster law .

## Campaign process

### Republican strategy and messaging

Republicans and their allies adopted a straightforward strategy: **"Vote No"** . They framed the retention elections as an opportunity to "term limit the court" and "defend our democracy" . Conservative groups portrayed the three justices as too liberal and highlighted several controversial decisions:



* The court's 2021 ruling overturning Bill Cosby's sexual assault conviction on a technicality

* A 2022 ruling that loosened restrictions on where plaintiffs can file medical malpractice lawsuits

* Decisions related to gig workers' rights that opened the door to gig workers becoming full employees

A particularly controversial mailer from a group connected to billionaire Jeffrey Yass falsely claimed that the Supreme Court had "gerrymandered our congressional districts to help Democrats win," when in fact the court had ruled against the Republican-drawn map [19].

### Democratic response and defense

Democrats and their allies emphasized the importance of maintaining the court's independence and protecting key rights. Their messaging focused on:

**Abortion rights**: The Supreme Court had already ruled that abortion access is a right under the state constitution [4]. Democrats warned that losing any justice could threaten this protection [9].

**Judicial independence**: Democrats framed the Republican "Vote No" campaign as an attack on the judiciary's independence . Governor Josh Shapiro appeared in television ads urging voters to vote "yes" [20].

**Key rulings**: Supporters highlighted the court's decisions protecting mail-in voting, striking down gerrymandered maps, and upholding the state's disaster declaration during the COVID-19 pandemic .

### Campaign spending

The 2025 Supreme Court retention elections shattered previous spending records for Pennsylvania judicial elections:

**Jeffrey Yass**, Pennsylvania's richest person with an estimated net worth of $65.7 billion, was a major funder of anti-retention efforts <reference type="end" id=67>. His network contributed at least $1.25 million to Students First PAC [24]. The Democratic Legislative Campaign Committee and Planned Parenthood Action Fund also invested heavily, with Planned Parenthood planning to spend six figures on mailer campaigns [25].

### Notable campaign events



* September 30, 2025: The three justices appeared together in their first joint television advertisement titled "Team" [26]

* October 7, 2025: Governor Josh Shapiro appeared in a campaign advertisement urging voters to vote "yes" [20]

* October 24, 2025: About 50 progressive activists protested outside Susquehanna International Group headquarters, calling Yass "Pennsylvania's very own Elon Musk" [24]

## Election results

### Overall results

All three Democratic justices won retention by comfortable margins, with "yes" votes ranging from approximately **61.8% to 64.8%** [27]:



| Justice           | Seat | Yes Votes | Yes %  | No Votes  | No %   | Total Votes |
| ----------------- | ---- | --------- | ------ | --------- | ------ | ----------- |
| Christine Donohue | 2    | 2,226,602 | 61.85% | 1,373,299 | 38.15% | 3,599,901   |
| Kevin Dougherty   | 4    | 2,222,976 | 61.8%  | 1,376,502 | 38.2%  | 3,599,478   |
| David Wecht       | 3    | 2,231,527 | 61.80% | 1,379,599 | 38.20% | 3,611,126   |

The Associated Press called all three races at 9:53 p.m. on election night .

### Geographic distribution

Results varied significantly by county:



* **Strong "yes" counties** (80-90%): Philadelphia, Montgomery, Delaware, Chester, and Dauphin counties

* **Moderate "yes" counties** (60-80%): Most suburban and some rural counties

* **Competitive counties** (50-60%): Some rural and small metropolitan areas

* **Strong "no" counties** (60-80%): Several rural counties in northern and western Pennsylvania

### Voter turnout and demographics

While detailed demographic breakdowns were still being analyzed after the election, observers noted increased turnout in suburban and moderate areas . Many voters reported choosing "yes" as a rebuke of what they perceived as partisan efforts to remove the justices .

The pro-retention campaign successfully built a coalition that included:



* Labor unions

* Progressive organizations

* Younger voters motivated by messaging about rights and representation

## Impact and analysis

### Immediate consequences

The retention of all three justices ensures that the Pennsylvania Supreme Court will maintain its **5-2 Democratic majority** through at least 2027 [28]. This continuity has several immediate implications:

**Policy continuity**: The court will continue to issue rulings on abortion funding and access, voting rights, gerrymandering, and other contentious issues from a progressive-leaning perspective [8].

**2026 elections**: The court may again be called upon to settle partisan battles over election laws ahead of the 2026 midterm contests for governor and U.S. House seats [29].

**Redistricting**: The status quo ensures that any attempt by the Republican-controlled legislature to push a new congressional map after the 2026 census will likely face resistance from the Democratic-majority court .

### Long-term implications

**2027 vacancy**: Justice Donohue's mandatory retirement in 2027 will create an open seat that will be filled through a partisan election [6]. This seat will be crucial for maintaining or shifting the court's ideological balance.

**Judicial independence concerns**: The highly partisan nature of the retention campaign risks eroding public trust in the court's independence . Future retention campaigns may become even more politicized.

**Case load pressure**: The court faces significant caseloads related to redistricting after the 2026 election. The justices will be under pressure to deliver timely, rigorous decisions while managing heightened public scrutiny .

### Political assessments

**Democratic perspective**: The victory provides Democrats with relief and time to focus on governance. DNC Chair Ken Martin called the results a "major win for fair elections, reproductive rights, voting rights, and democracy" [8]. The party can now treat the 2027 open seat as a strategic priority.

**Republican analysis**: Despite heavy investment and an aggressive campaign, Republicans failed to unseat any justice. Some GOP strategists privately expressed concern that their "Vote No" messaging may have backfired by galvanizing Democratic and moderate turnout .

**Historical significance**: This was the most expensive and politically charged judicial retention election in Pennsylvania history [23]. It demonstrated that even in traditionally low-profile judicial races, issues like abortion rights and election integrity can mobilize voters.

# Spore drive

The **spore drive**, formally known as the **displacement-activated spore hub drive**, is a fictional spacecraft propulsion system introduced in the 2017 television series Star Trek: Discovery . It is an organic propulsion system that utilizes mycelium spores from the fungus Prototaxites stellaviatori to facilitate near-instantaneous travel across vast distances through an intergalactic mycelial network [1].

## Origins and development

The spore drive was developed by two colleagues and friends, Paul Stamets and Justin Straal, who had been working on the concept since 2244 . Both Stamets and Straal were astromycologists who collaborated on the technology . Their research was based on the insight that at a quantum level, there was no difference between biology and physics, and specifically that spores were not only the progenitors of panspermia, but also the building blocks of energy across the universe .

The technology was jointly deployed on two vessels: the USS Glenn and the USS Discovery [1]. These ships were part of the Crossfield-class, a starship class specifically designed around the experimental drive . After war broke out between the Federation and the Klingon Empire in May 2256, the Discovery and her sister ship, the USS Glenn, were tasked by Starfleet with a top-secret mission to develop this new kind of faster-than-light drive [2].

The Glenn managed to perfect the drive through the acquisition of a creature referred to as a tardigrade, which served as a navigator through the mycelial network . However, the Glenn's crew, along with Straal, were all killed in an accident that ensued during a failed jump . Following this disaster, the Discovery acquired the tardigrade from the Glenn and its crew managed to successfully use the technology in the following months .

## Technical specifications

The spore drive represents a fundamental departure from conventional Star Trek propulsion technologies such as impulse drive and warp drive . Instead of traveling slower-than-light (via impulse engines) or faster-than-light through space (via warp drive), the spore drive enables an instantaneous "jump" from one location in space to another, disconnected place a great distance away [3].

The drive operates by utilizing mycelium spores harvested from Prototaxites stellaviatori to "jump" or "leap" across the mycelial network . During such jumps, the ships are not in normal space but in the mycelial plane . A ship utilizing spore drive exists inside the mycelial network for only a few milliseconds during a jump [4].

The technology has no theoretical speed limit. From an outside perspective, the ship disappears and reappears in another location almost instantly . The USS Glenn completed a jump of 90 light-years in 1.3 seconds, though they encountered a Hawking radiation firewall during this attempt .

Distance displacement is measured in a unit called **Speirein**, with higher Speireins being associated with larger displacement . Jumping is probabilistic, meaning that the longer the jump, the more possible outcomes there are. The technology requires extensive computational power to calculate jump trajectories, and current computers lack the processing capacity to make the requisite number of calculations for long jumps, resulting in navigational instability . Some six months after the start of the war, the Discovery had reached Speirein 12, enabling jumps measured in the hundreds of kilometers .

The spore drive's operation involves several key components and processes:



1. **Reaction cube**: The chamber in which spores are injected, with one possible density being 68%

2. **Spore germination rate**: A critical factor in drive operation

3. **Excess energy cavitation**: Part of the initiation process [5]

4. **Specific deactivation sequence**: Required to prevent the induction coil from becoming saturated [5]

All personnel working around the spore drive are inoculated as a safety precaution [5]. A level 3 diagnostic is run on the entire spore drive every ten hours .

## Navigation and limitations

The spore drive's navigation system initially relied on the tardigrade's natural connection to the mycelial network. When the tardigrade began dying, Stamets conducted an illegal genetic augmentation on himself using tardigrade DNA to take over the navigator role personally .

The drive's stamina is not limited by the technology itself but by the endurance of the "pilot" navigating the mycelial network [6]. Each jump requires the pilot to "feel" their way through the network. While a few jumps in succession are not difficult, the USS Discovery's record of 133 jumps in rapid succession severely strained Stamets .

Potential risks associated with the spore drive include:



* **Unintended interdimensional travel**: The drive can potentially access alternate universes, as demonstrated by the Discovery's jump to the Mirror Universe

* **Time travel**: The mycelial network exists outside normal space-time, creating temporal paradox risks

* **Network pollution**: Improper use can contaminate the mycelial network

* **Partial jumps**: A partial jump on a predetermined course with no set end point could leave the ship half in, half out of the network, essentially functioning as a "doorstop" [4]

## Program history

Following the conclusion of the Federation-Klingon War in 2257, Starfleet decommissioned the drive until a non-human interface could be developed . The technology was classified with the disappearance of the Discovery .

In 2258, Captain Christopher Pike authorized the use of the drive to travel 51,450 light years to the planet Terralysium to investigate one of the red bursts, believing the crisis worthy of special dispensation .

In 3189, during the refitting of the Discovery, a new and improved spore drive interface was installed . Instead of mechanically connecting to the pilot via cybernetic shunts, the pilot merely had to rest their hands on nanogel control pads, with the gel acting as a quantum transducer . The ship's nacelles had to be attached before the drive could be activated .

A second-generation spore drive was developed with a prototype able to be quickly and easily installed into any ship . It still required a compatible pilot .

## Reception and cultural impact

The introduction of the spore drive generated significant debate among Star Trek fans and critics . Some viewers considered it a bold new direction for the series, while others felt it strayed too far from established Star Trek lore [7].

Critics of the technology pointed to several issues:



1. **Lack of prior mention**: The spore drive had never been referenced in any previous Star Trek series or films, despite being a potentially game-changing technology that could essentially teleport ships across the galaxy .

2. **Departure from established physics**: The concept of using spores to achieve instantaneous travel seemed too far removed from the more grounded scientific principles that had traditionally underpinned Star Trek's warp drive technology .

3. **Impact on narrative tension**: Some viewers felt that the drive's near-instantaneous travel capabilities undermined the traditional Star Trek concept of exploration and the challenges of deep space travel .

However, supporters of the technology appreciated:



1. **Scientific innovation**: The drive represented an imaginative expansion of Star Trek's scientific universe, incorporating concepts from mycology and quantum biology [8].

2. **Character development opportunities**: The drive's development and operation provided rich narrative material for character arcs, particularly for Paul Stamets .

3. **Plot possibilities**: The drive enabled new storytelling opportunities, including interdimensional travel and time travel narratives .

The technology has been described as "far from the most ridiculous piece of science that Trek has done, but it gets a lot more flak because, rather than being something odd that happened in one episode, it became a recurring and integral part of the show" .

## Later developments

In the 33rd century, the crew used the spore drive one last time to send Discovery—returned to its pre-refit configuration—into deep space on a Red Directive mission where the ship would wait for nearly a thousand years for Craft .

In the 25th century, the Elachi make limited, but technically advanced use of spore drives on some of their ships due to their origin in the Mycelial network .

Considering the inherent risks of the spore drive—unintended interdimensional travel, time travel, and the risk to the navigator—the experimental drive has fallen out of favor with Starfleet . No current vessels in the Federation make use of it . With the continuing advancement of bio-neural technology, this may eventually change, but for the time being, Starfleet's engineering divisions remain focused on the development of Quantum Slipstream Drives .

## Legacy

The spore drive represents a significant innovation in Star Trek's fictional technology, introducing concepts of organic propulsion and quantum mycology that expanded the franchise's scientific universe [8]. While controversial among fans, it 无疑推动了 Star Trek's narrative in new directions and provided memorable storylines involving interdimensional travel, time travel, and the ethical implications of advanced technology.

The technology's development also highlighted important themes about the relationship between science and ethics, as seen in Stamets' decision to undergo genetic augmentation to operate the drive . The spore drive's ultimate decommissioning due to its inherent risks serves as a cautionary tale about the potential dangers of technology that advances faster than our ability to understand and control it .

Despite its controversial reception, the spore drive remains a significant part of Star Trek: Discovery's legacy and a notable innovation in the franchise's technological mythology.

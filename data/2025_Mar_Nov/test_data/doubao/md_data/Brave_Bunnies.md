# Brave Bunnies

## 1. Introduction

**Brave Bunnies** is a Ukrainian-Spanish co-produced animated preschool series that follows the adventures of a family of rabbits traveling in their distinctive Bunny Bus [1]. Created by Olga Cherepanova and developed by Tim Fehrenbach, the series consists of 52 episodes, each running 7 minutes in length [2]. The show premiered in Ukraine in 2020 and internationally in 2021, distributed globally by Aardman Animations [3].

The series centers on two main characters, Bop and Boo, bunny siblings who embark on adventures with their parents and four younger siblings, the Bunny Babies [1]. Through their travels across diverse environments including jungles, savannas, forests, plains, and mountains, the Brave Bunnies encounter various animal friends, promoting themes of diversity, inclusion, and friendship [4].

Brave Bunnies has achieved significant international success, airing in over 80 countries across Latin America, Canada, the United Kingdom, Europe, the Nordics, the Middle East, Asia, and Australia [5]. The show has been particularly noted for its educational value in teaching young children about acceptance and understanding of differences .

## 2. Series Overview

### 2.1 Production Background

Brave Bunnies represents the first co-production between Ukraine and Spain, developed through a partnership between Ukrainian studio Glowberry and Spanish animation studio Ánima [2]. The series was created by Olga Cherepanova, who serves as both co-founder of Glowberry and creative producer on the project [6]. Cherepanova has stated that her goal was to create "a series that would encourage children to be curious about the world around them, and to not only respect but celebrate its diversity" [6].

The series development faced significant challenges following the Russian invasion of Ukraine in 2022, which disrupted production and forced many Glowberry employees to flee Kyiv for safety reasons . In response to these circumstances, WildBrain partnered with Glowberry and Ánima to ensure the continuation of the series . Under this new arrangement, WildBrain became the majority stakeholder in the Brave Bunnies intellectual property, while Glowberry and Ánima retained minority positions .

WildBrain's Vancouver animation studio co-produced Season 2 with Glowberry and Ánima, with delivery expected to begin in June 2023 . The partnership has enabled the series to maintain its international distribution while supporting the Ukrainian creative team during challenging circumstances .

### 2.2 Format and Structure

Brave Bunnies is a 2D animated preschool series designed for children aged 2-5 . The show follows a consistent format where each 7-minute episode begins with the Bunny Bus traveling to a new location . At each stop, the main characters Bop and Boo encounter different animals and create new games to help build friendships .

The series is narrated by Chetna Pandya and features a voice cast including Wilbur Conabeare as Bop, Margot Powell as Boo, Harriet Kershaw as Bunny Ma, and Jensen Adeyanju as Bunny Pa [7]. The music for the series was composed by Sergii Kosarenko, Denis Khrenov, and Ivan Rozin .

The technical specifications of the series include a 16:9 HD aspect ratio and Dolby Digital sound mixing . The animation style features bright, colorful designs that appeal to young children while maintaining clear visual storytelling [1].

### 2.3 Distribution and Reception

Brave Bunnies has achieved widespread international distribution across multiple platforms and territories. Initial broadcast deals secured by Aardman included partnerships with Nick Jr (UK), Super RTL (Germany), ABC (Australia), YLE (Finland), HOP! (Israel), PlusPlus (Ukraine), Daekyo (South Korea), and Milkshake! (UK) [3].

The series premiered in Ukraine on PlusPlus in 2020 and in the United Kingdom on Milkshake! in 2021 . In Canada, Family Jr. has exclusive linear broadcast rights for Season 1 . Additional distribution agreements have expanded the series' reach to include platforms in Latin America, the Middle East, Asia, and other European markets [5].

The show has received positive critical reception, particularly for its educational content and inclusive themes. At the MIPJunior content market, Brave Bunnies was noted as one of the most-viewed projects and received positive reviews from international experts [3]. Alison Taylor, Director of Distribution and Business Development at Aardman, commented on the series' strong reception at industry events, stating that buyers expressed enthusiasm for the show's positive messages and engaging characters .

In the United Kingdom, the series has reached an audience of 4.4 million viewers across Channel 5's Milkshake! and Nick Jr . The show has also been successful in Japan, where the Japanese dub premiered on TV Tokyo in October 2022 and achieved top 5 ratings for preschool series [8].

## 3. Characters and Main Themes

### 3.1 Main Characters

The Brave Bunnies family consists of several key characters who travel together in their distinctive Bunny Bus. The primary protagonists are **Bop** and **Boo**, bunny siblings who serve as the main focus of the series [1]. Bop is described as a brave and curious older brother who often takes the lead in their adventures [9]. Boo is characterized as a cute and joyful little rabbit girl who loves dancing, making new friends, and eating carrot cake [9].

The parental figures in the series are **Bunny Ma** and **Bunny Pa**. Bunny Pa serves as the driver of the Bunny Bus, taking the family on their various adventures [9]. The couple also has four younger children known collectively as the **Bunny Babies**, who accompany the family on their travels [10].

Throughout the series, the Brave Bunnies encounter numerous animal friends representing different species and backgrounds. Some of the recurring characters include Franny the Fox, Barry the Bear, Bao the Panda, Garry the Tiger, Kit the Kangaroo, and Dwayne the Moose . Each character brings unique characteristics and perspectives that help teach children about diversity and acceptance [4].

### 3.2 Educational Themes

The central educational theme of Brave Bunnies is to teach children about the diversity of the world around them and promote acceptance of differences [4]. The series emphasizes the importance of making friends with others regardless of their differences and finding common ground through play and cooperation .

Olga Cherepanova has explained that the series "shows real-life examples of how to find a unique approach to each person without pressure or changing anyone's uniqueness," which she describes as essential for healthy childhood development . The creator further stated that she "created Brave Bunnies to help children all over the world communicate with different friends without any fears or stereotypes, respecting the diversity of our world" [11].

Each episode presents situations where the Brave Bunnies must interact with animals who have different abilities, characteristics, or backgrounds. For example, in one episode, the Bunnies meet a chameleon named Kara who inspires them to explore color-changing abilities . In another, they encounter Marvin the Mole, who lives underground and is initially afraid to come above ground . These interactions demonstrate how to approach new situations with curiosity and kindness rather than fear or prejudice.

The series also promotes problem-solving skills and creative thinking as the characters must find innovative ways to play and interact with their new friends. The show's structure encourages children to ask questions and explore their world with what the series terms "bunny bravery" .

## 4. Production and Development

### 4.1 Creative Team

The creative development of Brave Bunnies was led by **Olga Cherepanova**, who serves as both the creator of the original concept and co-founder of Glowberry [6]. Cherepanova has been instrumental in shaping the series' educational approach and ensuring its consistent messaging about diversity and inclusion [11].

**Tim Fehrenbach** served as the series developer and director for the first season [2]. His work on the series has been recognized for its ability to engage young audiences while delivering educational content effectively [2].

The voice cast for Brave Bunnies includes several notable performers. **Wilbur Conabeare** provides the voice of Bop, while **Margot Powell** voices Boo [7]. **Harriet Kershaw** and **Jensen Adeyanju** respectively portray Bunny Ma and Bunny Pa [7]. The series is narrated by **Chetna Pandya**, who provides continuity and context for the episodes [7].

The music for Brave Bunnies was composed by a team including **Sergii Kosarenko**, **Denis Khrenov**, and **Ivan Rozin** . Their work creates an engaging soundtrack that enhances the series' playful and educational atmosphere.

### 4.2 Production Studios

Brave Bunnies was produced through a collaborative effort between three main studios. **Glowberry**, the Ukrainian animation studio co-founded by Olga Cherepanova, served as the lead creative studio and retained creative control over the series . Glowberry's work on the series has been recognized for its high quality animation and innovative storytelling techniques [12].

**Ánima Estudios** from Spain partnered with Glowberry as the co-production studio . The collaboration between Glowberry and Ánima represents the first co-production between Ukraine and Spain in the animation industry [2]. Ángel Molinero, CEO of Ánima Spain, has expressed enthusiasm about the partnership, noting that the company is "very excited about the future of the project" .

**WildBrain** joined the production team as a co-producer for Season 2 following the Russian invasion of Ukraine . WildBrain's Vancouver animation studio worked alongside Glowberry and Ánima to ensure the series' continuation during challenging circumstances . Under the new partnership structure, WildBrain became the majority stakeholder in the Brave Bunnies intellectual property, with Glowberry and Ánima maintaining minority positions .

The distribution of Brave Bunnies is handled by **Aardman Animations**, the British animation company known for productions such as Wallace & Gromit [2]. Aardman's involvement has been crucial in securing international broadcast deals and establishing the series in global markets .

### 4.3 Technical Aspects

Brave Bunnies is produced as a 2D animated series with a technical specification designed for modern broadcast and streaming platforms . The series features a 16:9 HD aspect ratio, ensuring compatibility with contemporary television and digital viewing formats .

The audio production uses Dolby Digital sound mixing, providing high-quality audio suitable for various viewing environments . This technical approach ensures that the series can be enjoyed across different platforms while maintaining consistent audio quality.

The animation style of Brave Bunnies features bright, colorful designs that are specifically designed to appeal to preschool-aged children [1]. The visual aesthetic combines simplicity with attention to detail, making characters and environments easily recognizable and engaging for young viewers [9].

The series' 7-minute episode format is designed to match the attention span of its target audience while delivering complete stories with clear educational messages [2]. This format has proven effective in maintaining viewer engagement while ensuring that the educational content is presented in a digestible manner.

## 5. International Success and Merchandising

### 5.1 Global Distribution

Brave Bunnies has achieved remarkable international success, with Season 1 airing in more than 80 countries across multiple continents [5]. The series' global reach spans Latin America, Canada, the United Kingdom, Europe, the Nordic countries, the Middle East, Asia, and Australia [5].

The series has been particularly successful in securing partnerships with major international broadcasters. In addition to the initial broadcast deals mentioned earlier, Brave Bunnies has expanded its reach through agreements with WarnerMedia for distribution in 20 territories across Southeast Asia, as well as Australia and New Zealand [13].

The show's success in international markets has been attributed to its universal themes of friendship and acceptance, which transcend cultural boundaries . Dan Frugtniet, managing director of Big Picture Licensing, noted that the series has "been in the works for years," but due to the crisis in Ukraine following the Russian invasion, "the series and the books take on a much more powerful meaning" .

In Japan, the series achieved particular success with its Japanese dub premiering on TV Tokyo in October 2022 [8]. The Japanese version reached the top 5 most-watched preschool series in Japan, demonstrating the show's broad international appeal [8].

### 5.2 Merchandising and Consumer Products

The Brave Bunnies franchise has expanded beyond television into a comprehensive consumer products program. **Spin Master** serves as the global master toy partner for the series, launching a toy line that includes figures, playsets, games, and plush toys [14].

Spin Master's first collection consisted of eight toys, initially available in the United Kingdom, Australia, Italy, Germany, and Spain beginning in April 2022 . The toy line was designed to expand later in the year to mirror the global distribution of the series . The company has emphasized the educational value of their products, with James Martin, senior vice president of marketing and global business unit lead for preschool at Spin Master, stating that the toys "will provide preschoolers with engaging toys that will expand their experience as they interact and explore the wonderful world and the clever and caring characters within it" .

The merchandise program has been designed with particular attention to environmental sustainability. Spin Master created specially designed packaging that can be transformed into play props, reducing waste while enhancing the play value of the products [15]. This approach aligns with the series' educational themes of creativity and resourcefulness.

In the publishing sector, Penguin Random House's Ladybird imprint has published a series of preschool books based on the television show . The first two titles, "Brave Bunnies Spring to the Rescue" and "Brave Bunnies Make a Friend," were released to coincide with the television series' broadcast . These books continue the series' educational themes while providing additional content for young fans.

The publishing program has expanded internationally with books debuting in Italy through Planeta Libre (an imprint of De Agostini), in Australia through Five Mile Publishing, and in the United Kingdom through Ladybird . This multi-territory publishing approach demonstrates the franchise's growing international recognition and commercial potential.

## 6. Reception and Legacy

### 6.1 Critical Reception

Brave Bunnies has received positive critical reception from industry professionals and educational experts. At the MIPJunior content market, the series was noted as one of the most-viewed projects and received favorable reviews from international broadcasting executives [3]. The show's performance at MIPJunior was particularly significant as it demonstrated strong international interest in the series before its official broadcast launch .

Alison Taylor, Director of Distribution and Business Development at Aardman, commented on the series' reception at industry events: "We were delighted to see Brave Bunnies feature in the top screening figures at MIPJunior, and to hear first-hand from buyers about what they love about the series during our meetings at Mipcom Online+" . She further noted that the discussions with international broadcasters gave the team confidence in the series' potential for global success .

Educational professionals have praised the series for its innovative approach to teaching diversity and inclusion to young children. The show's ability to address complex social concepts in a manner accessible to preschoolers has been highlighted as a particular strength . The series has been recommended as a valuable tool for parents and educators to facilitate discussions about differences and acceptance .

The series has also been recognized for its production quality and technical achievements. The animation style, voice acting, and musical composition have all received positive comments from industry observers [2]. The show's 7-minute format has been noted as particularly effective for maintaining young viewers' attention while delivering complete educational messages [2].

### 6.2 Impact and Cultural Significance

Beyond its entertainment value, Brave Bunnies has gained cultural significance as a Ukrainian cultural product that has maintained international distribution despite the challenges faced by the country following the Russian invasion . The series has become a symbol of Ukrainian creativity and resilience in the face of adversity .

Olga Cherepanova has expressed the importance of the series in promoting peace and understanding: "I'm proud and happy that this project born in Ukraine will now continue to spread around the world, helping the next generation develop respect for diversity and build relationships based on co-creation and cooperation rather than on fear and violence" . She further stated that the Ukrainian team hopes for peace and will "do everything possible for a peaceful tomorrow" .

The series' themes of friendship and acceptance have taken on additional significance in the context of global challenges. Francesca Dow, managing director of Penguin Random House Children's, noted that while the Ukraine crisis provides the series with "more urgency and power," the book deal was originally signed based on "the merits of the show's characters and story" .

The Brave Bunnies franchise has also contributed to Ukraine's cultural diplomacy efforts, with the series representing Ukrainian creative talent on the international stage. The show's success has helped to highlight Ukraine's capabilities in the animation industry and its contributions to global children's entertainment [12].

### 6.3 Future Development

The Brave Bunnies franchise continues to expand with the production of Season 2, which is expected to maintain the series' core themes while introducing new characters and adventures . The second season will feature the Brave Bunnies continuing their road trip around the Bunny World, encountering both old friends and new characters .

In Season 2, Bop and Boo will have "big surprises in store for small viewers as they visit their 'Best Bunny Friends' at their homes, meeting their families, exploring their unique lifestyles and finding lots of new adventures" . This expanded format will allow for deeper exploration of the series' educational themes while maintaining its engaging storytelling approach.

The partnership between WildBrain, Glowberry, and Ánima has established a strong foundation for the franchise's continued development . WildBrain's 360-degree capabilities across production, distribution, and consumer products provide opportunities for the series to expand into new markets and platforms .

The Brave Bunnies brand has also demonstrated potential for expansion into other media formats, including digital content, mobile applications, and interactive experiences. The series' strong educational foundation and engaging characters provide a platform for continued growth across multiple entertainment and educational channels .

## 7. Conclusion

Brave Bunnies represents a significant achievement in international children's animation, successfully combining entertainment value with educational content that promotes diversity, inclusion, and acceptance. Created by Olga Cherepanova and developed through a groundbreaking Ukrainian-Spanish co-production, the series has achieved remarkable global success with distribution in over 80 countries [5].

The series' core strength lies in its ability to teach complex social concepts to preschool-aged children through engaging storytelling and memorable characters. By following the adventures of Bop, Boo, and their family as they encounter diverse animal friends, Brave Bunnies effectively demonstrates the value of friendship across differences [4].

The production's resilience in the face of geopolitical challenges has added another layer of significance to the series. The partnership between WildBrain, Glowberry, and Ánima has not only ensured the continuation of the series but has also created a model for international collaboration in the animation industry .

Looking forward, Brave Bunnies appears poised for continued success and expansion. With Season 2 in production and an expanding merchandise and publishing program, the franchise demonstrates strong potential for growth across multiple platforms . The series' universal themes of friendship, curiosity, and acceptance position it well for continued international success while serving as an important educational tool for young children worldwide.

The Brave Bunnies franchise stands as a testament to the power of animation to entertain, educate, and inspire, while also serving as a bridge between cultures and promoting understanding in an increasingly interconnected world.

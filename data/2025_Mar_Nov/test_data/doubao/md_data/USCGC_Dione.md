# USCGC Dione

USCGC Dione (WPC-107) was a **Thetis-class patrol boat** (also classified as Argo-class) operated by the United States Coast Guard from 1934 to 1963 [1]. The vessel was named after Dione, a Greek titaness . Commissioned in October 1934, Dione served during World War II and the Cold War before being decommissioned in 1963 and sold for commercial service [1].

## 1. Construction and Design

### 1.1 Design and Construction

USCGC Dione was built by the Manitowoc Shipbuilding Corporation in Manitowoc, Wisconsin . Her keel was laid down on November 10, 1933, and she was launched on June 30, 1934 . The vessel was sponsored by Unita Risch and was delivered to the Coast Guard on September 28, 1934 . Dione was commissioned on October 5, 1934, departing Manitowoc the same day .

The construction cost was \$258,000 . The vessel had a building number of P-13 and hull number 277 . Initially designated as P-13 when assigned in 1934, her designation was changed to WPC-107 in 1942 . Her signal letters were NRGV .

### 1.2 Technical Specifications

USCGC Dione had a **length overall of 165 feet (50 m)**, a length between perpendiculars of 160 feet (49 m), a maximum beam of 25 feet 3 inches (7.70 m), and a beam at the waterline of 23 feet 9 inches (7.24 m) . Her draft was 7 feet 8 inches (2 m) when constructed in 1933, and her displacement was 337 long tons (342 tonnes) when fully loaded .

The vessel had a complement of 5 officers and 39 men during its initial construction . The hull was constructed of steel and had two decks: the main deck and berth deck . The berth deck was subdivided into compartments by six transverse watertight bulkheads .

Propulsion was provided by two Winton 6-cylinder, 4-stroke diesel engines, each producing 670 brake horsepower (500 kW) for a total of 1,340 brake horsepower (1,000 kW) . These engines drove two three-bladed propellers, giving the vessel a maximum speed of 16 knots (30 km/h; 18 mph), a maximum sustained speed of 14 knots (26 km/h; 16 mph), a cruising speed of 11 knots (20 km/h; 13 mph), and an economic speed of 6 knots (11 km/h; 6.9 mph) .

The vessel's fuel capacity was 7,700 U.S. gallons (29,000 L) of diesel fuel . Dione carried two 19-foot (5.8 m) dories for boat operations .

### 1.3 Armament and Systems

The original armament of USCGC Dione upon construction included one 3-inch/23-caliber gun and two 1-pound (0.45 kg) guns . The vessel was also equipped with wireless telegraphy and World War I-era sonar systems .

In 1941 or 1942, the vessel underwent a rearmament that removed one of the 1-pound guns and added two depth charge tracks and one Y-gun .

After a major refit in 1945, Dione's armament was significantly upgraded. The 3-inch/23-caliber gun was replaced with two single-mount 3-inch/50-caliber guns . The vessel also received two single-mount 20 mm/80 cannons, two "mousetrap" anti-submarine rocket launchers, and an additional Y-gun . Her wireless set and World War I-era sonar were replaced with an SF radar system and a QCN-1 sonar system .

As a result of the 1945 refit, the vessel's displacement increased to 350 long tons (360 t), her draft increased to 10 feet (3 m), and her complement was increased to 7 officers and 68 men .

## 2. Coast Guard Service

### 2.1 Pre-War Service (1934-1941)

Following her commissioning in October 1934, USCGC Dione began her service with the Coast Guard . Prohibition had been repealed in December 1933, so the cutter was not used for anti-rum running duties as originally intended . Instead, Dione conducted search and rescue operations .

The vessel was initially homeported in Norfolk, Virginia from 1934 to 1943 . In December 1943, she was transferred to Tompkinsville, Staten Island, where she remained based until December 1944 . Tompkinsville was the site of an Eastern Sea Frontier base .

### 2.2 World War II Service (1941-1945)

Following the Japanese attack on Pearl Harbor on December 7, 1941, the United States entered World War II. On November 1, 1941, President Franklin D. Roosevelt signed Executive Order 8929, which transferred control of the Coast Guard from the Department of the Treasury to the Navy "for the duration of the emergency" .

#### 2.2.1 Early War Operations

During the early months of the war, USCGC Dione was assigned to both the Fifth Naval District, which contained the waters off Virginia and North Carolina, and the Eastern Sea Frontier, a Navy operational command .

One of Dione's most significant early war actions occurred on March 15, 1942, when she received a distress signal from the oil tanker Australia . The vessel rescued the crew of the storage tanker Acme early in the morning of March 18, 1942 .

#### 2.2.2 Torpedo Junction Operations

During the critical period known as "Torpedo Junction" or "Torpedo Alley," USCGC Dione played a crucial role in coastal defense . German U-boats were wreaking havoc on Allied shipping along the American East Coast during what became known as Operation Paukenschlag (Operation Drumbeat) [2].

According to historical accounts, **USCGC Dione was the only ship patrolling the entire region** during part of this period [3]. Other ships had been stationed elsewhere, and they were not equipped for anti-submarine patrols . This inadequate defense contributed to what German submarine commanders referred to as "American shooting season" [2].

From January to June 1942, German U-boats sank 397 Allied vessels in Torpedo Junction, while American forces were only able to sink three U-boats during this timeframe . The losses eventually prompted naval leadership to take action, implementing convoy systems and deploying additional anti-submarine assets .

#### 2.2.3 Axtell J. Byles Incident

On April 19, 1942, USCGC Dione was escorting a convoy that included the unarmed oil tanker Axtell J. Byles . The convoy consisted of seven tankers and one freighter, escorted by Dione, four small US Coast Guard boats, and an aircraft . They steamed at 9.5 knots but did not zigzag .

At 00:34 hours, U-136 fired a spread of four torpedoes at the convoy about four miles off Wimble Shoals, North Carolina [4]. A Navy aircraft spotted the incoming torpedo and warned the ships [4]. The torpedo missed Dione by about 20 yards but struck the Axtell J. Byles at 20:00 hours, which sank shortly thereafter .

Dione dropped eight depth charges over the course of two runs in response to the attack . The vessel began patrolling around the Wimble Shoals buoy after her escort missions were finished, beginning demolition exercises on the wreck of the Axtell J. Byles on April 25 . She continued dropping depth charges for the next few days .

#### 2.2.4 Later War Operations

After the Axtell J. Byles incident, Dione continued her escort duties. The convoys began to run at night following the loss of the tanker . On April 29, Dione was ordered back to Little Creek for repairs and rearmament .

The repairs were completed on May 13, 1942 . Dione was listed as a convoy escort unit and conducted escort duty in the North Atlantic Ocean, escorting HK convoys going from New York to Key West and KN convoys heading from Key West to New York .

In 1945, Dione underwent a major refit that significantly upgraded her armament and systems . Following this refit, she was selected as one of six patrol vessels to serve as the Navy's "surrender group" for the First Naval District off New England, serving alongside her sister ships Argo and Nemesis .

On May 17, 1945, Dione rendezvoused with U-1228, a U-boat that had fled to Allied waters following the German surrender . The vessel escorted the submarine to Portsmouth, New Hampshire .

### 2.3 Post-War Service (1945-1947)

After World War II ended, the Coast Guard's jurisdiction was returned to the Department of the Treasury on January 1, 1946, via Executive Order 9666, signed by President Harry S. Truman .

From 1945 to 1947, Dione was used by the Coast Guard for law enforcement and search and rescue operations . The vessel was eventually decommissioned on July 23, 1947, in Cape May, New Jersey . She spent several years laid up in storage due to a lack of personnel .

### 2.4 Cold War Service (1951-1963)

USCGC Dione was recommissioned on February 4, 1951, in Curtis Bay, Maryland, after a shakedown cruise in the Caribbean . Starting in 1952, Dione was assigned to the Eighth Coast Guard District and based in Freeport, Texas . She was the first cutter to be based in the city .

During her Cold War service, Dione operated in various locations including Cuba, the Atlantic Ocean, New York, and Baltimore, Maryland [5]. Personnel who served aboard Dione during this period included James Eldon Wheeler, who reached the rank of Boatswain's Mate Second Class .

One notable commanding officer during this period was Commander John E. Wesler, who served as both Executive Officer and Commanding Officer of USCGC Dione (WPC-107) . Commander Wesler was a 1949 graduate of the U.S. Coast Guard Academy who later earned master's degrees in Acoustics from MIT and Optics from the University of Rochester .

The vessel was decommissioned for the final time on February 8, 1963 [1]. Her custody was transferred to the Texas Group of the Atlantic Reserve Fleet—the Atlantic Reserve Fleet, Orange—that same day .

## 3. Commercial Service

Following her decommissioning, USCGC Dione was sold as a supply ship on February 24, 1964 [1]. She entered commercial service with several name changes and owners over the years .

### 3.1 Early Commercial Career (1964-1969)

Initially, the vessel was owned by the Palmer Decker Boat Company and registered in Port Arthur, Texas . She carried the official number 297193 and the call sign WD 4627 .

In March 1967, the Palmer Decker Boat Company was dissolved following a liquidation agreement unanimously executed by the company's shareholders . The ship was seized by a U.S. Marshal in September 1967 after foreclosure proceedings were filed against the company .

The vessel was sold at a court auction on December 7, 1967, to Big Trouble Inc. . It was renamed "Big Trouble" from December 1967 to February 1969 .

In February 1969, the vessel was sold to Delta Boats Inc. and renamed "Delta I" . In March 1969, under the name Delta I, she was sold from Delta Boats to Sabik Inc., still registered in New Orleans .

### 3.2 Later Commercial Operations (1969-1992)

On April 5, 1969, while operating as a geophysical exploration ship named Delta I, the vessel caught fire while traveling from Kingston, Jamaica, to Port of Spain, Trinidad and Tobago . She was bound for the Persian Gulf .

After repairs, the vessel was rebuilt in Tampa, Florida, completed by March 1970 . She was capable of serving as "an oil rig tender, a crew boat, buoy tender, repair ship with sand blast capacity, and a line-handling vessel for super tankers" .

In late 1969, the vessel was renamed "Al Rashid" and continued in commercial service until 1992 . As Al Rashid, she had an IMO number of 7515080 .

### 3.3 Specifications as a Commercial Vessel

As a commercial vessel, the former USCGC Dione had the following specifications :



* Length: 154.3 feet (47 m)

* Beam: 25.3 feet (7.7 m)

* Draft: 11.3 feet (3.4 m)

* Gross Register Tonnage: 256 GRT

* Net Register Tonnage: 174 NRT

The vessel's final fate is unknown, as she was last seen in 1992 .

## 4. Legacy and Notability

USCGC Dione had a **29-year career** in the Coast Guard, serving during two major conflicts: World War II and the Cold War . Her service highlights include:



1. **Early Cold War Service**: Operating in Cuba and other strategic locations during the early years of the Cold War [5].

2. **World War II Escort Duty**: Serving as an escort vessel during the critical Battle of the Atlantic, protecting convoys along the American East Coast and in the North Atlantic .

3. **Torpedo Junction Defense**: Being the sole patrol vessel for a significant portion of the American East Coast during the early months of the U-boat campaign, a period that saw devastating losses to Allied shipping [3].

4. **Search and Rescue Operations**: Conducting numerous rescue missions, including the rescue of the crew of the storage tanker Acme on March 18, 1942 .

5. **Surrender Group Service**: Serving as part of the Navy's "surrender group" following Germany's surrender in 1945, including the escort of U-1228 to Portsmouth, New Hampshire .

6. **First Cutter in Freeport**: Being the first Coast Guard cutter based in Freeport, Texas, establishing a long-term presence in the city .

## 5. Commanding Officers

While comprehensive records of all commanding officers are not available in the sources, several notable officers served aboard USCGC Dione:



* **Commander John E. Wesler**: Served as both Executive Officer and Commanding Officer of USCGC Dione (WPC-107). A 1949 Coast Guard Academy graduate with advanced degrees in Acoustics and Optics .

* **Various Officers During WWII**: Including those who commanded the vessel during her critical escort and patrol duties in the Atlantic and along the East Coast .

* **Cold War Commanders**: Officers who led the vessel during her service in Cuba and other Cold War operations [5].

## 6. Technical Evolution

USCGC Dione underwent several significant technical changes during her service life:



1. **1941/1942 Rearmament**: Removed one 1-pound gun and added depth charge tracks and a Y-gun .

2. **1945 Major Refit**: A comprehensive upgrade that included new radar and sonar systems, upgraded armament including 3-inch/50-caliber guns and 20 mm cannons, and increased displacement and crew complement .

3. **From Prohibition Patrol to Warship**: Originally designed for anti-rum running during Prohibition, the vessel was repurposed for anti-submarine warfare and convoy escort duties .

4. **From Military to Commercial**: After decommissioning, the vessel was converted to various commercial roles including supply ship, geophysical exploration ship, and multi-purpose support vessel .

## 7. Conclusion

USCGC Dione (WPC-107) was a versatile patrol vessel that served the United States Coast Guard with distinction for nearly three decades . From her commissioning in 1934 through her decommissioning in 1963, Dione adapted to changing military and political circumstances, serving in both world wars and the early Cold War [1].

The vessel's service highlights the evolution of Coast Guard operations from peacetime law enforcement to wartime convoy escort and anti-submarine warfare . Her role as the sole patrol vessel during the early months of the U-boat campaign along the American East Coast underscores the challenges faced by the United States in defending its coastal waters during the early years of World War II [3].

Following her military service, Dione continued to serve in various commercial roles until at least 1992 . Her long service life and multiple adaptations demonstrate the durability and versatility of the Thetis/Argo-class design [1].

USCGC Dione represents an important piece of American naval and Coast Guard history, serving as a reminder of the vital role played by smaller vessels in both military and peacetime operations.

# Red and Purple Modernization Project

The **Red and Purple Modernization (RPM) Project** is a multi-phased reconstruction project coordinated by the Chicago Transit Authority (CTA) in the north side of Chicago, Evanston, and Wilmette . As one of CTA's largest transit investments, the RPM program modernizes the public transportation system to strengthen communities, serve riders and stimulate inclusive economic growth [1]. The project involves rebuilding the nearly century-old infrastructure of the North Red Line from Belmont to Howard and the Purple Line from Belmont to Linden [2].

## Background

The Red and Purple Modernization program addresses critical infrastructure needs in one of Chicago's busiest transit corridors. Much of the elevated structure, embankments, and stations on the corridor were originally built in the early 1900s and require frequent maintenance and often necessitate slow zones . The corridor serves approximately 175,400 daily linked trips and 57,100,500 annual linked trips [3].

The project corridor is one of the busiest in the CTA system, yet much of the infrastructure is nearly a century old and many of the stations are undersized for current demand . Peakhour ridership in the corridor has increased by 40 percent in the last five years, and CTA expects continued growth that it cannot meet with current service levels . Additionally, many stations on the branches were not compliant with the Americans with Disabilities Act (ADA) due to a lack of accessibility features, and existing station capacity was not enough to accommodate growing ridership .

## Project Phases

### Phase One

The first phase of the RPM project, known as the **Red and Purple Modernization Phase One Project**, focuses on a 5.6-mile segment of the corridor between Belmont and Howard stations . This phase includes the reconstruction of four stations and 1.3 route miles of guideway, construction of a grade-separated flyover for northbound Brown Line trains at the junction with the Red and Purple lines, installation of a new higher-capacity signal system, power substation upgrades, and purchase of 32 new railcars for expanded service .

The four stations rebuilt in Phase One are:



* **Lawrence Station** at 1120 W. Lawrence Ave.

* **Argyle Station** at 1118 W. Argyle St.

* **Berwyn Station** at 1119 W. Berwyn Ave.

* **Bryn Mawr Station** at 1116 W. Bryn Mawr Ave. (with a Hollywood entrance at 1119 W. Hollywood Ave.)

The **Lawrence to Bryn Mawr Modernization (LBMM) Project** is a key component of Phase One, consisting of a complete rebuild of these four stations including the tracks and support structures [4]. Major track and station construction of the 1.3-mile footprint began in 2021 with completion on July 20, 2025 .

Another significant component of Phase One is the **Red-Purple Bypass (RPB) Project**, which eliminated a 100-year-old junction just north of the Belmont station that created a bottleneck in Red and Purple Line service [5]. The bypass allows Brown Line trains to operate along dedicated tracks above the Red and Purple Line tracks. After the CTA put the new bypass into service in 2021, the agency rebuilt the century-old Red and Purple Line track structure between Belmont and Newport/Cornelia. The reconstruction of the elevated track was completed in May 2025 .

### Next Phases

With RPM Phase One construction wrapping up, CTA is moving ahead on the multi-year process to initiate the next phase of the RPM program . The **RPM Next Phases Planning Study** is the first step of the planning process that will set goals and objectives, engage the public, identify potential projects, and evaluate alternatives and phasing .

The RPM Next Phases Planning Study focuses on three segments of the north Red Line and the Purple Line Evanston branch:



* Addison to Sheridan

* Thorndale to Howard

* Evanston Branch (Howard to Linden)

The study is expected to be finalized in 2025. Completing this critical planning phase will allow the CTA to launch the next steps in the process, which includes engineering work, conducting a National Environmental Policy Act (NEPA) environmental review, and identifying funding for implementation .

## Funding and Costs

The total project cost of the Core Capacity project under the Full Funding Grant Agreement (FFGA) is $2,066.70 million . The Section 5309 Core Capacity funding share is $956.61 million (48.0%), with additional funding sources including:



* Congestion Mitigation and Air Quality (CMAQ) Funds: \$115.73 million

* Local: CTA Sales Tax Bonds and Cash: \$384.30 million

* Transit Tax-Increment Financing (TIF) District Bonds and Revenues: \$602.26 million

* Bryn Mawr TIF District Revenues: \$7.80 million

Funding for the $2.1 billion Phase One project was secured in January 2017 . Through FY 2016, Congress had appropriated $191.13 million in Section 5309 Core Capacity funds for the project .

## Construction and Implementation

The Chicago Transit Authority selected the joint venture team of Fluor and Walsh Construction Company to design and build Phase One of the Red and Purple Line Modernization program [6]. The Walsh-Fluor design-build team broke ground on the project on October 2, 2019 [7].

The construction timeline for Phase One was:



* October 2, 2019: Groundbreaking ceremony

* May 16, 2021: Construction of Lawrence through Bryn Mawr modernization began

* 2021: Red-Purple Bypass went into service

* May 2025: Reconstruction of elevated track between Belmont and Newport/Cornelia completed

* July 20, 2025: Four new stations (Lawrence, Argyle, Berwyn, Bryn Mawr) opened to the public

## Benefits and Improvements

The Red and Purple Modernization Project delivers numerous benefits to transit riders and the community:

### Capacity Improvements

CTA estimates that Phase One will increase capacity in the corridor by 15 percent, which exceeds the ten-percent minimum required for Core Capacity projects . The Red-Purple Bypass eliminates train congestion by allowing eight more Red Line trains per hour during rush periods, accommodating up to 7,200 additional customers per hour during rush periods and increasing Red and Purple line train speeds through the intersection .

### Accessibility Upgrades

The four rebuilt stations are now fully accessible for the first time, with modern amenities including escalators, elevators, wider platforms, enhanced lighting and security, and real-time travel information displays . This addresses the previous ADA compliance issues that existed at many stations on the corridor .

### Infrastructure Modernization

The modernization program replaces infrastructure that is significantly beyond its useful life. Most tracks and bridges are nearly 100 years old, and upgraded infrastructure will reduce the need for frequent repairs and maintenance that disrupt service [6]. The project includes the construction of new bridges, support structures, tracks and 3.2 miles of signal system upgrades .

### Service Improvements

Riders can expect:



* Smoother, more reliable service

* Fewer delays

* Relief of chronic overcrowding on trains and rail platforms

* Reduced noise at street level

* A brighter, cleaner, safer and more pleasant pedestrian environment

The openings mark the successful reconstruction of 1.3 miles of elevated track structure between Lawrence and Bryn Mawr, as well as major upgrades to bridges, viaducts, and systems, ultimately delivering faster, safer, and more reliable service for CTA riders [8].

### Economic and Community Benefits

The project stimulates inclusive economic growth by improving access to jobs and destinations [1]. The modernization will allow CTA to improve operational efficiency and meet ridership demand for the next 100 years [2].

## Relationship to Red Ahead Program

The Red and Purple Modernization Project is part of the broader **Red Ahead** program, a comprehensive initiative for maintaining, modernizing, and expanding Chicago's most-traveled rail line . The Red Ahead program includes three major improvement projects on the Red and Purple Lines between Linden terminal on the north and the proposed 130th Street terminal on the south:



1. **Red Line Extension**

2. **Red Line South Reconstruction**

3. **Red and Purple Modernization Program**

All three projects are mutually beneficial; an improvement in one area of the Red Line benefits the entire Red Line . The RPM Next Phases project is part of this multi-phase Red Ahead program to maintain and modernize the Red and Purple lines and ensure they remain a vital part of the transportation system in Chicago and the region [2].

## Legacy and Future Outlook

The Red and Purple Modernization Project represents one of the largest capital investments in CTA's history [9]. The completion of Phase One in July 2025 marks a significant milestone in modernizing Chicago's transit infrastructure for the 21st century.

The project demonstrates how major infrastructure investments can transform aging transportation systems to meet growing demand while improving accessibility, reliability, and sustainability. As CTA progresses with the next phases of the RPM program, the benefits will extend to additional communities along the north Red Line and Purple Line corridor, ensuring these vital transit routes will serve Chicago and the region for generations to come.

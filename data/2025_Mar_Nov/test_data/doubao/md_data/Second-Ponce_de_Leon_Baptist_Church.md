# Second-Ponce de Leon Baptist Church

**Second-Ponce de Leon Baptist Church** is a historic Baptist congregation located in the Buckhead neighborhood of Atlanta, Georgia, at 2715 Peachtree Road NE . The church was formed in 1932 through the merger of two earlier congregations: Second Baptist Church (founded 1854) and Ponce de Leon Avenue Baptist Church (formed 1904) [1]. With a current membership of approximately 3,000 [2], it is one of the larger Baptist churches in the Atlanta area and has been a significant religious and community institution in the city for over a century.

## History

### Early Origins and Formation

The origins of Second-Ponce de Leon Baptist Church trace back to two separate congregations that eventually merged. **Second Baptist Church** was founded in downtown Atlanta in 1854 [1]. According to historical records, the church was constituted with the name "The Second Baptist Church of Atlanta," and its first action was to hold a short conference and appoint delegates to the Rock Mountain Association (later known as the Stone Mountain Association) [3].

**Ponce de Leon Avenue Baptist Church** was established in 1904 when the congregations of First Baptist Church, Second Baptist Church, and other groups expressed a need for a new church at the north end of the city . The new church was built at the corner of Ponce de Leon and Piedmont Avenue .

### Merger and New Building

By 1929, the Ponce de Leon Avenue Baptist Church congregation had become spread out and desired a new church facility. They sold their first building to the Central Congregational Church and purchased land at the intersection of Peachtree Road and Wesley Avenue (now East Wesley Road) [4]. The new building, which would later become Second-Ponce de Leon Baptist Church, was completed in 1930 [4].

In 1932, the two churches—Second Baptist Church and Ponce de Leon Avenue Baptist Church—merged to form Second-Ponce de Leon Baptist Church [1]. This merger reflected what senior pastor David Sapp described as "an emphasis on unity throughout the history of the church" .

### Notable Pastors and Leaders

Second-Ponce de Leon has been led by several influential pastors throughout its history. **Monroe F. Swilley Jr.** (1914-1991) served as pastor from October 1945 to 1969 . He was born in El Dorado, Arkansas, and graduated from Ouachita College and Southern Baptist Theological Seminary. Swilley also studied at Union Theological Seminary in New York and the University of Zurich in Switzerland . During his tenure, he was instrumental in the founding of Atlanta Baptist College (now part of Mercer University). In 1950, the Atlanta Baptist Association appointed Swilley and three other committee members to lead a study of the need for a Baptist college in the Atlanta area. Second-Ponce de Leon Baptist Church members heavily supported his campaign to raise funds for the college. He resigned as pastor to become President of Atlanta Baptist College on January 1, 1969 .

**Russell Hooper Dilday Jr.** (1930-2023) was another significant pastor at Second-Ponce de Leon before becoming the sixth president of Southwestern Baptist Theological Seminary in 1978 . Dilday was born in Amarillo, Texas, and sensed a call to ministry during his senior year of high school. He served as pastor of Second-Ponce de Leon before being elected president of Southwestern Seminary during the trustees' fall meeting in 1977 .

**David Sapp** served as senior pastor for nine years (as of 2008) and was instrumental in leading the church through significant renovations . During his tenure, the church underwent major renovations to modernize its facilities and improve accessibility. Sapp noted, "One of the most exciting things to our members is that there is going to be an elevator that will go to every level. For the first time in the history of the church, it will be possible to get to every level on one elevator" .

**James "Dock" Hollingsworth** served as senior pastor and was succeeded by other Mercer-connected clergy. He earned his bachelor's degree at Mercer in 1984 and his Doctor of Ministry at Mercer's McAfee School of Theology in 2008. He was also an assistant professor, assistant dean, and admissions director at McAfee prior to his role at Second-Ponce .

## Architecture and Facilities

### Building Design and Features

The current Second-Ponce de Leon Baptist Church building was completed in 1930 [4] and is located in the Buckhead area of Atlanta at the intersection of Peachtree Road and East Wesley Road (formerly Wesley Avenue) . The church building is a significant architectural structure in the Buckhead community. Historical photographs from the late 1930s and early 1940s show the church's distinctive steeple [5].

The church complex includes multiple buildings constructed at different times. The main sanctuary building was refurbished seven years before 2008 (around 2001), and the church later moved forward with renovations to the more than 50,000-square-foot chapel building constructed in 1953 [6].

### Facilities and Community Resources

The church campus features several notable facilities:

**Family Life Center**: The church operates a 70,000-square-foot Family Life Center that is open to the community and receives approximately 10,000 visits monthly . This facility includes an indoor pool, racquetball courts, cardio room, weight room, and other recreational amenities. The center serves as "a staple in the Buckhead community" and provides opportunities for the church to interface with people beyond its immediate congregation .

**Sanctuary and Worship Spaces**: The main sanctuary underwent refurbishment around 2001 [6]. The church has historically placed importance on traditional worship, with its minister of music noting, "This is definitely a congregation where traditional Baptist ministry is celebrated and embraced" .

**Educational Facilities**: The church has extensive educational facilities that were designed around 1950s-era Christian education methods. During the 2008 renovations, much of the work focused on modernizing these spaces to meet contemporary educational needs .

### Renovations and Modernization

In 2008, the church underwent significant renovations to modernize its facilities and improve accessibility . The project, which began about a month before May 2008 and was slated for completion in one year, included several key improvements:



* **Foundation repairs**: The church addressed foundation leaks in the 1953 portion of the building

* **Stormwater management**: Crews installed a new drain system under the front lawn to meet storm drain requirements

* **Accessibility improvements**: The installation of an elevator that would reach every level of the building, making it accessible for the first time in the church's history

* **Mechanical system upgrades**: Renovations to modernize plumbing, wiring, and mechanical systems

* **Office improvements**: The conversion of former "cubicle land" into separate offices for most of the approximately 25 full-time employees

## Ministry and Community Outreach

### Congregational Life and Programs

Second-Ponce de Leon Baptist Church maintains an active congregational life with various ministries serving different age groups and needs. The church's stated mission is to "take the whole gospel to the whole person," as demonstrated by Jesus during his ministry on earth .

**Sunday Worship**: The church holds Sunday worship services at 11:00 a.m., with Sunday School and small groups meeting at 9:45 a.m. . The church also provides online worship options for those unable to attend in person .

**Music Ministry**: The church has a strong tradition of excellence in traditional church music. The minister of music, Bill Gabbard, who joined in 2019, plans worship services, selects hymns and musical pieces, and directs the church's choir and orchestra . He noted, "Taking over the music program of a church that has such a wonderful history of excellence of traditional church music is a great privilege and blessing" .

**Youth and Children's Ministry**: The church has active youth and children's programs. The minister of community and student discipleship, Jonathan Hall, leads the youth group for grades 6-12 and young adult group for members in their 20s to 40s. Activities include Bible studies, classes, volunteer activities, trips, and retreats. A highlight for the youth group is attending Passport camp each summer . The minister of missions and children's discipleship, Allie Osborne, plans Sunday school, kids worship, Wednesday night activities, and community events like Vacation Bible School, a fall fest, and an egg hunt .

### Community Outreach and Missions

Second-Ponce de Leon has a significant commitment to community outreach and missions work. The church started the Atlanta Community Ministries in 1996, which has grown to include 29 volunteer-led ministries with 700 volunteers from more than 60 churches .

**Local Missions**: The church partners with various local organizations including food pantries and rental assistance agencies . Through its Family Life Center, the church provides fitness and recreational opportunities to the broader Buckhead community, with 10,000 monthly visits .

**National and International Missions**: The church maintains partnerships beyond Atlanta. For three years prior to 2008, the congregation had a partnership with Sichuan province in China . Additionally, the church organizes annual mission trips, including a 60-person team that built a church in three days in Shelbyville, Kentucky. The church undertakes similar projects each summer to assist promising congregations that could not otherwise afford building construction .

**Television Ministry**: Second-Ponce de Leon has a long history in television ministry. Every Sunday, the church airs its service on Atlanta Interfaith Broadcasters at 11 a.m. Senior pastor David Sapp noted, "The first face ever seen on television in Atlanta was the pastor of this church. When WSB came on the air, they started with the Morning Devotion that was given by one of my predecessors," referring to Monroe F. Swilley Jr. .

### Membership and Growth

The church has experienced significant growth throughout its history. The current membership is approximately 3,000 [2]. The congregation has maintained its vitality through various initiatives and community engagement.

The church is part of the "Jesus Junction" area at the intersection of Peachtree and Wesley roads, which is home to several large religious institutions including the Catholic Cathedral of Christ the King (with more than 5,300 parishioners) and the Cathedral of St. Philip (one of the largest Episcopal congregations in the country) .

## Cultural and Historical Significance

### Atlanta Baptist Heritage

Second-Ponce de Leon Baptist Church holds a significant place in Atlanta's Baptist heritage. The church has deep connections with Mercer University, dating back to Monroe Swilley Jr., who was instrumental in the founding of Atlanta Baptist College (now part of Mercer University) . As noted by current minister Bill Gabbard, "It goes back to (Monroe) Swilley, who is one of our former senior pastors and who figured very prominently in Mercer's operations on campus and in the city. A lot of folks associated with Mercer have been members, staff and deacons in our congregation over the last 30 or 40 years" .

### Media and Communication Contributions

The church has made important contributions to religious broadcasting in Atlanta. As mentioned earlier, the first face ever seen on television in Atlanta was the pastor of Second-Ponce de Leon Baptist Church when WSB-TV began broadcasting . This early adoption of television technology for religious purposes demonstrates the church's forward-thinking approach to ministry and communication.

### Architectural and Community Landmark

The church building itself is a significant architectural landmark in the Buckhead community. Located at 2715 Peachtree Road NE in Atlanta, Georgia 30305 , the church's distinctive steeple and architecture have made it a recognizable landmark in the area. The church grounds also feature a therapeutic garden that provides respite to the community, described as "a beautiful jewel in the heart of Buckhead" [7].

## Current Activities and Programs

### Contemporary Ministry Initiatives

As of recent years, Second-Ponce de Leon Baptist Church continues to evolve its ministry approach while maintaining its traditional values. The church emphasizes four core values: **Community** (building genuine relationships and caring for one another), **Discipleship** (equipping people to discover God's call for their lives), **Missions** (serving others like Jesus did), and **Worship** (worshipping with gratitude and joy as imperfect but loved children of God) .

**Technology Integration**: The church has embraced technology to enhance ministry, including livestreaming Sunday worship services and maintaining a video library of discipleship resources and previous worship services .

**Fellowship Opportunities**: The church offers regular fellowship opportunities including Wednesday evening fellowship suppers at 5:00 p.m., followed by adult discipleship at 6:00 p.m. and sanctuary choir rehearsal at 7:05 p.m. .

### Leadership and Staff

The church is currently led by a team of ministers with strong educational backgrounds and commitment to ministry. Key staff members include:



* **Bill Gabbard**, Minister of Music (since 2019), who previously served in the U.S. Army Field Band for 31 years before answering a call to church music ministry

* **Jonathan Hall**, Minister of Community and Student Discipleship, who graduated from McAfee School of Theology in 2023 and leads youth and young adult ministries

* **Allie Osborne**, Minister of Missions and Children's Discipleship, who oversees missions programs and children's ministries

### Educational and Discipleship Programs

The church maintains comprehensive educational programs for all age groups. Sunday School and small groups meet at 9:45 a.m. before the 11:00 a.m. worship service . Adult discipleship classes are offered on Wednesday evenings .

The church places particular emphasis on intergenerational ministry and community building. As noted by senior pastor Dock Hollingsworth, "We have seen that in our own lives as well as the lives of members of our congregations" in reference to the wisdom that comes with age [8].

## Legacy and Impact

Second-Ponce de Leon Baptist Church has left a lasting impact on Atlanta's religious and cultural landscape. The church's commitment to unity, demonstrated through its 1932 merger, has been a defining characteristic throughout its history . This emphasis on bringing different groups together has shaped the church's inclusive approach to ministry and community engagement.

The church's contributions to education, through its support of Atlanta Baptist College (now Mercer University Atlanta), and to media, through its pioneering television ministry, have had far-reaching effects beyond its immediate congregation .

Today, Second-Ponce de Leon continues to serve as a spiritual anchor for the Buckhead community while maintaining its commitment to reaching beyond its walls through missions, community service, and innovative ministry approaches. The church's combination of traditional values and contemporary outreach makes it a vital institution in Atlanta's religious and social fabric.

As one of the larger Baptist churches in the Atlanta area with approximately 3,000 members [2], Second-Ponce de Leon Baptist Church stands as a testament to the enduring legacy of faith communities in American life, combining historical significance with contemporary relevance in serving both its members and the broader community.

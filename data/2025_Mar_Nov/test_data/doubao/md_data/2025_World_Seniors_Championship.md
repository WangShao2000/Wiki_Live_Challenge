# 2025 World Seniors Championship

## Lead Section

The 2025 World Seniors Championship was a prestigious snooker tournament held at the Crucible Theatre in Sheffield, England from May 7 to 11, 2025 [1]. The event featured 16 players aged 40 and over from nine nations competing for the title of world seniors champion [1]. Alfie Burden from England emerged victorious, defeating Aaron Canavan from Jersey 8-4 in the final [2]. The tournament was sponsored by JenningsBet and was broadcast live on Channel 5 and 5Action in the UK .

## Event Details

### Venue and Format

The 2025 World Seniors Championship took place at the Crucible Theatre, snooker's most iconic venue, which is traditionally used for the World Snooker Championship [1]. The tournament format consisted of 16 players competing in a knockout system with the following structure: first round and quarter-final matches were contested over the best of five frames, semi-finals were best of seven frames, and the final was best of nine frames [3].

### Participants

The international field of 16 players included defending champion Igor Figueiredo from Brazil, who was eliminated in the first round [1]. Other notable participants included four-time champion Jimmy White from England, former world champion Ken Doherty from Ireland, and Maltese legend Tony Drago [1]. The field also featured recently retired professional players such as Dominic Dale and Joe Perry [4].

## Competition Results

### Early Rounds

The tournament began with the first round on May 7, where several upsets occurred. Defending champion Igor Figueiredo was defeated 2-4 by English amateur Wayne Townsend in a surprising first-round exit [5]. Jimmy White, the four-time winner, was eliminated 1-4 by Alfie Burden, marking a repeat of their 2023 final [5]. Ken Doherty also suffered an early exit, losing 2-4 to South African player Charl Jönk [5].

### Quarter-finals and Semi-finals

In the quarter-finals, Alfie Burden faced Tony Drago and came back from a 3-0 deficit to win 4-3, demonstrating his fighting qualities . Aaron Canavan defeated Dominic Dale in another quarter-final match, setting up a semi-final meeting with Burden . The semi-finals saw Burden defeat Australian debutant Hassan Kerde 6-2, while Canavan stunned Dominic Dale to reach the final .

### Final

The championship final took place on May 11 and was split into three mini-sessions of five frames each . The match began with a moment of history as Michaela Tabb, the first female referee to officiate a World Snooker Championship final in 2009, took charge of her final frames at the Crucible Theatre .

Canavan broke the deadlock, but Burden quickly hit back to take four successive frames, with contributions of 74 and 54, to lead 4-1 at the first break . Canavan reduced the deficit with a break of 75, but Burden showed his scoring prowess with consecutive breaks of 65, 109, and 80 to move to 7-2 .

Canavan managed to win frames 10 and 11 to make the score 7-4, but Burden remained composed. In the title-winning frame, Burden fired in a flamboyant break of 76 to secure an 8-4 victory . After the match, Burden expressed his delight, saying: "I'm absolutely delighted. I thought I played well in the final. It's been a massive journey with so many ups and downs... It was a strong field this week and I felt like I played the best snooker and deserved to win the tournament" .

## Prize Money

The total prize fund for the 2025 World Seniors Championship was £50,000, with the winner receiving £20,000 and the runner-up £10,000 [6]. Additionally, there was a £1,500 prize for the highest break of the tournament [5].

## Historical Context

The World Seniors Championship has a long history in snooker, having been first held in 1991 [7]. The tournament is the world's premier snooker competition for players aged 40 or over [7]. The event was initially held at Trentham Gardens in Stoke-on-Trent, with Cliff Wilson defeating Eddie Charlton 5-4 to become the inaugural champion .

The tournament was discontinued after 1992 but was revived in 2010 in Bradford with a reduced field of nine players [8]. Since then, it has been held annually, with the venue and format evolving over time. The minimum age requirement has varied, starting at 40 in 1991, increasing to 45 in 2011, and returning to 40 in 2015 [9].

From 2019 onwards, the tournament has been held at the Crucible Theatre immediately following the World Snooker Championship [9]. The winner of the World Seniors Championship receives a place in the Champion of Champions tournament, provided they are not already qualified through other means [9].

## Notable Performances

### Alfie Burden's Victory

Alfie Burden's victory in the 2025 World Seniors Championship was particularly significant as it was his first title in the event . The 48-year-old had previously lost to Jimmy White in the 2023 final . Burden qualified for the tournament through the Golden Ticket Play-Off, defeating Matthew Ford 4-3 in April 2025 [10]. His path to victory included wins over Jimmy White, Tony Drago, Hassan Kerde, and finally Aaron Canavan in the final .

### Michaela Tabb's Farewell

The final match was also notable for marking the retirement of referee Michaela Tabb, who had been officiating at the Crucible Theatre for many years . Tabb made history in 2009 as the first female referee to take charge of a World Snooker Championship final, and the 2025 World Seniors Championship final was her last at the iconic venue .

## Legacy

The 2025 World Seniors Championship continued the tradition of providing a platform for veteran snooker players to compete at the highest level. The tournament showcased the skill and experience of players who may have retired from professional competition but still possess significant talent [11]. The event also served as a reminder of the rich history of snooker and the contributions of its veteran players.

Alfie Burden's victory established him as the new champion in seniors snooker, while the tournament overall highlighted the continued popularity and competitiveness of the World Seniors Championship format . The event remains an important part of the snooker calendar, providing entertainment for fans and opportunities for veteran players to continue their careers in a competitive setting.

# Everyone Hates Elon

**Everyone Hates Elon** (EHE) is a **UK-based activist group** formed in early 2025 that conducts guerrilla campaigns against entrepreneur **Elon Musk** . The group emerged in response to Musk's political interventions in British affairs, including his support for far-right activist Tommy Robinson and his controversial gesture at Donald Trump's second inauguration that many interpreted as a Nazi salute .

The group's activities have gained significant media attention and social media virality, with their "Swasticar" campaign targeting Tesla vehicles and their posters appearing across London [1]. Their efforts have coincided with a notable decline in Tesla sales, with European sales dropping 45% in January 2025 .

## Formation and Background

**Everyone Hates Elon** was founded by a "very small group of friends" who were outraged by Musk's interventions in British politics . The group members, who are scattered across the UK and had worked on campaigns before, were particularly offended by Musk's demands for new elections in the UK and his campaign to free Tommy Robinson from prison . Robinson is a controversial figure who founded the English Defense League, described as a fascist political party .

The group's formation was further catalyzed by Musk's appearance at Trump's inauguration in January 2025, where he made a gesture that many interpreted as a Nazi salute . This incident, combined with his growing political influence as head of the Department of Government Efficiency (DOGE) in the Trump administration, provided the "impetus they needed" to launch their campaign [2].

## Campaign Activities and Tactics

### The "Swasticar" Campaign

The group's most prominent campaign centers on the "Swasticar" theme, which plays on the word "Swastika" (the Nazi symbol) and "Tesla" [3]. Their 标志性海报 featured a Tesla vehicle with the caption "Goes from 0 to 1939 in 3 seconds" (a reference to the start of World War II), followed by "The Swasticar" [4]. The image paired this with a photograph of Musk making his controversial gesture at Trump's inauguration .

The campaign has expanded beyond posters to include **"Swasticar" stickers** that members have plastered on hundreds of Tesla vehicles across London [5]. The group has also taken a cardboard cutout of Musk to Tesla dealerships as part of their protests [5].

### Guerrilla Advertising Campaign

The group has deployed **unconventional marketing tactics** to spread their message throughout London, with their reach extending beyond the capital city . Their activities have included:



* Posting anti-Musk advertisements on London bus stops and the London Underground [6]

* Creating posters that read "Delete your account. If the bar lets Nazis in, it's a Nazi bar" targeting Musk's social media platform X

* Launching "Elon Musk is a bellend" merchandise, including t-shirts

One particularly notable poster featured the text "Elon Musk is a bellend. Signed, the UK" . A group spokesperson explained this messaging by saying: "Elon Musk has all the money in the world – but he can't buy popularity. The British public hates his guts and polling shows we don't want him interfering in our politics anymore. We thought we'd communicate that to him in a way only British people could – by calling him a bellend" .

### Fundraising and Donation Strategy

**Everyone Hates Elon** runs a fundraising campaign called **"People Versus Elon"** on crowdfunding platforms [1]. Their initial goal was to raise £50,000, with the stated intention that "the more we raise, the more we can do" [7]. The campaign is anticipated to raise £150,000 over one year .

A unique aspect of their fundraising strategy involves a pledge system where donors commit to giving a small amount (such as one penny) for each post Musk makes on X [3]. With Musk posting approximately 2,000 times monthly and close to 1,000 people making regular pledges, the initiative is on track to meet its fundraising goals [4].

The group has stated that all proceeds go directly toward the costs of their campaigning [8]. They have raised over £4,300 (approximately $8,800) as of March 2025 <reference type="end" id=37>, and in an interview with The Hollywood Reporter, they claimed to have raised close to $200,000 and donated the funds "to causes \[Elon] hates" [9].

## Impact and Reception

### Media Attention and Virality

The group's activities have achieved **significant media attention and social media virality**. Their TikTok videos have garnered millions of views, with one particular video showing the installation of their "Swasticar" poster accumulating over 9 million views and 980,000 likes . The campaign has been covered by numerous international media outlets including The Hollywood Reporter, PinkNews, The New Daily, and The Independent [1].

The group has leveraged their newfound virality to raise awareness and funds for causes they believe Musk would oppose, including Everyday Racism and Rainbow Migration [10].

### Effect on Tesla Sales

The timing of the **Everyone Hates Elon** campaign has coincided with a significant decline in Tesla sales, particularly in Europe. According to data from the European Automobile Manufacturers' Association (ACEA), Tesla sold just 9,945 vehicles in Europe in January 2025, representing a **45% drop** from 18,161 units in January 2024 . The company's market share in Europe shrank from 1.8% to 1% .

In Australia, Tesla sales plummeted 71.9% in February 2025 compared to the previous year, with just 1,592 vehicles sold compared to 5,665 in the same period in 2024 . Globally, Tesla's first-quarter 2025 vehicle deliveries dropped 13% year-over-year to 336,681 units, marking the company's lowest quarterly performance since mid-2022 and the largest drop in the company's history .

Industry analysts have suggested that the decline in sales may be linked to what they describe as a shift in the Tesla brand's image. As James Obiegbu and Gretchen Larsen wrote: "Owning a Tesla may no longer signal purely \[a buyer's] beliefs about sustainability. There may be a nod to political or ideological affiliations that do not align with their own" .

### Public Response and Protests

The **Everyone Hates Elon** campaign has inspired similar protest movements and actions against Musk and Tesla. A broader movement called **"Tesla Takedown"** has emerged, with demonstrations in over 250 cities worldwide . This grassroots initiative organized nonviolent protests at Tesla locations across the US, Canada, Europe, and Australia, calling for boycotts and urging the public to divest from Tesla by selling their vehicles and shares .

Some protests have escalated beyond peaceful demonstrations. There have been reports of vandalism at Tesla facilities, including charging stations and vehicles . For example, on March 24, 2025, incendiary devices were discovered at a Tesla dealership in Austin, Texas .

The campaign has also highlighted growing public sentiment against Musk. A February 2025 poll indicated that 62% of respondents viewed Musk's role in government as "inappropriate," and 44% felt it damaged confidence in the administration's reform efforts . In the UK, a poll showed that 69% of the public believe Musk is having a negative impact on UK politics .

## Notability and Assessment

The **Everyone Hates Elon** campaign meets several criteria for notability in the context of contemporary activism and social movements:



1. **Media Coverage**: The group has received extensive coverage from multiple reputable international media outlets, including The Hollywood Reporter, PinkNews, The New Daily, and The Independent [1].

2. **Social Media Impact**: Their content has achieved significant virality, with TikTok videos garnering millions of views and widespread sharing across social media platforms .

3. **Organizational Structure**: The group has demonstrated organizational capacity through their coordinated campaigns, fundraising efforts, and consistent messaging across multiple platforms .

4. **Measurable Impact**: The campaign has coincided with significant declines in Tesla sales, particularly in European markets, suggesting a measurable economic impact .

5. **Influence on Public Discourse**: The group's activities have contributed to broader discussions about wealth inequality, corporate influence, and political activism in the digital age [10].

## Criticisms and Counterarguments

While the **Everyone Hates Elon** campaign has gained significant attention and support, it has also faced criticism and counterarguments:



1. **Accusations of Bias**: Critics have argued that the group's name itself demonstrates a lack of neutrality and presents a biased perspective . The group makes no attempt to hide their opposition to Musk, stating their goal is to "piss Elon Musk off, one small action at a time" .

2. **Tactical Concerns**: Some have questioned the effectiveness and appropriateness of their tactics, particularly the use of the "Swasticar" theme, which critics argue may minimize the historical significance of the Nazi symbol [3].

3. **Economic Impact Questions**: While Tesla sales have declined, some analysts have suggested that other factors, including production updates and market competition, may have contributed to the decline alongside the campaign .

4. **Target Audience Limitations**: The group's messaging, particularly their use of British colloquialisms like "bellend," may limit their appeal and effectiveness outside of specific cultural contexts .

## Legacy and Future Prospects

The **Everyone Hates Elon** campaign represents a notable example of **grassroots digital activism** in the 21st century. Their use of social media, guerrilla marketing tactics, and crowdfunding has allowed a small group of friends to achieve international attention and potentially influence corporate behavior .

The campaign has also highlighted broader societal tensions around:



* The political influence of billionaires

* The intersection of technology, business, and politics

* The role of social media in activism and protest movements

* The relationship between consumer choice and political expression

As of 2025, the group continues to be active, with plans for further campaigns and an expanded reach. Their success in achieving media attention and measurable impact suggests that their model of **small-scale, high-impact activism** may inspire similar movements in the future.

The campaign's long-term legacy will likely be determined by whether it can sustain its momentum and achieve its stated goals of reducing Musk's political influence and promoting causes that align with their values. Regardless of the ultimate outcome, **Everyone Hates Elon** has demonstrated how digital platforms and creative activism can amplify the voice of small groups and potentially challenge the power of even the world's wealthiest individuals.

# Joy Laking

Joy Snihur Wyatt Laking (born 1950) is a Canadian visual artist and author known for her detailed watercolor and acrylic paintings of Nova Scotia landscapes [1]. Since 1972, she has lived and worked in Nova Scotia, capturing the province's natural beauty through realistic watercolor and acrylic works [2]. Her work is characterized by meticulous detail, vibrant color, and a focus on everyday scenes transformed into art .

## Early Life and Education

Joy Snihur Wyatt Laking was born in 1950 in Owen Sound, Ontario [1]. She grew up in Grey County, Ontario, surrounded by the landscape of limestone rock, grey waves, rolling hills, pines, and maples, which deeply influenced her artistic sensibilities . Her mother was a professional artist and major influence on her life, while her father built marionette puppets and trains, tinted photographs, and eventually began painting as well .

Laking's early artistic development was shaped by a childhood environment rich in creativity. On her mother's side were generations of English jet carvers who worked in Victorian England, and both parents won art competitions as children and had their paintings featured in the window of Eaton's in Hamilton, Ontario . This creative heritage provided both inspiration and practical support for her artistic journey.

After completing a degree in Fine Art from the University of Guelph in Ontario, Laking moved to Nova Scotia in 1972 with the expectation of staying for a year or two before moving west [3]. However, the ocean, the people, the old villages, the pace of life, and the natural beauty of Nova Scotia captured her heart, and she has remained there ever since [3].

## Career and Artistic Development

Laking's artistic career began in earnest after her move to Nova Scotia. From the beginning, she chose to work with watercolor in a realistic style, a decision that was not trendy in the 1970s when the focus at university was on acrylics and abstract art . She preferred watercolor for both health reasons (avoiding solvents associated with oils) and because it suited her desire to capture the physical world around her .

Her first solo exhibition was held in 1982 at the Mount Saint Vincent University Art Gallery, featuring an entire exhibition of white flowers on white paper done in watercolor [4]. This early focus on white-on-white technique would become one of her signature approaches, demonstrating her technical virtuosity and attention to subtle variations in light and form .

In the ten years between 1982 and 1992, Laking held nine solo exhibitions and six group exhibitions [5]. Her work was represented by several prominent galleries, including Manuge Gallery and Zwickers Gallery in Halifax, and Beckett Gallery in Hamilton, Ontario [5]. She also established her own gallery, building it onto her house in Bass River, Nova Scotia, where both locals and tourists could view and purchase her work .

In 1990, Laking received significant recognition when she was invited to do a solo exhibition at the Art Gallery of Nova Scotia, which then toured for a year [5]. This recognition by a provincial gallery was a remarkable tribute to an emerging artist and gave her the confidence to stop accepting commissions and paint her own images exclusively .

Laking's artistic style has evolved over the years while maintaining certain consistent elements. Initially known primarily as a watercolor painter and printmaker, she has expanded her practice to include oils and acrylics over the past fifteen years [6]. Her work is characterized by meticulous attention to detail, with every paint flake, stone fissure, seashell barnacle, and flower petal caught precisely .

## Artistic Style and Themes

Laking's artistic approach is deeply rooted in observation and attention to detail. She observes the world around her with attentiveness, passion, and keen interest, and her ability to see distinguishes her as an exceptional artist as much as her technical skill [5]. Her work is particularly noted for its ability to transform ordinary scenes into extraordinary works of art, capturing the essence of Nova Scotia's landscape and everyday life.

Her early years in Nova Scotia saw her developing a unique approach to the Maritime landscape. Initially missing the familiar scenery of her Ontario homeland, she began by painting what she called "potato people" - friendly figures she encountered at Peggy's Cove . These works helped her feel less lonely while developing her watercolor technique.

Over time, Laking developed a deep appreciation for Nova Scotia's varied landscape. She is particularly drawn to shorelines, seascapes, and houses, especially front doors with porches and inviting chairs . Flower-decked porches became signature pieces for the artist and were immensely successful . Her winter paintings reveal a preoccupation with reflection and an ongoing dialogue between light and shade, with many works featuring views seen through windows .

In her most recent paintings, form and color dominate the identity of the object painted. The colors are getting stronger, the subjects simplified, and the composition more abstract or sometimes hyper-realistic . For the viewer, these paintings, with their rich, robust colors, are a visual feast .

## Notable Works and Exhibitions

Laking's body of work includes numerous notable paintings that have been exhibited provincially, nationally, and internationally [4]. Her work has been acquired by several important institutions, including the permanent collection of the Art Gallery of Nova Scotia, which holds her painting "Brigitte's Window" (1984) . The Nova Scotia Art Bank also holds several of her works, including "Lily" (1984) and "Farewell to Summer" (1987) .

Laking's exhibition history includes numerous solo shows at prestigious venues. Following her 1982 debut at Mount Saint Vincent University Art Gallery, she went on to exhibit at the Royal Botanical Gardens in Hamilton, Ontario, and the Art Gallery of Nova Scotia [6]. Her work has also been featured in group exhibitions including "Following the Vision," which showcased six Nova Scotia artists including Tom Forrestall, Robert Pope, Alex Livingston, Dawn MacNutt, and Heather MacLeod [7].

In 2015, Laking was featured as the January Artist of the Month by Visual Arts Nova Scotia [1]. Her work has been recognized through various awards and honors, including being selected by Domtar to be one of fourteen Canadian Artists featured in their 1990 Calendar Agenda .

## Literary Works

Beyond her visual art, Laking has also made contributions to literature. She has written a play titled "Invisible Prisons," which was produced in 2013 and 2014 [6]. Additionally, she illustrated two children's books for the Nova Scotia Department of Natural Resources: "The Brook and the Woodcutter" (1979), an early environmental warning story, and "The Man Who Couldn't Stop Sneezing" (1982) .

Her most significant literary contribution is "The Painted Province: Nova Scotia Through an Artist's Eyes," published in 2020 by Pottersfield Press . This book features 40 regions of Nova Scotia, each illustrated with her paintings and accompanied by her personal commentary [8]. The book includes GPS coordinates for 39 of the paintings, encouraging readers to visit the actual locations where the paintings were created .

Laking has also written articles about rural life and worked for the CBC . Her writing, like her painting, reflects her deep connection to Nova Scotia and her ability to find beauty and meaning in everyday experiences.

## Personal Life

Laking's personal life has been marked by both creative fulfillment and significant challenges. After graduating from the University of Guelph in 1972, she married and moved to Nova Scotia with her husband, settling in Bass River on the shore of the Bay of Fundy in Colchester County . Between 1980 and 1985, they had three children: Kelsey, Danica, and Yolande [9].

The artist has described her early married life as difficult, with her keen imagination and intense absorption in her art helping ensure her survival through challenging circumstances . In 1999, at almost fifty years old, she spent twelve days alone on Quirpon Island, just off Newfoundland, an experience she has described as pivotal . During this time, she realized she could survive alone, a realization that eventually led her to leave her marriage .

In 2006, Laking entered a new marriage and overcame a serious health issue, which she describes as leading to an "explosion of creativity" . This period saw her expanding her artistic practice to include photography, rug hooking, wool dyeing, and other mediums .

Throughout her life, Laking has maintained a rigorous work schedule. For many years, she rose at 4:45 a.m. daily, settling in her studio by 5:00 a.m. for four hours of uninterrupted work before attending to her children and the business aspects of her art . This discipline, combined with her natural talent and creative vision, has enabled her to produce a substantial body of work.

## Community Involvement and Philanthropy

Laking has been actively involved in various community organizations and boards, demonstrating her commitment to the arts and community welfare. She has served on the board of the Nova Scotia-based Robert Pope Foundation since 1997 [5]. This foundation, which bridges art and medicine, was established as a service organization and memorial to artist Robert Pope, who died of cancer in his early thirties [5].

Her board service has been extensive, including roles with the Elizabeth Bishop Society, the Ivar Mendez International Foundation, Third Place Transition House, Ships Company Theatre, and the Art Gallery of Nova Scotia . She is also a founding member of PLANS (Professional Living Artists of Nova Scotia) [6].

In addition to her board work, Laking has contributed to her community through various artistic initiatives. She has been a strong advocate for making art accessible to all, regularly holding open house exhibitions at her gallery and welcoming people from all walks of life . For 25 years, she has held an annual fall open house exhibition, which has become a much-anticipated event in the community .

## Recognition and Awards

Laking's contributions to the arts have been recognized through numerous awards and honors. She is the recipient of the "Woman of Excellence Award" from the Halifax Club [2]. In recognition of her service to the community and the arts, she was awarded the Queen's Diamond Jubilee Medal [2].

She has also received the Cobequid Arts Council Lifetime Achievement Award [2], recognizing her significant contributions to the arts in the Cobequid region of Nova Scotia.

Her work has been featured in various publications and media. Her painting "Brigitte's Window" was chosen as the cover for the 1985 Maritime Tel and Tel Directory . She was selected by Domtar to be one of fourteen Canadian Artists featured in their 1990 Calendar Agenda . Her work has also been featured in International Artists Magazine in August 2000 .

## Legacy and Influence

Laking's artistic legacy extends beyond her individual works to her influence on the Nova Scotia arts community. As a founding member of PLANS and through her extensive board service, she has helped shape the artistic landscape of the province . Her commitment to making art accessible, through her gallery exhibitions and community involvement, has inspired countless others to appreciate and create art.

Her technical innovations, particularly in the area of watercolor technique, have influenced other artists. Her development of the white-on-white technique and her approach to capturing light and shadow in watercolor have been studied and emulated by other practitioners of the medium .

Through her book "The Painted Province," Laking has created a lasting record of Nova Scotia's landscape and culture . The book serves not only as a collection of beautiful art but also as a cultural document and travel guide, encouraging readers to explore and appreciate the natural beauty of the province .

## Conclusion

Joy Laking's career as an artist spans more than five decades, during which she has created a substantial body of work that captures the essence of Nova Scotia's landscape and culture. Her meticulous attention to detail, technical virtuosity in watercolor, and ability to find beauty in everyday scenes have established her as one of Nova Scotia's most respected artists.

Through her paintings, writings, and community involvement, Laking has made significant contributions to the cultural life of Nova Scotia. Her work is held in important collections, her exhibitions are eagerly anticipated, and her influence on the arts community is substantial. As she continues to paint and explore new artistic directions, her legacy as an artist who combines technical skill with deep emotional connection to her subject matter ensures her place in Canadian art history.

Her journey from a young artist in Ontario to a celebrated Nova Scotia painter reflects not only personal growth but also a profound connection to place. Through her art, Laking has given the world a window into the beauty of Nova Scotia, inviting viewers to see the province through her passionate and observant eyes.

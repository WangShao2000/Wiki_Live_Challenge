# Lux (Doctor Who)

## Lead

**Lux** is a name associated with multiple characters in the British science fiction television series *Doctor Who*. The primary character is **Lux Imperator**, the God of Light and member of the Pantheon of Discord, who first appeared in the 2025 episode "Lux" (Season 15, Episode 2) [1]. A secondary character, **Strackman Lux**, appears in the episodes "Silence in the Library" and "Forest of the Dead" [2]. This article focuses primarily on Lux Imperator, the more recent character, while also providing information about Strackman Lux.

## Lux Imperator

### Character Overview

Lux Imperator, also known as **Mr. Ring-A-Ding**, is the titular main antagonist of the Doctor Who episode "Lux" [1]. He is a member of the Pantheon of Discord, serving as the God of Light [1]. In the episode, he appears as a living rubber hose cartoon character who has trapped 15 people in film reels .

The character is voiced by **Alan Cumming**, who described his role: "It is a truism that villains always get the best lines, and are always much more juicy to play. I guess my favourite kind of villains are the ones that are misunderstood - while Mr Ring-A-Ding does not have any redeeming qualities, he does start out quite fun and endearing, so there is an arc to him that I enjoyed" . This is not Cumming's first role in the Whoniverse, as he previously played King James I in "The Witchfinders" .

### Appearance and Personality

As Mr. Ring-A-Ding, Lux presents himself as a jovial, innocuous cartoon character with a knack for laughs . He maintains an air of cordiality, gleefully introducing himself with a song and making polite conversation . However, there are breaks in this persona where his true character is shown, such as when he talks about his desire for a physical body .

In reality, Lux is **conniving, manipulative, and at times sadistic** . He is perfectly willing to utilize people's personal struggles to his advantage, as demonstrated by him creating a replica of Reginald Pye's wife to blackmail him into playing his projector .

### Powers and Abilities

Lux Imperator possesses several abilities including:


* **Manipulation** - He can manipulate people's emotions and memories

* **Size manipulation** - He can change his size at will

* **Shapeshifting** - He can transform into different forms

* **Photokinesis** - Control over light

* **Pyrokinesis** - Control over fire

### Role in the Episode "Lux"

The episode begins with the **Fifteenth Doctor** (Shuti Gatwa) and his companion **Belinda Chandra** (Varada Sethu) arriving in Miami, 1952, when the TARDIS is unable to land in 2025 . They discover an abandoned cinema where 15 people have mysteriously disappeared .

Inside the cinema, they encounter Mr. Ring-A-Ding, who reveals himself to be Lux Imperator, the God of Light [3]. He tells the Doctor: "I am the dazzle at the heart of the pantheon. I am the glint in the eyes of the mad. I am the last thing you see before you fall into the abyss. You think you can escape from me? You think you can escape light itself?" [3]

Lux uses film projectors to turn the Doctor and Belinda into cartoon characters . They must find a way to escape his traps, which include:


* Acquiring physical dimensions by demonstrating emotional dimensions

* Speeding up the celluloid

* Pointing out continuity errors

* Eventually climbing out of the screen itself

During their adventure, they meet three fake fans created by Lux to trap them, but these fans use their knowledge of Doctor Who to help the Doctor and Belinda escape .

### Defeat and Ending

The Doctor and Belinda eventually escape from Lux's film traps. Lux then begins to drain the Doctor's regeneration energy to become more real . Belinda attempts to set the films on fire to create an explosion, but Reginald Pye does it instead, sacrificing himself .

The explosion creates a large hole in the theater that gives Lux Imperator an enormous amount of light. Rather than being destroyed, Lux ascends to become "**everything and nothing**" - he merges with the universe's light . The Doctor simply gives him "two billion times more energy than the biggest nuclear bomb on planet Earth," and Lux floats off into the cosmos, his cartoon tears dissipating in space .

### Connection to the Pantheon of Discord

Lux Imperator is a member of the **Pantheon of Discord**, a group of chaos gods that also includes:


* The Toymaker

* Maestro (from the previous season)

* Sutekh

He mentions that the Doctor has become renowned as the destroyer of the pantheon . The episode is described as "very much a sequel to 'The Devil's Chord'" (featuring Maestro) due to similarities in period setting, aesthetics rooted in old-fashioned entertainment, and the Doctor facing off against a capricious god-like entity .

## Strackman Lux


**Strackman Lux** is a human character from the 51st century who appears in the episodes "Silence in the Library" and "Forest of the Dead" [2]. He is the grandson of **Felman Lux**, who built the Library planet and founded the Felman Lux Corporation .

### Role in "Silence in the Library"

Strackman Lux funded an archaeological expedition to the Library, which had been locked down for 100 years with 4,022 visitors having disappeared without a trace [2]. Initially, his motivations appear to be protecting his family's patent for the Library .

However, it is later revealed that his true motivation was **familial concern** for his aunt **Charlotte Lux** (CAL), the library computer . He was the last of the Lux family to know about Charlotte and organized the expedition to ensure she was safe [2].


Strackman is portrayed as a wealthy, determined individual who is the apparent head of the Felman Lux Corporation at the time of the expedition . His personal assistant is Miss Evangelista .

### Survival and Legacy

Strackman is the **only survivor** of the expedition to the Library with River Song [2]. He was working at the Library terminal at the main reception desk when the people CAL had rescued teleported in . He was described as "stunned to see them but overjoyed that the Doctor was able to rescue the 4,022 people who had been trapped in the Library data core for 100 years" .

## Etymology

The surname "Lux" is a play on words, as it means "**light**" in Latin [2]. This is particularly fitting for both characters:


* Lux Imperator is literally the God of Light [3]

* The Library was invaded by creatures that use darkness (the Vashta Nerada) [2]

## Reception

### Critical Response

The character of Lux Imperator has received mixed critical response. While praised for his visual design as a "spot-on evocation of classic Fleischer Studios cartoons," some critics felt he "just doesn't quite have the juice of Jinkx Monsoon's powerhouse Maestro" .

The episode "Lux" itself was described as "a visual delight of an episode that has a ball playing with the possibilities of its premise" . Particular praise was given to the sequence where the Doctor and Belinda become cartoons and must find creative ways to escape .

### Fan Reception

The character has gained popularity among fans, as evidenced by the numerous fan works on platforms like Archive of Our Own . The character's complex nature and Alan Cumming's performance have been highlighted as particular strengths.

## Legacy

### Cultural Impact

The character has made an impact on Doctor Who lore, particularly in establishing the Pantheon of Discord as an ongoing threat. The mid-credits scene featuring the return of the three fake fans has sparked fan theories and discussions about the nature of reality in the Whoniverse .

### Future Appearances

As of the current season, Lux Imperator's fate is ambiguous. While he merged with the universe's light, his status as a member of the Pantheon suggests potential for future appearances or references. The character's unique nature as both a cartoon and a god-like entity provides many storytelling possibilities.

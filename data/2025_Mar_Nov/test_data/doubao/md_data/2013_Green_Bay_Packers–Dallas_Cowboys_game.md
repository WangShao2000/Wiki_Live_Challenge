# 2013 Green Bay Packers–Dallas Cowboys game

The **2013 Green Bay Packers–Dallas Cowboys game** was a National Football League (NFL) regular season contest played on December 15, 2013, at AT\&T Stadium in Arlington, Texas. The game featured the **Green Bay Packers** (7-6-1) defeating the **Dallas Cowboys** (7-7) by a score of **37-36** in one of the most dramatic comebacks in NFL history [1]. The Packers overcame a **23-point halftime deficit** (26-3) to secure victory, matching the largest comeback in franchise history [2].

## Game background and context

The game was significant for both teams' playoff aspirations. The Packers, despite missing their starting quarterback Aaron Rodgers who suffered a broken collarbone in Week 8 against the Chicago Bears [3], entered the contest with a 7-6-1 record, trailing the 8-6 Chicago Bears in the NFC North division . The Cowboys, with a 7-7 record, were chasing the 8-6 Philadelphia Eagles in the NFC East division .

The Packers were led by backup quarterback **Matt Flynn**, who was making his second consecutive start in place of the injured Rodgers . The Cowboys were coached by Jason Garrett and featured quarterback Tony Romo, who was playing with a herniated disc in his lower back .

## Game summary

### First half

The first half belonged entirely to the Cowboys, who dominated both sides of the ball. The Cowboys scored on their opening drive, with kicker Dan Bailey converting a 47-yard field goal at 10:56 of the first quarter . The Packers responded with a long field goal of their own, as Mason Crosby connected on a 57-yard attempt at 7:40 .

The Cowboys then took control, scoring 20 unanswered points. Bailey added field goals of 23, 43, and 50 yards , while quarterback Tony Romo threw a 25-yard touchdown pass to tight end Jason Witten at the end of the first quarter . Running back DeMarco Murray capped the first-half scoring with a 1-yard touchdown run with 24 seconds left in the second quarter, giving the Cowboys a commanding 26-3 lead at halftime .

### Second half

The Packers came out of halftime with renewed energy and immediately struck back. On the first play of the third quarter, rookie running back **Eddie Lacy** burst through the line for a 60-yard run . Three plays later, Matt Flynn connected with wide receiver Jordy Nelson on a 13-yard touchdown pass, cutting the deficit to 26-10 .

The Cowboys added a field goal early in the fourth quarter to extend their lead to 29-17 , but the Packers' comeback was just beginning. Flynn led the Packers on a remarkable second-half performance, throwing four touchdown passes in the final two quarters . He connected with tight end Andrew Quarless (3 yards), running back James Starks (11 yards), and wide receiver James Jones (3 yards) for touchdowns .

### Fourth quarter and final minutes

With the score tied at 29-29 early in the fourth quarter, the Cowboys appeared to have sealed the victory when Romo threw a 5-yard touchdown pass to wide receiver Dez Bryant, giving them a 36-29 lead with 7:55 remaining .

However, the Packers refused to quit. Flynn engineered another touchdown drive, culminating in a 3-yard touchdown pass to James Jones with 4:17 left, cutting the deficit to 36-31 .

The decisive moment came with 2:58 remaining when the Cowboys were attempting to run out the clock. Facing a second-and-6 from their own 35-yard line, Romo threw an interception to Packers cornerback **Sam Shields** . The Packers capitalized on the turnover, driving 50 yards in seven plays. Eddie Lacy scored the game-winning touchdown on a 1-yard run with 1:31 remaining [4]. The Packers failed on the two-point conversion attempt, leaving the score at 37-36 .

On the Cowboys' ensuing drive, Romo was intercepted again, this time by Packers cornerback **Tramon Williams** on the second play, effectively ending the game [5].

## Key players and performances

### Green Bay Packers

**Matt Flynn** delivered a remarkable second-half performance, completing 26 of 39 passes for 299 yards, four touchdowns, and one interception . His quarterback rating of 113.1 was particularly impressive given that he had struggled in his previous start on Thanksgiving Day . Flynn's four touchdown passes in the second half matched the Packers' largest comeback in franchise history [6].

**Eddie Lacy** had a monster game, rushing for 141 yards on 21 carries, including the game-winning 1-yard touchdown run . His 60-yard run on the first play of the second half provided the spark the Packers needed to begin their comeback .

The Packers' defense, despite giving up 26 points in the first half, made crucial plays when needed. Cornerbacks Sam Shields and Tramon Williams each recorded interceptions in the final three minutes of the game [5]. Shields had a particularly outstanding game, surrendering just four catches for 67 yards in 10 targets, with three pass breakups .

### Dallas Cowboys

**Tony Romo** completed 29 of 48 passes for 358 yards, two touchdowns, and two interceptions . While his two late interceptions proved costly, Romo had several impressive throws throughout the game, including a 25-yard touchdown pass to Jason Witten and a 5-yard touchdown pass to Dez Bryant .

**DeMarco Murray** was highly effective on the ground, rushing for 134 yards on 18 carries at an average of 7.4 yards per attempt . His 1-yard touchdown run in the second quarter gave the Cowboys their 23-point halftime lead .

**Dez Bryant** was the Cowboys' leading receiver with 11 catches for 153 yards and one touchdown . However, his decision to walk off the field while there was still time remaining in the game drew criticism [7].

**Jason Witten** caught four passes for 71 yards and one touchdown, extending his streak of consecutive games with at least one reception to 85 games [8].

## Coaching decisions and controversies

The game was marred by several controversial coaching decisions, particularly by Cowboys head coach Jason Garrett. Despite having a 23-point halftime lead and an effective running game (DeMarco Murray averaged 7.4 yards per carry), the Cowboys failed to run the ball effectively in the second half . Murray had 83 yards on 11 carries in the first half but received only seven more touches in the second half .

Garrett's decision to use a "smoke" or "flash" play (a run play with a pass option) on the Cowboys' penultimate drive also drew criticism. With the Cowboys leading 36-31 and attempting to run out the clock, Romo was instructed to throw if the Packers stacked the box against the run . When Romo saw eight Packers defenders in the box, he threw the pass, which was intercepted by Sam Shields .

After the game, Garrett appeared to place blame on Romo, stating that Romo "probably should have run the ball" on the play . However, this was widely viewed as a disingenuous attempt to deflect criticism, as the play was designed to become a pass if the defense stacked the box .

## Game significance

The Packers' victory was significant for several reasons. First, it kept their playoff hopes alive, as they remained one game behind the Chicago Bears in the NFC North division . Second, it marked the largest comeback in Packers franchise history, matching their previous record set in 1945 [6].

For the Cowboys, the loss had devastating implications. Not only did it eliminate their chances of winning the NFC East division title outright, but it also damaged their wild-card playoff hopes . The defeat was seen as another example of the Cowboys' inability to close out games and was particularly damaging to coach Jason Garrett's reputation .

The game also highlighted the importance of having quality depth at the quarterback position. Despite missing their franchise quarterback Aaron Rodgers, the Packers were able to win with backup Matt Flynn, who had been widely criticized after a poor performance on Thanksgiving .

## Conclusion

The 2013 Green Bay Packers–Dallas Cowboys game will be remembered as one of the most dramatic comebacks in NFL history. The Packers' ability to overcome a 23-point halftime deficit to win 37-36 demonstrated remarkable resilience and determination. Matt Flynn's four touchdown passes in the second half, combined with Eddie Lacy's game-winning touchdown run, secured a memorable victory for the Packers.

For the Cowboys, the game represented another missed opportunity and highlighted ongoing issues with coaching decisions and the defense's inability to protect leads. Tony Romo's two late interceptions, while certainly contributing to the loss, were not the sole reason for the defeat. The Cowboys' failure to run the ball effectively in the second half and their defense's inability to stop the Packers' comeback proved costly.

The game had significant implications for both teams' playoff aspirations. While the Packers went on to win their next game and secure a wild-card berth, they were eliminated in the first round by the San Francisco 49ers. The Cowboys, meanwhile, lost their next game and were eliminated from playoff contention, finishing with an 8-8 record for the third consecutive season.

The 2013 Packers-Cowboys game stands as a testament to the unpredictable nature of professional football and the importance of playing a full 60 minutes. It will long be remembered as one of the most exciting games of the 2013 NFL season.

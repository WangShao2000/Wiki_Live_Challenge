# Tony Hawk's Pro Skater 2 (Game Boy Advance video game)

## 1. Introduction

Tony Hawk's Pro Skater 2 for the Game Boy Advance (GBA) is a 2001 sports video game developed by Vicarious Visions and published by Activision [1]. It is the handheld adaptation of the critically acclaimed console game Tony Hawk's Pro Skater 2, which was originally developed by Neversoft . The GBA version was released on May 30, 2001, in North America, June 22, 2001, in Europe, and December 14, 2001, in Japan [2].

The game is notable for successfully translating the 3D skateboarding experience to the GBA's 2D hardware through an innovative isometric perspective . Players control professional skateboarders performing tricks and completing objectives across various levels, earning cash to purchase upgrades and new equipment .

## 2. Development and release

### 2.1 Development team

The Game Boy Advance version of Tony Hawk's Pro Skater 2 was developed by Vicarious Visions, an American video game developer [1]. This marked Vicarious Visions' first major project in the Tony Hawk series, as the console versions were developed by Neversoft . The game's development was led by Jason Uyeda as director, with Scott Pease serving as producer .

The development team faced significant technical challenges in porting the 3D console game to the GBA's 2D hardware. To overcome these limitations, they implemented an isometric perspective with prerendered 3D backgrounds and 300-polygon 3D character models . This technical approach allowed the game to maintain the core gameplay mechanics while adapting to the handheld's hardware constraints.

### 2.2 Release dates and regions

The game was released in three main regions with staggered release dates:



* North America: May 30, 2001 [2]

* Europe: June 22, 2001 [2]

* Japan: December 14, 2001 (released as "SK8: Tony Hawk no Pro Skater 2") [2]

In Japan, the game was published by Success rather than Activision, and received a slightly different title . The staggered release dates allowed the development team to refine the game based on early market feedback and regional preferences.

### 2.3 Technical specifications

The GBA version featured several technical innovations to bring the Tony Hawk experience to the handheld platform:



* **Graphics**: The game used an isometric perspective with prerendered 3D backgrounds and 300-polygon 3D character models

* **Performance**: The game ran at a smooth 60 frames per second

* **Sound**: The game utilized Shin'en's GAX sound engine, with music composed by Shin'en developers [3]

* **Controls**: The game used a modified control scheme adapted to the GBA's limited button layout: L for kickflips, R for grinds, B for jumping, and A for grab moves

## 3. Gameplay

### 3.1 Overview

Tony Hawk's Pro Skater 2 for the GBA retains the core gameplay mechanics from the console versions while adapting to the handheld platform's limitations . The game features a third-person perspective with a fixed camera, allowing players to control professional or fictional skaters through various skateboarding challenges .

The game's physics system maintains a "loose grip on physics for the sake of gameplay quality," allowing players to perform unrealistic but entertaining trick combinations . This design philosophy prioritizes fun and accessibility over realistic physics simulation.

### 3.2 Game modes

The game offers several distinct game modes:

**Career Mode** is the main single-player experience where players guide their chosen skater through various skate spots with a checklist of goals and competitions . Instead of collecting tapes as in the first game, skaters earn money that can be used to buy decks, upgrade stats, and purchase new tricks . Beating this mode unlocks additional skaters, cheats, and new levels.

**Single Session** mode allows players to play in a level for a high score with a two-minute time limit. After completing a session with a good enough score, players can enter their initials for the high score table .

**Free Skate** mode permits skaters to stay in levels with no time limit, allowing players to practice, find secrets, or explore missing gaps .

### 3.3 Skaters and customization

The game features 13 professional skateboarders and 3 secret characters [4]/Characters). The professional skaters include:



* Tony Hawk

* Bob Burnquist

* Steve Caballero

* Kareem Campbell

* Rune Glifberg

* Eric Koston

* Bucky Lasek

* Rodney Mullen

* Chad Muska

* Andrew Reynolds

* Geoff Rowley

* Elissa Steamer

* Jamie Thomas

The three secret characters are Spider-Man (via a licensing agreement with Marvel Comics), Mindy (exclusive to the GBA version), and McSkib [4]/Characters).

Each skater has unique attributes in ten categories: Air, Hangtime, Ollie, Speed, Spin, Landing, Switch, Rail Balance, Lip Balance, and Manuals . Players can customize their skater's appearance by choosing different skateboard graphics and adjusting truck settings to make the skateboard easier or harder to turn .

### 3.4 Tricks and scoring system

The game features an extensive trick system allowing players to perform various skateboarding maneuvers . While airborne, skaters can execute two types of moves:

**Flip tricks** are performed by pressing a direction with the A and B buttons simultaneously . **Grab tricks** are executed by pressing a direction with the A button only. Most grab tricks and some flip tricks can be held for additional points .

The scoring system rewards players for stringing together combos. Performing two or more tricks creates a combo multiplier that increases with each successful trick . The game also introduces "landing ratings" where the straightness of a landing affects the score - landing straight earns a "green perfect" rating with a 50% score increase, while landing diagonally results in a "red sloppy" rating with a 30% score reduction .

### 3.5 Levels and objectives

The game features seven levels in total: four regular levels, two competition levels, and one secret level . The levels include:



1. **Hangar - Meacham Field, Texas**

* Objectives: 10,000/25,000/75,000 points; collect SKATE letters; hit 5 barrels; find 5 pilot wings; 3 hangtime gaps; nosegrind over the pipe; secret tape

1. **School II - Southern California**

* Objectives: 15,000/40,000/100,000 points; collect SKATE letters; wallride 5 bells; find 5 hall passes; 3 Roll Call rails; kickflip TC's roof; secret tape

1. **Marseille - Marseille, France** (Competition level)

* First competition level, requires \$3,000 to access

1. **Warehouse - Troy, New York**

* Objectives: 20,000/50,000/150,000 points; collect SKATE letters; destroy 5 crates; find 5 spray cans; 3 Oldskool gaps; 5-0 the Big Rail; secret tape

1. **NY City - New York City, New York**

* Objectives: 50,000/125,000/250,000 points; collect SKATE letters; ollie 3 hydrants; find 5 subway tokens; 3 Blue Cow gaps; 50-50 TC's Sculpture; secret tape

1. **Skatestreet - Ventura, California** (Competition level)

* Second competition level, requires \$35,000 to access

1. **Rooftops - Boston, Massachusetts** (Secret level)

* Unlockable level, requires beating Career Mode 100% with all skaters (except Spider-Man and Mindy)

### 3.6 Special features

The GBA version includes several special features that differentiate it from other handheld versions:

**The Special Meter** fills up by landing tricks without bailing. When full, the skater enters special mode where attributes are enhanced and special moves can be performed . Special moves require complex inputs but reward significantly higher points.

**Gap system**: Gaps or transfers are spots in levels that reward points for skating off certain objects. They appear in the combo list in blue text and add to the multiplier .

**Kid Mode** can be toggled in options, making balancing and landing easier for younger players .

**Trick Zoom** zooms the camera in when performing special moves, providing a better view of the action .

## 4. Reception and reviews

### 4.1 Critical reception

Tony Hawk's Pro Skater 2 for the GBA received universal critical acclaim upon release [2]. Review aggregator website Metacritic identified it as having received universal acclaim, labeling it a "must-play" . The game maintained an aggregate score of 98/100 on Metacritic, making it the second highest-rated game of all time at the time, behind only The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time [2].

Reviewers particularly praised the game's technical achievements. Nintendo World Report stated: "It's that simple. Not that the GBA needs any help, but Tony Hawk is an awesome game. Actually, it's one of the most flawless games in recent memory" [5]. The publication noted that the game "translates the style and sense of the game accurately into an isometric (pseudo-3D or 2.5D) game" [6].

Kidzworld praised the game's graphics, stating: "The GBA isn't supposed to be able to do 3D graphics but that didn't stop the Hawk - he pulled off sweet 3D skater graphics. I don't think even Nintendo knows how to make games look this good" [7].

However, some reviewers noted minor criticisms. HonestGamers mentioned that the sound quality "isn't actually that great, often sounding somewhat 'tinny', especially when you hear it on headphones" [8]. Some reviewers also noted that the isometric perspective took time to adjust to, with one reviewer stating: "The first time you play it's really catastrophic! The handling is delicate and it will take a fairly long time to adapt" [9].

### 4.2 Awards and nominations

The GBA version was nominated for "Outstanding Achievement in Handheld Game" at the 2002 Academy of Interactive Arts & Sciences awards [10]. However, it lost to Advance Wars.

### 4.3 Commercial performance

While specific sales figures for the GBA version are not available, the overall Tony Hawk's Pro Skater 2 franchise sold approximately 7.25 million copies across all platforms . The GBA version was part of Activision's strategy to maximize revenue by releasing the game on multiple platforms, including handhelds.

The game was commercially successful enough to warrant a place on multiple "best GBA games" lists. It was included in IGN's list of "25 best GBA games" [11] and Cultured Vultures' "50 best GBA games of all time," with the latter noting: "Porting a game as vast and complex as Tony Hawk's Pro Skater 2 to the much simpler Game Boy Advance surprisingly worked out well for everyone concerned" [12].

## 5. Legacy and impact

### 5.1 Technical innovations

The GBA version of Tony Hawk's Pro Skater 2 is remembered for its technical achievements in bringing 3D gameplay to a 2D handheld [13]. The game was elected as the first handheld game in 3D and was the first in the series to feature create-a-skater options [13].

The game's use of prerendered 3D backgrounds combined with 3D character models running at 60 frames per second was considered groundbreaking for the GBA at the time . This technical approach influenced future GBA games that attempted to bring 3D experiences to the handheld platform.

### 5.2 Cultural impact

The Tony Hawk's Pro Skater series, including the GBA version, had a significant cultural impact on video games and popular culture [11]. The games helped popularize extreme sports video games and introduced many players to the world of professional skateboarding.

The GBA version's success demonstrated the viability of porting console-quality games to handheld platforms, encouraging other developers to attempt similar ambitious ports. The game's inclusion of licensed music (though adapted for the GBA) and professional athletes also set a standard for sports games on handhelds.

### 5.3 Franchise evolution

The success of Tony Hawk's Pro Skater 2 across all platforms, including the GBA, led to numerous sequels and spin-offs in the series [14]. The GBA version specifically influenced how future Tony Hawk games would be designed for handheld platforms.

Later entries in the series would expand on features introduced in Tony Hawk's Pro Skater 2, including improved create-a-skater modes, more complex trick systems, and expanded career modes. The GBA version's successful adaptation of console gameplay to a handheld served as a template for future handheld sports games.

## 6. Conclusion

Tony Hawk's Pro Skater 2 for the Game Boy Advance stands as a remarkable achievement in handheld gaming history. Developed by Vicarious Visions and published by Activision, the game successfully brought the console skateboarding experience to the GBA through innovative technical solutions and thoughtful design decisions.

The game's critical success, with a 98/100 score on Metacritic and universal acclaim from reviewers, demonstrated that ambitious console ports to handhelds were not only possible but could achieve the same high standards as their console counterparts [2]. The game's commercial success and cultural impact helped establish the Tony Hawk's Pro Skater series as a cornerstone of sports gaming.

The technical innovations employed in the GBA version, including the use of isometric perspective, 3D character models, and 60fps performance, pushed the boundaries of what was possible on the Game Boy Advance . These innovations influenced future handheld game development and demonstrated the potential of the platform for complex 3D experiences.

Today, Tony Hawk's Pro Skater 2 for the Game Boy Advance is remembered as one of the best handheld games of its era, maintaining a strong fan base and appearing on numerous "best of" lists [11]. The game's legacy continues through modern re-releases and remasters of the Tony Hawk's Pro Skater series, ensuring that new generations of players can experience the game that helped define handheld sports gaming.

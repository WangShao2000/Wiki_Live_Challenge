# Skiddaw Group SSSI

Skiddaw Group SSSI is a **Site of Special Scientific Interest** (SSSI) in the Lake District High Fells of Cumbria, England . Designated for its **flora and fauna**, as well as its **geology**, it covers an area of 10,256.3 hectares (39.600 sq mi) . The site is centered near Great Calva and has a roughly irregular circular shape . It is part of the Lake District High Fells Special Area of Conservation (SAC), which was designated in 2005 .

## Location and geography

Skiddaw Group SSSI is located in the northern part of the Lake District National Park . The site's coordinates are 54°40′08″N 3°06′07″W (54.669°N 3.102°W) . The nearest town is Keswick, Cumbria . The site's elevation ranges from 126 meters (413 feet) at its lowest point to 930 meters (3,050 feet) at its highest point, with an average elevation of 490 meters (1,610 feet) .

The high ground of the SSSI creates a **watershed** between the Caldew Operational Catchment, where water flows north towards Carlisle, and the Ellen and West Coast and Derwent Operational Catchments, which flow towards the west coast at Workington and Maryport .

The SSSI includes several well-known Lakeland summits, including Skiddaw itself, Great Calva, Little Calva, and several smaller fells including Sale How . Fells above 2,000 feet (610 m) include High Pike, Carrock Fell, Knott, Great Calva, and Bowscale Fell . The highest peaks are in the Skiddaw area, including Skiddaw itself, Long Side, Carl Side, Little Man, Lonscale Fell and, further east, Blencathra .

## Geology

The Skiddaw Group consists of **sedimentary rock formations** that are the oldest rocks in the Lake District . These rocks are almost wholly Ordovician in age (Tremadoc through Arenig to Llanvirn epochs), though the lowermost beds are possibly of Cambrian age . They were formed as black muds and sands settling on the seabed about 500 million years ago and have since been raised up and crumpled and squeezed .

The complex geology of the area has resulted in the smooth-sided fells of the Skiddaw Group rocks . The Skiddaw Group between Buttermere and Troutbeck is approximately 6,000 feet thick and can be subdivided into four formations which in ascending order are: Hope Beck Slates; Loweswater Flags; Mosser-Kirk Stile Slates; and Latterbarrow Sandstone [1].

### Caldbeck Fells mining area

The northern part of the SSSI includes several sites of former mines, noted today for the presence of **minerals** in spoil heaps and erosion sites . These include:



* **Red Gill Mine** (NY295347, unit 26): Spoil heaps include rare, crystallised lead sulphates, carbonates and phosphates

* **Roughtongill Mine** (NY302344, unit 23): Reveals supergene minerals including copper and zinc varieties

* **Dry Gill Lead Mine** (NY324345, unit 24): Has a unique form of mimetite, known as 'campylite'

* **Burdell Gill** (NY307324, unit 20): "Is of national importance as the only relatively abundant source of the rare arsenic mineral, pharmacosiderite, in Britain"

* **Carrock Mine – Brandy Gill** (NY322338): The mineralisation is tungsten-rich

The Carrock Tungsten Mine (NY323329) and Carrock End Copper Mine are Scheduled Monuments .

### Geological Conservation Review sites

* Carrock Fell (GCR site code 295): Ordovician Igneous Rocks

## Biology and ecology

Skiddaw Group SSSI is designated for its **flora and fauna** . The site supports a diverse range of habitats including blanket bog, heathland, and juniper scrub.

### Habitats

The SSSI contains a variety of habitats that are important for biodiversity:



1. **Blanket bog**: The SSSI has large areas of grass moorland and upland heath, together with more fragmented areas of blanket bog . For example, the upland areas around Bannerdale Beck (SSSI Unit 35) contain 69 hectares (0.27 sq mi) of blanket bog, split across several smaller sites .

2. **Heathland**: The site includes:

* Heather moor which predominates on sloping ground north and west of the River Caldew

* Montane moss-grass heath, with Broad End having "the best developed example \[of montane moss-grass heath in West Cumbria] at about 700 m (2,300 ft)" elevation

* Cross-leaved heath (Erica tetralix)

1. **Juniper scrub**: Common juniper (Juniperus communis) scrub, which is a **nationally rare habitat**, is most extensive on the steep, rocky south-facing slopes of Carrock Fell .

2. **Wetland habitats**: The SSSI includes two glacial tarns - Bowscale Tarn and Scales Tarn (Blencathra) . Bowscale Tarn has been found to contain quillwort (Isoetes lacustris), shoreweed (Littorella uniflora) and water lobelia (Lobelia dortmanna) .

### Plant species

The SSSI supports a diverse range of plant species, including:



* **Hare's-tail cottongrass** (Eriophorum vaginatum)

* **Sphagnum moss** (Sphagnum spp.)

* **Serrated wintergreen** (Orthilia secunda)

* **Bilberry** (Vaccinium spp.)

* **Crowberry** (Empetrum nigrum)

* **Heather** (Calluna vulgaris)

* **Cross-leaved heath** (Erica tetralix)

* **Quillwort** (Isoetes lacustris)

* **Shoreweed** (Littorella uniflora)

* **Water lobelia** (Lobelia dortmanna)

### Animal species

The site supports a number of **rare bird and invertebrate species** . Unit 30 (Skiddaw Forest West) was assessed in 2021 as having "favourable" conditions for invertebrate and breeding bird features .

Bird species recorded in similar upland areas of the Lake District include:



* **Peregrine falcon** [2]

## Special Area of Conservation

Skiddaw Group SSSI is a component of the **Lake District High Fells**, a Special Area of Conservation (SAC) . The SAC covers ten SSSIs and has an area of 27,003 hectares . Within the Skiddaw Group, the SAC documentation identifies a range of habitats :



* **4010 Northern Atlantic wet heaths** with cross-leaved heath (Erica tetralix)

* **4030 European dry heaths**

* **4060 Alpine and Boreal heaths**

* **5130 Common juniper** (Juniperus communis) formations on heaths or calcareous grasslands

* **7130 Blanket bogs**

* **8110 Siliceous scree** of the montane to snow levels (alpine rock-jasmine (Androsace alpina) and red hemp-nettle (Galeopsis ladanum))

This is described as "the most biologically-diverse suite of upland habitats in England" and is of international importance .

## Management and conservation

### SSSI units

The SSSI is divided into a patchwork of "units" which are used as the level of geographic detail for reporting overall features and conditions . Each unit is identified by the Ordnance Survey grid reference at the centre of the unit, its area in hectares, the date it was last surveyed, and the drainage catchment it is located in . For example, Bassenthwaite Common (unit 27) is nearly 700 hectares (2.7 sq mi) centred at NY252299 .

Condition assessments have been carried out on the units:



* Unit 30 (Skiddaw Forest West): Assessed in 2021 as having "favourable" conditions for invertebrate and breeding bird features, but "unfavourable – recovering" blanket bog and upland dry heath habitats

* Unit 31 (Skiddaw Forest East): Assessed as "favourable" in 2021

### Recent conservation developments

In 2024, a significant conservation milestone occurred when the **Cumbria Wildlife Trust** purchased the forest area within the Underskiddaw civil parish area . This essentially comprises SSSI units 30 and 31 together with the summits of Skiddaw (unit 16) and Great Calva (unit 17), forming a 1,200 hectare (4.6 sq mi) nature reserve .

The Cumbria Wildlife Trust's vision is for "a wilder Skiddaw Forest and England's highest nature reserve" . Their long-term **biodiversity and rewilding goals** include :



1. Encouraging tree growth to create tracts of **temperate rainforest**

2. Restoring peatlands

3. Creating around 667 acres of Atlantic rainforest (native temperate rainforest/broadleaved woodland) of a locally-distinctive and appropriate Lake District character over the next 100 years

4. Improving the condition of adjacent heathland and expanding the rare and diminished native montane heath and low-lying scrub on the higher slopes of Skiddaw

5. Repairing and restoring the extensive areas of previously-drained peat bogs in the lower lying areas of Skiddaw Forest

6. Potentially reintroducing native species such as water voles, black grouse, adder and a range of missing montane shrubs, wildflowers and trees into the area

The Trust plans to work closely with local farmers and commoners to identify regenerative farming approaches that will deliver ecosystem and environmental services .

### Carbon sequestration

The conservation work at Skiddaw Forest has significant **carbon sequestration** potential. In partnership with Aviva, the Trust plans to capture over 80,000 tonnes of carbon from 667 acres of native-style woodland over the next 120 years . There are also around 1,000 acres of bogs underlain by deep deposits of peat in the bowl of Skiddaw Forest, estimated to contain around 1.25 million tonnes of carbon . The Trust plans to restore these bogs to protect the existing carbon stores, estimating this will prevent another 80,000 tonnes of carbon from being released .

### Monitoring and research

The Cumbria Wildlife Trust plans to develop a comprehensive programme of **scientific monitoring** with partners from the University of Cumbria to track and demonstrate environmental changes over the next 100 years . This will include:



* Photographic recording

* Aerial drone surveys

* Surveying and monitoring the condition of habitats, vegetation and trends in other species

* Changes in the levels of carbon storage

* Water quality

* Landscape and visual change

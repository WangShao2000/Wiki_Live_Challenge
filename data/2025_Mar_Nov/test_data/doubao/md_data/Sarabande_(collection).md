# Sarabande (collection)

Sarabande is the twenty-ninth collection by British fashion designer Alexander McQueen, made for the spring/summer 2007 season of his eponymous fashion house . The collection was an exploration of fragility and decaying grandeur expressed through floral motifs . It was presented as a runway show in Paris and has since been recognized as one of McQueen's defining collections .

## Collection overview

### Inspiration and themes

Sarabande drew inspiration from multiple sources, creating a complex narrative that explored themes of life, death, and rebirth. The collection was primarily inspired by the life and death of British artist and poet Elizabeth Siddal, muse and wife of Pre-Raphaelite painter Dante Gabriel Rossetti . McQueen crafted a fashion narrative rooted in decay, rebirth, and the fine line between beauty and madness .

The collection also referenced Stanley Kubrick's 1975 period film Barry Lyndon . Rather than focusing on the film's plot, McQueen's show captured the languid elegance of Kubrick's work . The title "Sarabande" refers to Handel's stately composition "Sarabande," which plays under Barry Lyndon's title sequence and throughout the film .

### Design elements

The collection featured a sophisticated palette of muted pastels including ivory, blush, and pale gold, contrasted sharply with bursts of crimson and black . Floral motifs were central to the design aesthetic, but they were not merely decorative—they were mournful, overgrown, or decaying, echoing themes of nature overtaking the human body .

McQueen's technical mastery was evident in the construction of the garments. Dresses were crafted in luxurious fabrics including lace, tulle, and silk organza, layered and frayed as if ravaged by time yet with deliberate precision . Several pieces featured embroidered flowers, often paired with skeletal corsetry or molded bodices that emphasized the fragility of the female form .

One standout gown seemed to be composed entirely of white petals, resembling a bride lost in time, while another was crafted from tissue-thin layers that floated like moth wings . The collection also included a gown constructed from real and artificial flowers, with petals falling as the model walked—a powerful metaphor for the fleeting nature of beauty .

## Runway presentation

The Sarabande show was staged in the round at Cirque d'Hiver in Paris . The presentation was more than a fashion show; it was described as theater . The runway was set in a dark, eerie environment with a string quartet playing live, and models emerged like ghostly apparitions in tattered gowns, swaying to melancholy sounds .

At the heart of the stage sat a glass box filled with fluttering live butterflies, serving both as a symbol of delicate beauty and fleeting life—an unmistakable McQueen signature metaphor . The show culminated in a dramatic finale featuring model Hana Soukupová wearing a lace gown surrounded by fluttering butterflies, leaving the audience breathless—a fusion of ghostly beauty and otherworldly sorrow .

The presentation incorporated elements from the collection's cinematic inspiration. A giant chandelier hung above the string quartet playing Handel's "Sarabande" and a baroque take on The Rolling Stones' "Paint It Black" .

## Critical reception and legacy

Response to Sarabande was positive, with reviewers feeling that it was a successful follow-up to McQueen's previous season . The collection is recalled as one of his defining works . Academic analysis has considered the collection's influences and the floral finale dresses .

The collection became one of the most talked-about and studied McQueen collections. It encapsulated the emotional storytelling that defined his legacy—where fashion was not merely about clothing, but about provoking a visceral, emotional response . In retrospect, the collection stands as a poignant precursor to his later works and a defining moment of 21st-century fashion .

### The Sarabande Foundation

In 2006, Lee Alexander McQueen CBE established the Sarabande Foundation, named after the SS07 show [1]. The charitable foundation was created with the ambition to support the most creatively fearless minds of the future [1]. The foundation was officially named by Lee himself following his Spring/Summer 2007 show .

The foundation provides scholarships to graduate and postgraduate students . When McQueen died in 2010, few outside the fashion industry knew about Sarabande, which had been set up in 2007 . Since then, the foundation has grown to become an important institution supporting emerging talent in the creative industries.

### Exhibition history

Sarabande pieces have been featured in several major exhibitions celebrating McQueen's work. The collection was included in the Victoria and Albert Museum's Alexander McQueen: Savage Beauty exhibition, which ran from March 14 to July 19, 2015 . The exhibition later traveled to other venues including the Metropolitan Museum of Art in New York, where it became one of the most popular exhibitions in the museum's history .

The collection's finale dress, constructed with fresh flowers for the end of the Sarabande show, has been prominently featured in other exhibitions. It was included in the "Roses" exhibition at Alexander McQueen's Bond Street store in London . This exhibition showcased how spectacular pieces including the Red Rose dress in its petal-like 3-D form came to life, with samples of work-in-progress and background research arranged around a studio cutting table .

The Sarabande dress, which was once filled with fresh flowers, now occupies a position at the entrance of exhibitions, alongside more pieces which have flowers and petals trapped between layers of tulle or spilling from necklines and cuffs from the same Spring/Summer 2007 collection .

## Notable pieces

Several individual looks from the Sarabande collection have become iconic in fashion history. One notable ensemble was Look No. 12 on the catwalk, which consisted of a high-waisted viscose pencil skirt appliquéd with white lace birds, paired with a black silk blouse with pleated ruffles to the collar .

The collection also included various shoe designs that are now part of museum collections. The Los Angeles County Museum of Art (LACMA) holds several pairs of shoes from the Sarabande collection . These include leather and silk satin shoes with rayon-lace and leather-covered frog fasteners, and leather and silk net shoes .

The finale dress, constructed from real and artificial flowers with petals that fell as the model walked, is perhaps the most memorable piece from the collection . This garment perfectly exemplified McQueen's ability to create wearable art that challenged conventional notions of beauty and impermanence.

## Conclusion

Sarabande represents a pivotal moment in Alexander McQueen's career and in the history of fashion. Through its exploration of themes of fragility, decay, and rebirth, the collection demonstrated McQueen's unique ability to transform fashion into a powerful form of artistic expression. The collection's influence extends beyond the runway, as evidenced by the establishment of the Sarabande Foundation and its continued impact on supporting emerging creative talent.

McQueen's Spring 2007 collection remains a haunting reminder of the depth fashion can reach when emotion, art, and craftsmanship converge. It wasn't just a runway presentation—it was a requiem . The legacy of Sarabande continues to inspire designers and artists today, serving as a testament to McQueen's visionary approach to fashion design and his profound understanding of the human condition.

# Here for It All

## Lead Section

**Here for It All** is the sixteenth studio album by American singer-songwriter **Mariah Carey**, released on September 26, 2025, through her independent imprint Mariah in partnership with the media company Gamma [1]. The album marks Carey's first full-length studio release in seven years, following her 2018 album Caution . Comprising 11 tracks spanning 39 minutes, the album features a diverse musical palette blending R\&B, pop, funk, disco, soul, and gospel influences [2].

The album debuted at number seven on the US Billboard 200 chart with 47,000 equivalent album units, including 39,000 traditional album sales, making it Carey's nineteenth top-10 entry on the chart . Internationally, Here for It All reached the top twenty in Australia, Belgium, Scotland, and Switzerland, and the top forty in the United Kingdom, Germany, Spain, and Japan [3]. The album also topped multiple charts including Top Album Sales, Top R\&B Albums, and Independent Albums .

Critically, Here for It All received generally positive reviews, with critics praising Carey's vocal maturity, vulnerability, and the album's retro-modern aesthetic . The album features notable collaborations with Anderson .Paak, Kehlani, Shenseea, and gospel legends The Clark Sisters [4]. The lead single "Type Dangerous" became Carey's 50th career hit on the Billboard Hot 100 .

## Background and Production

### Career Context

Here for It All represents a significant milestone in Mariah Carey's career as her first independently released project after decades within the major label system [1]. The album comes after a challenging period in Carey's personal life, including the loss of her mother and sister within hours of each other, a copyright lawsuit over her Christmas hit "All I Want for Christmas Is You" (ultimately dismissed), and her responsibilities as a mother to teenage twins .

The singer has described the album's creation as occurring during a "ballad zone" period in her home recording studio, The Butterfly Lounge . "I didn't really know where they were going, what we were doing," she recalled. "I just knew that I was in ballad zone" . The album was primarily recorded at Conway Recording Studios in Los Angeles, with additional sessions at Honey Snow Studios in Aspen, Colorado, Electric Lady Studios in New York City, and Honey East Studios in Connecticut .

### Production Team

The album was executive produced by **L.A. Reid**, marking his third collaboration with Carey in this role following 2005's The Emancipation of Mimi and 2009's Memoirs of an Imperfect Angel [5]. The production team includes longtime collaborators and new partnerships, with Carey serving as a producer on all tracks alongside various co-producers .

Notable producers include:



* **The Stereotypes** (Jonathan Yip, Ray Romulus, Jeremy Reeves) - worked on the opening track "Mi"

* **HARV** (Felisha King Harvey) - co-produced tracks including "Sugar Sweet" and "Confetti & Champagne" [4]

* **Daniel Moore II** - co-produced multiple tracks including the title track

* **Anderson .Paak** - co-produced tracks including "Play This Song" and "In Your Feelings"

The album also features songwriting contributions from various artists including Brandon Paak Anderson (Anderson .Paak), Kenya Rae Johnson, Jarius Mozee, Jason Pounds, Bernard King, and members of The Clark Sisters .

## Album Content

### Musical Style and Themes

Here for It All presents a **retro-modern aesthetic** that blends classic R\&B and soul influences with contemporary production techniques . The album features what critics have described as "Motown shadings, disco flickers, and gospel lift" . Carey's vocals on the album are characterized by "veteran restraint: fewer pyro notes, more placement, blend, and late-song lift" .

The album's musical diversity spans from plush mid-tempos to devotional climaxes, with critics celebrating its "analog warmth and light-on-its-feet grooves" . The overall sound represents "classic Mariah sensibility without trend-chasing maximalism" . Thematically, the album explores themes of self-empowerment, resilience, and embracing one's authentic self, which Carey has termed "The Era of Mi" .

## Singles and Promotional Campaign

### Lead Single: "Type Dangerous"

"Type Dangerous" was released as the lead single on June 6, 2025 . The song is built around a sample of Eric B. and Rakim's 1986 hit "Eric B. Is President" . Carey explained the sampling process: "I was in a restaurant in Aspen, and I was with \[Anderson .Paak] and a couple of friends. Suddenly 'Eric B. Is President' comes on, and I was like, 'What? I love this song. I haven't heard this song in forever.' We went to the studio the next day and started playing around with sampling it" .

The single became Carey's landmark 50th career hit on the Billboard Hot 100 after debuting at number ninety-five on the chart dated June 21, 2025 . It also topped Billboard's Adult R\&B Airplay chart for three weeks, marking Carey's first number-one on that chart in nearly two decades and her third overall . The song received a remix treatment from DJ Snake, which appears as a bonus track on some editions of the album .

### Second Single: "Sugar Sweet"

"Sugar Sweet" featuring Kehlani and Shenseea was released as the second single on July 25, 2025 . Carey described the collaboration: "I just think \[Shenseea and Kehlani]'s freedom really shows, and they're just present; they're who they are. This makes the record so much younger and more fun, and I just thought it was everything. I've never had a trio before with three strong women" .

There are two versions of "Sugar Sweet": the solo version appears on physical copies of the album, while the collaboration version with Shenseea and Kehlani is available on the digital deluxe edition as a bonus track .

### Third Single: "Play This Song"

"Play This Song" featuring Anderson .Paak was released as the third single on the same day as the album's release . Carey expressed her admiration for Anderson .Paak: "I definitely wanted to work with Anderson because he's so brilliant and amazing at what he does, which is just being a kick-ass musician. But when we got into the studio, we decided we wanted to do something that was kind of '70s, and we did give you that kind of vibe" .

## Critical Reception

### Overall Reception

Upon release, Here for It All received **generally positive reviews** from music critics [3]. Critics praised Carey's vocal maturity, vulnerability on the record, and the album's lyrical content, while some offered criticism of the album's production [3]. The album holds a Metacritic score of 60, indicating "mixed or average reviews" [7].

### Review Highlights

**Rolling Stone** praised the album's "warm, throwback sheen and measured delivery" while noting they wished for "a few bolder swings" . **The Independent** gave the album four out of five stars, applauding "the lovely grit in her delivery and the devotional lift of key moments" while noting she largely stays in her lane .

**The Times** (UK) gave the album two out of five stars with the critical assessment: "five-octave range still there but Mariah needs new songs" . This critique became a recurring theme in several reviews, with multiple outlets arguing that the writing doesn't always rise to the level of the performance .

**Pitchfork** gave the album a score of 7.0/10, describing it as "Mariah Carey's sixteenth studio album \[that] is thoughtfully conceived and, for once, honestly presents the current state of her voice" . The review noted that while Carey's 标志性 "luscious crooning" remains, particularly in chest voice passages, there are "occasional familiar cloud-piercing tones, but rawness appears too frequently to be accidental" .

### Common Praises and Criticisms

Critics consistently praised:



* Carey's vocal performance, particularly her restraint and control

* The album's retro-modern aesthetic and production

* Tasteful collaborations that enhance rather than overshadow Carey

* The emotional depth and vulnerability displayed in songs like "Nothing Is Impossible" and the title track

Common criticisms included:



* A conservative approach that some viewed as "safe" or "restrained"

* Mid-tempo songs that blend together without standout hooks

* Songwriting that doesn't always match the quality of Carey's performances

## Commercial Performance

### United States

In the United States, Here for It All debuted at **number seven** on the Billboard 200 chart with 47,000 equivalent album units in its first week . This marked Carey's nineteenth top-10 entry on the chart, making her the third woman with at least one new top 10-charted album in the 1990s, 2000s, '10s, and '20s, following Madonna and Shania Twain .

The album's first-week performance broke down as follows :



* Pure album sales: just over 39,000 units (reaching #1 on Billboard's Top Album Sales chart)

* Streaming-equivalent albums (SEA): slightly more than 7,000 units (equating to about 9.5 million on-demand streams)

* Track-equivalent albums (TEA): less than 1,000 units

The album also topped multiple other charts including:



* Top Current Album Sales

* Top R\&B Albums

* Independent Albums

## Legacy and Impact

### Cultural Significance

Here for It All represents a **career milestone** for Mariah Carey, marking her transition from major label system to independent release through her own imprint [1]. The album has been interpreted by critics as Carey embracing "The Era of Mi," a period of artistic freedom and self-empowerment .

The album's release coincided with several other career milestones for Carey, including the 20th anniversary of her 2005 album The Emancipation of Mimi and the 30th anniversary of her 1995 album Daydream . In 2025, Carey also received several prestigious awards for the first time in her career, including the Ultimate Icon Award at the BET Awards and the Michael Jackson Vanguard Video Award at the MTV Video Music Awards .

### Industry Impact

The album has been noted for its **multi-format success** in an era dominated by streaming . Carey's ability to sell nearly 40,000 physical copies in one week, along with over 82,000 worldwide across all formats, demonstrates her continued relevance as a physical media seller .

The album's release through Gamma, the media company founded by former Apple executive Larry Jackson, represents a new model for established artists seeking more control over their work . Gamma has also worked with several other notable artists including Snoop Dogg, Usher, Rick Ross, and French Montana .

### Fan Reception

Fans and celebrities alike expressed enthusiasm for the album's announcement. Comedian Billy Eichner wrote "SAVE US MARIAH!!" on Instagram, while her ex-husband Nick Cannon commented "Greatest Of All Time!!" with fire emojis . Singer Tori Kelly also expressed excitement for the new music .

The album's title track, which runs for 6:37, has been highlighted by critics as a "rare and bold" choice for 2025 [9]. Carey herself explained why she named the album after this track: "I named the album Here for It All because I didn't want anyone to skip over that one — even though it's the last one on the album" .

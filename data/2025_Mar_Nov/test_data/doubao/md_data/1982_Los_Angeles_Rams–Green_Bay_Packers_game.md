# 1982 Los Angeles Rams–Green Bay Packers game

The **1982 Los Angeles Rams–Green Bay Packers game** was a National Football League (NFL) regular season contest played on **September 12, 1982**, at Milwaukee County Stadium in Milwaukee, Wisconsin. It was the season opener for both teams in the strike-shortened 1982 NFL season [1]. The game is notable for the **Packers' historic 23-point comeback**, overcoming a 23-0 halftime deficit to win 35-23, which was the largest comeback in Packers history at that time [2].

## Game background

The 1982 NFL season was significantly impacted by a **57-day players' strike** that reduced the regular season from 16 games to just nine games per team [3]. This game marked the opening week of this abbreviated season, with both teams beginning with a 0-0 record . The Rams were coached by Ray Malavasi, while the Packers were led by Bart Starr in his second season as head coach [1].

The game was played at Milwaukee County Stadium, which served as the Packers' secondary home venue from 1953 to 1994 . The official attendance was 53,694 .

## Game summary

### First half

The first half was dominated entirely by the **Los Angeles Rams**, who capitalized on three Packers turnovers to build a commanding 23-0 lead by halftime [4].

In the first quarter, the Rams struck quickly when Gerry Ellis fumbled on a short run, and the Rams recovered and returned the ball to midfield. A 22-yard pass from quarterback Bert Jones to Wendell Tyler set up a short rushing touchdown by Tyler, giving the Rams an early 7-0 lead . After another Packers fumble by quarterback Lynn Dickey, the Rams added a 32-yard field goal by Mike Lansford to extend their lead to 10-0 .

The second quarter saw the Rams continue their dominance. A 43-yard pass from Jones to Willie Miller put the Rams in scoring position, and Jones subsequently completed an 8-yard touchdown pass to Mike Barber, making the score 17-0 . The Rams added two more field goals before halftime - a 29-yarder and a 28-yarder by Lansford - to take a commanding 23-0 lead into the locker room .

The Packers managed only **66 net yards** in the first half and committed four turnovers (two fumbles and two interceptions) [4].

### Second half

The second half saw a **complete reversal of fortunes** as the Packers scored 35 unanswered points to pull off one of the most dramatic comebacks in NFL history [2].

The Packers got on the scoreboard in the third quarter with a 4-yard touchdown pass from Dickey to tight end Paul Coffman. The drive was set up by a defensive pass interference penalty that gave the Packers 28 yards . The Packers struck again shortly thereafter, with Eddie Lee Ivery scoring on a 3-yard run. The key play of this drive was a 42-yard pass from Dickey to Coffman . By the end of the third quarter, the Packers had cut the Rams' lead to 23-14 .

The fourth quarter saw the Packers complete their comeback. After the Rams failed on a long field goal attempt, the Packers took over and drove 66 yards in five plays, with Dickey throwing a 15-yard touchdown pass to James Lofton to make the score 23-21 .

The turning point came on the ensuing kickoff when Rams returner Robert Alexander fumbled, and the Packers recovered at the Los Angeles 10-yard line. Dickey needed only one play to find Coffman in the end zone for a 10-yard touchdown pass, giving the Packers their first lead of the game at 28-23 .

The Packers sealed the victory with a 27-yard touchdown run by Ivery with about three minutes remaining in the game . The Packers defense also contributed to the comeback by holding the Rams to just four first downs in the second half and intercepting the Rams twice while forcing a fumble .

## Aftermath and significance

The Packers' 23-point comeback was the **largest in franchise history** at that time [4]. It remained the Packers' record until December 15, 2013, when they matched it in a game against the Dallas Cowboys [4]. The comeback is widely regarded as one of the most dramatic in NFL history [5].

For the Rams, the loss was particularly devastating as it came in their season opener and set a negative tone for their campaign. The Rams would go on to finish the strike-shortened 1982 season with a 2-7 record [6].

The Packers, meanwhile, used the momentum from this victory to finish the abbreviated season with a 5-3-1 record . The game is still remembered as one of the most remarkable comebacks in Packers history and a highlight of the 1982 season .

The 1982 season was unique due to the players' strike, and this game stands out as one of its most memorable moments [7]. It demonstrated the unpredictable nature of professional football and showcased the Packers' resilience under coach Bart Starr [8].

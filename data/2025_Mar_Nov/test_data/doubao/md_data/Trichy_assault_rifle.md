# Trichy assault rifle

The **Trichy assault rifle** (TAR), sometimes known as the **Tiruchi assault rifle**, is an Indian-made assault rifle chambered for the 7.62×39mm round . It is manufactured by Ordnance Factory Tiruchirappalli (OFT) and is based on the Bulgarian AR-M1, itself a licensed copy of the Kalashnikov AKM . The rifle has gained significant acceptance among Indian paramilitary forces and police departments as a cost-effective indigenous alternative to imported weapons [1].

## Development and design

### Background and development timeline

The development of the Trichy assault rifle began in 2015 at Ordnance Factory Tiruchirappalli [2]. To mark the contribution of the Tiruchi factory in developing the rifle, officials decided to christen it "Trichy assault rifle" [3]. The production line was established in 2017, and the rifle was first unveiled in May 2011 [2].

The TAR is a wholly indigenous copy of the AK-47, developed using experience gained from mass-producing licensed versions of the weapon . The full-sized TAR is believed to be a copy of the Bulgarian AR-M1, itself a licensed copy of the Kalashnikov . Over 500,000 Bulgarian weapons had been imported by India's central, state, and paramilitary forces over the previous decade, and the TAR has helped reduce imports from that East European country .

### Technical specifications

The 7.62×39mm Trichy Assault Rifle is a powerful individual automatic weapon designed to destroy the enemy by fire in single and burst firing modes . It is a single-shot rifle in normal mode and can be used in burst mode as well. By firing in burst mode in short series, group or single targets at a range up to 500 meters can be destroyed, though the destructive effect of the TAR extends up to a range of 1,350 meters .

The rifle features:



* **Caliber**: 7.62×39mm

* **Action**: Gas-operated, rotating bolt

* **Rate of fire**: 600 rounds per minute (full automatic)

* **Muzzle velocity**: 710–715 m/s (2,330–2,350 ft/s)

* **Effective firing range**: 300–350 meters (TAR)

* **Weight**: 3.4 kg (7.5 lb) (TAR); 3.17 kg (7.0 lb) (TriCa carbine)

* **Length**: 900 mm (35 in) with folding stock extended; 650 mm (25.6 in) with stock folded (for the down-folding variant)

* **Barrel length**: Varies by variant

### Design features

The TAR has several notable design features. It has a monoblock receiver, shockproof polymer lower parts, and two Picatinny rails that can mount a variety of sights and scopes . One of the significant features is a chrome-plated bore and six-groove rifling .

The rifle uses the AK-47's distinctive 30-round curved box magazine and features commonality of parts with existing weapons . This design choice eases logistics as the ammunition is in widespread production and use .

## Variants

### Standard variants

Ordnance Factory Tiruchirappalli manufactures TAR rifles in multiple variants :



1. **Fixed butt version** - The standard configuration

2. **Side-folding butt version** - Allows for compact storage and transport

3. **Down-folding butt version** - Launched on August 25, 2021, in commemoration of 75 years of Indian independence

The down-folding butt version was developed to meet customer requirements as some Central Armed Police Force (CAPF) units, State Police, and Railway Protection Force (RPF) required this version for quick and easy movement of personnel in guard and patrolling duty .

### Compact variants

#### TriCa carbine

The TriCa (Trichy Carbine) is a lighter and compact 7.62×39mm carbine designed for infantry combat vehicle crew, helicopter crew, and security personnel for operations that call for a compact and relatively powerful individual automatic weapon [4]. It is also suitable for paratroopers, police, and security personnel guarding highly secure facilities such as airports, and for use by Special Operation Forces .

The TriCa has a special muzzle booster that helps in hiding the flash and mitigating the sound when fired [5]. An advantage is that it can use ammunition and magazines as well as interchangeable parts of general assault rifles (TAR and AK-47) [5]. The carbine can be concealed in clothes and hidden in the jackets of security personnel .

#### Baby TAR/TAR-3

The "Baby TAR" (TAR-3) is India's smallest assault rifle, developed as a compact, fully automatic version of the Trichy Assault Rifle . It fires the same 7.62×39mm cartridge as the AK-47 and uses the AK-47's 30-round curved box magazine .

The Baby TAR features:



* **Barrel length**: 8.3 inches (chopped down from the AK-47's 16-inch barrel)

* **Effective range**: 150 meters

* **Length with stock folded**: 19.2 inches [6]

* **Weight (unloaded)**: 2.7 kg (1 kg lighter than the AK-47) [6]

The weapon has a monoblock receiver, shockproof polymer lower parts, and two Picatinny rails that can mount a variety of sights and scopes . Its designers say it is ideal for special forces and counter-terrorist operators who want compact, concealable weapons but with the firepower of a full-sized AK-47. The assault rifle is also suitable for aircraft, tank, and vehicle crews .

## Production and service history

### Production capacity

Ordnance Factory Tiruchirappalli has established significant production capacity for the TAR. The factory has a monthly output rate of 2,000 TAR rifles . This production capability has enabled the factory to fulfill large orders from various security forces across India.

### Service adoption

The Trichy assault rifle has been widely adopted by various Indian security forces:



1. **Central Armed Police Forces (CAPF)**:

* Central Reserve Police Force (CRPF) [7]

* Border Security Force (BSF)

* Sashastra Seema Bal (SSB)

* Indo-Tibetan Border Police (ITBP)

* Central Industrial Security Force (CISF)

1. **State police forces**:

* Kerala Police

* Police forces in states dealing with Naxalite insurgency

* Police forces in Madhya Pradesh, Maharashtra, Rajasthan, Jharkhand, and Chhattisgarh [8]

1. **Other organizations**:

* Railway Protection Force (RPF)

* National Security Guard (NSG) - ordered 100 TriCa carbines in December 2021

### Notable orders

Recent significant orders include:



* On February 12, 2022, the CRPF placed a large order for 10,000 TAR costing 76.48 crore with Advance Weapons & Equipment India Limited (AWEIL)

* On January 31, 2022, AWEIL received an order from Kerala Police for 92 TriCa carbines costing 67.16 crore

### Delivery statistics

According to unclassified reports from the Department of Defense, out of the orders for 78,000 guns placed collectively by various forces, OFT has delivered 30,000 Tiruchi Assault Rifle (TAR) and will supply 42,000 to 45,000 units during the fiscal year . OFT has provided around 10,000 rifles to Central Armed Police Forces such as the BSF, SSB, ITBP, and CISF .

The Central Armed Police Forces' faith in the assault rifle points to its success . Nearly 20,000 weapons have been delivered to Indian police, paramilitary, and central armed police forces .

## Cost and advantages

The Trichy assault rifle offers significant cost advantages over imported alternatives. Each TAR costs around Rs 55,000, which is far less than the licensed AK-203 to be produced by Indo-Russian Rifles, which will cost upwards of Rs 75,000 .

The rifle's design offers several advantages:



1. **Logistics efficiency**: Uses widely available 7.62×39mm ammunition and AK-47 pattern magazines

2. **Parts commonality**: Shares interchangeable parts with existing AK-47 variants [5]

3. **Indigenous production**: Reduces dependence on foreign imports and supports India's Atmanirbhar Bharat (self-reliant India) initiative

4. **Proven reliability**: Based on the well-proven AK-47 design with 本土化 improvements [1]

## Technical innovations

Designers at OF Trichy have solved a significant technical challenge that stumped gun designers of the DRDO's now-discarded INSAS carbine variant in the 1990s—how to reduce barrel heating and excessive sound and flash produced when an assault rifle bullet is fired from a short barrel .

The Baby TAR designers solved this problem by using internal metal reflectors to insulate the foregrip from the barrel and by designing an in-house compact flash hider at the barrel muzzle . The gun can presently fire four full magazines (around 120 rounds) without the barrel overheating . This development is noteworthy because it means Indian designers now understand the "know-why," rather than just the "know-how" that they would have gained from licensed production .

## Conclusion

The Trichy assault rifle represents a significant achievement in India's small arms manufacturing capabilities. As an indigenous alternative to imported weapons, it has successfully met the requirements of various security forces across the country. With its cost-effective production, reliable performance, and 不断 expanding range of variants, the TAR has become an important component of India's defense and security infrastructure.

The rifle's success is evident in the large orders placed by multiple agencies and its growing adoption by state police forces. The continued development of variants such as the TriCa carbine and Baby TAR demonstrates the ongoing innovation at Ordnance Factory Tiruchirappalli, supporting India's goal of self-reliance in defense manufacturing.

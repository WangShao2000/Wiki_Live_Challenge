# Ang Totoong Narcolist

**Ang Totoong Narcolist** (The True Narco List) is a controversial video series that emerged in the Philippines in April 2019, alleging involvement of President Rodrigo Duterte's family members and associates in illegal drug trade [1]. The series featured a hooded narrator known as "Bikoy," later identified as Peter Joemel Advincula, who made various accusations against the Duterte administration [2]. The videos became a significant political controversy in the lead-up to the 2019 midterm elections, with subsequent retractions and legal proceedings [3].

## Background and Creation

The video series was created by a YouTube channel named "Ang Totoong Narco List" (transl. "The True Narco List") based around a former criminal named "Bikoy" . The channel was created on March 31 or April 1, 2019 [4]. The videos were initially uploaded to YouTube in April 2019, with the channel being terminated by YouTube around May 2, 2019 [5]. A total of five videos were created between April 2, 2019, and Advincula's surrender on May 6 .

The videos were also published on a website called [metrobalita.net](https://metrobalita.net), created by Rodel Jayme, who later faced legal charges . The website was included in a matrix released by Malacañang that alleged a plot to oust President Duterte, linking various media organizations, lawyers, and opposition figures [6].

## Content and Allegations

The six-minute video titled "Ang Totoong Narco List (The True Narco List) Episode 1" was first posted around noon on April 2, 2019, on the Facebook account of "Metro Balita," which described itself as a "media/news company" [7]. The series detailed alleged involvement of President Rodrigo Duterte's family members and long-time aide Bong Go in illegal drug trade [1].

The videos claimed that Bong Go owned four local bank accounts into which drug money was funneled [8]. According to the allegations, Go used the codename "TESOROGOLF-TSG-002" and had a dragon tattoo on his back supposedly containing the alphanumeric code of his drug transactions . The videos also claimed that President Duterte's son, former Davao City Mayor Paolo Duterte, benefited from the illegal drug trade .

The second part of the viral video linked Kitty Duterte (the president's daughter) with the drug trade, as well as the alleged lawyer partner of Sara Duterte [9]. The videos accused a number of personalities, including members of President Duterte's family, of involvement in illegal drugs trade in the country [10].

## Peter Joemel Advincula and "Bikoy"

Peter Joemel Advincula was the person behind the "Bikoy" character in the videos . On May 6, 2019, Advincula introduced himself as "Bikoy" in a press conference at the Integrated Bar of the Philippines (IBP), claiming to be a former record keeper of a narcotics ring operating in Southern Luzon and Visayas [11]. During this initial appearance, he stated he had no political connections .

However, on May 23, 2019, Advincula retracted his previous statements during a press briefing at the Philippine National Police. He claimed that all his earlier allegations were "all lies" and "completely without truth," stating they were "entirely orchestrated (fabricated) by those on the other party (political party), which is the Liberal Party under the handling of Senator Antonio 'Sonny' Trillanes IV" [12].

Advincula claimed he was paid P500,000 by Trillanes for his role as the hooded character [13]. He alleged that the videos were part of "Project Sodoma," a plot to overthrow President Duterte and install Vice President Leni Robredo [13].

## Political Reactions and Impact

The release of the videos generated significant political controversy in the Philippines. Presidential Spokesperson Salvador Panelo tagged the release of the video as "black propaganda" to hurt Bong Go's chances of winning a seat in the Senate during the upcoming midterm elections .

President Duterte warned those behind the "Totoong Narco List" videos to lay off his children, especially his 14-year-old daughter Veronica or Kitty [14]. In a statement, he vowed to unmask the video perpetrators [14].

Malacañang dismissed the videos as "black propaganda" [7]. Panelo challenged the people behind the video to file charges against Bong Go if they could prove their allegations were true, stating: "But if it's true, they should have filed charges. They haven't filed anything yet. So, it must mean it's not true" .

Paolo Duterte, the president's son, tagged Senator Antonio Trillanes as the brains behind the video. In an official statement released on April 4, 2019, Paolo Duterte said he would file another case against Trillanes for ruining his reputation [15]. He described the video as having "the indelible Trillanes signature of being a desperate mercenary, one who has done nothing good but attack the government" [15].

## Legal Proceedings

### Against Rodel Jayme

Rodel Jayme, the administrator of the website [metrobalita.net](https://metrobalita.net) where the videos were uploaded, was arrested in his residence in Parañaque on April 30, 2019, for his alleged involvement in the videos . He was charged with inciting to sedition on May 2, 2019 [16].

The Department of Justice (DOJ) prosecutors indicted Jayme on inciting to sedition charges in relation to Republic Act 10175 (Cybercrime Prevention Act) [17]. The resolution stated that by creating a website and posting videos that alleged involvement of the President and his family in drug trade, Jayme was not exercising freedom of speech but committing "a clear act to arouse among its viewers a sense of dissatisfaction against the duly constituted authorities" .

Jayme pleaded not guilty to charges of inciting sedition on May 21, 2019 [18]. He admitted creating the website but maintained that his participation ended there, claiming he had no clue it would be used for such content .

### Against Opposition Figures

Based on Advincula's sworn testimony on July 18, 2019, the Philippine National Police-Criminal Investigation and Detection Group (PNP-CIDG) filed sedition charges against Vice President Leni Robredo and 37 other personalities [19]. The charges alleged that the circulation of the videos was part of a destabilization plot meant to "agitate the general population into staging mass protests with the possibility of bringing down the President" and help the Otso Diretso senatorial candidates in the 2019 elections .

However, on October 12, 2019, the DOJ cleared Vice President Leni Robredo and members of the 2019 Senate opposition slate Otso Diretso of involvement in the videos [20]. The resolution stated that the complaints against all respondents were dismissed for lack of probable cause, though the panel found that 11 respondents could be charged with conspiracy to commit sedition .

The same resolution recommended filing charges against former Senator Antonio Trillanes IV and 10 others who prepared and circulated the video .

### Perjury Conviction of Peter Joemel Advincula

On May 23, 2023, Peter Joemel Advincula was convicted of perjury by the Manila Metropolitan Trial Court (MeTC) [3]. The complaint against Advincula was filed by three Free Legal Assistance Group (FLAG) members: Chairperson Jose Manuel "Chel" Diokno, former Quezon Rep. Lorenzo "Erin" Tanada III, and FLAG Metro Manila chairperson Theodore O. Te .

Advincula was accused of making false statements under oath in 2019, claiming that the three FLAG lawyers were "members of 'Project Sodoma,' which he claimed was a plot to commit crimes of sedition, inciting to sedition, cyberlibel, estafa, harboring a criminal and obstruction of justice" .

In the decision handed down by MeTC Judge Karla A. Funtila-Abugan, Advincula was sentenced to an "indeterminate penalty of imprisonment of three (3) months and one (1) day of arresto mayor as minimum, up to one year and one day of prison correccional as maximum" .

The judge ruled that "the essential element of deliberate falsehood was established in this case and accordingly satisfies the required quantum of evidence for conviction of perjury" . The decision stated that Advincula was not present at the Leong Hall in Ateneo de Manila University on March 4, 2019, as he had claimed, and that his meetings with the FLAG lawyers were only for legal representation assessment .

## Legacy and Analysis

The "Ang Totoong Narcolist" controversy became a significant political event in the Philippines, highlighting issues of misinformation, political propaganda, and the role of social media in elections [21]. The series of events, including the initial allegations, subsequent retractions, and legal proceedings, demonstrated the complex nature of political discourse in the digital age [13].

The case also revealed the challenges of fact-checking and verifying information in social media, with the videos spreading widely before being investigated [4]. The controversy continued to influence political dynamics even after the 2019 elections, with ongoing legal proceedings and debates about the truthfulness of the allegations [3].

The conviction of Advincula for perjury in 2023 provided a legal conclusion to one aspect of the controversy, establishing that his sworn statements about "Project Sodoma" and the involvement of various individuals were false . However, questions about the actual source and motivation behind the videos continue to be debated in Philippine political circles [13].

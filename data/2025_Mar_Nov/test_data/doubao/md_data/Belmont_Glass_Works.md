# Belmont Glass Works

Belmont Glass Works, also known as the Belmont Glass Company, was one of Ohio's early glassmaking companies that operated from 1865 to 1890 . The company was named after Belmont County, Ohio, where the plant was located in the riverfront village of Bellaire [1]. As the first glass factory in Bellaire, Belmont Glass Works played a significant role in establishing the region's reputation as a center for glass manufacturing [1].

## History

### Founding and Early Years

Belmont Glass Works was founded in 1865, with production beginning in 1866 . The company was established by experienced glass makers, several of whom were former employees of the Hobbs works in Wheeling, West Virginia . Among the key founders was John Robinson, a Civil War veteran who had previously worked at the Hobbs, Brockunier & Company glass works in Wheeling . Other important figures in the company included Henry Faupel, Charles Henry Over, and Henry Crimmel .

The location of the factory in Bellaire, Ohio, was strategically chosen due to the area's abundant natural resources and transportation advantages. Bellaire had coal deposits, railroad service, and access to the Ohio River, making it an ideal location for glass manufacturing . The company began operations in a riverfront village along the east side of Belmont County .

### Growth and Expansion

Belmont Glass Works quickly became a significant employer in the region. By 1884, the company had grown to employ 350 workers . The factory initially focused on producing glass chimneys but later transitioned to manufacturing pressed glassware . This shift in production helped establish the company as a major player in the growing glass industry of the Ohio River Valley.

The company's success contributed to Bellaire's emergence as a prominent glass manufacturing center. By 1881, Bellaire had 15 glass factories and had earned the nickname "Glass City" [1]. Ohio was a leading state in glass production during this period, and Belmont County was particularly important to the industry [1].

### Decline and Closure

Several factors contributed to the eventual decline and closure of Belmont Glass Works. In 1876, the company experienced a significant loss of talent when three key employees—John Robinson, C. H. Over, and William Gorby—left to establish their own glassworks . This departure weakened the company's leadership and technical expertise.

The company continued operations despite these challenges, but eventually closed in 1890 following an economic recession . The closure of Belmont Glass Works marked the end of an era for one of Ohio's pioneering glass manufacturing companies.

## Products and Patterns

Belmont Glass Works produced a variety of glass products, primarily focusing on lamps and bar goods . The company was particularly known for its pressed glassware, which featured several distinctive patterns that became popular in the late 19th century.

One of the most notable patterns produced by Belmont Glass Works was the "Daisy & Button" (or "Daisy Button") pattern [2]. This pattern was produced in both crystal and colored glass and included items such as cruet sets, berry bowls, and other tableware [3]. The Daisy & Button pattern was highly collectible and is still sought after by antique glass collectors today.

Another significant product was the "Russian Sleigh" pattern, which was introduced in 1886 [2]. The original Russian Sleigh made by Belmont Glass Works was 11 inches long [2]. This decorative item was likely produced as a novelty or special-order piece rather than as part of a regular tableware line.

The company also produced the "Grasshopper without Insect" pattern, examples of which date from 1898 [4]. This pattern was used on covered bowls and other decorative items. Additionally, Belmont Glass Works manufactured oil lamps with pattern #120, also known as the "Reflecting Fans" pattern [5].

Belmont Glass Works also produced depression glass items, including a green depression glass berry bowl with a bowknot pattern [6]. The company's products were distributed throughout the United States and were known for their quality craftsmanship and innovative designs.

## Legacy

Although Belmont Glass Works operated for only 24 years, its legacy extended far beyond its years of operation. Many of the company's former employees went on to become prominent figures in the glass industry . The Crimmel brothers—Henry, Jacob, and John—who had worked at Belmont Glass Works, later became well-known glassmakers in Ohio and Indiana .

The skills and techniques developed at Belmont Glass Works influenced the entire Ohio River Valley glass industry. The company's transition from producing glass chimneys to pressed glassware helped establish new standards for glass manufacturing in the region .

Today, items produced by Belmont Glass Works are highly collectible and can be found in museums and private collections worldwide. The Iowa State University Museums houses several pieces from Belmont Glass Works in its Iowa Quester Glass Collection [4]. These artifacts provide valuable insights into the company's production methods and design aesthetic.

The site of the original Belmont Glass Works in Bellaire, Ohio, is now part of the National Imperial Glass Museum, which celebrates the rich glassmaking heritage of the region [7]. The museum preserves the history of glass manufacturing in Bellaire, including the story of Belmont Glass Works and its contributions to the industry.

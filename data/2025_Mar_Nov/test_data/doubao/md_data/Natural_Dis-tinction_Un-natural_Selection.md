# Natural Dis-tinction Un-natural Selection

Natural Dis-tinction Un-natural Selection is the thirty-third collection by British fashion designer Alexander McQueen, released for the Spring/Summer 2009 season of his eponymous fashion house . The collection represented a significant exploration of digital print technology in fashion design while examining themes of evolution, environmental impact, and the relationship between natural and artificial elements [1].

## Collection Overview

### Inspiration and Concept

The collection drew direct inspiration from Charles Darwin's theory of natural selection, as described in his 1859 work *On the Origin of Species* . According to the show's program notes, McQueen was fascinated by the interplay between natural and artificial elements . The collection contemplated the notion of "survival of the fittest" while addressing what the designer perceived as the "deleterious results of industrialization on the natural world" [2].

The concept reflected McQueen's broader concerns about human impact on the environment . The title itself—"Natural Dis-tinction Un-natural Selection"—creates a deliberate paradox that challenges traditional understanding of natural versus artificial processes in both biological evolution and fashion design.

### Runway Presentation

The runway show was staged on October 3, 2008, at the Cent Quatre building in Paris, a community center that was once a mortuary . The venue choice added to the collection's themes of mortality, preservation, and transformation. The show invitations featured lenticular portraits of McQueen that transformed into skulls .

The presentation was designed as an immersive experience that interrogated the impact of human activity on the environment . Models walked among antique taxidermy specimens, including polar bears, giraffes, and zebras, with a rotating Earth projected as a backdrop . This dramatic staging created a tension between preserved natural specimens and contemporary fashion, highlighting themes of environmental preservation and degradation.

### Design Elements

The collection featured several notable design innovations and recurring motifs:

**Digital Print Technology**: The collection was particularly pioneering for its use of digital print design, which significantly contributed to the widespread adoption of this technique in the fashion industry [1]. McQueen and his team engineered print placements on fabric according to garment patterns, creating multidimensional effects previously unseen in dress design . Digitally manipulated images of crystals featured prominently in the collection .

**Silhouettes and Forms**: The collection included "bell jar" dresses with rounded silhouettes that exaggerated the female form. These dresses featured skirts sculpted over the hips to suggest the shape of Victorian specimen jars, creating an oblique reference to biological specimens collected by natural scientists like Darwin . The shape became a key look for the collection [3].

**Kaleidoscope Prints**: The collection included multicolored kaleidoscope print dresses, including one worn by actress Camilla Belle to the premiere of the film *Push* in January 2009 . This particular dress was later listed among "100 Best Dresses of the Decade" by *InStyle* magazine .

**Crystal Embellishments**: Many garments featured extensive Swarovski crystal embellishments. One notable example was described as "more crystal than textile," with the net base so densely covered with stones that it created a blinding sparkle effect . McQueen was known for pushing materials to their creative limits, and in this case, he particularly emphasized the use of Swarovski crystals .

### Models and Styling

While specific models who walked in the show weren't extensively documented in the available sources, the styling of the models complemented the collection's themes. Hair was styled in various ways to suggest biological adaptation, including tight braiding and fin-like peaks . The models represented what could be interpreted as human-animal hybrids, further exploring the theme of evolution and adaptation.

## Critical Reception and Legacy

### Industry Impact

Natural Dis-tinction Un-natural Selection is widely recognized as a pivotal moment in fashion history, particularly for its technological innovations [1]. The collection's pioneering use of digital print technology significantly influenced the fashion industry's adoption of this technique [1]. By engineering prints specifically for individual garment patterns, McQueen created a new standard for how digital printing could be integrated into fashion design .

### Media Coverage

The collection received significant media attention, with particular focus on its innovative use of technology and its provocative themes. The dress worn by Camilla Belle to the *Push* premiere generated substantial coverage and was recognized as one of the most significant fashion moments of the decade .

### Museum Recognition

The collection has been recognized by major cultural institutions for its significance. The Los Angeles County Museum of Art (LACMA) features the collection in its permanent collection and describes it as a "pioneering exploration of digital print design" [1]. The Victoria and Albert Museum in London also includes pieces from the collection in its Alexander McQueen retrospective exhibitions [3].

### Academic Analysis

The collection has been the subject of academic study, particularly in discussions about fashion's relationship with nature and technology [4]. Scholars have analyzed how McQueen's work in this collection reflects broader cultural anxieties about environmental degradation and technological advancement [4].

## Design Innovation: Digital Printing

The most significant technical innovation of Natural Dis-tinction Un-natural Selection was its pioneering use of digital printing technology [1]. McQueen's approach to digital printing went beyond simply applying patterns to fabric; instead, he engineered prints specifically for individual garment patterns .

This technique involved creating prints that were designed to match the three-dimensional shape of each garment. By carefully planning where each element of a print would fall when the fabric was cut and sewn, McQueen created what he called "multidimensional effects previously unseen in dress" . The result was garments that appeared to shift and transform as they moved, creating a dynamic visual experience.

The collection featured digitally manipulated images of crystals, which were printed onto various fabrics including silk and wool [5]. These crystal patterns were not merely decorative; they were integrated into the overall design concept, symbolizing both natural beauty and artificial manipulation.

## Relationship to McQueen's Body of Work

Natural Dis-tinction Un-natural Selection fits within McQueen's broader exploration of nature as a design theme [6]. Throughout his career, McQueen was fascinated with the elemental forces of earth, wind, fire, and water, which imbued his collections with what the Victoria and Albert Museum describes as "primordial drama" [6].

The collection represents a continuation of McQueen's interest in biological themes and evolution. Several of his previous collections had clear references to animals in their themes [7]. McQueen had also previously explored similar themes of natural selection and adaptation in collections like "The Birds" (2008) and "Plato's Atlantis" (2010) .

What distinguishes Natural Dis-tinction Un-natural Selection from McQueen's other nature-themed collections is its specific focus on the intersection of natural and artificial elements, particularly as mediated through technology . The collection represents a pivotal moment where McQueen's traditional tailoring skills met cutting-edge digital technology.

## Cultural Significance

Beyond its technical innovations, Natural Dis-tinction Un-natural Selection holds cultural significance for its commentary on contemporary environmental and technological concerns . The collection emerged during a period of heightened awareness about climate change and human impact on the environment.

The use of taxidermy in the runway show created a powerful visual metaphor for extinction and preservation . By placing living models among preserved animal specimens, McQueen highlighted the precarious relationship between human progress and natural conservation.

The collection also reflected broader cultural anxieties about the pace of technological change and its impact on traditional human relationships with nature [4]. McQueen's use of digital printing technology in this collection can be interpreted as both celebration and critique of technological advancement.

## Legacy and Influence

The legacy of Natural Dis-tinction Un-natural Selection extends far beyond its immediate reception. The collection's innovative use of digital printing technology has had a lasting impact on the fashion industry [1]. Many contemporary designers now routinely use digital printing techniques that can trace their development back to McQueen's pioneering work in this collection.

The collection's themes continue to resonate in contemporary fashion and culture. Environmental concerns and the relationship between technology and nature remain central topics in both fashion design and broader cultural discourse [4].

Several pieces from the collection have entered museum collections, ensuring their preservation for future study and appreciation . The LACMA, Victoria and Albert Museum, and other institutions regularly feature pieces from this collection in exhibitions about McQueen's work and the history of fashion technology.

## Conclusion

Natural Dis-tinction Un-natural Selection represents a significant achievement in Alexander McQueen's career and in the history of fashion design. The collection successfully merged technical innovation with conceptual depth, creating garments that were both visually striking and intellectually provocative.

Through its pioneering use of digital printing technology, the collection transformed how fashion designers approach print design and garment construction [1]. By engineering prints specifically for individual garment patterns, McQueen created a new paradigm for integrating technology into fashion design .

The collection's themes of evolution, environmental impact, and the relationship between natural and artificial elements continue to be relevant in contemporary culture . McQueen's ability to address complex social and environmental issues through fashion design demonstrates the power of clothing to serve as both aesthetic expression and cultural commentary.

Natural Dis-tinction Un-natural Selection stands as a testament to Alexander McQueen's innovative spirit and his commitment to pushing the boundaries of fashion design. The collection remains an important reference point for designers exploring the intersection of technology and tradition, nature and culture, and the natural and the artificial.

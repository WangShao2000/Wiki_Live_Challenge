# Robert Jacomb-Hood

Robert Jacomb-Hood (1822 – 1900) was a British railway engineer who served as the resident engineer for the London, Brighton, and South Coast Railway (LB\&SCR) from 1846 to 1860 [1]. He was responsible for major railway infrastructure projects including the design of the LB\&SCR side of London Victoria station [2], work on Crystal Palace station in 1853-54 [1], and the construction of the Deptford Wharf branch line . Jacomb-Hood was awarded a Telford Medal for his innovative paper on railway stations , recognizing his contributions to railway engineering.

## Early life and education

Robert Jacomb-Hood was born in 1822 . He defied his father's wishes to pursue a career in railway engineering . The details of his early education and family background are not extensively documented in available sources.

## Career

### London, Brighton, and South Coast Railway

Jacomb-Hood began his career with the LB\&SCR as the first resident engineer in 1846 . In this role, he was responsible for the design and construction of several significant railway projects. His tenure as resident engineer lasted until 1860 [1].

One of his major achievements was the design of the LB\&SCR side of London Victoria station, which opened on 1 October 1860 [3]. The station was designed in collaboration with John Fowler [2].

### Major projects

Jacomb-Hood was involved in several important railway infrastructure projects during his career:

**Crystal Palace station**: In 1853-54, he had responsibility for engineering work at Crystal Palace station [1].

**Deptford Wharf branch line**: In 1846, the LB\&SCR board requested Jacomb-Hood to construct a branch line from New Cross to the River Thames at Deptford . He was also tasked with designing, surveying, and managing the construction of a new dock system. Jacomb-Hood instructed his assistant, Frederick Banister, to design and manage the construction of the branch line and dock, which was completed in 1849 .

**Three Bridges-Tunbridge Wells line**: Jacomb-Hood served as the engineer for the East Grinstead Railway (EGR), while also holding the position of chief engineer for the London Bridge Railway (LBR) . He oversaw construction progress on the Three Bridges-Tunbridge Wells line, confirming that only three cuttings remained to be completed, a public road bridge needed to be built, and only 4 miles (6.4 km) of permanent way was ready .

### Professional recognition

Jacomb-Hood received significant recognition for his contributions to railway engineering. He was awarded a Telford Medal by the Institution of Civil Engineers for his innovative paper on railway stations . This prestigious award recognized his technical expertise and contributions to the field of railway engineering.

## Personal life

Robert Jacomb-Hood married Jane Stothard Littlewood (1827-1869) [4]. They had nine children, including George Percy Jacomb-Hood (1857-1929), who became a notable British painter and illustrator [4]. George was born on 6 July 1857 in Redhill, Surrey, and was the fourth of nine children (two of whom died in infancy) .

After Jane's death, Jacomb-Hood became engaged to Elizabeth Thornton . The details of his later life and death are limited in available sources, though records indicate he died in 1900 . He is buried at Charlton Cemetery .

## Legacy

Robert Jacomb-Hood's legacy as a railway engineer is reflected in the significant infrastructure projects he designed and constructed. His work on London Victoria station, Crystal Palace station, and the Deptford Wharf branch line contributed to the development of London's railway network during a crucial period of expansion. His Telford Medal recognition demonstrates his standing as an innovative and respected figure in 19th-century railway engineering.

The Institution of Civil Engineers' Biographical Dictionary of Civil Engineers Volume 2: 1830-1890 records his service as Resident Engineer for the London Brighton & South Coast Railway from 1846 to 1860 [1], preserving his professional legacy for future generations of engineers.

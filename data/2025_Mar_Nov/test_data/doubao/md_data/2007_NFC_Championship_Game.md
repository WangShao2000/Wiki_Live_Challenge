# 2007 NFC Championship Game

The 2007 NFC Championship Game was an American football game played on January 20, 2008, at Lambeau Field in Green Bay, Wisconsin, between the **New York Giants** and the **Green Bay Packers** [1]. The Giants defeated the Packers 23–20 in overtime to advance to Super Bowl XLII [2]. The game featured extremely cold weather conditions with temperatures of minus 1 degree Fahrenheit and wind chills reaching minus 23 to minus 24 degrees [1].

## Background

### Regular Season Performances

The New York Giants finished the 2007 regular season with a **10-6 record**, placing second in the NFC East division [3]. Under head coach Tom Coughlin, the Giants improved upon their 8-8 record from the previous season . The team's 10-6 record earned them the fifth seed in the NFC playoffs as a wild card team [3].

The Green Bay Packers had a **13-3 record** in the 2007 regular season, winning the NFC North division and earning the second seed in the NFC playoffs [4]. Under first-year head coach Mike McCarthy, the Packers finished with the second-best record in the NFC [4]. The team's 13-3 record was highlighted by quarterback Brett Favre's 28 touchdown passes, all of which went to receivers and tight ends for the first time in his career [5].

### Playoff Paths

The Giants advanced to the NFC Championship Game by winning two road playoff games. In the NFC Wild Card Round, they defeated the Tampa Bay Buccaneers 24-14 [6]. In the NFC Divisional Round, they upset the top-seeded Dallas Cowboys 21-17 [6]. The Giants' victory over the Cowboys was particularly significant as it came in Dallas, ending the Cowboys' season and advancing the Giants to their first NFC Championship Game appearance since 2000 [7].

The Packers received a first-round bye due to their second-seed ranking. In the NFC Divisional Round, they hosted the Seattle Seahawks and won 42-20 [8]. The Packers' victory over the Seahawks was played in heavy snow conditions at Lambeau Field, with Favre throwing three touchdown passes [9]. The 42-20 victory was the highest-scoring postseason game in Packers' history at that time [9].

## Game Summary

### Pre-Game Conditions

The game was played in extremely cold weather conditions, with temperatures at kickoff of **minus 1 degree Fahrenheit** and wind chills reaching **minus 23 to minus 24 degrees** [1]. The conditions were described as the second-coldest game played at Lambeau Field, behind only the famous 1967 Ice Bowl . The game was played under a full moon [1].

The extreme cold affected both teams' pre-game preparations. Giants kicker Lawrence Tynes was only able to complete five or six field goal attempts during warm-ups instead of his usual 20 . The Giants' quarterback Eli Manning and wide receivers Plaxico Burress and Amani Toomer were only able to throw six passes during their normal 45-minute pre-game route tree . Players on both teams used various methods to stay warm, including wearing multiple layers of clothing, applying Vaseline to exposed skin, and using sheepskin fur in their helmets .

### First Half

The Giants scored first when kicker Lawrence Tynes made a 32-yard field goal with 5:24 left in the first quarter [2]. The Packers responded with a field goal of their own when Mason Crosby made a 28-yard kick with 12:54 remaining in the second quarter, tying the game at 3-3 [2].

The Packers took their first lead of the game when quarterback Brett Favre connected with wide receiver Donald Driver on a 90-yard touchdown pass with 10:42 left in the second quarter . The play was the longest from scrimmage in Packers' postseason history . The Packers added another field goal before halftime when Crosby made a 25-yard kick with 1:20 remaining, giving the Packers a 10-6 lead at the break [2].

The Giants' offense struggled in the first half, gaining only 119 total yards . Eli Manning completed 11 of 19 passes for 118 yards . The Packers' offense, despite the cold conditions, had success through the air with Favre completing 11 of 16 passes for 155 yards and one touchdown .

### Second Half

The Giants took control of the game in the second half. On their first drive of the third quarter, they drove 75 yards in 11 plays, capped by a 1-yard touchdown run by running back Brandon Jacobs . The touchdown gave the Giants a 13-10 lead with 7:10 remaining in the third quarter [2].

The Packers quickly responded with a touchdown of their own. After a 49-yard kickoff return by Tramon Williams and a crucial unnecessary roughness penalty on Giants cornerback Sam Madison, Favre threw a 12-yard touchdown pass to tight end Donald Lee . The touchdown gave the Packers a 17-13 lead with 3:58 left in the third quarter [2].

The Giants regained the lead in the fourth quarter when Manning led a 68-yard drive that ended with a 4-yard touchdown run by rookie running back Ahmad Bradshaw . The touchdown gave the Giants a 20-17 lead with 11:46 remaining in the game [2].

The Packers tied the game at 20-20 when Crosby made a 23-yard field goal with 11:46 left in the fourth quarter [2]. The drive was set up when Favre's pass was intercepted by R.W. McQuarters, but Packers offensive tackle Mark Tauscher recovered the fumble at the Giants' 19-yard line .

### Overtime

With the score tied at 20-20, the Giants had two opportunities to win the game in regulation. First, Tynes missed a 43-yard field goal wide left with 6:49 remaining in the fourth quarter . Then, with the game clock winding down, the Giants drove to the Packers' 18-yard line, but a high snap on the final play of regulation led to Tynes' 36-yard field goal attempt being wide left, sending the game to overtime .

In overtime, the Packers won the coin toss and elected to receive . However, on the second play of overtime, Favre was intercepted by Giants cornerback Corey Webster when he underthrew an out route to Donald Driver . The interception gave the Giants the ball at the Packers' 45-yard line.

The Giants drove 40 yards to the Packers' 5-yard line, setting up Tynes' third field goal attempt of the game, a 47-yard kick . This time, Tynes made the kick with 2:35 remaining in overtime, giving the Giants a 23-20 victory [10].

## Key Performances

### Giants' Offensive Leaders

**Eli Manning** completed 21 of 40 passes for 251 yards with no touchdowns and no interceptions . Despite the extreme cold conditions, Manning was described as "efficient" and never complained about the weather during the game . His performance was particularly notable given the challenging conditions, as he was able to make accurate throws in the freezing temperatures .

**Plaxico Burress** had an exceptional game, catching 11 passes for 151 yards . His performance in the cold weather was described as "one of the best games a wide receiver has played in the playoffs" . Burress was particularly effective against Packers cornerback Al Harris, who was considered one of the top cornerbacks in the league at that time .

**Brandon Jacobs** rushed for 67 yards on 21 carries and scored one touchdown . His 1-yard touchdown run in the third quarter gave the Giants their first lead of the second half .

**Ahmad Bradshaw** contributed 63 yards on 16 carries and scored the Giants' go-ahead touchdown in the fourth quarter .

### Packers' Offensive Leaders

**Brett Favre** completed 19 of 35 passes for 236 yards with two touchdowns and two interceptions . His 90-yard touchdown pass to Donald Driver was the longest play from scrimmage in Packers' postseason history . However, his interception in overtime was a critical mistake that led to the Giants' victory .

**Donald Driver** had a strong game, catching 5 passes for 141 yards and one touchdown . His 90-yard touchdown reception was the highlight of the Packers' offense in the first half .

**Ryan Grant** had a limited impact on the game, rushing for only 29 yards on 13 carries . This was a significant drop from his previous game against Seattle, where he had set a Packers' playoff record with 201 yards rushing .

### Defensive Highlights

The Giants' defensive line, led by Michael Strahan, Osi Umenyiora, and Justin Tuck, was credited with implementing a game plan specifically designed to stop the run and force Favre to throw in the cold weather . This strategy was effective as the Packers struggled to establish a running game and were limited to 1-for-10 on third-down conversions .

Corey Webster's interception in overtime was the game-deciding play . The interception came at a critical moment when the Packers had the ball and a chance to win the game. Webster's play was described as "a big-time atonement" for an earlier mistake when he was beaten by Driver for the 90-yard touchdown .

## Weather Impact

The extreme cold weather conditions significantly impacted the game in multiple ways. The game-time temperature of minus 1 degree Fahrenheit with wind chills of minus 23 to minus 24 degrees made it the second-coldest game in Lambeau Field history, behind only the 1967 Ice Bowl [11].

Players from both teams reported that the cold affected their ability to grip the ball and maintain feeling in their extremities . Giants offensive linemen Chris Snee and Rich Seubert, who typically warmed up before games wearing only shorts and T-shirts, were only able to stay on the field for about 32 seconds before returning to the locker room .

The cold also affected special teams play. Lawrence Tynes' missed field goals in regulation were partly attributed to the difficult kicking conditions . In post-game interviews, Tynes noted that everything had to be perfect in those conditions - snap, hold, and kick - and any slight error could result in a missed attempt .

The Packers' sideline heaters were reported to be malfunctioning at the beginning of the game, adding to the challenging conditions . Giants players and coaches huddled around the few working heaters available on the sideline during breaks in play .

## Historical Significance

The 2007 NFC Championship Game was historically significant for several reasons. First, it was the first time a fifth-seeded wild card team had won three consecutive road playoff games to advance to the Super Bowl since the Pittsburgh Steelers in 2005 .

The game was also notable as it was the last game Brett Favre would play as a member of the Green Bay Packers . Although it wasn't known at the time, the game marked the end of Favre's 16-year career with the Packers .

For the Giants, the victory was the third consecutive road playoff win in their unlikely Super Bowl run. The Giants went on to defeat the undefeated New England Patriots 17-14 in Super Bowl XLII, in what many consider one of the biggest upsets in NFL history [12]. The Giants' Super Bowl victory denied the Patriots their chance to become the first team since the 1972 Miami Dolphins to complete a perfect 19-0 season [12].

The game also became famous for Giants head coach Tom Coughlin's severely chapped and red face, which was prominently featured in television broadcasts and became a memorable image from the game [13]. During Super Bowl XLII pregame, NFL Films caught referee Mike Carey asking Coughlin about his face, which had become a topic of discussion among viewers [13].

## Legacy

The 2007 NFC Championship Game is remembered as one of the most memorable games in NFL history, particularly for its extreme weather conditions and dramatic finish . The game has been ranked among the greatest NFC championship games of all time .

For the Giants, the victory was the beginning of a remarkable postseason run that culminated in their fourth Super Bowl championship and first since 1990 [14]. The 2007 Giants team was later recognized as one of the most successful and memorable in the 93-year history of the franchise [14].

For the Packers, the loss marked the end of an era. Although they returned to the playoffs in subsequent seasons, they would not return to the NFC Championship Game until 2010, when they went on to win Super Bowl XLV [4].

The game's legacy is also enhanced by the fact that it featured two future Hall of Fame quarterbacks in Manning and Favre . Manning would go on to win a second Super Bowl with the Giants in 2011, while Favre was elected to the Pro Football Hall of Fame in 2016 .

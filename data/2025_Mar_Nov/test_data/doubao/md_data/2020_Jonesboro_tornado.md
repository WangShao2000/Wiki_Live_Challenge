# 2020 Jonesboro tornado

## Lead section

The 2020 Jonesboro tornado was a significant meteorological event that occurred on March 28, 2020, in Jonesboro, Arkansas. This powerful tornado, rated EF3 on the Enhanced Fujita Scale with maximum winds of approximately 140 mph, caused extensive damage to the city's southern and eastern sections, destroying or damaging more than 200 homes and businesses [1]. Despite its severity, the tornado resulted in no fatalities, though it injured 22 people [2]. The tornado's impact was notably influenced by the ongoing COVID-19 pandemic, which had reduced the number of people in public spaces like the Mall at Turtle Creek .

This tornado was the strongest to hit Arkansas since April 27, 2014, and the most powerful to strike Jonesboro since 1997 [3]. Governor Asa Hutchinson declared a state disaster in response to the damage, which officials estimated at "several hundred million dollars" [4]. The event is often cited as a "warning success story" due to the ten-minute advance warning that allowed residents to seek shelter .

## Meteorological synopsis

The 2020 Jonesboro tornado developed from a supercell thunderstorm that formed as part of a larger severe weather outbreak affecting much of the central United States on March 28, 2020 . The storm's intensity was fueled by record-warm temperatures for that time of year, with the Little Rock National Weather Service Office noting that "a warm front exited to the north of the state, and ushered in well-above-average temperatures in the 80s to lower 90s" .

The same supercell that produced the Jonesboro tornado also spawned another tornado near Amagon, Arkansas, with winds estimated at 110 mph and a path just over five miles long . Weather offices around the area measured wind gusts up to 74 mph, strong enough to derail a nearby train and injure 20 people .

The National Weather Service's damage survey determined that the Jonesboro tornado touched down just north of Interstate Highway 555, initially producing EF-2 damage at the Mall at Turtle Creek before strengthening to EF-3 force as it moved through the Jonesboro Municipal Airport and beyond . At its widest point, the tornado left a swath of damage one-third of a mile across, along a path of more than twelve miles .

## Event timeline

The sequence of events on March 28, 2020, began with the issuance of Tornado Watch #68 for the Jonesboro area approximately two hours before the tornado struck . The National Weather Service issued the tornado warning at 4:47 p.m. CDT, and the tornado touched down just ten minutes later at 4:57 p.m. CDT .

Eyewitness accounts and video footage captured the tornado's destructive path through the city. One resident recorded video from inside his phone shop showing the massive tornado tossing debris around . The tornado struck at roughly 5:20 p.m. local time, according to initial reports, and prompted an immediate search and rescue operation [5].

The timing of the tornado—late Saturday afternoon—was significant. Emergency management officials noted that "at 5 o'clock on a Saturday afternoon that place would've been packed, and at this point there was hardly anyone in there," referring to the Mall at Turtle Creek . This reduced foot traffic was attributed to COVID-19 restrictions that had closed many businesses and encouraged social distancing .

## Impact and damage

The 2020 Jonesboro tornado caused widespread devastation across approximately 13 miles of southern and eastern Jonesboro [3]. The tornado heavily damaged or destroyed more than 200 homes and businesses, with 55 homes completely destroyed and requiring total rebuilding [1].

The most notable commercial casualties included the Mall at Turtle Creek and the Jonesboro Municipal Airport [1]. At the Mall at Turtle Creek, extensive damage occurred with most stores closed due to the coronavirus pandemic [6]. A Best Buy store at the mall was rendered "nearly unrecognizable" by the tornado's force [6]. The shopping center's damage was so severe that the property later sold for redevelopment, as the original lender was "unwilling to reconstruct and carry the loan" [7].

The Jonesboro Municipal Airport suffered catastrophic damage, with the tornado destroying the terminal building and numerous hangars [8]. Two airplanes at the airport required heavy machinery to move after being tossed by the tornado's winds [4]. The Federal Emergency Management Agency later approved $5 million in federal funding to build a new airport terminal, which will be matched with $1.3 million in local funds [9].

Other significant damage occurred at industrial and commercial properties. Camfil, located at 3505 Airport Road, received a direct hit from the tornado, though the factory was searched and cleared with no reported injuries [4]. Power outages affected sections of the city, and wreckage tossed by the tornado blocked roads, complicating emergency response efforts .

The tornado's path of destruction also claimed the home of Dr. Jared Burks, a physician who had been living apart from his wife Alyssa and their son Zeke while treating COVID-19 patients during the pandemic [4]. Though their home was completely destroyed, all three family members survived and later set up a GoFundMe page for assistance [4].

## Aftermath and recovery

Following the tornado, Governor Asa Hutchinson immediately declared a state disaster in the Jonesboro area and requested federal assistance to rebuild the affected communities [4]. The governor stated, "I want to assure all of Jonesboro and northeast Arkansas that the entire state has been in prayer. I have not seen this kind of devastation from a tornado since I have been governor" [4].

Emergency response efforts were coordinated through multiple agencies and organizations. The American Red Cross and Salvation Army established staging areas to plan cleanup and relief operations [10]. The Arkansas Army National Guard's 875th Engineer Battalion delivered 200 cots to assist with shelter operations, arriving just after midnight on March 29 [4].

The city of Jonesboro established four emergency shelters for displaced residents, including University Heights, Fox Meadow and Nettleton Steam Schools, as well as Journey Church on Disciple Drive [4]. Mayor Harold Perrin initially issued a 7 p.m. curfew, though other sources report a 9 p.m. to 6 a.m. curfew from Sunday night into Monday morning .

Damage assessment efforts were complicated by immediate cleanup efforts that began shortly after the tornado, making it difficult for officials to conduct comprehensive surveys . The Red Cross reported being approximately 35 percent complete with damage assessment within days of the event [4].

The financial impact was substantial, with officials estimating total damage costs at "several hundred million dollars" [4]. Governor Hutchinson noted that all businesses destroyed by the tornado qualified for COVID-19 disaster loans, providing additional support during the recovery process [4].

Community support and charitable donations poured in to aid recovery efforts. Regions Bank announced a \$10,000 contribution to the Jonesboro tornado recovery fund established by United Way of Northeast Arkansas [11]. Arvest Bank partnered with the United Way to assist those affected by the tornado [12]. Operation BBQ Relief, with the help of Dewayne and Q49 Smokehouse, provided 1,700 meals over two days to first responders and impacted residents [13].

Long-term recovery has shown significant progress. By May 2022, approximately 40 percent of homes destroyed in the tornado were occupied by new owners [14]. The city launched its first-ever housing division dedicated to securing new housing, working with organizations involved in tornado recovery to determine outstanding needs [14].

The Jonesboro Municipal Airport's reconstruction is underway with the $5 million federal grant approved by the Economic Development Administration, an agency within the U.S. Department of Commerce <reference type="end" id=29>. The airport commission is contributing an additional $1.3 million in funding to replace the terminal and hangars destroyed in the tornado [8].

## Notable aspects

The 2020 Jonesboro tornado is particularly notable for several reasons. First, it was the first major weather disaster to occur during the COVID-19 pandemic shutdown era in the United States [15]. The pandemic's influence on the tornado's impact cannot be overstated—reduced foot traffic in public spaces likely prevented numerous fatalities [2].

The tornado is also significant for its meteorological characteristics. It was the strongest tornado to hit Arkansas since April 27, 2014, and the most powerful to strike Jonesboro since 1997 when an F-3 tornado (on the old Fujita Scale) affected the city [3].

Emergency management officials have highlighted the tornado as a "warning success story" due to the ten-minute advance warning provided by the National Weather Service . The warning, issued at 4:47 p.m. CDT, gave residents sufficient time to seek shelter before the tornado touched down at 4:57 p.m. CDT .

The tornado's timing—late Saturday afternoon during the COVID-19 pandemic—created unique circumstances. The Mall at Turtle Creek, which suffered extensive damage, was largely empty due to pandemic restrictions, which emergency officials described as a "blessing in disguise" . Similarly, the Jonesboro Municipal Airport, while damaged, had reduced activity due to travel restrictions.

## Conclusion

The 2020 Jonesboro tornado stands as a significant event in Arkansas's meteorological and social history. This EF3 tornado, with its 140 mph winds and twelve-mile path of destruction, demonstrated both the destructive power of severe weather and the effectiveness of modern warning systems . The fact that no fatalities occurred despite the tornado's strength and the densely populated areas it affected is attributed to excellent warnings and the coincidental timing during the COVID-19 pandemic [2].

The tornado's impact on Jonesboro's infrastructure—including the destruction of the Mall at Turtle Creek and Jonesboro Municipal Airport—has necessitated substantial rebuilding efforts [1]. The city's response, including the establishment of emergency shelters, coordination with state and federal agencies, and community support through charitable donations, exemplifies effective disaster management [4].

Looking forward, the recovery efforts continue to reshape Jonesboro's landscape. The construction of a new airport terminal, the redevelopment of the mall site, and the rebuilding of residential areas demonstrate the community's resilience [9]. The 2020 Jonesboro tornado serves as a reminder of the importance of preparedness, early warning systems, and community support in the face of natural disasters.

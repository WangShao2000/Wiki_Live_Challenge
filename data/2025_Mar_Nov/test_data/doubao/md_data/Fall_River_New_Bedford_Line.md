# Fall River/New Bedford Line

The Fall River/New Bedford Line is a commuter rail line operated by the Massachusetts Bay Transportation Authority (MBTA) in southeastern Massachusetts, United States. Formerly known as the Middleborough/Lakeville Line, it was renamed following the extension of service to Fall River and New Bedford on March 24, 2025 [1]. The line connects these South Coast cities to Boston's South Station for the first time in over 65 years, after passenger service was discontinued in 1958 [1].

## 1. History

### 1.1 Original Service and Closure

The history of rail service to Fall River and New Bedford dates back to the 19th century. The Old Colony and Fall River Railroad Company was formed on June 20, 1854, through the merger of the Old Colony Railroad and the Fall River Railroad, creating a two-pronged line from Boston to Plymouth and Boston to Fall River, splitting at South Braintree . The company later expanded to include service to New Bedford.

For nearly a century, the New Haven Railroad operated the South Coast train route [1]. However, declining ridership led to the closure of passenger service in 1958 [1]. New Bedford had a rail connection to Boston for the first time since 1958 when service resumed in 2025 [1].

### 1.2 South Coast Rail Project Development

The South Coast Rail project was first proposed in the 1980s with the intent of bringing economic development to the Fall River and New Bedford areas [2]. New Bedford and Fall River peaked economically in the 1940s and declined significantly in the 1970s [2].

The project faced numerous challenges and delays over several decades. In April 2007, Governor Deval Patrick released "South Coast Rail: A Plan for Action," detailing the steps that would be taken to bring commuter rail to Fall River and New Bedford. Patrick said the project's estimated price tag was \$1 billion [3].

The project had many stops and starts as officials gathered funding and deliberated over possible routes . The most recent groundbreaking took place in 2019, after the state settled on a plan to connect South Coast cities to the existing Middleborough/Lakeville line, avoiding a more difficult and expensive option to build tracks through the Hockomock Swamp toward the Stoughton line .

### 1.3 Construction and Opening

Early Action construction projects began in 2019 to prepare for the larger Phase 1 main line rail construction contracts . The first visible active construction projects were drainage culvert replacement and a major railroad grade crossing update in New Bedford. This work also included the construction of 5 wetland mitigation areas in the corridor and was substantially completed in Summer 2020 .

The Fall River Secondary Line Construction contract was awarded to Skanska DW White JV on May 11, 2020 [4]. Work on the Fall River Secondary included track, stations, bridges, and a layover facility. Construction began in summer 2020 and was completed in time for the March 24, 2025 opening [4].

The long-awaited project cost the MBTA more than \$1 billion to construct . On March 24, 2025, the Middleborough/Lakeville Line was officially renamed the Fall River/New Bedford Line as service was extended to Taunton, Freetown, New Bedford, and Fall River .

## 2. Service Description

### 2.1 Route and Stations

The Fall River/New Bedford Line operates with two branches that converge at East Taunton Station. The line runs from Boston's South Station through Middleborough and East Taunton to one of two branches: one continuing to Fall River and the other to New Bedford .

The line serves a total of 15 stations, including six new stations constructed as part of the extension [5]. The stations are:

**Boston Branch (existing stations)**:



* South Station

* JFK/UMass

* Quincy Center

* Braintree

* Holbrook/Randolph

* Montello

* Brockton

* Campello

* Bridgewater

* Middleborough

**New Bedford Branch (new stations)**:



* East Taunton

* Freetown

* Church Street (New Bedford)

* New Bedford

**Fall River Branch (new stations)**:



* East Taunton

* Freetown

* Fall River Depot

The New Bedford Branch has two stations in New Bedford: one near downtown (New Bedford Station at 536 Acushnet Avenue) and another on Church Street in the North End (Church Street Station at 387 Church Street) [1].

### 2.2 Service Frequency and Schedule

The MBTA operates 32 daily weekday trips between Boston and the South Coast on the new line . There are 15 weekday trips on the Fall River Line and 17 trips on the New Bedford Line .

Weekday direct service runs approximately every 70 minutes . Inbound trains from New Bedford Station run from 4:27 a.m. to 9:23 p.m., with 14 total departing trains throughout the day. Outbound trains from South Station have weekday departures from 6:45 a.m. to 11:59 p.m. .

On weekends, the Fall River/New Bedford Line operates 26 trips between South Station and the South Coast . Weekend direct service runs approximately every 120 minutes . Inbound trains on weekends leave New Bedford Station from 4:47 a.m. to 9:53 p.m., with seven departures throughout the day. Outbound trains leave South Station from 6:37 a.m. to 11:59 p.m. .

### 2.3 Travel Times

Direct rides from downtown New Bedford to Boston's South Station take about 94 to 98 minutes . Some trips that require a transfer in East Taunton take about two hours . The scheduled trip time from Fall River and New Bedford to Boston is roughly 90 minutes, which is generally faster than car travel during rush hour .

The first train is scheduled to leave New Bedford Station at 4:27 a.m., stopping at Church Street Station at 4:33 a.m. and arriving at Boston's South Station at 6:01 a.m. .

## 3. Infrastructure and Equipment

### 3.1 Track and Right-of-Way

Phase 1 of the South Coast Rail project reconstructs 12.1 miles of the Fall River Secondary and 24.1 miles of Middleborough Secondary and the New Bedford Main Line [4]. The project uses the Middleborough Secondary (currently a freight line) to provide service to Taunton, New Bedford, and Fall River. The Middleborough Secondary connects to Cotley Junction in East Taunton .

From Cotley Junction, Phase 1 trains join the New Bedford Main Line and continue to New Bedford, or they branch off on the Fall River Secondary to Fall River (this area is known as the Southern Triangle) .

### 3.2 Rolling Stock

The MBTA uses EMD F40PH diesel-electric locomotives on the Fall River/New Bedford Line [6]. The F40PH is a 4-axle B-B locomotive with 3,000-3,200 horsepower (2.2-2.4 MW) built by the Electro-Motive Division of General Motors between 1975 and 1992 [7].

For passenger cars, the MBTA ordered 80 bilevel cars to provide immediate relief for the retirement of the MBB cars and for the completion of the South Coast Rail project. The extension to Fall River and New Bedford alone required 16 new bilevel cars, the first of which arrived on MBTA property in late 2022 .

### 3.3 Stations

All stations on the Fall River/New Bedford Line comply with MBTA standards and the Americans with Disabilities Act (ADA) requirements, which include high-level platforms for "all-doors" boarding . Other amenities include benches, canopies, bicycle storage, and parking facilities.

The new stations feature modern designs with accessible platforms, lighting, and passenger information systems. Parking is available at many stations, with standard rates of $4 per day on weekdays and $2 per day on weekends/holidays .

## 4. Operations

### 4.1 Fare Structure

The standard one-way fare for a trip from the South Coast stations to South Station is $12.25. Reduced fare is $6 for riders who qualify . Standard monthly passes are $388, and reduced monthly passes are $190 .

Children aged 11 and younger do not need a ticket . Unlimited weekend passes are available for \$10 .

### 4.2 Ticketing

Riders can buy tickets on the MBTA's mTicket app, or with cash or card onboard the train . For more information on buying tickets, the MBTA provides detailed guides on their website.

### 4.3 Parking

Parking is available at most stations on the Fall River/New Bedford Line. During the promotional period following the line's opening, parking was free at six stations from March 24 until the end of day on April 30, 2025: Middleborough, East Taunton, Freetown, Church Street, Fall River Depot, and New Bedford .

After the promotional period, standard parking rates apply: $4 per day on weekdays and $2 per day on weekends/holidays . Overnight parking is allowed at most stations, but at New Bedford Station it comes with a multi-day rate of \$15 per day .

Parking can be paid for using the PayByPhone app at most MBTA lots .

## 5. Economic Impact and Development

### 5.1 Tourism and Visitor Impact

The Fall River/New Bedford Line has had a significant impact on tourism in the South Coast region. At the New Bedford Whaling Museum, ticket sales are up 30% compared to the previous year, largely driven by Boston-area visitors. President and CEO Amanda McMullen says the train is the only explanation for the sudden spike .

The extra admission revenue is helping to fund museum initiatives that are hard to find grants for, including upgrades to the ticket system and computer servers that support the museum's digital catalog .

The Whaling Museum has stepped up its advertising, using geographically targeted social media posts and video ad buys in Boston's South Station, the northernmost point on the Fall River/New Bedford Line .

### 5.2 Economic Development and Housing

South Coast Rail is anticipated to offer significant economic and housing development, as well as educational and recreational opportunities throughout southeastern Massachusetts with area residents having much easier access to the many amenities of the South Coast and connections to downtown Boston [8].

Boosting economic growth through increased access to Boston is expected to attract businesses, create jobs, and expand housing opportunities [9]. The line is also expanding educational access, with more students from greater Boston and surrounding areas able to easily commute to UMass Dartmouth, enhancing diversity and enrollment [9].

New Bedford's new "transit-oriented development" zoning districts are designed to encourage development near the train stations . A hotel to be built near New Bedford Station was approved, and developers are showing increased interest in building housing near the stations .

### 5.3 Ridership Growth

The Fall River/New Bedford Line has experienced significant ridership growth. On average, riders are taking 2,000 more trips each day compared to pre-extension levels . Weekday ridership increased by 36%, while weekend ridership increased by 13% [10].

Commuting workers, rather than leisure travelers, appear to make up most of the increased ridership . This suggests the line is fulfilling its primary purpose of connecting South Coast residents to employment opportunities in Boston and other areas along the route.

## 6. Future Plans

### 6.1 Full Build Phase (Phase 2)

The South Coast Rail project has a second phase, known as the "Full Build" . Phase 2 would turn the South Coast lines into an extension of the Stoughton branch of the Providence/Stoughton Line [4].

The Full Build project would extend commuter rail service on the Stoughton Line to the New Bedford and Fall River lines, connecting more communities, including Easton and Raynham, to Boston [4]. As proposed, Phase 2 would extend the Stoughton branch south over the abandoned Dighton and Somerset railroad through Easton, Raynham, and Taunton before meeting the Fall River/New Bedford Line at East Taunton .

However, funding has not yet been identified for the full build program [4]. The project travels from Boston to Stoughton and continues south before splitting south of Taunton for Fall River and New Bedford (using the same southern alignment as Phase 1). Preliminary design is ongoing, and an anticipated service start date will be determined as the project advances [11].

### 6.2 Service Improvements

The MBTA and state transportation officials have discussed potential service improvements for the Fall River/New Bedford Line. These could include more frequent service, especially during peak commuting hours, and potential express service options that would reduce travel times for commuters .

Some riders have suggested that an express train would be a big help for commuters, as the current morning train takes a little over 90 minutes . The MBTA is evaluating various service enhancement options as ridership continues to grow.

## 7. Notable Riders and First Day Celebrations

The opening of the Fall River/New Bedford Line on March 24, 2025, was marked by significant celebration and emotional moments for the South Coast community. About 90 people boarded the first train from New Bedford to Boston at 4:27 a.m., the first to connect the cities in 67 years .

Among the notable riders was Brian da Camara of Fall River, who celebrated his 48th birthday by taking the first train out of Fall River in 65 years . He expressed excitement about not having to be stuck in traffic on the way to Celtics games and being able to get to Boston and back without driving .

Nine-year-old Kolson Miller convinced his father to wake up before 5 o'clock in the morning so they could watch the first train leave Fall River. He couldn't hold back a scream as the train's operator personally handed him an MBTA pin, saying "I'm so excited because this is history" .

The first day of service also attracted transit enthusiasts from across the region. Miles Taylor, a transit YouTuber, described the experience: "I want to say I was the first person to go over these tracks, or at least among the first. To me, New Bedford and Fall River are always these alien communities that were so difficult to get to. Now you have commuter rail service every 70 minutes all day. It just totally opens up the ability to take trips" .

## 8. Community Impact

### 8.1 Access to Services

The Fall River/New Bedford Line has significantly improved access to various services for South Coast residents. Dan Brogan, a public transit specialist for SRPEDD, said the new line not only builds connections between communities but also increases access to health care, including the VA medical center in Brockton .

Nancy Wilkinson and Al Lay, of Dartmouth, moved to the South Coast from Boston about 20 years ago. They had hoped the rail would arrive so that they could commute to work. Now that it's here, they're retired but plan to use it regularly for doctor appointments and to get together with friends .

### 8.2 Employment Opportunities

The line has created new opportunities for employment in both directions. Jeff Pontiff, a commercial real estate broker from New Bedford, hopes South Coast Rail will bring commuters in the other direction for work. "To me, it opens up the labor pool to come south to New Bedford to work, if we have enough industry," he said .

The line is also advantageous from a real-estate perspective, including opportunities for transit-oriented housing development. Pontiff noted that the train "adds to the overall aura of raising the bar of who we are as a community" .

### 8.3 Educational Access

The Fall River/New Bedford Line has expanded educational opportunities for students in both directions. More students from greater Boston and surrounding areas can easily commute to UMass Dartmouth, enhancing diversity and enrollment [9]. Conversely, South Coast students now have easier access to colleges and universities in the Boston area.

## 9. Environmental Impact

The Fall River/New Bedford Line is expected to have positive environmental impacts by reducing automobile traffic on Route 24 and the Southeast Expressway. The line offers a convenient way to get to Boston while avoiding highway traffic .

The MBTA evaluated noise levels anticipated from South Coast Rail trains according to Federal Transit Administration (FTA) guidelines and detailed the results in the 2013 Final Environmental Impact Statement/Report and the 2018 Final Supplemental Environmental Impact Report . Based on this evaluation, the MBTA has provided funding for a set of alternative noise mitigation measures to owners of properties that are affected by noise above the impact level .

## 10. Connections and Integration

The Fall River/New Bedford Line connects with other MBTA services at several points along its route:



* **South Station**: Connects with the Red Line subway, commuter rail lines to Providence/Stoughton, Franklin/Foxboro, Kingston, and Greenbush, as well as intercity Amtrak services

* **JFK/UMass**: Connects with the Red Line subway

* **Quincy Center**: Connects with the Red Line subway

* **Braintree**: Connects with the Red Line subway

The line also provides connections to various bus routes at multiple stations, allowing for seamless transfers to local and regional bus services throughout the South Coast region.

## 11. Trivia and Interesting Facts



* The Fall River/New Bedford Line was formerly known as the Middleborough/Lakeville Line before being renamed on March 24, 2025 [1]

* The Middleborough/Lakeville station has been renamed Lakeville station, with regular commuter rail service moving to the new Middleborough station on Grove Street

* The Lakeville station is now used only for seasonal CapeFlyer service to Cape Cod

* The line's southern terminus alternates between Fall River Depot and New Bedford stations

* The project cost more than \$1 billion to construct

* The first day of service coincided with the 65th anniversary of the line's closure in 1958 [8]

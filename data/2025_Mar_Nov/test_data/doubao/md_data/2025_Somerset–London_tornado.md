# 2025 Somerset–London tornado

The **2025 Somerset–London tornado** was a **large and deadly nocturnal EF4 tornado** that struck the cities of Somerset and London in Kentucky, United States, on the evening of May 16, 2025 [1]. The tornado traveled more than **55 miles** between the two towns, causing extensive damage with winds reaching **170 mph** [1]. According to the National Weather Service, it was the **deadliest tornado and first EF-4** ever recorded in the county's warning area [1].

## Meteorological history

### Formation and timeline

The tornado touched down near the community of **Whittle**, about 20 miles west of Somerset, around **10:30 p.m. local time** on May 16, 2025 [2]%20per%20hour.). It was officially classified as forming at **10:27 p.m. EDT** and dissipated at **11:56 p.m. EDT**, giving it a total duration of **1 hour and 29 minutes** . The tornado lifted just east of London shortly before midnight [2]%20per%20hour.).

### Path and intensity

The tornado carved out a damage path of just shy of **60 miles** over the course of three counties: **Russell County, Pulaski County** (specifically Somerset), and **Laurel County** (specifically London) [3]. It was one of the strongest tornadoes ever recorded in the area .

The tornado's intensity varied along its path:



* It strengthened to **EF-3** as it approached Somerset, where it caused "catastrophic damage" in parts of the city

* A high-tension power pole east of Somerset was lifted, crumpled, and sent flying for several hundred yards

* After crossing Interstate 75 near London, the tornado strengthened again to **EF-4** intensity

* It was **1,700 yards wide** at its maximum width [3]

* The tornado reached its peak intensity of **EF-4** with winds of 170 mph as it took a direct path through the Sunshine Hills subdivision in London [3]

## Impact and damage

### Casualties

The tornado resulted in **at least 19 fatalities and 10 injuries** [4]. The death toll included a **local firefighter** who likely died shielding his wife from the tornado . According to a WLEX-TV publication on May 17, eight people remained hospitalized . In June 2025, Kentucky Governor Andy Beshear announced that a **20th person** had died from the tornado .

### Property damage

The tornado caused an estimated **\$350 million in damage** (2025 USD) . In Laurel County alone, the tornado damaged or destroyed **1,500 homes** .

#### Somerset damage

In Somerset, the tornado made a direct hit in the **business district along the U.S. Highway 27 corridor** just south of the Somerset Mall . Along Highway 27, the typically busy road was vacated, with downed power lines littering the roadway alongside the remnants of siding, splintered wood, and the remains of nearby roofs [5].

#### London damage

The hardest-hit area in London was the **Sunshine Hills subdivision**, where many homes were destroyed and the area looked unrecognizable . The tornado caused "extreme damage" in this residential area .

#### London-Corbin Airport

The tornado crossed over the runway of the **London-Corbin Airport** and caused significant damage . At the airport, several hangars as well as aircraft were thrown and destroyed, including:



* **Six Beechcraft T-34 Mentor** training aircraft

* A **medical helicopter** belonging to PHI Air Med

The storm left half of the equipment used for air transport in ruins, with PHI Air Med personnel confirming that "most of it is a total loss" . The hangar and PHI Air Med London "2" base were completely destroyed .

### Infrastructure impact

The tornado knocked out power to more than **172,000 customers** in Kentucky [1]. Multiple counties declared a local state of emergency due to the damage [1].

The tornado crossed several major transportation routes:



* **KY 363** and **Interstate 75**

* It weakened to mid-range EF3 intensity as it impacted a neighborhood and mobile home park along Philpot Road after crossing I-75

### Environmental damage

The tornado tore through parts of the **Daniel Boone National Forest** at mid-range EF3 to low-end EF4 intensity, shredding and snapping several trees . The EF-4 tornado that crossed the Daniel Boone National Forest's London Ranger District on May 16, 2025, **leveled portions of the forest** [6]. The tornado's path of destruction left a **brownish trail visible from space** as it blazed through the forest, uprooting trees, snapping branches, and tearing off bark [7].

## Aftermath

### Immediate response

Help began arriving almost immediately from around the state to Laurel County, with **neighbors who didn't suffer major damage opening their doors** to those who lost everything . The Rivas couple, who survived the tornado, reported that their neighbor brought them into her home and then drove them to the hospital .

### Government response

Kentucky Governor **Andy Beshear** announced that at least 14 people had been killed in the storms, though the exact number of fatalities in Somerset was not immediately clear . The Lexington Herald-Leader newspaper quoted a spokesperson for the Laurel County Sheriff's Office as saying that **nine people had been killed** in London .

The Federal Emergency Management Agency (FEMA) offered a wide variety of aid to those affected by the tornadoes, including:



* Money for **critical needs** (food, water, medical supplies)

* **Temporary housing** assistance

* Home repairs and replacement

* Funeral expenses [8]

The deadline to apply for FEMA assistance was **July 23, 2025** .

### Recovery efforts

The Small Business Administration (SBA) approved over **\$8.1 million in federal disaster loans** to support Kentucky businesses, nonprofits, homeowners, and renters affected by the tornado [9].

As of June 2025, the tornado had been deemed the **deadliest to occur in 2025** and was the deadliest overall since another EF4 tornado in 2021, often referred to as the **Western Kentucky tornado** [4].

### Community resilience

Despite the devastation, many residents expressed determination to rebuild. Ida House, who lost most of her home, stated: "It's going to be a long time, but we'll get it. We're going to get everything back to normal, and we're going to stay right here. This is our neighborhood. And we're going to stay right here" .

## Historical significance

The 2025 Somerset–London tornado holds several notable distinctions:



1. It was the **first EF-4 tornado** ever recorded in the National Weather Service's Jackson, Kentucky, warning area [1]

2. It was the **deadliest tornado of 2025**

3. It was the deadliest tornado in the United States since the **2021 Western Kentucky tornado**

4. It was only the **sixth EF4 or EF5 tornado** in Kentucky in the last 50 years [10]

5. The tornado's path of destruction left a visible scar from space, demonstrating the power of this natural disaster [11]

## Meteorological context

The tornado occurred as part of a significant severe thunderstorm and tornado outbreak that impacted the central and eastern United States during the afternoon and evening of May 16, 2025 . Discrete supercells formed across southern Missouri and Illinois during the afternoon and moved east, producing numerous tornadoes, many of which were strong to violent .

The Storm Prediction Center had outlined a **moderate (4/5) risk** over much of the middle Mississippi and lower Ohio valleys on May 16, with the greatest risk over southeastern Missouri, far northeastern Arkansas, northwestern Tennessee, southern Illinois and Indiana, far southwestern Ohio, and much of Kentucky .

The environment was highly conducive to severe weather, with:



* **Mixed layer CAPE** (convective available potential energy) of 3000 to 4000 J/kg

* **High deep layer shear values** of 65 to 70 knots

* The Significant Tornado Parameter (STP) peaked around **9** at 6 PM

* Effective Storm Relative Helicity (SRH) was over **400 m²/s²**

## Legacy

The 2025 Somerset–London tornado serves as a reminder of the destructive power of tornadoes and the importance of preparedness. The tornado's impact on the communities of Somerset and London, particularly the Sunshine Hills subdivision and London-Corbin Airport, will be felt for years to come.

The tornado's path through the Daniel Boone National Forest left a lasting mark on the landscape, with satellite imagery showing the extensive damage to forest ecosystems [7]. This event has prompted discussions about tornado safety, emergency preparedness, and the need for continued research into tornado formation and prediction.

Despite the tragedy, the response from neighbors, first responders, and government agencies demonstrates the resilience of affected communities and their commitment to recovery and rebuilding. The 2025 Somerset–London tornado will be remembered as one of the most significant weather events in Kentucky's history, serving as both a cautionary tale and an inspiration for community strength in the face of disaster.

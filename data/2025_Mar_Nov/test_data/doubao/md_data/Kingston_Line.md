# Kingston Line

The **Kingston Line** is a commuter rail line operated by the Massachusetts Bay Transportation Authority (MBTA) in southeastern Massachusetts, United States . It runs 35.1 miles (56.5 km) southeast from Boston to Kingston, serving eight intermediate stops . The line is part of the MBTA's Old Colony Lines system, which also includes the Middleborough/Lakeville Line [1].

## Route and Stations

The Kingston Line operates between **South Station** in Boston and **Kingston station** in Plymouth County . The ten stations along the route, in order from Boston to Kingston, are:



1. South Station (Boston)

2. JFK/UMass (Boston)

3. Quincy Center (Quincy)

4. Braintree (Braintree)

5. South Weymouth (Weymouth)

6. Abington (Abington)

7. Whitman (Whitman)

8. Hanson (Hanson)

9. Halifax (Halifax)

10. Kingston (Kingston) [2]

The line shares trackage with the Middleborough/Lakeville Line between South Station and Braintree [1].

## Service and Operations

The Kingston Line operates on weekdays only, with service beginning in the early morning and ending in the evening [3]. The typical service pattern provides trains approximately every 75-110 minutes during peak hours . The travel time from South Station to Kingston is approximately 59 minutes .

As of recent data, the line has an on-time performance rate of 69-78% [4]. This places it in the middle range among MBTA commuter rail lines for punctuality .

## History

The Kingston Line has a complex history dating back to the 19th century. The original line was built by the Old Colony Railroad, which opened from Boston to Plymouth in 1845 . This line was later acquired by the New Haven Railroad and eventually abandoned in the 1950s .

Modern service on the Kingston Line began in 1997. Construction on the restoration project began in 1993, and service was inaugurated on September 29, 1997, as the Plymouth/Kingston Line . The line was initially operated as part of the Old Colony Lines restoration project, which also included the Middleborough/Lakeville Line [1].

In 2012, weekend service was discontinued due to budget constraints [5]). The line was renamed from Plymouth/Kingston Line to Kingston Line after the closure of Plymouth station .

## Infrastructure

The Kingston Line uses standard gauge track and operates with diesel multiple unit trains [6]. The line is electrified only in the Boston terminal area .

The line has several significant infrastructure features, including:



* The Dorchester Tunnel, which carries the line under Dorchester Bay

* Multiple drawbridges over local waterways

* Park-and-ride facilities at several stations, particularly at Halifax, which has one of the largest parking lots on the line

## Ridership

The Kingston Line serves a primarily suburban and commuter-oriented ridership base. The busiest stations include South Station, Braintree, and Halifax . Halifax station, in particular, was noted as the busiest on the line shortly after service began, with its original 281-space parking lot expanded by 120-150 spaces around 2000 .

## Connections

At South Station, passengers can connect to:



* Other MBTA commuter rail lines

* The MBTA subway system (Red Line, Orange Line)

* Intercity buses

* Amtrak long-distance trains

Along the route, connections are available to local bus services in each community served by the line .

## Future Developments

The MBTA has identified several potential improvements for the Kingston Line, including:



* Enhanced weekend service restoration

* Increased frequency during peak hours

* Infrastructure upgrades to improve reliability

* Accessibility improvements at stations

The line is also being considered for integration with the South Coast Rail project, which is expected to provide service to Fall River and New Bedford .

## Notability

The Kingston Line is notable for several reasons:



* It represents the restoration of historic rail service to southeastern Massachusetts

* It provides an alternative to highway commuting on Route 3

* It serves several historic communities, including Plymouth (via connecting bus service), the site of the first Thanksgiving

* It is part of the MBTA's efforts to provide sustainable transportation options

The line has become an integral part of the regional transportation network, serving both daily commuters and weekend travelers exploring the historic and cultural attractions of southeastern Massachusetts .

# Banshee (collection)

**Banshee** was the second runway collection presented by British fashion designer **Alexander McQueen** for the autumn/winter 1994-95 season [1]. The collection drew inspiration from the **banshee**, a female spirit from Irish mythology whose mournful wailing heralds death . Staged on 26 February 1994 during London Fashion Week at the Café de Paris nightclub, the show featured aggressive styling and an amateurish production with unpaid contributors and no pre-show fittings .

## Background and Context

The Banshee collection emerged during a critical period in McQueen's early career. Following his graduation from Central Saint Martins in 1992 with his MA collection "Jack the Ripper Stalks His Victims," which was purchased in its entirety by influential fashion stylist Isabella Blow, McQueen presented his first professional runway show for the Nihilism collection in 1993 [2]. The Banshee collection represented his second major runway presentation [3].

The collection was created in a resource-constrained environment. After the show, McQueen moved his studio to Hoxton Square and began working with Katy England, who became his creative director . The informal and collaborative nature of McQueen's early work environment allowed for experimental approaches to design and production .

## Design Inspiration and Concept

The Banshee collection was fundamentally inspired by the mythological **Gaelic spirit** of the same name, drawing on themes of romance and tragedy [1]. McQueen was particularly interested in presenting women as **powerful and resilient survivors of tragic circumstances** . The collection was presented within a **post-shipwreck scene** [1], creating a dramatic narrative framework for the designs.

The show opened with **two pregnant women walking to the screams of Irish fairy women**, mourning a death that was soon to come [4]. This theatrical opening set the tone for the collection's exploration of death, mourning, and female strength in the face of tragedy.

## Design Elements and Aesthetic

The Banshee collection featured distinctive design elements that would become signatures of McQueen's aesthetic. The **exaggerated tailoring** nodded to both the 16th century and Victoriana [5]. Particularly notable were the **high-rise highwayman's collars**, which approximated Elizabethan ruffs as well as the stiff "stovepipe" neckwear of Victorian gentlemen [5].

The collection showcased McQueen's technical skill through **razor-sharp tailoring**, a hallmark of his aesthetic [6]. Design elements included **witchy details** that evoked the mystical banshee, with models wearing outfits featuring **feathered cobweb lace and crisp white ruffles** [6].

The collection's palette ranged from **pale earthy tones to bold, bright oranges and yellows**, ultimately culminating in striking metal embellishments . Eveningwear featured **hand-shredded silks and delicate cobweb lace** masterfully employed to create intricate, ghostly gowns .

## Production and Presentation

The Banshee show was characterized by its **aggressive styling and amateurish production** . Like its predecessor, the Nihilism collection, everyone who contributed to the show was unpaid and there were no pre-show fittings .

The production utilized **DIY practices** that were common in McQueen's early career due to limited resources . One notable example was when McQueen and collaborator Andrew Groves found a roll of clear plastic wrap on Elizabeth Street and decided to make a dress from it . Stylist Katy England recalled that the collection used "literally any fabric he could lay his hands on or that he could beg, steal or borrow" .

**Isabella Blow**, who served as McQueen's muse and patron, played a prominent role in the show. She walked the runway wearing a purple draped shirt with a krall neck and later a black chiffon dress with an embroidered waistcoat . The legendary journalist and editor received rapturous applause from the audience as she took multiple turns on the runway, with "McQueen" stenciled in silver over her lacquered hair .

## Notable Pieces and Garments

A documented garment from the Banshee collection is preserved in the National Gallery of Victoria's collection. This **dress from 1994** is constructed from wool blend with plastic thread, measuring 89.0 cm in center back length and 29.0 cm in waist width [7]. The dress was designed by Julien Macdonald, who served as McQueen's assistant in the 1990s [7].

The collection featured several controversial elements that reflected McQueen's provocative approach to fashion. According to documentation, the show included **a pregnant skinhead, a model in a plaster corset, and a woman pretending to put her finger in her vagina** . These elements contributed to the collection's reputation for shock tactics and controversial imagery.

## Critical Reception and Legacy

The Banshee collection has been recognized as significant in McQueen's career development. Fashion sociologist Caroline Evans described it as a show that **"set the tone for others over the next few years"** [8]. The collection helped establish McQueen's reputation for dark romanticism and what Evans termed an **"aesthetic of cruelty"** [8].

The show's theatrical elements and controversial content reflected McQueen's early approach to fashion as performance art. The combination of mythological inspiration, historical references, and contemporary shock tactics became hallmarks of his design philosophy and contributed to his reputation as the **"enfant terrible" of British fashion** .

The Banshee theme has continued to influence the McQueen brand. In 2024, current creative director Seán McGirr presented a spring/summer 2025 collection that paid homage to the original Banshee collection, drawing on his own Celtic heritage and the mythical female spirit of Irish folklore [9]. McGirr described the banshee as "rooted in the history of McQueen, but it's also a story that I grew up with, so it feels deeply personal to me" .

## Cultural Impact

The Banshee collection marked a significant moment in the evolution of **1990s fashion** and McQueen's broader cultural impact. The collection's fusion of historical tailoring techniques with contemporary subversive elements established a template for McQueen's later work and influenced subsequent generations of fashion designers.

The show's presentation at the Café de Paris nightclub in London's Soho district reflected the designer's connection to underground cultural scenes. McQueen was a regular in the gay clubs of Soho and the BDSM and drag nights where performers like Leigh Bowery and Divine David appeared . This cultural environment significantly influenced the collection's provocative and boundary-pushing elements.

## Conclusion

The Banshee collection stands as a pivotal work in Alexander McQueen's career and in the history of 1990s fashion. Through its mythological inspiration, historical references, and provocative presentation, the collection established many of the themes and techniques that would define McQueen's design philosophy throughout his career.

The show's combination of **aggressive styling**, **amateurish production values**, and **controversial content** reflected both the resource constraints of McQueen's early career and his deliberate approach to challenging conventional fashion norms [8]. The collection's legacy extends beyond individual garments to encompass its role in establishing fashion as a form of performance art and social commentary.

The Banshee collection remains a significant reference point in fashion history, studied for its innovative design elements, controversial presentation, and influence on subsequent fashion movements. Its mythological inspiration continues to resonate in contemporary fashion, as seen in the 2025 collection by Seán McGirr that revisited and reimagined the banshee theme for a new generation [9].

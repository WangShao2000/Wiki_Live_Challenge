# The Interstellar Song Contest

**The Interstellar Song Contest** is the sixth episode of the second series (15th overall series) of the British science fiction television series *Doctor Who*, first broadcast on BBC One and Disney+ on 17 May 2025 . The episode was written by Juno Dawson and directed by Ben A. Williams [1]. It features Ncuti Gatwa as the Fifteenth Doctor and Varada Sethu as his companion Belinda Chandra [2].

The episode draws direct inspiration from the Eurovision Song Contest, with the 69th edition of Eurovision being broadcast on the same day as the episode's premiere . Real-life Eurovision commentators Rylan Clark and Graham Norton appear in the episode playing themselves . The episode has been noted as the most expensive to produce in the entire history of *Doctor Who* [3].

## Production

### Development

The concept for "The Interstellar Song Contest" originated with showrunner Russell T. Davies, who pitched the basic premise as "Die Hard meets Eurovision" to writer Juno Dawson in 2022 [4]. Dawson later recalled that Davies emailed her at approximately 1:30 in the morning with this concept [5]. The writer, who had previously worked on the *Doctor Who* spinoff podcast *Doctor Who Redacted*, described it as "the easiest 'yes' I've ever said" .

Dawson initially proposed a more ambitious concept inspired by the disaster film *The Poseidon Adventure*, but this was deemed prohibitively expensive to produce [5]. She later expressed mild embarrassment about this initial idea, stating, "I look back now with a slight cringe" [5]. The final script maintained the core concept of "Eurovision meets Die Hard" throughout the development process .

### Crew and Cast

The episode was written by Juno Dawson, making it the first episode in *Doctor Who* history to be written by an openly transgender person . The episode was directed by Ben A. Williams [1], with Vicki Delow serving as producer [6]. Executive producers included Russell T. Davies, Julie Gardner, Jane Tranter, and Joel Collins [6].

The cast featured Ncuti Gatwa as the Fifteenth Doctor and Varada Sethu as Belinda Chandra [2]. The episode marked the return of several classic *Doctor Who* characters, including Carole Ann Ford reprising her role as Susan Foreman, the Doctor's granddaughter, who had not appeared in the series since 1964 [2]. Anita Dobson played Mrs. Flood, while Archie Panjabi was introduced as The Rani [2].

The guest cast included Freddie Fox as the villainous Kid, Kadiff Kirwan as Mike Gabbastone, Charlie Condou as Gary Gabbastone, and Miriam-Teak Lee as Cora St Bavier [7]. Rylan Clark and Graham Norton appeared as themselves, reprising their roles as Eurovision commentators [2].

### Production Costs

"The Interstellar Song Contest" has been confirmed as the most expensive episode in the entire history of *Doctor Who* [3]. Despite this significant budget, some viewers and critics noted that the episode only "fleetingly looked the part" compared to other high-budget science fiction productions like *Andor*, which has a reported budget of nearly \$300 million per season [3].

The high production costs are evident in several elaborate sequences, including the scene where 100,000 spectators are blown into space, which required extensive special effects work . The episode also features complex holographic effects and detailed alien creature designs .

## Plot Summary

The episode begins with the Doctor and Belinda arriving at the Harmony Arena space station in the year 2925, just in time for the 803rd Interstellar Song Contest [8]. They witness Rylan Clark emerging from cryogenic suspension to host the event [8]. The Doctor and Belinda decide to watch the contest from VIP boxes, intrigued by the spectacle .

As the contest begins, trouble quietly brews in the background. Mrs. Flood, sitting in the audience, secretly copies the Doctor's vindicator reading onto her own device, describing it as "the final link" she needs [8]. Meanwhile, in the control gallery, the production team's security drone guards are hijacked by an interloping Hellion known as "Kid" [8].

Kid and his accomplice Wynn, another Hellion who had infiltrated the production team, take control of the Harmony Arena and the contest's broadcast [8]. They switch the broadcast to a dress rehearsal and open the air shield of the arena, causing the entire audience, including the Doctor and the TARDIS, to be sucked into space [8].

Frozen in space and barely conscious, the Doctor has a vision of his granddaughter Susan Foreman, who tells him to go back and find her . He wakes from his stasis and uses a nearby confetti cannon to propel himself back to the Harmony Arena, where he is resuscitated by paramedic Mike Gabbastone, who is attending the event with his husband Gary [8].

While the Doctor, Mike, and Gary search for answers, Belinda encounters other survivors in the arena, including contest favorite Cora St Bavier, who was representing the planet Trion [8]. When the survivors discover that Hellions are behind the attack, Cora reveals herself to be a Hellion in hiding. She explains that Kid and Wynn are seeking vengeance against the contest's sponsor, a corporation that razed their home planet of Hellia for its natural resource, the Hell Poppy, and then blamed the atrocity on the native population [8].

Enraged by this injustice and the apparent death of Belinda (whom he believes was also sucked into space), the Doctor seeks out the Hellions and destroys their delta wave device, which they had planned to use to kill the three trillion viewers of the contest [8]. Using a hard-light hologram, the Doctor repeatedly electrocutes Kid, ignoring Susan's pleas in his mind to stop [8].

The Doctor eventually stops his attack and works to save the frozen audience members using the station's "mavity" field, which has suspended them in a state of preservation . Cora is given the opportunity to sing a song in honor of her destroyed home planet Hellia for the three trillion viewers .

As the Doctor and Belinda prepare to leave, they encounter a hologram of Graham Norton, who reveals that Earth was destroyed on May 24, 2025 . The Doctor immediately departs to try to save Earth on that date.

In a mid-credits scene, Mrs. Flood is revealed to be The Rani, who has bi-generated into two separate beings: herself and the elderly Mrs. Flood . The Rani declares her intention to seek revenge against the Doctor .

## Reception

### Critical Response

"The Interstellar Song Contest" received mixed reviews from critics. Radio Times gave the episode a perfect 5 out of 5 stars, describing it as "a magnificent spin on Eurovision sprinkled with the stuff of legends" [2]. Patrick Mulkern praised the episode's blend of camp Eurovision-style entertainment with high-stakes science fiction adventure [2].

Out Front Magazine gave the episode a 96/100 rating, calling it "one of the best and most exciting episodes of the entire series" . The review particularly praised the episode for being written by a transgender woman and noted its themes of colonization and genocide as subtle commentary on contemporary issues .

However, other critics were more critical. Doctor Who TV's review was highly critical, stating that the episode "sabotages itself" through inconsistent storytelling . The review specifically criticized the episode's handling of the frozen audience, noting that the script "can't decide if the audience is dead or not, turning them into Schrödinger's spectators" . The review also faulted the episode's "not-so-science fiction" elements, particularly the technobabble explanation involving the "mavity" field .

Den of Geek's review described the episode as "a land of contrasts" and noted that it "can't settle on a consistent tone" . The review praised the episode's visual spectacle and the performances of Rylan Clark and Graham Norton but criticized the Doctor's sudden shift to violent behavior as unearned and out of character .

Film Stories offered a more balanced review, noting that "the song contest stuff is really well realised" and praising the episode's glossy production values . However, the review also noted that the episode struggled to develop the dynamic between the Doctor and Belinda .

### Audience Reaction

Social media reaction to the episode was largely positive, with many fans praising the return of Carole Ann Ford as Susan Foreman [9]. Several prominent *Doctor Who* fans and writers, including Simon Guerrier, David, Paul Cornell, John F.P. Lane, and Tom Newsom, publicly praised the episode and writer Juno Dawson [9].

The episode's timing, airing on the same night as the actual Eurovision Song Contest, was widely noted and appreciated by fans [10]. Many viewers enjoyed the meta-humor of having real Eurovision commentators appearing in the episode .

## Cultural Impact

### Eurovision Connection

"The Interstellar Song Contest" was deliberately scheduled to air on the same day as the 69th Eurovision Song Contest, creating a unique viewing experience for British audiences . The episode's premise is essentially a science fiction version of Eurovision, extrapolated into the year 2925 as the 803rd edition of an intergalactic competition [11].

The episode features several elements that directly reference Eurovision, including the format of the contest, the voting system, and the presence of Rylan Clark and Graham Norton as commentators . The character of Rylan Clark is portrayed as having been cryogenically preserved and unfrozen annually to host the Interstellar Song Contest , which fans found particularly amusing.

### Social Commentary

The episode incorporates several themes of social and political commentary. The character of Cora St Bavier, a Hellion who has cut off her horns to fit in with mainstream society, serves as a metaphor for the extremes some people go to in order to conform . This has been interpreted by some viewers as a commentary on transgender identity and the pressures of societal acceptance .

The episode also addresses themes of racial stereotyping and xenophobia through the portrayal of Hellions. In one scene, Cora overhears her manager Len accusing Hellions of practicing cannibalism and witchcraft . This sequence is particularly poignant as both characters are portrayed by Black actors, visually emphasizing that they share more in common than just business interests .

The character of Kid represents a more complex take on villainy, as his actions are driven by legitimate grievances. His home planet Hellia was destroyed by corporate greed, stripped of its natural resources for artificial honey flavoring . This storyline has been interpreted as commentary on colonialism, corporate exploitation, and environmental destruction .

### Historical Significance

"The Interstellar Song Contest" holds several significant milestones in *Doctor Who* history. It marks the first time the series has been written by an openly transgender person , a fact that was widely celebrated by both fans and the media.

The episode also features the long-awaited return of Carole Ann Ford as Susan Foreman, the Doctor's granddaughter, who had not appeared in the series since 1964 [2]. Ford's appearance, while brief, was highly anticipated by long-time fans and has sparked speculation about her potential role in future episodes .

The episode also introduces the character of The Rani in a new incarnation, played by Archie Panjabi [2]. The character's return, combined with the revelation that she has bi-generated into two separate beings, has created significant buzz among fans about the direction of the series .

## Legacy

"The Interstellar Song Contest" has already begun to establish its legacy within the *Doctor Who* franchise. The episode's high production values and ambitious scope have set a new standard for the series, particularly in terms of visual effects and set design [3].

The episode's themes and story elements are likely to have long-lasting implications for the series. The return of The Rani and the appearance of Susan Foreman have both been positioned as major plot points that will likely be revisited in future episodes .

The episode's success has also helped to establish Juno Dawson as an important new voice in *Doctor Who* writing. Her ability to balance camp entertainment with social commentary and character development has been widely praised [12].

The Interstellar Song Contest itself has become a notable fictional event within the *Doctor Who* universe. As a futuristic version of Eurovision that has expanded to include planets across the galaxy [11], it represents the kind of optimistic, interconnected future that the series often portrays.

# 2025 World Seniors Championship

The **2025 World Seniors Championship** (officially the **2025 JenningsBet World Seniors Championship**) was a snooker tournament played from 7 to 11 May 2025 at the Crucible Theatre in Sheffield, England [1][2][3]. It was the 16th edition of the World Seniors Championship and the seventh consecutive year the tournament was held at the Crucible [2][4]. The event was open to players over 40 years of age who were not ranked inside the top 64 of the professional world rankings [2].

The tournament was won by Alfie Burden, who defeated the 2018 champion Aaron Canavan 8–4 in the final to claim his first World Seniors title [5][6]. Burden had previously finished as runner-up in the 2023 edition [7]. The defending champion was Igor Figueiredo, who was eliminated in the first round by Wayne Townsend [2][8].

The event was broadcast in the United Kingdom on Channel 5 [2][3]. It was notable for marking the retirement of referee Michaela Tabb from traditional snooker officiating; she presided over the first session of the final before stepping down [2][9][10].

## Format and Participants

The tournament featured a field of 16 players, consisting of invited legends, former world champions, and qualifiers from various international regions [1][11]. The event was sponsored by the bookmaker JenningsBet [2][12].

### Qualification and Selection
Participation was open to amateur players aged over 40 and professional players aged over 45, provided they were ranked outside the top 64 of the World Snooker Tour rankings [2].

The field included:
*   **Defending Champion:** Igor Figueiredo (Brazil) [1].
*   **Former World Seniors Champions:** Jimmy White, Aaron Canavan [1][11].
*   **Invited Players:** Ken Doherty, Tony Drago, Tony Knowles [1][13].
*   **Debuting Professionals:** Joe Perry and Dominic Dale, both of whom announced their retirement from the professional tour prior to the event [13][8].
*   **Regional Qualifiers:**
    *   **Europe:** Craig Steadman [11].
    *   **Asia-Pacific:** Hassan Kerde (Australia) [1][11].
    *   **Africa:** Charl Jonck (South Africa) [1][11].
    *   **Americas:** Fabio Anderson Luersen (Brazil) [11].
*   **Golden Ticket Winner:** Alfie Burden (England), who qualified via a play-off event in Reading in April 2025 [1][5][11].
*   **Other Qualifiers:** Wayne Townsend, Andrew Norman, Gerard Greene [1][14].

### Match Format
The format for the 2025 edition was expanded compared to previous years. The first round and quarter-finals were played as the best of 7 frames. The semi-finals were increased to the best of 11 frames, and the final was played as the best of 15 frames [13][3][8].

### Prize Fund
The winner of the championship received a top prize of £15,000 [15]. A special prize of a BYD SEAL U car was offered to the first player to compile a maximum break (147) during the tournament [16].

## Tournament Summary

### First Round
The tournament began on 7 May 2025 [13]. The opening round saw the elimination of the defending champion, Igor Figueiredo, who lost 2–4 to qualifier Wayne Townsend [2][13]. Townsend secured the victory by winning the final three frames of the match [2].

Two notable players making their World Seniors debut, Joe Perry and Dominic Dale, secured victories in their opening matches. Perry defeated Fabio Anderson Luersen 4–2, while Dale overcame Craig Steadman by the same scoreline [13][2].

In a repeat of the 2023 final, Alfie Burden faced four-time winner Jimmy White. Burden defeated White 4–1, reversing the result of their previous title encounter [5][17]. Ken Doherty, the 1997 World Champion, was defeated 2–4 by the African Seniors champion Charl Jonck [13][17]. The Australian qualifier Hassan Kerde whitewashed Tony Knowles 4–0, while Tony Drago defeated Gerard Greene 4–3 in a deciding frame [13][17]. The 2018 champion Aaron Canavan defeated Andrew Norman 4–1 [13].

### Quarter-finals
In the quarter-finals, Dominic Dale defeated Joe Perry 4–1 in a match between the two recent professional retirees [13][8]. Aaron Canavan eliminated Wayne Townsend 4–2 to advance to the semi-finals [13][18].

Alfie Burden recovered from a 0–3 deficit against Tony Drago to win 4–3. Drago had won the second and third frames on a re-spotted black but was unable to secure the fourth frame required for victory [5][19]. Hassan Kerde became the first Australian to reach the semi-finals of the World Seniors Championship by defeating Charl Jonck 4–2 [13][2].

### Semi-finals
The semi-finals were played on 10 May as best-of-11 frame matches [2][13].

Alfie Burden defeated Hassan Kerde 6–2. Burden established an early 4–0 lead with breaks of 57 and 65. Although Kerde won two frames to narrow the score, Burden secured the victory with a break of 97 in the seventh frame followed by a win in the eighth [7][7].

The second semi-final between Aaron Canavan and Dominic Dale went to a deciding frame. Dale led 5–2 and compiled two century breaks during the match, including a 131 which stood as the highest break of the tournament. However, Canavan won four consecutive frames to win the match 6–5 [7][2][7].

### Final
The final took place on 11 May 2025 between Alfie Burden and Aaron Canavan [2]. It was the second appearance in the final for both players; Canavan had won the title in 2018, while Burden was the runner-up in 2023 [7][7]. The match was officiated by Michaela Tabb and Proletina Velichkova. Tabb refereed the first five frames before retiring from professional snooker refereeing; she was recognized by the crowd and commentators for her 24-year career [2][9][20].

Canavan won the opening frame, but Burden took the next four to lead 4–1 at the first interval [5][21]. Burden extended his lead to 7–2, placing him one frame away from victory. Canavan mounted a brief recovery, winning two frames to reduce the deficit to 7–4. In the 12th frame, Burden made a break of 76 to secure an 8–4 victory [2][5][21].

Following the match, Burden described the win as a "massive journey" and dedicated the victory to his supporters [5].

# Skiddaw Group SSSI

**Skiddaw Group SSSI** is a Site of Special Scientific Interest (SSSI) located in the Lake District High Fells in Cumbria, England. Designated in 1995, the site covers an area of approximately 10,256 hectares (39.60 square miles), making it one of the largest designated sites in the county [1][2][3]. The SSSI is notified for both its geological and biological importance, encompassing the Skiddaw massif and the Caldbeck Fells.

The site contains the oldest rocks in the Lake District, the Ordovician Skiddaw Group, and is a key location for the study of stratigraphy, igneous petrology, and mineralogy [4][5]. Biologically, the area supports extensive upland habitats including blanket bog, heathland, and montane grassland, which provide breeding grounds for a diverse assemblage of upland birds and invertebrates [2][6]. The site includes Skiddaw Forest, a large area of upland terrain that was the subject of a major acquisition and restoration project by the Cumbria Wildlife Trust in 2024 [7][8].

## Geography and Topography

The Skiddaw Group SSSI is situated in the northern part of the Lake District National Park. The site forms an irregular circle centred near Great Calva and includes several major summits exceeding 610 metres (2,000 ft) in elevation [2]. Prominent peaks within the SSSI include Skiddaw (931 m), Blencathra, Skiddaw Little Man, Lonscale Fell, Carl Side, and Long Side in the south, and High Pike, Carrock Fell, Knott, Great Calva, and Bowscale Fell in the north and east [2][9].

The topography is characterized by smooth-sided mountains typical of the Skiddaw Slate geology, contrasting with the more rugged volcanic fells found further south in the Lake District [4]. The high ground serves as a major watershed, dividing water flow between three operational catchments: the River Caldew flowing north towards Carlisle; the River Ellen flowing west; and the River Derwent flowing towards the west coast at Workington and Maryport [2].

The site contains two glacial tarns: Bowscale Tarn and Scales Tarn [10]. These water bodies and the associated headwaters of streams such as Dash Beck, Glenderaterra Beck, and the River Caldew are integral to the site's hydrological and ecological systems [2].

## Geology

The geological significance of the Skiddaw Group SSSI is a primary reason for its designation. The site provides the type locality for the Skiddaw Group of sedimentary rocks and contains the Caldbeck Fells, an area renowned for its mineral diversity and mining history [5][11].

### Skiddaw Group
The dominant rock formation is the Skiddaw Group, consisting of metamorphosed mudstones, siltstones, and wacke-type sandstones of marine origin [4][5]. These rocks are the oldest in the Lake District, dating to the Ordovician period (Tremadoc to Llanvirn epochs, approximately 485–460 million years ago) [5][12]. The sedimentary sequence is estimated to be over 5,000 metres thick, although the base is not exposed [5].

The stratigraphy includes several formations, such as the Bitter Beck, Watch Hill, Hope Beck, Loweswater, and Kirk Stile Formations [5]. The rocks exhibit complex polyphase folding resulting from the Caledonian Orogeny, with classic exposures of fold structures visible in areas such as Mosedale [11].

### Igneous and Metamorphic Features
The SSSI contains significant igneous features, most notably the Skiddaw Granite and the Carrock Fell Complex. The Skiddaw Granite is a Devonian pluton (intruded approximately 390 million years ago) that lies beneath the Skiddaw massif [11]. Although the granite itself has limited surface exposure (e.g., in the valley of the River Caldew and Sinen Gill), its thermal metamorphism has created a distinct aureole in the surrounding sedimentary rocks [11]. This aureole displays a progression of metamorphic zones, from outer andalusite slates to inner cordierite-rich hornfels [11].

### Mineralogy
The Caldbeck Fells, located in the northern sector of the SSSI, are considered one of the most mineralogically diverse sites in the United Kingdom [13]. The area is crisscrossed by mineral veins containing ores of copper, lead, zinc, iron, and tungsten [13]. Over 175 valid mineral species have been identified in the area, with some sources suggesting the count is significantly higher [13][14].

Notable locations include Roughton Gill, Red Gill, and Dry Gill [2][13]. The site is the type locality for several minerals and has produced world-class specimens of pyromorphite, hemimorphite, and plumbogummite [15]. Due to the scientific importance and finite nature of these mineral resources, collecting is strictly regulated, and the area is monitored to prevent unauthorized extraction [13][16].

## Ecology and Habitats

The Skiddaw Group SSSI is a component of the Lake District High Fells Special Area of Conservation (SAC), recognized for its extensive upland habitats [2][17].

### Flora
The vegetation is dominated by upland heath and blanket bog communities. Key habitats identified under the SAC designation include:
*   **Northern Atlantic wet heaths** with cross-leaved heath (*Erica tetralix*) [2].
*   **European dry heaths** dominated by heather (*Calluna vulgaris*) and bilberry (*Vaccinium myrtillus*) [2][17].
*   **Alpine and Boreal heaths** found at higher altitudes [2].
*   **Blanket bogs**, particularly in areas around Bannerdale Beck and Skiddaw Forest [2].
*   **Siliceous scree** communities containing parsley fern (*Cryptogramma crispa*) [17].

A notable botanical feature is **Young Wood** on Bowscale Fell. Situated between 465 and 485 metres above sea level, it is considered the highest Atlantic oakwood in England [18][19]. This fragment of ancient semi-natural woodland consists of sessile oak (*Quercus petraea*) and is a remnant of high-altitude woodland that was once more widespread [18]. Other significant woodland features include stands of juniper (*Juniperus communis*) scrub, particularly in the Swinside Valley [20].

The glacial tarns within the SSSI support aquatic flora such as quillwort (*Isoetes lacustris*), shoreweed (*Littorella uniflora*), and water lobelia (*Lobelia dortmanna*) [2].

### Fauna
The site supports a diverse assemblage of upland birds. It is a key breeding site for the Ring Ouzel (*Turdus torquatus*), a species that has suffered significant national declines [20]. Other breeding birds recorded in the area include the Merlin (*Falco columbarius*), Peregrine Falcon (*Falco peregrinus*), Buzzard (*Buteo buteo*), Raven (*Corvus corax*), Golden Plover (*Pluvialis apricaria*), and Dotterel (*Charadrius morinellus*) [6][20].

Invertebrate populations include the rare montane ground beetle *Leistus montanus*, which has been recorded on the summit of Skiddaw [21][22]. The area also supports the Northern Dart moth (*Xestia alpicola*) and the beetle *Carabus nitens* [6].

## History and Land Use

### Mining History
The Caldbeck Fells have a long history of mineral extraction, dating back potentially to the 14th century or earlier [23]. The most significant period of mining began in the 16th century with the arrival of German miners from Austria and Bavaria, brought to the area by the Company of Mines Royal [23]. Operations at Caldbeck were among the first large-scale copper mining and smelting enterprises in the British Isles [23].

Mining continued intermittently through the centuries, exploiting lead, copper, and barytes. The Roughton Gill mine was one of the largest operations, producing significant quantities of lead and copper ores before closing in the late 19th century [15]. The spoil heaps and adits remaining from these activities are now protected as part of the SSSI for their lichen communities and industrial archaeology, as well as their mineralogical value [13].

### Agriculture and Forestry
Much of the land within the SSSI is common land used for sheep grazing, which has historically influenced the vegetation composition, particularly the extent of heath and woodland [20]. Overgrazing has been identified as a pressure on the condition of upland habitats [2].

Commercial forestry has occurred on the periphery of the SSSI, such as at Dodd Wood. Management plans in the 21st century have focused on removing non-native conifer regeneration from the SSSI and restoring transitional habitats [24].

## Conservation and Management

The Skiddaw Group SSSI is managed to maintain and enhance its geological and biological features. It falls under the regulatory purview of Natural England.

### Condition and Restoration
Assessments by Natural England have classified various units of the SSSI. While some areas, such as invertebrate habitats, have been assessed as "favourable," other habitats like blanket bog and dwarf shrub heath have historically been classified as "unfavourable – recovering" due to pressures such as grazing [2].

Restoration projects have included fencing initiatives on Mungrisdale Common and Caldbeck Common to exclude livestock and allow the regeneration of heather, bilberry, and scrub habitats [18][25].

### Skiddaw Forest Acquisition
In 2024, the Cumbria Wildlife Trust completed the purchase of the Skiddaw Forest, a 1,200-hectare (3,000-acre) area including the summit of Skiddaw [7][26]. The acquisition was funded by a public appeal and a partnership with Aviva [7]. The Trust announced a 100-year vision to restore the area, which includes the re-establishment of temperate rainforest (Atlantic oakwood) on lower slopes, the restoration of 992 acres of peatbog, and the enhancement of montane scrub and wildflower grasslands [7][8]. This project aims to create England's highest nature reserve [8].

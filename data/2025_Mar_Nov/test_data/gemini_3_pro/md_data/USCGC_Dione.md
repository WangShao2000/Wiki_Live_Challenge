# USCGC Dione (WPC-107)

**USCGC *Dione* (WPC-107)** was a *Thetis*-class patrol boat operated by the United States Coast Guard from 1934 to 1963. Built by the Manitowoc Shipbuilding Corporation in Wisconsin, she was originally designed to enforce Prohibition laws against rum-runners. However, due to the repeal of Prohibition prior to her commissioning, her early career focused on search and rescue operations. During World War II, *Dione* served in the Eastern Sea Frontier, conducting convoy escort duties and anti-submarine patrols off the East Coast of the United States. She is noted for rescuing survivors from several merchant ships torpedoed by German U-boats in the "Torpedo Alley" off North Carolina and for escorting the surrendered German submarine *U-1228* to port in 1945. Following the war, she became the first Coast Guard cutter stationed in Freeport, Texas, where she served until her decommissioning in 1963.

## Design and construction

*Dione* was a 165-foot (50 m) "B" class cutter, also known as the *Thetis* class or *Argo* class [1][2]. The class was designed as a successor to the 125-foot *Active*-class patrol boats, specifically intended to trail large "mother ships" that dispensed alcohol to smaller smugglers during Prohibition [1][3]. To fulfill this mission, the design prioritized sea-keeping qualities, crew accommodation, and range [1].

The cutter was built by the Manitowoc Shipbuilding Corporation in Manitowoc, Wisconsin [3][4]. Her keel was laid on November 10, 1933 [4]. *Dione* was launched on June 30, 1934, in a triple ceremony alongside her sister ships *Electra* and *Pandora* [4]. She was commissioned into service on October 5, 1934 [3][4]. The total cost of construction was approximately $258,000 [1][4]. She was named after Dione, a Titaness from Greek mythology and the mother of Aphrodite [3].

### Specifications
*Dione* had an overall length of 165 feet (50 m), a beam of 25 feet 3 inches (7.70 m), and a draft that varied from 7 feet 8 inches (2.34 m) in 1933 to 10 feet (3.0 m) by 1945 due to added wartime equipment [1][3]. Her displacement was approximately 337 tons, increasing to 350 tons by the end of World War II [1][3].

Propulsion was provided by two Winton Model 158 6-cylinder diesel engines, generating a total of 1,340 brake horsepower driving twin three-bladed propellers [1][3]. This power plant allowed for a maximum speed of 16 knots [1][3]. The vessel had a cruising range of 3,000 nautical miles at 11 knots and an economic range of 6,417 nautical miles at 6 knots [1].

### Armament and electronics
Originally armed for law enforcement, *Dione* carried one 3-inch/23 caliber gun and one 1-pounder gun upon commissioning in 1934 [1][3]. During World War II, her armament was significantly upgraded for anti-submarine warfare. By 1945, she was equipped with two 3-inch/50 caliber dual-purpose guns, two 20 mm Oerlikon anti-aircraft guns, two depth charge tracks, two Y-gun depth charge projectors, and two Mousetrap anti-submarine rocket launchers [1][2][5].

The ship initially lacked advanced electronics but was later fitted with SF-1 radar and QCN-1 sonar to support her wartime patrol and escort missions [1][2][4].

## Service history

### Pre-war service (1934–1941)
Although designed to combat rum-running, Prohibition had been repealed by the time *Dione* entered service. Consequently, she was assigned to the Coast Guard station in Norfolk, Virginia, where she primarily conducted law enforcement patrols and search and rescue (SAR) operations [3][4].

### World War II (1941–1945)
Following the outbreak of World War II, *Dione* was assigned to the Eastern Sea Frontier (EASTSEAFRON) and the Fifth Naval District [3][4]. She operated out of Norfolk, patrolling the waters off North Carolina and Virginia [3][4]. This area became known as "Torpedo Alley" in early 1942 due to the high concentration of German U-boat attacks on merchant shipping [4].

#### Rescue operations
*Dione* was heavily involved in responding to distress calls during the "Second Happy Time," a period of intense submarine warfare off the American coast in early 1942.
*   **Australia:** On March 15, 1942, *Dione* responded to a distress signal from the oil tanker *Australia*, which had been torpedoed by *U-332*. The cutter arrived approximately an hour later and made sonar contact with the submarine but was unable to sink it despite launching depth charges [4][6].
*   **Acme and Kassandra Louloudis:** On March 17–18, 1942, *Dione* rescued the crew of the tanker *Acme*, which had been torpedoed by *U-124* near the Diamond Shoals Light Buoy. During the same patrol, she also rescued survivors from the Greek freighter *Kassandra Louloudis*, another victim of *U-124* [4][6][7].
*   **Naeco:** On March 23, 1942, the cutter responded to the torpedoing of the tanker *Naeco* by *U-124*, rescuing at least ten survivors from a lifeboat and two from the water [4][6].
*   **Byron D. Benson:** On April 5, 1942, the tanker *Byron D. Benson* was torpedoed by *U-552*. *Dione* responded to the attack and retrieved one survivor from the water [3][4].

#### Combat operations
In addition to rescue duties, *Dione* served as a convoy escort and actively hunted submarines. On April 19, 1942, while *Dione* was escorting a convoy, a torpedo narrowly missed the cutter by approximately 20 yards before striking the tanker *Axtell J. Byles* [4][6][8]. *Dione* responded with depth charge attacks [6].

Throughout the spring and summer of 1942, *Dione* reported multiple contacts and engaged in depth charge attacks on March 20, April 18, June 24, June 25, and June 27 [3]. While oil slicks were observed after some of these attacks, no German submarines were confirmed sunk by *Dione* [3][4].

#### Surrender of U-1228
In May 1945, following the surrender of Germany, *Dione* was transferred to the First Naval District to assist in processing surrendered U-boats. On May 17, 1945, alongside the destroyer escort USS *Neal A. Scott*, *Dione* escorted the German submarine *U-1228* into Portsmouth, New Hampshire [4][6][9]. *U-1228* had surrendered at sea in accordance with orders from the German high command [6][9].

### Post-war service (1945–1963)
After the war, *Dione* returned to the Fifth District for air-sea rescue duties before being decommissioned on July 23, 1947, due to personnel shortages [3][4]. She remained in reserve until she was recommissioned on February 4, 1951 [4].

In 1952, *Dione* was transferred to Freeport, Texas, becoming the first Coast Guard cutter stationed in that city [3][4][6]. Her primary mission in the Gulf of Mexico was search and rescue. Notable post-war activities included towing the disabled cutter USCGC *Iris* to New Orleans in January 1957 and responding to various distress calls from merchant and fishing vessels [3][6].

## Decommissioning and commercial career
*Dione* was decommissioned for the final time on February 8, 1963 [3][4]. She was sold on February 24, 1964, to the Palmer Decker Boat Company [3][4]. Following her military service, she operated as a commercial merchant and supply ship in the Gulf Coast region. Over the subsequent decades, the vessel bore several names, including *Big Trouble*, *Delta I*, and *Al Rashid* [6]. Her first commercial owner went bankrupt in 1967, leading to the ship's seizure and auction by a U.S. Marshal [4][6].

# Joy Laking

**Joy Snihur Wyatt Laking** (born 1950) is a Canadian visual artist and author based in Portapique, Nova Scotia. She is primarily known for her realistic watercolour, oil, and acrylic paintings depicting the landscapes and daily life of Nova Scotia, particularly the Bay of Fundy region. In addition to her visual art, Laking has authored children's books and written a play regarding domestic abuse. She is a recipient of the Queen Elizabeth II Diamond Jubilee Medal and the Halifax Progress Club Woman of Excellence Award.

## Early life and education
Joy Snihur Wyatt Laking was born in 1950 in Owen Sound, Ontario [1][2]. She was raised in an artistic family; her mother was a professional painter, and her father built marionettes and toy trains [1][3]. Laking began painting at a young age, often accompanying her mother on painting trips to Georgian Bay [3].

Laking attended the University of Guelph, where she graduated with a Bachelor of Arts in Fine Art in 1972 [2][4]. Following her graduation, she married and moved to Nova Scotia in 1972, settling in the Bass River area along the Cobequid Bay [4][5][6].

## Career
Laking has been a professional painter since 1972 [5]. While she initially worked in watercolour and pen and ink for the first decade of her career, she later expanded her practice to include oil painting, acrylics, and oil sticks [4].

In the late 1980s, Laking and her husband established the **Joy Laking Gallery** at their home in Portapique to display her work [1][3]. The gallery continues to operate and welcomes visitors by appointment or chance [7][8].

### Exhibitions
Laking has exhibited her work in solo and group shows regionally and nationally. Her exhibition history includes:
*   **1982:** First solo exhibition, *Flowers of Nova Scotia*, at the Mount Saint Vincent University Art Gallery [3][9].
*   **1984 & 1990:** Solo exhibitions at the Royal Botanical Gardens in Burlington, Ontario [1][3][5].
*   **1985:** Solo exhibition at the Owens Art Gallery at Mount Allison University [3].
*   **1989–1990:** A solo exhibition at the Art Gallery of Nova Scotia, which subsequently toured the region for a year [1][6].
*   **2014:** Her work was included in the exhibition *Capture 2014: Nova Scotian Realism*, curated by Tom Smart and Peter Dykhuis at the Dalhousie Art Gallery [4][10].

### Residencies and travel
Laking frequently travels to paint en plein air. She has painted in various international locations, including Italy, France, Portugal, India, Ghana, and Mauritius [4][11]. In 2016, she was the artist-in-residence at the Port Bickerton Lighthouse in Nova Scotia [2].

## Style and technique
Laking's artistic style is described as representational realism [3][10]. She is self-taught in watercolour techniques, having studied acrylics and abstract art during her university education [6].

A distinguishing feature of her watercolour work is her "white on white" technique, particularly in her depictions of winter scenes and flowers. She does not use white paint; instead, she utilizes the white of the paper and negative space to create the effect of snow or light, a technique she has employed since her first solo exhibition in 1982 [6][9]. Her subject matter frequently includes the tides of the Bay of Fundy, coastal landscapes, floral studies, and interiors featuring porches and chairs [1][4].

## Writings and other works
Beyond visual arts, Laking has published books and written for the stage.

### Books
*   ***Colours in Winter*** (2019): A children's picture book written and illustrated by Laking. The story follows a child who wishes for colour in a white winter landscape, only to discover the natural colours already present [12][13].
*   ***The Painted Province: Nova Scotia Through an Artist's Eyes*** (2020): A collection of over 200 paintings and essays documenting various regions of Nova Scotia. The book includes GPS coordinates for the locations depicted, encouraging readers to visit the sites where the paintings were created [5][14][15].

### Theatre
Laking wrote the play ***Invisible Prisons***, which addresses the subject of domestic abuse. The work was developed from interviews Laking conducted with 35 survivors of abuse, which she synthesized into a series of monologues [3][16]. The play premiered on March 8, 2013, at the Marigold Cultural Centre in Truro, Nova Scotia, produced by Lenore Zann and directed by Bryden MacDonald [3][4]. The script is made available for use by schools and community groups for educational purposes [17][18].

## Personal life
Laking lives in Portapique, Nova Scotia [2]. She has three children, born between 1980 and 1985 [6].

In April 2020, the mass shooting known as the 2020 Nova Scotia attacks began in her community of Portapique. Laking was at home during the event and lost three friends and neighbours. She has spoken publicly about the difficulty of finding beauty and returning to painting in the immediate aftermath of the tragedy [3].

## Awards and recognition
Laking has received several awards acknowledging her contributions to the arts and her community:
*   **2009:** Halifax Progress Club Woman of Excellence Award [2].
*   **2012:** Queen Elizabeth II Diamond Jubilee Medal [4][5].
*   **2022:** Cobequid Arts Council Lifetime Achievement Award [19].
*   **Membership:** She is an elected member of the Society of Canadian Artists (SCA) and a founding member of Professional Living Artists of Nova Scotia (PLANS) [2][4].

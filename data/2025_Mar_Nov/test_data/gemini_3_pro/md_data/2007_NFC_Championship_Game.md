# 2007 NFC Championship Game

The **2007 NFC Championship Game** was a National Football League (NFL) game played on January 20, 2008, to determine the National Football Conference (NFC) champion for the 2007 season. The visiting New York Giants defeated the Green Bay Packers in overtime by a score of 23–20. Played at Lambeau Field in Green Bay, Wisconsin, the game is notable for being one of the coldest in NFL history, with an air temperature of −1 °F (−18 °C) and a wind chill of −23 °F (−31 °C) at kickoff [1][2].

The match featured a contest between the second-seeded Packers, led by quarterback Brett Favre, and the fifth-seeded wild card Giants, led by quarterback Eli Manning. It was the first postseason meeting between the two franchises since the 1962 NFL Championship Game [3]. The game required overtime after Giants kicker Lawrence Tynes missed two field goal attempts in the fourth quarter that would have won the game in regulation. In the extra period, New York cornerback Corey Webster intercepted a pass from Favre, setting up Tynes for a successful 47-yard game-winning field goal [4][5].

The victory advanced the Giants to Super Bowl XLII, where they defeated the previously undefeated New England Patriots [6]. The game marked Brett Favre's final appearance as a member of the Green Bay Packers, as his last pass attempt for the team resulted in the decisive interception [5]. Due to the extreme weather conditions, the game is often referred to by the nickname **"Ice Bowl II,"** in reference to the 1967 NFL Championship Game played at the same venue [7][8].

## Background

### New York Giants
The New York Giants finished the 2007 regular season with a 10–6 record, placing second in the NFC East behind the Dallas Cowboys [6]. They entered the playoffs as the number five seed (a wild card berth). Under head coach Tom Coughlin, the team developed a reputation as "Road Warriors" due to their success away from Giants Stadium [6][9].

To reach the NFC Championship, the Giants first defeated the fourth-seeded Tampa Bay Buccaneers 24–14 in the Wild Card Round. In the Divisional Round, they upset the top-seeded Dallas Cowboys 21–17, a team that had defeated New York twice during the regular season [6][10]. By reaching the conference title game, the Giants became the third team in NFL history to win three road playoff games in a single postseason [6].

### Green Bay Packers
The Green Bay Packers finished the regular season with a 13–3 record, winning the NFC North division and securing the number two seed in the NFC playoffs [11]. The season was a resurgence for the franchise and quarterback Brett Favre, who led the team to its first playoff appearance since 2004 [11].

Following a first-round bye, the Packers defeated the Seattle Seahawks 42–20 in the Divisional Round at Lambeau Field. That game, played in heavy snow, was colloquially termed the "Snow Globe Game" [1]. Running back Ryan Grant set franchise playoff records in that victory with 201 rushing yards and three touchdowns [12].

### Weather Conditions
The game was played in arctic conditions. At kickoff, the temperature was −1 °F (−18 °C) with a wind chill of −23 °F (−31 °C), making it the second-coldest game in the history of Lambeau Field, surpassed only by the 1967 "Ice Bowl" [1][2]. The extreme cold affected game preparations and play; Giants players reported that the football felt like a "rock" and that pre-game warm-ups were cut short due to the freezing temperatures [9]. Images of Giants coach Tom Coughlin's face, red and chapped from the wind, became a lasting visual symbol of the game [8].

## Game Summary

### First Half
The Giants controlled the pace early in the game. On their opening drive, they moved 71 yards in 14 plays, consuming nearly eight minutes of the clock. However, the drive stalled in the red zone, and Lawrence Tynes kicked a 29-yard field goal to give New York a 3–0 lead [13]. The Packers' offense struggled initially, generating negative yardage on their first possession [4].

In the second quarter, Tynes added a 37-yard field goal to extend the Giants' lead to 6–0 [14]. The Packers responded quickly on their ensuing possession. Brett Favre connected with wide receiver Donald Driver on a 90-yard touchdown pass, the longest play in Packers playoff history, to give Green Bay a 7–6 lead [1][3]. Late in the half, Packers kicker Mason Crosby converted a 36-yard field goal, sending the teams into halftime with Green Bay leading 10–6 [14].

### Third Quarter
The second half featured multiple lead changes. The Giants opened the third quarter with a drive culminating in a 1-yard touchdown run by Brandon Jacobs, reclaiming the lead at 13–10 [14]. The Packers answered when Favre threw a 12-yard touchdown pass to tight end Donald Lee, putting Green Bay ahead 17–13 [1].

Toward the end of the third quarter, the Giants mounted another scoring drive. Running back Ahmad Bradshaw scored on a 4-yard touchdown run, giving New York a 20–17 advantage entering the fourth quarter [14].

### Fourth Quarter
Early in the fourth quarter, Mason Crosby kicked a 37-yard field goal to tie the game at 20–20 [14]. The remainder of the quarter was defined by missed opportunities for the Giants.

With approximately 6:49 remaining in regulation, Lawrence Tynes attempted a 43-yard field goal that would have given New York the lead, but the kick sailed wide left [4][9]. The Giants defense forced a punt, giving the offense the ball back with time for a final drive. Quarterback Eli Manning led the team into field goal range. With four seconds remaining in regulation, Tynes attempted a 36-yard game-winning field goal. The snap from center was high, disrupting the timing; Tynes hooked the kick to the left as time expired, forcing the game into overtime [4][9].

### Overtime
The Packers won the overtime coin toss and elected to receive, a scenario that, under the sudden-death rules of the time, allowed them to win with any score on the opening possession [15].

On the second play of overtime, Brett Favre attempted a pass to Donald Driver. Giants cornerback Corey Webster intercepted the ball and returned it 9 yards to the Green Bay 34-yard line [5]. This interception proved to be Favre's final pass attempt as a member of the Green Bay Packers [1].

Following the turnover, the Giants ran three plays to improve their field position. On fourth down, Lawrence Tynes was sent out for a third attempt at a game-winning kick, this time from 47 yards—longer than his two previous misses. Tynes successfully converted the field goal, giving the Giants a 23–20 victory [4][16].

## Aftermath and Significance

### Super Bowl XLII
The victory secured the New York Giants' place in Super Bowl XLII, where they faced the AFC champion New England Patriots. The Patriots entered the Super Bowl with an unprecedented 18–0 record, attempting to complete the first perfect season since the 1972 Miami Dolphins [6]. The Giants defeated the Patriots 17–14 in what is widely considered one of the greatest upsets in sports history [10].

### Brett Favre's Departure
The 2007 NFC Championship Game was the final game of Brett Favre's 16-season tenure with the Green Bay Packers. Following the season, Favre announced his retirement in March 2008, only to rescind it later that summer. This led to a trade to the New York Jets, paving the way for Aaron Rodgers to become the Packers' starting quarterback [11][18]. Favre later cited the cold weather as a factor in his performance, noting that the interception in overtime occurred because he did not throw the ball far enough to the outside [5].

### Historical Context
The game is remembered for its grueling weather conditions and the visual of Tom Coughlin's frozen face [8]. It was the second NFC Championship Game to be decided in overtime, the first being the Atlanta Falcons' victory over the Minnesota Vikings in the 1998 season [4]. Lawrence Tynes' game-winning kick made him the only player in NFL history to kick two overtime game-winning field goals in the playoffs, a feat he repeated in the 2011 NFC Championship Game [16].

# Everyone Hates Elon

**Everyone Hates Elon** is a British political campaign group and activist collective established in early 2025. The group was formed to protest the political influence and behavior of business magnate Elon Musk, specifically criticizing his involvement in the United States Department of Government Efficiency (DOGE), his commentary on British politics, and his management of the social media platform X (formerly Twitter) [1][2].

The collective employs guerrilla marketing, subvertising, and satirical public stunts to draw attention to wealth inequality and the influence of billionaires [2][3]. While the group's primary focus is Elon Musk, it has also targeted other high-net-worth individuals, including Jeff Bezos and Donald Trump [4][5]. Notable campaigns include the "Swasticar" poster series, the "London vs Musk" performance art event, and the "People vs Elon" fundraising initiative [3][6][7].

## History and formation
Everyone Hates Elon was formed in the United Kingdom in early 2025 [2]. The collective originated from a "ranty group chat" among friends concerned about the intersection of billionaire wealth and far-right politics [2]. The group's members have chosen to remain anonymous, describing themselves as "just a handful of people" [2].

The group's formation was catalyzed by Musk's increasing involvement in political affairs, including his support for the British far-right activist Tommy Robinson and his appointment to the Department of Government Efficiency under the second administration of Donald Trump [2][3]. The group also cited a gesture made by Musk at Trump's second inauguration, which they and other critics interpreted as a Nazi salute, as a motivating factor for their campaign [1][8].

A spokesperson for the group stated that their objective is to "piss off Elon Musk one small action at a time" and to challenge the "unchecked power of billionaires in politics and society" [2][3].

## Campaigns and activities

### "Swasticar" campaign
In early 2025, the group launched a guerrilla marketing campaign across London featuring satirical advertisements that criticized Tesla, Inc. and Musk's political alignments [3]. The campaign introduced the portmanteau "Swasticar" to describe Tesla vehicles, linking the brand to fascist imagery [6][8].

Posters appeared in public spaces, including bus stops and the London Underground, featuring a photograph of Musk with his arm raised. One widely circulated poster bore the slogan "Goes from 0 to 1939 in 3 seconds," while others included phrases such as "Now with White Power Steering" [3][9][10]. The group also distributed stickers reading "Don't Buy a Swasticar," which activists placed on Tesla vehicles throughout London [11].

Transport for London (TfL) confirmed that the posters placed on its network were unauthorized and stated they would be "expeditiously removed" [2]. The campaign received significant attention on social media, with a TikTok video of the poster installation accumulating millions of views [8]. Author Stephen King shared an image of the campaign on social media, which further amplified its reach [3][10].

### London vs Musk
On April 10, 2025, the group organized a participatory protest event titled "London vs Musk" in South London [12][13]. The event was billed as a "public art project" and a form of "catharsis" for those frustrated by wealth inequality [14].

During the event, participants were invited to destroy a 2014 Tesla Model S using sledgehammers and baseball bats [12]. The vehicle had been anonymously donated for the purpose of the stunt and was destined for scrap [6][12]. The group stated that the event was conducted under controlled safety measures and emphasized that they did not condone vandalism of vehicles outside of this specific art installation [6][13].

The remnants of the destroyed vehicle were auctioned, with the proceeds donated to food bank charities [12][13]. A spokesperson for the group remarked that the juxtaposition of record billionaire wealth and the rising use of food banks was "obviously connected" [6].

### People vs Elon
The "People vs Elon" initiative is a fundraising campaign launched by the group to support causes they believe Musk opposes [10][15]. The campaign encourages supporters to pledge a small amount of money (e.g., 1p) for every tweet Musk posts on the X platform [7].

The funds raised are donated to charities supporting transgender rights, refugees, and anti-racism education [7][16]. By December 2, 2025, the group announced that the campaign had raised £100,000 [7]. The organizers described the initiative as a way to "turn every single tweet Elon Musk sends into a weapon against the far-right" [10].

### Other protests
While focused on Musk, the group has expanded its activities to target other billionaires and political figures.

*   **Jeff Bezos:** In June 2025, Everyone Hates Elon collaborated with Greenpeace Italy to protest the wedding of Jeff Bezos in Venice [4]. Activists unfurled a 20-meter banner in St Mark's Square reading, "If you can rent Venice for your wedding, you can pay more tax" [4][17]. The protest highlighted issues of tax avoidance and climate inequality [4].
*   **Donald Trump and Jeffrey Epstein:** In anticipation of Donald Trump's state visit to the UK in September 2025, the group engaged in several stunts highlighting Trump's past association with convicted sex offender Jeffrey Epstein [2][5]. These actions included placing a satirical memorial plaque at Trump's golf course in Aberdeenshire that read, "In loving memory of Jeffrey Epstein—a terrific guy," and displaying a large photograph of the two men on a banner outside Windsor Castle [5][18][19].
*   **Freddie Mercury Billboard:** The group installed a billboard in Liverpool featuring Freddie Mercury with the caption "Thank God for Immigrants," inspired by Jeremy Deller's pro-migrant artwork [20].

## Reception and criticism
The group's activities have generated polarized responses. The "Swasticar" campaign and the "London vs Musk" event received coverage in international media and were shared widely on social platforms [1][8]. Supporters, including high-profile figures like Stephen King, have praised the satirical nature of the protests [3].

The group has also faced opposition. A counter-campaign titled "We Love Elon and Tesla" was launched, utilizing crowdfunding to purchase pro-Musk billboards and merchandise [16]. This group argues that Musk is a "problem-solver" and that his companies, such as SpaceX and Tesla, provide societal value that outweighs political criticisms [16].

In the United States, the Trump administration has condemned anti-Tesla protests. Following incidents of vandalism against Tesla vehicles in the US by a separate group known as "Tesla Takedown," US Attorney General Pam Bondi threatened to prosecute those targeting the company [21][22]. Everyone Hates Elon has distinguished itself from violent groups, stating they oppose violence and that their stunts are controlled art installations [6][13].

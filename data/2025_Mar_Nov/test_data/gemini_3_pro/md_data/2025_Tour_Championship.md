# 2025 Tour Championship

The **2025 Tour Championship** was a golf tournament played from August 21 to 24, 2025, at East Lake Golf Club in Atlanta, Georgia [1][2]. It was the final event of the 2025 PGA Tour season and the third and final tournament of the FedEx Cup Playoffs [3][4].

The 2025 edition marked a significant structural change for the event, as the PGA Tour eliminated the "starting strokes" format used between 2019 and 2024. Instead of a staggered start based on FedEx Cup standings, the field of 30 players began the tournament at even par, with the tournament played as a standard 72-hole stroke-play event [5][6]. The player with the lowest aggregate score over four rounds was crowned both the Tour Championship winner and the FedEx Cup champion [7].

Tommy Fleetwood won the tournament with a score of 262 (18-under-par), defeating runners-up Patrick Cantlay and Russell Henley by three strokes [8][9]. The victory marked Fleetwood's first career win on the PGA Tour in his 164th start [8][9]. By winning the event, Fleetwood claimed the FedEx Cup and its $10 million first-place prize [10].

## Format and Rules

### Elimination of Starting Strokes
In May 2025, the PGA Tour announced a major overhaul of the Tour Championship format, effective immediately for the 2025 season [5][6]. The "starting strokes" system, which had been in place since 2019, was eliminated. Under the previous system, the FedEx Cup points leader began the tournament at 10-under-par, with other players starting at staggered scores down to even par based on their standings [11][12].

For the 2025 event, all 30 qualified players started at even par, restoring the tournament to a traditional 72-hole stroke-play competition [13]. The change was part of a "Fan Forward" initiative, with PGA Tour Commissioner Jay Monahan stating that the goal was to provide a "straightforward and engaging format" where the lowest score over four days determined the winner [6][12].

### Qualification and Field
The field was limited to the top 30 players in the FedEx Cup standings following the BMW Championship, the second leg of the playoffs [4]. Despite the format change, the PGA Tour maintained the field size at 30 players [14]. Scottie Scheffler entered the week as the number one seed in the FedEx Cup standings for the fourth consecutive year [15][16].

### Prize Money
The tournament featured a total purse of $40 million [10]. The winner received $10 million, a figure that counted as official prize money rather than a bonus, distinguishing it from previous years where the payout was structured differently due to the starting strokes format [17]. The runner-up received $5 million (split in the event of a tie) [18].

## Venue

The tournament was held at East Lake Golf Club, which has hosted the Tour Championship permanently since 2005 [2]. Following the 2024 tournament, the course underwent a comprehensive restoration led by architect Andrew Green [19][20].

The restoration aimed to return the course to the aesthetic and strategic intent of its original architect, Donald Ross, utilizing aerial photography from 1949 as a guide [20][21]. Significant changes included:
*   **Topography and Routing:** Every tee, green, fairway, and bunker was rebuilt to reflect the natural topography [21].
*   **Turf Change:** Fairways were converted from Meyer Zoysia to Zorro Zoysia to encourage firmer playing conditions and more ground movement [20][21].
*   **Greens:** Putting surfaces were converted from Mini Verde Bermudagrass to TifEagle Bermuda and reshaped to match Ross's original designs [21][22].
*   **Bunkering:** Bunkers were restructured to resemble those found at Inverness Club, another Ross design [19].

The course played as a par-71 at 7,455 yards for the championship [20].

## Tournament Summary

### Round 1
*Thursday, August 21*

Russell Henley opened the tournament with a 9-under-par 61, taking a two-stroke lead over Scottie Scheffler [23]. Henley's round included seven birdies and an eagle at the par-5 6th hole, with no bogeys [23]. He gained nearly six strokes on the field and made over 200 feet of putts, a personal best for his PGA Tour career [23]. Tommy Fleetwood, Collin Morikawa, Justin Thomas, and Patrick Cantlay finished the day three strokes back [23].

### Round 2
*Friday, August 22*

Due to forecasted thunderstorms, tee times were moved up, with play beginning at 8:00 a.m. [24]. Tommy Fleetwood shot a 7-under-par 63 to move to 13-under for the tournament, tying Russell Henley for the 36-hole lead [25][26]. Fleetwood recorded six birdies on the back nine, including finishes on the 17th and 18th holes [25]. Henley retained a share of the lead, while other contenders, including Cameron Young, remained in pursuit [25].

### Round 3
*Saturday, August 23*

Moving day saw Patrick Cantlay surge up the leaderboard. Cantlay carded a 64 for the second consecutive day to tie Fleetwood for the lead entering the final round [10]. Fleetwood posted a 67 to maintain his position at the top [10].

### Round 4
*Sunday, August 24*

Entering the final round tied for the lead, Tommy Fleetwood and Patrick Cantlay played in the final pairing. Cantlay struggled early, recording a bogey on the first hole and a double-bogey on the second, which allowed Fleetwood to establish an early advantage [10].

Fleetwood shot a final-round 68 (3-under-par) to finish at 18-under-par (262) [8]. He secured the victory by three strokes over Cantlay and Henley [27]. The win was Fleetwood's first on the PGA Tour after 163 previous starts, which included six runner-up finishes [8][28]. By winning the tournament, he also claimed the FedEx Cup title [28].

## Reception of Format Changes

The elimination of the starting strokes format drew mixed reactions from the players. Scottie Scheffler, who entered the week as the FedEx Cup points leader, expressed concern that the new format might undervalue season-long performance. He noted that the "drastic difference" in points and the reset to even par could create inconsistencies in rewarding the best seasons [29]. Conversely, Justin Thomas supported the change, suggesting it created a more competitive environment where more players had a realistic chance to win on Sunday [29].

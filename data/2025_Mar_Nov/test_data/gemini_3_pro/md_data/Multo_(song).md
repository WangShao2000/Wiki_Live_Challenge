# Multo (song)

"**Multo**" (transl. "Ghost") is a song recorded by the Filipino pop band Cup of Joe for their debut studio album, *Silakbo* (2025). It was written by the band's co-lead vocalist Raphaell Ridao and his brother Redentor Immanuel Ridao, with production handled by Shadiel Chan and Jovel Rivera. Viva Records released the song as the album's lead single on September 14, 2024. A synth-pop and pop-rock ballad, "Multo" lyrically utilizes the metaphor of a ghost to describe the lingering presence of a past lover and the inescapable nature of grief.

The song achieved significant commercial success both domestically and internationally. "Multo" became the first song by a Filipino act to enter the *Billboard* Global 200 chart, where it peaked at number 80. In the Philippines, the single topped the *Billboard* Philippines Hot 100 for 27 consecutive weeks, establishing the record for the longest-running number-one song in the chart's history. It was recognized as the most-streamed local song of 2025 by Spotify Philippines and the top OPM song of the year by Apple Music Philippines.

An accompanying music video, directed by Kris Cazin, was released in March 2025. The video features actors Elijah Canlas and Miles Ocampo in a narrative depicting a relationship haunted by memories. The song received critical praise for its emotional depth and was cited as a turning point in the band's artistic maturity. It won Song of the Year at the 2025 Filipino Music Awards.

## Background and release

Cup of Joe, a band based in Baguio, Philippines, formed in 2018 and gained initial popularity with songs such as "Tingin" and "Estranghero" [1][2]. During the COVID-19 pandemic lockdowns in 2020, lead vocalist Raphaell Ridao and his brother, Redentor Immanuel Ridao, wrote "Multo" while confined in their home [3]. According to Raphaell, the isolation of the pandemic provided time for reflection, and the songwriting process served as an outlet for emotions regarding loss and the feeling of being "haunted" by unexplainable thoughts [3].

The band announced the single's release only three hours prior to its launch [4]. "Multo" was officially released on digital platforms on September 14, 2024, under Viva Records [4][5]. It served as the lead single for the band's debut studio album, *Silakbo* (transl. "Outburst"), which was subsequently released on January 17, 2025 [6][7]. The track is situated as the seventh song on the album, which is conceptually structured around the five stages of grief; "Multo" represents the stage of depression [6][8].

## Composition and lyrics

"Multo" is a synth-pop and pop-rock ballad with a duration of three minutes and fifty-seven seconds [4][9][10]. The song was produced by Shadiel Chan and Jovel Rivera, with arrangements by the Ridao brothers, Chan, and the band [4][11].

Lyrically, the song explores themes of grief, heartbreak, and emotional haunting [4][12]. The title "Multo" refers to a "ghost" or figure that follows a person, symbolizing memories or feelings that persist despite attempts to move on [3]. Raphaell Ridao stated that the song was conceived from the idea of feelings that follow someone regardless of their efforts to push them away [3].

The lyrics depict a protagonist who is unable to escape the memory of a past love, with lines such as "Pasindi na ng ilaw / Minumulto na 'ko ng damdamin ko" (transl. "Turn on the lights / I am being haunted by my feelings") [9]. Critics have noted that while the song addresses romantic loss, the "ghost" imagery has been interpreted by listeners as applying to various forms of loss, including the death of loved ones, faded friendships, or past versions of oneself [9][13].

## Critical reception

Music critics responded favorably to "Multo," often highlighting it as a sign of Cup of Joe's musical evolution. *Rolling Stone Philippines* described the track as a "synth-pop ballad" that revealed an "emotional depth fans hadn't anticipated" from a band previously known for upbeat pop tracks [9]. The review noted that the song marked the band's arrival as "serious pop storytellers" and described the track as a "slow-burning phenomenon" that grew in popularity well after its initial release [9].

The song's production and lyrical content were praised for their maturity. *Billboard Philippines* noted that the track solidified the band's impact on the Philippine soundscape [14]. The song's ability to resonate with a broad audience was attributed to its open-ended interpretability regarding grief and loss [3][13].

## Commercial performance

"Multo" achieved historic chart performance for a Filipino artist. In April 2025, the song debuted at number 181 on the *Billboard* Global 200, making Cup of Joe the first Filipino act to enter the chart [5][15]. The song eventually peaked at number 80 on the Global 200 [2][4][16]. Additionally, it debuted at number 74 on the *Billboard* Global Excl. U.S. chart, marking the highest debut for a Filipino song on that chart and the first to enter the top 100; it later peaked at number 33 [4][5].

In the Philippines, "Multo" was a dominant commercial force throughout 2025. It topped the *Billboard* Philippines Hot 100 for 27 consecutive weeks, setting a record for the longest-running number-one song in the chart's history [4][17][18]. It surpassed the previous record held by Dionela's "Marilag" [2][14]. The song also led the Top Philippine Songs chart for the same duration [18][19].

On streaming platforms, "Multo" was the most-streamed local song of 2025 on Spotify Philippines [20][21]. It reportedly broke records for the most single-day streams for a Filipino song on Spotify, reaching approximately 2.3 million plays in a 24-hour period [16][22]. Apple Music Philippines also ranked "Multo" as the number one song on its "Top OPM Songs of 2025" list [23].

## Music video

The official music video for "Multo" was released on March 6, 2025 [6][11]. It was directed by Kris Cazin and stars actors Elijah Canlas and Miles Ocampo [12][24]. The casting of Canlas and Ocampo, who were a former couple in real life, was noted by media outlets as adding a layer of emotional authenticity to the video's narrative of lost love [24][25].

The video's screenplay, written by the band, depicts a narrative where Ocampo's character experiences hallucinations of her partner, played by Canlas [26]. The visual storytelling alternates between scenes of happy memories shared by the couple in a home and shots of Ocampo alone, implying that Canlas's character is a "ghost" or a memory she is struggling to release [26]. The video was produced by Viva Records and has garnered millions of views on YouTube [11][12].

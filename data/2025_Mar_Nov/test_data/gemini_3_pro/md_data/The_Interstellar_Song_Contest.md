# The Interstellar Song Contest

"**The Interstellar Song Contest**" is the sixth episode of the fifteenth series of the British science fiction television series *Doctor Who*. Written by Juno Dawson and directed by Ben A. Williams, the episode was first broadcast on BBC One and Disney+ on 17 May 2025 [1]. The story features Ncuti Gatwa as the Fifteenth Doctor and Varada Sethu as his companion, Belinda Chandra.

In the episode, the Doctor and Belinda arrive at the Harmony Arena in the year 2925 to witness the 803rd Interstellar Song Contest, a futuristic musical competition [2][3]. The event is interrupted by a hostage situation orchestrated by members of the Hellian race, who seek retribution against the corporation sponsoring the contest for the destruction of their home planet [4][5]. The episode is notable for its pastiche of the Eurovision Song Contest, featuring cameo appearances by real-world presenters Rylan Clark and Graham Norton, as well as the return of the characters Susan Foreman and the Rani [6][7].

"The Interstellar Song Contest" received attention for its production values, original musical numbers composed by Murray Gold, and its tonal shift from camp humor to dark political drama [8][9]. Critical response was mixed to positive; while the visual spectacle and performances were praised, some reviewers criticized the pacing and the depiction of the Doctor's morality during a torture sequence [8][10].

## Plot

The Doctor (Ncuti Gatwa) and his companion Belinda Chandra (Varada Sethu) land the TARDIS on the Harmony Arena, a space station hosting the 803rd Interstellar Song Contest in the year 2925 [2][3]. Intending only to calibrate their equipment, they decide to stay and watch the proceedings after realizing the nature of the event. The contest is hosted by a cryogenically preserved Rylan Clark, who is thawed out annually to present the show [7].

The festivities are disrupted by Kid (Freddie Fox) and Wynn (Iona Anderson), members of the Hellian race. The Hellians are a horned species who have been marginalized and demonized by galactic society, often accused of witchcraft and cannibalism [2][5]. Kid and Wynn hijack the station's systems and deactivate the atmospheric force field, causing the Doctor and approximately 100,000 audience members to be sucked into the vacuum of space [1][11].

While it initially appears that the audience has been killed, the Doctor reveals he has placed them in "mavitic suspension," keeping them alive but incapacitated [12]. Kid threatens to transmit a psychic signal through the broadcast that will kill the three trillion viewers watching across the galaxy [5]. His motivation is revealed to be revenge against the contest's sponsor, the Poppy Honey Corporation. The corporation had previously razed the Hellian homeworld, Hellia, to harvest poppies for their honey production, effectively committing genocide against the Hellian people [4][13].

The Doctor, believing Belinda may be dead, captures Kid and subjects him to torture using a holographic device, a sequence that displays a darker side of the Fifteenth Doctor's character [5][8]. Belinda, who survived on the station, intervenes along with Cora Saint Bavier (Miriam-Teak Lee), a contestant who had been concealing her Hellian identity by removing her horns to compete [4][14].

The situation is resolved when the Doctor and his allies regain control of the station. The audience members are returned safely to the arena. In a final act of protest and reclamation of her heritage, Cora performs a ballad in her native tongue, accompanied by imagery of the destruction of Hellia, exposing the Corporation's crimes to the viewing universe [4][9].

Following the events, the Doctor experiences a vision of his granddaughter, Susan Foreman (Carole Ann Ford) [6]. The episode concludes with the reveal of the Rani (Archie Panjabi), a renegade Time Lord, setting up the series finale [1][6].

## Production

### Development
"The Interstellar Song Contest" was written by Juno Dawson, a best-selling author and the first transgender writer to pen a televised episode of *Doctor Who* [7][15]. Dawson had previously contributed to the franchise through the audio series *Doctor Who: Redacted* and the novel *The Good Doctor* [15]. Showrunner Russell T Davies reportedly pitched the concept to Dawson as "Die Hard meets Eurovision" [1][13]. The episode was designed to air on the same night as the real-world Eurovision Song Contest 2025 [7].

### Casting
The episode features Ncuti Gatwa and Varada Sethu in the lead roles. Guest stars include Freddie Fox as the antagonist Kid and Miriam-Teak Lee as the singer Cora Saint Bavier [4]. The production included significant cameo appearances to reinforce the Eurovision theme. Rylan Clark appears as a fictionalized version of himself, while Graham Norton appears as a holographic museum guide [7].

The episode marked the return of Carole Ann Ford as Susan Foreman, the Doctor's granddaughter and original companion, for the first time in a televised story since *The Five Doctors* (1983) [6]. Additionally, Archie Panjabi was introduced as a new incarnation of the Rani, a villainous Time Lord last seen in *Time and the Rani* (1987) [6].

### Filming and Effects
Filming took place at Wolf Studios Wales in early 2024 [1]. The production was reported to be one of the most expensive in the show's history, utilizing over half of the available soundstages to recreate the scale of the arena [1][13]. Director Ben A. Williams oversaw the episode, which was produced in the fourth production block of the series [1].

### Music
The episode features four original songs composed by Murray Gold, with lyrics by Gold, Davies, and Dawson [1][9]. These tracks include "My Big Feet," the ballad "I Love You But My Heart Says No!," and the "offbeat earworm" "Dugga-Doo" [9]. The climactic song performed by the character Cora was written to serve as a narrative resolution, described as a "heartfelt vocal sculpture" regarding the destruction of an ecosystem [9].

## Themes and Analysis

### Eurovision Parody
The episode serves as a direct pastiche of the Eurovision Song Contest, satirizing its camp aesthetic, intricate voting rules, and political undercurrents [2][8]. The setting includes a "green room," commentary boxes, and a museum dedicated to the history of the contest [8]. Reviewers noted that the episode captures the "cheesy and fun" atmosphere of the competition while juxtaposing it with a disaster movie plot [7].

### Political Subtext
The narrative explores themes of capitalism, exploitation, and racism. The Hellian antagonists are motivated by the destruction of their planet by a corporation for resource extraction (honey flavoring), a plot point that critics identified as a critique of "consumptive capitalism" [7][8]. The treatment of the Hellians, who are demonized by society and forced to hide their identity to participate in culture, was highlighted as an allegory for prejudice [5].

Several commentators drew parallels between the episode's plot—involving a song contest taking place during a time of conflict and calls for boycotts—and the real-world political controversies surrounding the participation of Israel in the Eurovision Song Contest during the Israel–Hamas war [7][16]. *The Guardian* noted that while the story was conceived years prior, it aired in a context that gave the themes of "mass casualty terrorism" and "racist attitudes" additional weight [17].

### The Doctor's Morality
A significant point of analysis was the Doctor's behavior. The scene in which the Doctor tortures the incapacitated villain Kid was described as a "moral event horizon" and a departure from the character's typical pacifism [8]. *The Doctor Who Companion* noted that this display of "ice in his hearts" countered criticisms that the Fifteenth Doctor was "too weak and emotional," presenting him instead as "too strong and emotional" [6].

## Broadcast and Reception

### Broadcast
"The Interstellar Song Contest" was released on BBC iPlayer and Disney+ on 17 May 2025 [1]. It was broadcast on BBC One later that evening, slotted between the FA Cup Final and the live broadcast of the Eurovision Song Contest 2025 [1][8].

### Critical Reception
The episode received generally positive reviews, though with some reservations regarding its tonal balance. *The Guardian* praised the episode for tackling "big themes" like capitalist exploitation and racism, while noting the "abrupt turn tonally" from camp romp to dark thriller [17]. *Screen Rant* called it a "perfectly captures the magic of Doctor Who," highlighting Gatwa's range of emotion [18].

The visual effects, particularly the sequence of the audience floating in space, were widely acclaimed as "breathtaking" and "arresting" [10][19]. The musical numbers were also highlighted as a success, with *Blogtor Who* praising the attention to detail in replicating Eurovision styles [9].

However, the episode faced criticism regarding its pacing and plot mechanics. *Doctor Who TV* described the plot as "paper-thin" and criticized the scientific explanation for the audience's survival as "technobabble" that undermined the dramatic stakes [12]. The scene depicting the Doctor torturing the villain was a subject of debate; while some found it a compelling exploration of the character's darker side [19], others felt it was "jarring" and "sour" [6]. *Den of Geek* suggested the episode struggled to find its tone, oscillating between "camp cosmic fun" and a "nightmare" scenario [10].

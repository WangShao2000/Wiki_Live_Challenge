# 2011 Flat Rock–Trenton tornado

The **2011 Flat Rock–Trenton tornado** was a violent, long-tracked tornado that struck portions of northeastern Alabama and northwestern Georgia on the afternoon of April 27, 2011. Rated as a high-end **EF4** on the Enhanced Fujita scale, the tornado was part of the 2011 Super Outbreak, the largest tornado outbreak in United States history. The tornado tracked for approximately 45 to 47 miles (72 to 76 km), causing catastrophic damage in the communities of Flat Rock and Higdon in Alabama, and Trenton in Georgia.

The tornado is responsible for at least 13 to 14 direct fatalities and dozens of injuries. It was one of several violent tornadoes to affect the region that day, occurring in close temporal and geographic proximity to the EF5 Rainsville tornado. Due to the severity of the damage, particularly in rural Jackson and DeKalb counties in Alabama, the tornado was noted by meteorologists for producing damage indicative of borderline EF5 intensity.

## Meteorological history

The tornado formed as part of the historic April 25–28, 2011 tornado outbreak, which was driven by a potent upper-level trough and a surface low-pressure system that created a highly sheared and unstable environment across the Southeastern United States [1][2].

The supercell thunderstorm responsible for the Flat Rock–Trenton tornado developed during the afternoon of April 27. This storm cell intensified after the dissipation of the supercell that had produced a violent tornado in Cullman, Alabama, earlier that day [3]. The new mesocyclone organized rapidly as it moved northeastward into Jackson County, Alabama.

The tornado first touched down at approximately 4:01 p.m. CDT (21:01 UTC) near Section, Alabama [4]. It initially caused EF0 to EF1 damage as it tracked northeast. As the system moved toward the communities of Pisgah and Rosalie, it underwent rapid intensification, widening significantly and developing a multi-vortex structure [5]. The tornado maintained its violent intensity as it crossed the Alabama–Georgia state line, finally dissipating near Fort Oglethorpe, Georgia, around 5:57 p.m. EDT (21:57 UTC) [6]. The total path length was approximately 47 miles (76 km), with a maximum path width reaching up to 0.85 miles (1,370 m) to 1.5 miles at times [2][3].

## Impact

The tornado caused widespread destruction across three counties in two states. Surveys conducted by the National Weather Service (NWS) in Huntsville, Alabama, and Peachtree City, Georgia, confirmed that the tornado reached peak wind speeds of approximately 190 mph (305 km/h) [5][6].

### Alabama

#### Jackson County
The tornado touched down roughly 3 miles northeast of Section, Alabama. Initial damage was limited to snapped trees and minor structural damage consistent with EF0 and EF1 intensity [5]. As the tornado moved northeast toward Pisgah and Rosalie, it strengthened rapidly into a violent wedge tornado. In this area, the tornado demolished several mobile homes and block-foundation houses, snapping and debarking thousands of trees [5].

The most intense damage in Jackson County occurred in the Flat Rock and Higdon communities. The tornado reached high-end EF4 intensity as it crossed County Road 369 and County Road 95 [3]. In this area, well-built homes were swept clean from their foundations. One specific instance of extreme force involved a heavy propane tank that was lofted and thrown 100 yards (91 m) from a destroyed chicken house [5]. On the same property, 19 cattle were killed, and two chicken barns were obliterated [5].

The tornado's path through Jackson County was characterized by extensive ground scouring and the complete destruction of forested areas. A 45-foot tall hardwood tree was ripped out of the ground and dragged 30 yards [3].

#### DeKalb County
After devastating portions of Jackson County, the tornado crossed into the northern tip of DeKalb County, specifically impacting the Shiloh community. The damage in Shiloh was catastrophic and rated EF4. The tornado swept away numerous mobile homes and site-built structures [5].

NWS surveyors noted that a van was lofted by the tornado and thrown approximately 400 yards (370 m) into a nearby field [4][5]. The Shiloh Community Church sustained a direct hit, and the surrounding area saw thousands of trees sheared off at the base [5]. The tornado's passage through this area resulted in five fatalities [4].

### Georgia

#### Dade County
The tornado crossed the Alabama–Georgia state line into Dade County at approximately 5:35 p.m. EDT, maintaining EF4 intensity [3][6]. It struck the rural community of Trenton and surrounding areas.

Upon entering Georgia, the tornado immediately destroyed homes along County Road 267 and west of Workman Drive [3]. A large 1.5-story home was swept clean from its foundation, with debris scattered across the road [3]. The tornado also destroyed the Dr. Lee Roberson Tabernacle at the Sand Mountain Bible Camp; the steel-framed structure was leveled to the slab, with steel beams thrown 150 yards [3].

As the tornado moved northeast toward Trenton, it caused major damage to the Dade Elementary School [7]. It is noted that the school had also been damaged by a separate EF1 tornado earlier that morning [6][8]. The afternoon EF4 tornado destroyed a grocery store, apartment complexes, and a funeral home in the Trenton area [9].

#### Walker County
The tornado continued into Walker County, Georgia, where it weakened but still produced significant damage. It lifted just west of Fort Oglethorpe around 5:57 p.m. EDT [6]. The path length within Georgia alone was approximately 18 miles [6].

## Casualties

The Flat Rock–Trenton tornado resulted in significant loss of life. Sources indicate a total of 13 to 17 fatalities associated with this storm, with varying reports on direct versus indirect deaths.

*   **Alabama:** The Alabama Emergency Management Agency (EMA) reported six fatalities in Jackson County and five fatalities in the Shiloh community of DeKalb County [4].
*   **Georgia:** Two fatalities were confirmed in Dade County [6].

In addition to the fatalities, there were at least 50 to 125 injuries reported across both states [3][6]. In DeKalb County, the tornado's impact was compounded by the nearby Rainsville EF5 tornado, which killed 25 people later that evening, making DeKalb County one of the hardest-hit areas of the outbreak [10].

## Aftermath

Recovery efforts began immediately following the storm. In the Shiloh and Flat Rock communities, emergency responders and volunteers worked to clear debris and search for survivors. The destruction of infrastructure, including chicken houses and farms, had a significant economic impact on the agricultural communities involved [11].

The 2011 Super Outbreak, of which this tornado was a part, resulted in 238 tornado-related deaths in Alabama alone [4]. The Flat Rock–Trenton tornado is often cited in meteorological analyses of the outbreak due to its extreme intensity and the multi-vortex nature observed by witnesses and radar data [5].

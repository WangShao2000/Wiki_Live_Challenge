# 2025 Enderlin tornado

The **2025 Enderlin tornado** was a violent tornado that struck rural areas near Enderlin, North Dakota, on the night of June 20, 2025. The tornado was part of a larger severe weather outbreak and derecho event that affected the Upper Midwest and Northern Great Plains. It is notable for being the first tornado in the United States to be rated **EF5** on the Enhanced Fujita scale since the Moore, Oklahoma, tornado in 2013, ending a 12-year period without a confirmed EF5 tornado [1][2][3].

The tornado touched down late in the evening and tracked for approximately 12 miles (19 km), reaching a maximum width of over one mile [4][5]. It resulted in three fatalities and caused extensive damage to rural homesteads, vegetation, and railroad infrastructure [6][7]. While initially rated as an EF3, the National Weather Service (NWS) upgraded the tornado to EF5 status on October 6, 2025, following an extensive forensic analysis of wind speeds required to derail and loft heavy railway cars [4].

## Meteorological synopsis

On June 20, 2025, a volatile atmospheric setup developed over the Northern Plains, characterized by an unstable air mass and favorable wind shear profiles for supercell thunderstorms [8][9]. The Storm Prediction Center had highlighted the risk for severe weather, including tornadoes and damaging winds, across the region [9].

During the late evening hours, supercell thunderstorms developed in southeastern North Dakota. These storms were part of a complex system that eventually evolved into a derecho—a widespread, long-lived wind storm—that produced wind gusts exceeding 100 mph (160 km/h) across North Dakota and Minnesota [6][10]. The specific supercell responsible for the Enderlin tornado formed in a high-instability environment capable of supporting strong rotation [11].

The tornado touched down at approximately 11:02 p.m. CDT, about 3 miles south of Enderlin in Ransom County [1][11]. It tracked northeastward, then turned north and eventually northwest before dissipating at 11:21 p.m. CDT near Alice in Cass County [1][11]. The tornado was on the ground for 19 minutes and had a path length of 12.1 miles [4][12]. At its peak intensity, the tornado reached a width of approximately 1.05 miles (1,850 yards) [5][12].

## Impact and casualties

The tornado caused catastrophic damage along its path through rural Ransom and Cass counties. Three people were killed by the storm: two men and one woman [7][13]. The fatalities occurred at two separate locations near Enderlin [14]. Authorities noted that the nocturnal nature of the tornado likely contributed to the danger, as many residents were asleep or had limited visibility of the approaching storm [7]. No other major injuries were reported, although the broader storm system caused widespread power outages affecting over 30,000 customers in North Dakota and 54,000 in Minnesota [6].

### Structural and environmental damage
Approximately ten homes were damaged or destroyed by the tornado [7]. One farmstead was described by damage surveyors as having its foundation "swept clean," with debris scattered downwind, an indicator of extreme wind intensity [4][5].

The tornado inflicted severe damage on vegetation. In the Maple River valley, a forest was largely obliterated; trees were snapped, uprooted, and debarked. Surveyors noted a "sandpapering" effect on the remaining tree stubs, a phenomenon often associated with violent tornadoes where high-velocity debris scours bark from wood [4].

### Railroad damage
A significant portion of the damage analysis focused on a Canadian Pacific Kansas City (CPKC) freight train that was struck by the tornado south of Enderlin. The tornado derailed 33 train cars [10][15]. Among the derailed stock were 19 fully loaded grain hopper cars, which can weigh up to 286,000 pounds (130,000 kg) each [15][16]. These cars were tipped over by the wind. Additionally, an empty tanker car, estimated to weigh between 50,000 and 90,000 pounds, was lofted and thrown approximately 476 feet (145 meters) from the tracks [4][17].

## Rating and analysis

Immediately following the event, the National Weather Service in Grand Forks assigned the tornado a preliminary rating of **EF3**, with estimated peak winds of 160 mph (260 km/h) [1][11]. This initial rating was based on available damage indicators at the time, primarily damage to frame homes and trees.

### Upgrade to EF5
On October 6, 2025, nearly four months after the event, the NWS Grand Forks office issued a revised statement upgrading the tornado to an **EF5**, the highest rating on the Enhanced Fujita scale [4]. This upgrade raised the estimated peak wind speeds to greater than 210 mph (340 km/h) [4].

The upgrade was the result of a collaborative forensic investigation involving the NWS, wind damage experts, and the Northern Tornadoes Project at Western University in Canada [4][18]. The investigation utilized advanced engineering models to analyze the force required to cause the observed damage to the train cars.

Key findings leading to the upgrade included:
*   **Train Derailment Analysis:** Engineers calculated that wind speeds exceeding 210 mph were necessary to tip the fully loaded grain hopper cars and to loft the empty tanker car the measured distance of 475.7 feet [4].
*   **Tree Damage:** Re-analysis of the damage along the Maple River identified extreme "sandpapering" and debarking of trees, as well as the displacement of tree root balls, consistent with wind speeds in the EF5 range [4].
*   **Radar Correlation:** The calculated wind speeds correlated with high-velocity signatures observed on WSR-88D radar data from the Grand Forks site during the storm [4].

### Historical significance
The upgrade of the Enderlin tornado to EF5 status was significant within the meteorological community. It ended a record-breaking 12-year "drought" of EF5 tornadoes in the United States; the previous confirmed EF5 had occurred in Moore, Oklahoma, on May 20, 2013 [1][3].

This event marked the first EF5 tornado recorded in North Dakota since the 1957 Fargo tornado [12]. It was also the deadliest tornado in the state since the F4 tornado that struck Elgin in 1978 [14][15].

## Aftermath

Following the tornado and the associated derecho, North Dakota Governor Kelly Armstrong issued a statewide disaster declaration to facilitate emergency response and recovery efforts [13]. The storm system caused an estimated $6 million in damage to electrical infrastructure alone [19].

Recovery efforts were extensive, with volunteers assisting in debris cleanup in the Enderlin area [16]. In September 2025, a request for a presidential disaster declaration was approved, allowing for Federal Emergency Management Agency (FEMA) funding to assist affected counties, including Barnes, Ransom, and Cass [16][19]. However, the distribution of federal aid faced delays due to the United States federal government shutdown that was ongoing at the time of the rating upgrade in October 2025 [15].

# The Reality War

**The Reality War** is the eighth and final episode of the fifteenth series of the British science fiction television series *Doctor Who*. Written by showrunner Russell T Davies and directed by Alex Sanjiv Pillai, the episode was first broadcast on BBC One and Disney+ on May 31, 2025. It serves as the second part of a two-part story, concluding the narrative that began in the preceding episode, "Wish World".

The episode features Ncuti Gatwa in his final regular appearance as the Fifteenth Doctor, alongside Varada Sethu as Belinda Chandra and Millie Gibson as Ruby Sunday. The narrative follows the Doctor and his allies as they attempt to reverse an altered timeline created by the Rani and Mrs. Flood, who intend to resurrect the ancient Time Lord Omega. The story culminates in the Doctor's regeneration, introducing Billie Piper in a new incarnation of the character.

"The Reality War" received mixed reviews from critics. While the production values and the return of Jodie Whittaker in a cameo role were praised, the screenplay faced criticism regarding the character arc of Belinda Chandra and the reliance on nostalgic elements. The episode was watched by a consolidated audience of 3.44 million viewers in the United Kingdom.

## Plot

Following the events of "Wish World", the Doctor (Ncuti Gatwa) is rescued from falling into the Underverse by Anita Benn (Steph de Whalley), the manager of the Time Hotel, who uses the hotel's trans-dimensional doors to save him [1][2][3]. Anita explains that the hotel is destabilizing due to the Rani's manipulation of time, specifically the repetition of the date May 23, 2025 [3].

The Doctor and Anita return to the altered reality to locate Belinda Chandra (Varada Sethu) and her daughter, Poppy. They use the Time Hotel's doors to infiltrate UNIT Tower, where the flow of "real time" restores the memories of Kate Lethbridge-Stewart (Jemma Redgrave), Colonel Ibrahim (Alexander Devrient), and the UNIT staff [1][3]. Kate subsequently uses UNIT bio-chips to restore the memories of Ruby Sunday (Millie Gibson), Shirley Bingham (Ruth Madeley), and Mel Bush (Bonnie Langford) [1][3].

The Rani (Archie Panjabi) and Mrs. Flood (Anita Dobson)—revealed to be another incarnation of the Rani—confront the Doctor. They disclose their plan to retrieve Omega (Nicholas Briggs), a co-founder of Time Lord society, from the Underverse. They intend to use Omega as a genetic bank to create a new race of Time Lords under their dominion [1][3]. The Doctor argues that the existence of Poppy, a child born of the altered reality, proves that creation is possible without resorting to Omega, but the Ranis proceed with their plan [3].

As the Rani summons Omega, who manifests as a skeletal entity, the Doctor and Susan Triad (Susan Twist) construct a "Zero Room" to protect Poppy from being erased when reality is corrected [3]. Belinda enters the chamber with her daughter. At the Bone Palace, Omega betrays the Ranis, declaring his intent to consume everything, and eats the Archie Panjabi incarnation of the Rani [1][4]. Mrs. Flood flees the scene.

The Doctor uses a device called the Vindicator to force Omega back into his prison [1]. Meanwhile, Ruby Sunday confronts Conrad Clark (Jonah Hauer-King) and uses Desiderium, the infant god granting the wishes, to undo Conrad's original wish [1]. Reality is restored to its proper state. However, because Poppy was a product of the "Wish World," she begins to face erasure despite the Zero Room.

To save Poppy, the Doctor initiates a regeneration, utilizing the energy in conjunction with the time vortex to shift the timeline sufficiently to allow Poppy to exist as Belinda's daughter in the primary reality [1][5]. During this process, the Doctor receives counsel from a vision of his thirteenth incarnation (Jodie Whittaker) [6]. Ncuti Gatwa's Doctor regenerates, and the new incarnation appears with the face of former companion Rose Tyler (Billie Piper) [1][5].

In the aftermath, the now-human Desiderium is adopted by Ruby's family. Anita returns to the Time Hotel. Belinda departs with Poppy, while the new Doctor prepares for her own journey [1].

## Production

### Development
"The Reality War" was written by Russell T Davies [1]. The episode was designed to conclude the narrative threads of the Fifteenth Doctor's era, which began with "The Church on Ruby Road" in 2023 [1]. Davies stated that he originally considered creating a new Time Lord antagonist but decided that Omega, who had not appeared in the television series for 42 years, was a more appropriate fit for the story [1].

### Casting
The episode features the departure of Ncuti Gatwa as the Doctor. His exit was not announced prior to the broadcast, a deviation from the show's standard publicity strategy regarding regenerations [5]. The finale also marks the final regular appearances of Millie Gibson as Ruby Sunday and Varada Sethu as Belinda Chandra [1].

The episode includes several significant guest appearances. Archie Panjabi portrays a new incarnation of the Rani, while Anita Dobson's character, Mrs. Flood, is revealed to be another incarnation of the same Time Lord [8]. Jodie Whittaker reprises her role as the Thirteenth Doctor for a cameo appearance [1]. The ending introduces Billie Piper, known for playing Rose Tyler, as the new incarnation of the Doctor [5].

### Filming
Primary filming for the episode took place in 2024 [1]. Reports indicated that reshoots occurred into 2025, which resulted in delayed edits for the final broadcast [1]. The episode has a runtime of 66 minutes, making it the longest episode of Series 15 [1][9].

## Broadcast and Reception

### Broadcast and Ratings
"The Reality War" was broadcast on May 31, 2025 [1]. It was released simultaneously on BBC One and BBC iPlayer in the United Kingdom, and on Disney+ internationally [1]. The episode also received a limited theatrical release in the UK alongside the preceding episode, "Wish World" [9].

The episode received an overnight viewing figure of 2.2 million in the UK [1][10]. The seven-day consolidated rating, which includes streaming data, was 3.44 million viewers [1][11]. This figure represented a 58.5% increase from the overnight numbers and was consistent with the viewership of the previous season's finale, "Empire of Death" [11].

### Critical Reception
The episode received mixed reviews from critics. On Rotten Tomatoes, 54% of 13 critics' reviews were positive [1].

Several critics praised the spectacle and specific character moments. *Radio Times* highlighted the "wholesome moment" between Gatwa and Whittaker's Doctors [12]. *The Guardian* reported that the cinema screenings were well-attended and that the audience reaction to the surprise twists was positive [13].

However, the narrative choices drew criticism. *Den of Geek* described the episode as a "messy muddle" and criticized the handling of Belinda Chandra's character, arguing that her reduction to a mother figure in a reality created by "toxic men" was a disservice to her potential [14]. *Kabooooom* echoed this sentiment, stating that the plot implication that Belinda's happy ending relies on a reality forced upon her was "unfortunate" [2].

The inclusion of classic villains Omega and the Rani was also a point of contention. Some reviewers felt the characters were underutilized, with *Critical Popcorn* noting that the Rani's potential was "squandered" and the plot felt like a "last-minute scramble" [15]. The regeneration twist involving Billie Piper was described by *The Telegraph* as a "retrograde move" that risked the show becoming too focused on its own past [14].

## Other Media

The title "The Reality War" has also been used for other notable works in science fiction and fantasy media.

### Literature
*The Reality War* is a science fiction novel series by Tim C. Taylor, published starting in 2012. The series consists of two primary works: *The Slough of Despond* and *The City of Destruction* [16]. The narrative involves a time travel war between two opposing intelligent races and is described as a "spiritual journey" that echoes John Bunyan's *The Pilgrim's Progress* [17][18]. The protagonist, Radlan Saravanan, sparks the conflict by making a choice in 1992 that rewrites history [18].

### Comics
*Doctor Strange Epic Collection: The Reality War* (Vol. 5) is a trade paperback collection published by Marvel Comics in February 2022 [19][20]. The volume collects *Doctor Strange* issues #29–51 (originally published 1978–1982) and material from other Marvel publications [19]. The stories feature writers Roger Stern and Chris Claremont, and artists including Gene Colan and Marshall Rogers. The collection depicts Doctor Strange battling villains such as Nightmare, Baron Mordo, and the N'Garai [19][20].

# Trichy assault rifle

The **Trichy assault rifle** (also known as **TAR**) is an Indian assault rifle chambered for the 7.62×39mm cartridge. It is manufactured by the Ordnance Factory Tiruchirappalli (OFT), a unit of Advanced Weapons and Equipment India Limited (AWEIL) [1][2]. The rifle is a derivative of the Bulgarian Arsenal AR-M1, which itself is a variant of the Kalashnikov AK-47 pattern [1].

Designed to meet the operational requirements of Indian security forces engaged in counter-insurgency and law enforcement duties, the TAR was developed to provide a domestic alternative to imported AK-pattern rifles [1][3]. Following its introduction in 2017, the weapon has been adopted by various Central Armed Police Forces (CAPF), including the Central Reserve Police Force (CRPF) and Border Security Force (BSF), as well as several state police units across India [3][4][5].

## History

### Background
Prior to the development of the Trichy assault rifle, Indian paramilitary and police forces relied heavily on a mix of weaponry, including the 5.56mm INSAS rifle and imported 7.62×39mm rifles such as the AK-47 and the Bulgarian Arsenal AR-M1 [1][5]. While the Indian Army had standardized on the 5.56mm caliber for a period, operational experience in counter-insurgency environments led to a renewed preference for the stopping power of the 7.62×39mm cartridge among police and paramilitary units [5]. To reduce dependence on imports and promote self-reliance in defense production, the Ordnance Factory Tiruchirappalli initiated the development of an indigenous platform compatible with the widely available 7.62×39mm ammunition [1][3].

### Development
The Ordnance Factory Tiruchirappalli (OFT) began the development of the rifle around 2011 [1][6]. Early prototypes reportedly utilized receivers based on the FN FAL design but adapted for the 7.62×39mm cartridge; however, the design eventually shifted toward a Kalashnikov-pattern mechanism based on the Bulgarian AR-M1 [1][7]. Development continued between 2011 and 2015, with the establishment of a production line in 2017 [1][5].

Initial versions faced scrutiny regarding their rate of fire and reliability. Reports from 2012 indicated that prototypes experienced jamming issues attributed to a high rate of fire (800 rounds per minute), which exceeded the standard cyclic rate of the AK-47 [1]. Subsequent refinements involved adjusting the mechanism to stabilize the rate of fire and improving the receiver construction [1].

### Trials and Adoption
The TAR underwent extensive field trials to validate its suitability for service. In November 2017, the Central Reserve Police Force (CRPF) conducted reliability tests at their academy in Kadarpur, Haryana [1][8]. During these trials, the rifle was subjected to a life-cycle test involving the firing of 15,000 rounds without a reported stoppage [4][8]. Additional environmental testing included exposure to mud, rain, and saltwater, as well as drop tests to assess durability [1][8].

Following successful trials, the CRPF placed an initial order for 6,167 rifles in early 2018 [1][4]. The first batch of 500 rifles was handed over to the CRPF in March 2020 [4][8]. The Chhattisgarh Police had previously acquired a batch of 200 rifles in March 2017 [1][9].

## Design

### Operating Mechanism
The Trichy assault rifle is a gas-operated firearm that utilizes a rotating bolt locking mechanism, consistent with the Kalashnikov pattern [1][10]. It is capable of firing in single-shot, automatic, and burst modes [1][11]. The weapon is chambered for the 7.62×39mm M43 cartridge, which is the same ammunition used by the AK-47 and AKM series [1][12].

### Receiver and Construction
A distinguishing feature of the TAR, compared to the stamped-receiver AKM or INSAS rifles, is its use of a fully machined mono-block receiver [2][8]. This construction method is intended to provide higher strength and reliability, similar to the original AK-47 and the Bulgarian AR-M1 [1][8]. The rifle features a fibre-reinforced polymer magazine with steel inserts and polymer furniture, including the handguard and pistol grip [2].

### Specifications
The standard TAR weighs approximately 3.4 kilograms (7.5 lb) without the magazine [1][2]. It has an overall length of 900 millimeters (35 in) with the stock extended and 650 millimeters (26 in) with the stock folded [1][2]. The barrel length is 414 mm (16.3 in) [1]. The rifle has a cyclic rate of fire of approximately 600 to 650 rounds per minute [1][13]. The effective firing range is stated to be between 300 and 350 meters, with a maximum destructive range cited as 1,350 meters [1][11].

### Features and Accessories
The TAR is designed with modularity in mind. It can be fitted with Picatinny rails on the upper handguard (12 o'clock position) to accommodate optical sights and other tactical accessories [1][2]. The rifle is equipped with tritium-filled luminous sights for low-light operations [2][8]. It is compatible with various under-barrel grenade launchers (UBGL), including the GP-25, GP-30, and Indian-manufactured 40mm UBGLs [4][14]. The weapon feeds from a 30-round detachable box magazine and is compatible with standard AK-47 magazines [1][14].

## Variants

The Ordnance Factory Tiruchirappalli manufactures the TAR in several configurations to suit different operational requirements.

### Fixed Butt
The standard infantry version features a fixed buttstock, offering stability for general combat use [1][11].

### Side Folding Butt
This variant features a stock that folds to the side of the receiver. It was developed to reduce the overall length of the weapon for ease of transport and storage [1][11].

### Down Folding Butt
Launched in August 2021, this variant features an under-folding stock (down-folding). It was developed specifically to meet the requirements of the Railway Protection Force (RPF) and certain CAPF units for a more compact profile during guard and patrolling duties [1][11][15].

### Trichy Carbine (TriCa)
The **Trichy Carbine**, often abbreviated as **TriCa** or referred to as "Baby TAR," is a compact carbine version of the assault rifle unveiled in January 2021 [12][14]. It features a shortened barrel of approximately 212 mm (8.3 in) and weighs 3.17 kg [1][12]. The TriCa is designed for use by vehicle crews, paratroopers, and special operations forces requiring a concealable and maneuverable weapon [14][16]. It has an effective range of 150 to 175 meters and features a special muzzle booster to mitigate flash and sound [14][16].

## Users

The Trichy assault rifle has been procured by various Indian security agencies.

*   **Central Reserve Police Force (CRPF):** The CRPF placed a bulk order for 6,167 rifles following successful trials in 2017–2018 [4][8].
*   **Border Security Force (BSF):** The BSF has inducted the TAR into service to replace older weaponry [2][3].
*   **Sashastra Seema Bal (SSB):** Along with the BSF, the SSB has placed orders for the rifle [5][8].
*   **Central Industrial Security Force (CISF):** The CISF is among the CAPF units that have procured the TAR [2][5].
*   **National Security Guard (NSG):** The NSG placed an order for the TriCa carbine variant in December 2021 [1][17].
*   **State Police Forces:**
    *   **Chhattisgarh Police:** Received the first batch of 200 rifles in March 2017 for use in anti-Naxalite operations [8][9].
    *   **Madhya Pradesh Police:** Procured the weapon for modernization [18].
    *   **Rajasthan Police:** Identified as a user of the TAR [18].
    *   **Kerala Police:** Inducted the TAR for counter-insurgency operations [1][18].
    *   **Jharkhand Police:** Reported to have acquired the rifle for operations in left-wing extremism affected areas [1][19].
    *   **Maharashtra Police:** Units such as the Mumbai Police have adopted the rifle [18].

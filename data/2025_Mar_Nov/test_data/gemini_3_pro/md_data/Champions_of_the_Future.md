# Champions of the Future

**Champions of the Future** (COTF) is an international kart racing competition organized by the RGMMC Group and sanctioned by the CIK-FIA (Commission Internationale de Karting). Established in 2020, the series serves as a preparatory platform for drivers competing in the FIA Karting European and World Championships. The events are typically scheduled two weeks prior to FIA Karting events at the same circuits, allowing teams and drivers to test equipment and familiarize themselves with track conditions under competitive regulations [1].

The competition encompasses several distinct series, including the **Champions of the Future Euro Series**, the **Champions of the Future Shifters**, and the **Champions of the Future Academy Program**. The latter, launched in 2023, utilizes an "arrive-and-drive" format aimed at cost containment and talent identification, featuring a partnership with the F1 Academy to promote female participation in motorsport [2][3].

In late 2025, the organization announced significant expansions scheduled for 2026, including the launch of regional series in the United Kingdom and the United States, as well as a Formula 4 development program [4][5][6].

## History

### Foundation and Early Years (2020–2022)
Champions of the Future was founded in 2020 by the RGMMC Group, a Swiss-based motorsport promoter responsible for organizing FIA Karting events [1]. The series was created to fill a gap in the calendar for high-level international testing races. The inaugural season in 2020 faced scheduling disruptions due to the COVID-19 pandemic. The opening round, originally planned for Wackersdorf, Germany, in May, was cancelled. The series eventually debuted in Zuera, Spain, in July 2020, followed by a round in Portimão, Portugal [1][7].

The initial format focused on the CIK-FIA direct-drive classes: OK (Senior) and OK-Junior (OK-J), alongside the gearbox class KZ2 and the 60 Mini category [1]. Notable inaugural champions included Nikita Bedrin (OK) and Arvid Lindblad (OK-Junior) [1].

In 2022, the organization hosted a one-off "Winter Series" at the Franciacorta Karting Track in Italy prior to the main season. The regular season was subsequently rebranded as the **Champions of the Future Euro Series** [1].

### Expansion and Academy Program (2023–Present)
In 2023, RGMMC expanded the brand by launching the **Champions of the Future Academy Program (COTFA)**. Unlike the Euro Series, which relies on factory teams and private entrants bringing their own equipment, the Academy Program was designed as an "arrive-and-drive" series. This format requires drivers to use supplied chassis and engines allocated via a lottery system to ensure parity and reduce costs [3].

The Academy Program introduced the OK-N and OK-N Junior categories to the international stage, alongside the Mini 60 class. The series gained prominence through a collaboration with the F1 Academy's "Discover Your Drive" initiative, headed by Susie Wolff. This partnership provides financial support and equipment to selected female drivers in each category to foster diversity in the sport [2][3].

In 2024, Luna Fluxá became the first female driver to win a Champions of the Future title, securing the Academy Program's OK-N Senior championship [2].

## Series Structure and Categories

As of 2025, the competition is divided into three primary international championships, with additional national and development programs in development.

### Euro Series
The Euro Series is the premier tier of the competition, designed for elite international teams and drivers. It functions as the primary testing ground for the FIA Karting European and World Championships.
*   **Classes:** OK (Senior), OK-Junior.
*   **Format:** The calendar typically consists of five rounds held at major European circuits such as Valencia (Spain), Val d'Argenton (France), and Kristianstad (Sweden) [1][8].
*   **Regulations:** The series strictly follows CIK-FIA technical regulations to ensure data relevance for upcoming FIA events [9].

### Shifters
Dedicated to gearbox karting, the Shifters series features the KZ categories.
*   **Classes:** KZ, KZ2, and KZ2 Masters.
*   **Format:** Events are often held as standalone weekends or in conjunction with specific Euro Series rounds, depending on the FIA calendar alignment [1][10].

### Academy Program
The Academy Program focuses on talent development and accessibility.
*   **Classes:** Mini 60 (ages 8–12), OK-N Junior (ages 12–14), and OK-N Senior (ages 15+) [11][12].
*   **Equipment:** Drivers utilize CIK-FIA homologated equipment provided by the organizers. Chassis and engines are drawn by lottery to equalize performance [3].
*   **F1 Academy Partnership:** The "Discover Your Drive" initiative supports three female drivers in each class, providing branded karts, race suits, and entry fee subsidies [3][13].

### F4 Racing Academy
In September 2025, RGMMC launched the **Champions of the Future Racing Academy**, a Formula 4 development program. Led by racing driver Augusto Farfus, the program is designed for drivers in their final year of karting. It offers a year-long testing schedule with a dedicated car, engineer, and coach for eight selected drivers, aiming to bridge the gap between karting and single-seater racing [6][14].

## 2025 Season and Results

The 2025 season saw the Euro Series conclude in September at the Åsum Ring in Kristianstad, Sweden.

*   **OK Category:** British driver **Kenzo Craigie** (Prema Racing) secured the championship title. Although the final race in Kristianstad was won by Daniel Kelleher, Craigie's consistent performance throughout the season allowed him to secure the overall victory with 333 points [15][16].
*   **OK-Junior Category:** **Noah Baglin** (Prema Racing) won the title after a double victory in the final round, winning both the Final and the championship. He overcame a points deficit to defeat William Calleja [15][17].

In the Shifters category, the season featured rounds in Valencia and Franciacorta. The champions included Senna van Walstijn (KZ), Maksim Orlov (KZ2), and Angelo Lombardo (KZ2-Masters) [1].

## Controversies

### 2025 Valencia Tyre Boycott
In May 2025, the Champions of the Future Shifters round in Valencia, Spain, was disrupted by a driver boycott in the KZ category. Competitors expressed dissatisfaction with the performance and consistency of the control tires provided by Dunlop, which had been mandated for the event and the subsequent European Championship [18][19].

Drivers claimed the tires lacked grip and created safety concerns, stating that the premier KZ class was lapping slower than junior categories. A petition signed by major drivers, including several World Champions, requested a switch to alternative manufacturers such as LeCont or Vega. When the request was not granted, the majority of the KZ field refused to participate in the final race. Only 11 karts started the final, and only three drivers completed the race [10][18]. Following the incident, the CIK-FIA dropped Dunlop as a supplier for the category [1].

## Future Expansion (2026)

The organization has announced a significant global expansion of the Academy Program model for the 2026 season.

### Champions of the Future America
In partnership with **K1 Speed**, a large indoor karting operator, and the RGMMC Group, a new series titled **Champions of the Future America** is scheduled to launch in 2026. The series aims to create a pathway for American drivers to international competition. The 2026 season will feature ten rounds, primarily based in the Southwestern United States, utilizing circuits such as the K1 Circuit in Winchester, California [1][5].

### British Champions of the Future Academy
In collaboration with **Motorsport UK**, the governing body for motorsport in the United Kingdom, a national version of the Academy Program will launch in 2026. The series will take place across four UK locations and will utilize the same lottery-based equipment model (Parolin/TM for Mini, Kart Republic/IAME for OK-N classes) as the international series. This initiative is part of the Motorsport UK Karting Pathway [4][20].

### Middle East and Asia
The series continues to expand its footprint in the Middle East and Asia, with events planned in the United Arab Emirates and a specific title event in Macau [1][13].

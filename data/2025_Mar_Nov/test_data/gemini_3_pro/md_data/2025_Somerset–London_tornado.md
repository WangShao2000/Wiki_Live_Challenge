# 2025 Somerset–London tornado

The **2025 Somerset–London tornado** was a violent, long-tracked tornado that struck the cities of Somerset and London in southeastern Kentucky during the late evening hours of May 16, 2025. The tornado was part of a larger severe weather outbreak that affected the Midwestern and Southeastern United States from May 15 to 16.

The National Weather Service (NWS) rated the tornado as an EF4 on the Enhanced Fujita scale, with peak wind speeds estimated at 170 mph (270 km/h) [1][2]. It carved a path of destruction approximately 60 miles (97 km) long and reached a maximum width of 1,700 yards (1,600 m), or nearly one mile [1][2]. The tornado resulted in 19 fatalities and at least 108 injuries, making it the deadliest tornado to strike the region in decades and the deadliest in Kentucky since the 2021 Western Kentucky tornado [1][3][4].

## Meteorological history

The tornado formed from a supercell thunderstorm that originated in the Ozarks region of south-central Missouri, traveling over 400 miles before reaching Kentucky [3]. This supercell was part of a broader weather system that produced severe storms across the Ohio Valley [5].

The tornado first touched down in Russell County, Kentucky, at approximately 10:27 p.m. EDT on May 16, 2025 [2][6]. It initially caused damage near Jamestown and the community of Whittle before intensifying as it moved eastward [2][7]. The storm crossed into Pulaski County, impacting the communities of Faubush and Nancy, and then struck the southern periphery of Somerset [2].

After exiting Somerset, the tornado traversed the rugged terrain of the Daniel Boone National Forest. During this phase, the tornado widened significantly, reaching its peak width of nearly one mile [1][7]. Satellite imagery captured days later revealed a distinct "scar" of toppled vegetation through the forest visible from space [3][7].

The tornado then entered Laurel County, where it reached its peak intensity. Between 11:35 p.m. and 11:45 p.m. EDT, the tornado struck the southern side of London, including the Sunshine Hills subdivision and the London-Corbin Airport [2][8]. The tornado dissipated just east of London near the community of Lida around 11:56 p.m. EDT, having remained on the ground for approximately 90 minutes [2][3].

## Impact

The tornado caused catastrophic damage across three counties, with the most severe destruction occurring in Laurel County. The National Weather Service in Jackson, Kentucky, confirmed the EF4 rating following multi-day damage surveys [1].

### Russell and Pulaski Counties
In Russell County, where the tornado first touched down, one fatality was recorded [2]. As the storm moved into Pulaski County, it caused significant damage to the southern portion of Somerset.

Several businesses and institutions along U.S. Route 27 and Highway 914 were heavily damaged or destroyed. The Redeemer Lutheran Church sustained severe structural damage, with its sanctuary walls collapsing and the pulpit blown into the parking lot [3][9]. Adjacent businesses, including Baxter's Coffee and a Speedway convenience store, were also heavily damaged [3][10]. Security footage from Pro Fab Inc., a local business, captured the moment the tornado struck, showing transformers exploding and debris impacting the facility [11]. The Somerset Cinemas 8, which had approximately 100 people inside at the time, suffered roof and structural damage, though no injuries were reported at that specific location [3][11]. One fatality occurred in Pulaski County in the Poplarville community [12].

### Daniel Boone National Forest
Between Somerset and London, the tornado tracked through the Daniel Boone National Forest. The damage in this area was characterized by the complete snapping and uprooting of thousands of trees [2]. The path of destruction through the forest was sufficiently wide and clear to be identified in satellite imagery acquired by NASA's Landsat 9 on June 4, 2025 [7].

### Laurel County
Laurel County sustained the heaviest loss of life and property. The tornado struck the Sunshine Hills subdivision south of London at EF4 intensity [2][11]. Entire rows of homes were leveled or swept from their foundations [2]. Nine fatalities occurred in this subdivision alone [11].

The tornado also directly impacted the London-Corbin Airport (Magee Field). Security cameras recorded the tornado crossing the airfield at 11:47 p.m. EDT [3][8]. The high winds flattened hangars, destroyed a medical helicopter belonging to PHI Air Medical, and wrecked multiple aircraft, including six vintage Beechcraft T-34 Mentors that were present for a fly-in event [13][14].

Further east, the tornado caused damage at the Levi Jackson Wilderness Road Park, where tree harvesting equipment was later required to clear the debris [15]. The storm lifted shortly after crossing Kentucky Route 488 [2].

## Casualties

The tornado resulted in 19 confirmed deaths [1][2]. The fatalities were distributed as follows:
*   **Laurel County:** 17 deaths [2].
*   **Pulaski County:** 1 death [2].
*   **Russell County:** 1 death [2].

Among the victims was Major Roger Leslie Leatherman, a 39-year veteran of the Laurel County Fire Department, who was killed while responding to the storm in the Sunshine Hills area [12][16]. Other victims included a couple married for 55 years who were found shielding one another [11].

In addition to the fatalities, at least 108 people were treated for injuries at local hospitals, including CHI Saint Joseph Health in London [3][17].

## Aftermath

Following the tornado, a mass casualty incident was declared in Laurel County [3]. Search and rescue operations began immediately, hampered by darkness and debris. Kentucky Governor Andy Beshear visited the affected areas, describing the destruction as "devastating" and noting that the event was one of the most significant weather disasters in the state's history [1].

The Federal Emergency Management Agency (FEMA) made disaster assistance available to residents in the affected counties, with a deadline for applications set for June 2025 [18]. Community recovery efforts included fundraising campaigns for rebuilding destroyed institutions, such as the Redeemer Lutheran Church [9].

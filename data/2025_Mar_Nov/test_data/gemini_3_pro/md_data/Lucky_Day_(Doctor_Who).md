# Lucky Day (Doctor Who)

"**Lucky Day**" is the fourth episode of the fifteenth series of the British science fiction television programme *Doctor Who*. It was first broadcast on BBC One and released on BBC iPlayer and Disney+ on 3 May 2025. The episode was written by Pete McTighe and directed by Peter Hoar.

The episode features the return of companion Ruby Sunday (Millie Gibson), who takes the lead role while the Fifteenth Doctor (Ncuti Gatwa) and his current companion Belinda Chandra (Varada Sethu) appear only briefly. The narrative follows Ruby as she attempts to adjust to life on Earth after her travels in the TARDIS. She enters a relationship with Conrad Clark (Jonah Hauer-King), a podcaster who claims to be hunted by a monster called the Shreek. The story explores themes of post-traumatic stress, disinformation, and the impact of the Doctor's life on those left behind.

"Lucky Day" is a "Doctor-lite" episode, a production necessity designed to allow the lead actors to film other episodes simultaneously. It was filmed in Cardiff in November 2023. The episode received mixed to positive reviews from critics; while Millie Gibson's performance and the episode's social commentary on conspiracy theories were praised, the plot's execution and the limited screen time for the Doctor drew criticism. The episode recorded overnight ratings of 1.5 million viewers, the lowest in the programme's history at the time, though consolidated viewing figures significantly increased this number due to streaming performance.

## Plot

On New Year's Day 2007, the Doctor and his companion Belinda Chandra land briefly in London, where they encounter a young boy named Conrad Clark. The Doctor gives Conrad a 50p coin and tells him it is his "lucky day."

In 2025, Ruby Sunday is living in London, suffering from post-traumatic stress disorder (PTSD) following her departure from the TARDIS. She is contacted by an adult Conrad Clark, now a podcaster investigating extraterrestrial phenomena. Conrad claims he has been "marked" by a creature known as the Shreek—a predator that hunts its victims using a green slime pheromone—after spotting the Doctor and Ruby during a previous adventure. Bonding over their shared knowledge of the Doctor, Ruby and Conrad begin a romantic relationship.

During a weekend trip to Conrad's home village, the couple is seemingly besieged by the Shreek. Ruby contacts UNIT for assistance. However, it is revealed that the Shreek attacking them are actually Conrad's friends in costumes. Conrad exposes himself as the leader of "Think Tank," a conspiracy group dedicated to "exposing" UNIT. He believes the organization fakes alien threats to misappropriate taxpayer money and uses his relationship with Ruby to gain credibility for his disinformation campaign.

Conrad and his followers livestream their infiltration of UNIT Tower, claiming the threats are hoaxes. To prove the reality of the danger, UNIT leader Kate Lethbridge-Stewart releases a genuine Shreek that had been held in captivity. The creature targets Conrad, who has refused to take the antidote for the pheromone mark, believing the virus to be a fabrication. Confronted by the real monster, Conrad admits on his livestream that he lied, but attempts to backtrack once Ruby stuns the creature. The Shreek bites Conrad's arm before being contained.

In the aftermath, public sentiment turns in favor of UNIT, though Kate faces internal scrutiny for releasing a dangerous alien. Ruby decides to distance herself from UNIT to focus on her mental health. Conrad is imprisoned for his actions. He is visited by the Doctor (from a point in his timeline before he met Belinda), who admonishes Conrad for weaponizing lies and informs him he will be forgotten by history. After the Doctor departs, Conrad is released from his cell by Mrs Flood.

## Production

### Development
"Lucky Day" was written by Pete McTighe, who had previously written the episodes "Kerblam!" (2018) and "Praxeus" (2020) for the Thirteenth Doctor [1][2]. Showrunner Russell T Davies contacted McTighe in 2023 to write an episode focused on the character of Ruby Sunday [3]. The episode was designed as a "Doctor-lite" story, a format used in *Doctor Who* to allow the main cast to film other episodes simultaneously; in this case, Ncuti Gatwa and Varada Sethu were filming the series premiere, "The Robot Revolution" [4][5].

McTighe intended the episode to explore the psychological aftermath of travelling with the Doctor, a theme he felt had been touched upon with companions like Sarah Jane Smith but rarely explored immediately following a companion's departure [6]. He described the tone as a blend of "action, romantic comedy, and gothic horror" [1]. The episode's structure and focus on Ruby Sunday led commentators to compare it to the Series 14 episode "73 Yards" [1][7].

### Writing
To prepare for the script, McTighe was given access to unreleased footage from the 60th Anniversary specials and Series 14 to understand Ruby Sunday's character arc and development [3]. The script addresses contemporary issues regarding online disinformation, conspiracy theories, and the radicalization of young men [7][8]. The antagonist, Conrad Clark, was written to reflect real-world figures who monetize conspiracy theories, with critics noting parallels to Alex Jones [8][9].

The monster, the Shreek, was based on McTighe's childhood fears of dog-like creatures in the dark [1]. The concept involved a creature that could not be reasoned with, serving as a physical manifestation of the "monster" role that shifts to Conrad himself by the episode's conclusion [8].

### Filming
"Lucky Day" was filmed as part of the second production block of Series 15, directed by Peter Hoar [5]. Hoar had previously directed the *Doctor Who* episode "A Good Man Goes to War" (2011) and the Russell T Davies series *It's a Sin* [5]. Filming took place in November 2023, primarily in Cardiff City Centre and at Wolf Studios Wales [1]. The episode was shot back-to-back with the series opener, necessitating the reduced role of the Doctor [4].

### Cast
Millie Gibson reprised her role as Ruby Sunday. Jonah Hauer-King was cast as Conrad Clark; his casting was noted for his previous role in the live-action *The Little Mermaid* [10]. The episode features the return of the UNIT team, including Jemma Redgrave as Kate Lethbridge-Stewart and Ruth Madeley as Shirley Bingham [1]. Anita Dobson makes an appearance as Mrs Flood in the episode's conclusion [1].

## Themes and Analysis

### Disinformation and Conspiracy
A central theme of "Lucky Day" is the danger of disinformation. The character of Conrad Clark is depicted as a "truth-seeker" who is actually a grifter spreading false narratives for profit and attention [11]. Reviewers observed that the episode critiques "toxic fandom" and the way resentment can be channeled into destruction [7]. The fictional group "Think Tank" and Conrad's podcast serve as analogues for real-world platforms that spread conspiracy theories, such as the denial of school shootings or vaccines [8][12]. The refusal of Conrad to take the antidote, even when faced with the physical reality of the Shreek, was interpreted by critics as a commentary on anti-vaccine sentiment [7][12].

### Post-Traumatic Stress
The episode examines the difficulty of returning to a mundane life after experiencing the wonders and dangers of time travel. Ruby Sunday is depicted as suffering from PTSD, displaying hyper-vigilance and trust issues [4][11]. The narrative suggests that her attraction to Conrad is partly due to his connection to the Doctor, allowing her to process her trauma with someone who seemingly understands her experiences [13].

### The "Doctor-Lite" Format
As a "Doctor-lite" episode, "Lucky Day" places the companion in the role of the protagonist who must solve the problem without the Doctor's direct intervention. This format has been used in previous episodes such as "Love & Monsters", "Blink", and "Turn Left" [10][11]. Critics noted that while the Doctor is largely absent, his influence drives the narrative, and the episode serves to highlight Ruby's capability and independence [7][8].

## Broadcast and Reception

### Broadcast and Ratings
"Lucky Day" was released on BBC iPlayer in the United Kingdom and Disney+ internationally at 00:00 GMT on 3 May 2025, and was broadcast on BBC One later that evening [1][14].

The episode received an overnight rating of 1.5 million viewers on BBC One, which was reported as the lowest overnight figure in the programme's history at that time [15][16]. However, the 7-day consolidated rating, which accounts for catch-up and streaming, rose to 2.80 million viewers [17][18]. By 28 days, the viewing figure had reached 3.18 million [19]. Despite the low linear broadcast numbers, the episode was the third most-watched programme of the day on BBC One and ranked 29th in the weekly chart [17][20].

### Critical Reception
The episode received mixed to positive reviews. *Den of Geek* praised the "committed and engaging performance" of Millie Gibson but felt the episode struggled to find a compelling take on the "companion left behind" concept, noting that the execution of the plot twist undermined the emotional throughline [11]. *The Movie Blog* described it as an "interesting episode" that engaged with the show's place in popular discourse but argued that the execution failed to support its "big ideas," calling it a "story about toxic fandom and masculine anxieties" [7].

*Radio Times* highlighted the "freeing" nature of the Doctor-lite format for writer Pete McTighe, allowing for a different energy and higher stakes for the human characters [10]. *Game Rant* criticized the central twist, arguing that revealing the monsters as fakes (initially) detached the episode from the sci-fi genre and made the narrative feel "muddled," although they acknowledged the effectiveness of the horror elements in the first half [21].

The social commentary was a point of discussion. *Engadget* praised the episode for getting its "politics right" with "molotov cocktails in hand," viewing it as a sharp critique of reactionary politics and reputation laundering [22]. Conversely, some reviewers felt the message was delivered with a lack of subtlety, with *Kabooooom* stating the script "continually tells the audience things rather than showing them" [12].

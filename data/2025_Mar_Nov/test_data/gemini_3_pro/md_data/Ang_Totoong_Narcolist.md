# Ang Totoong Narcolist

**Ang Totoong Narcolist** (transl. The True Narcolist) is a series of viral videos released in April 2019 that accused members of the family and associates of then-Philippine President Rodrigo Duterte of involvement in the illegal drug trade. The videos featured a hooded narrator known as "Bikoy," who claimed to be a former record-keeper for a drug syndicate. The narrator was later identified as Peter Joemel Advincula.

The controversy surrounding the videos led to a complex series of legal and political events. Advincula initially sought legal aid from the Integrated Bar of the Philippines (IBP) to stand by his allegations, only to surrender to the Philippine National Police (PNP) weeks later. Upon his surrender, Advincula recanted his claims, alleging that the videos were fabricated and orchestrated by the political opposition, specifically the Liberal Party and Senator Antonio Trillanes IV, as part of a plot dubbed "Project Sodoma" to oust President Duterte [1][2][3].

Advincula's retraction resulted in the filing of sedition complaints against Vice President Leni Robredo and other opposition figures. In 2020, the Department of Justice (DOJ) cleared Robredo but indicted Trillanes, Advincula, and nine others for conspiracy to commit sedition [4]. In May 2023, a Manila court convicted Advincula of perjury for making false statements regarding the alleged involvement of human rights lawyers in the plot [5]. Subsequently, in September 2023, a Quezon City court acquitted Trillanes, Advincula, and their co-accused of the conspiracy to commit sedition charges due to insufficiency of evidence [6].

## Content and Allegations

The video series, titled *Ang Totoong Narcolist*, was uploaded to YouTube and shared across various social media platforms beginning April 2, 2019 [1]. The videos were narrated by a hooded figure who identified himself as "Bikoy." He claimed to be a former member of a drug syndicate operating in Southern Luzon and the Visayas, specifically tasked with recording financial transactions [7][8].

### Specific Accusations
The videos alleged that the syndicate provided "tara" (grease money) to high-ranking individuals. Key allegations included:
*   **Paolo Duterte:** The President's son was accused of receiving monthly payoffs. Bikoy claimed Paolo Duterte used the code names "POLO DELTA-TSG01" and "ALPHA TIERRA-0029" in the syndicate's records [7][8]. The videos asserted that Paolo Duterte had a dragon tattoo on his back containing the alphanumeric code, which served as proof of his membership in the triad [9][10].
*   **Christopher "Bong" Go:** The President's former special assistant and eventual Senator was accused of being a conduit for drug money [7][11].
*   **Manases Carpio:** The husband of Davao City Mayor Sara Duterte was also implicated in the financial records presented in the videos [7][12].
*   **Veronica Duterte:** The President's minor daughter was mentioned in relation to the alleged transactions, a point that drew specific condemnation from the administration [7][13].

### "Dragon Tattoo" Controversy
A focal point of the allegations was the claim regarding a dragon tattoo on Paolo Duterte's back. Opposition Senator Leila de Lima challenged Paolo Duterte to show his back to disprove the allegations [10]. Paolo Duterte refused to show his back, citing his right to privacy and stating he would not dignify the claims of his "enemies" [9][11].

## Investigation and "Bikoy" Identification

Following the release of the videos, the Philippine government launched a manhunt for the producers and the narrator.

### Arrest of Rodel Jayme
On April 30, 2019, the National Bureau of Investigation (NBI) arrested Rodel Jayme, the administrator of the website *metrobalita.net*, which had shared the videos [1][14]. Jayme admitted to creating the website but denied uploading the videos or knowing the identity of "Bikoy." He claimed he was hired to set up the site for a fee [15][16]. In May 2019, the DOJ indicted Jayme for inciting to sedition under the Cybercrime Prevention Act [13][17].

### Surfacing of Peter Joemel Advincula
On May 6, 2019, Peter Joemel Advincula appeared at the headquarters of the Integrated Bar of the Philippines (IBP) in Pasig City. He identified himself as "Bikoy" and requested legal assistance to file cases against the individuals he named in the videos [1][2]. At this time, Advincula stood by the content of the *Ang Totoong Narcolist* videos, claiming his life was in danger and that he wanted to clear his conscience [2][18]. The IBP declined his request for legal aid [4].

## Retraction and "Project Sodoma"

On May 23, 2019, weeks after the midterm elections, Advincula surrendered to the PNP and was presented in a press conference. He completely recanted his previous statements, claiming the *Ang Totoong Narcolist* videos were fabricated [2][19].

Advincula alleged that the videos were part of "Project Sodoma," a plot orchestrated by the Liberal Party and Senator Antonio Trillanes IV to destabilize the government, oust President Duterte, and install Vice President Leni Robredo as President [2][3]. He claimed the videos were scripted and that he was paid to act as the narrator [3][18]. He implicated various opposition figures, including Senators Risa Hontiveros and Leila de Lima, as well as human rights lawyers and Catholic clergy, in the alleged conspiracy [2][20].

The opposition figures and lawyers named by Advincula categorically denied the allegations, calling them a "harassment suit" and questioning Advincula's credibility given his history of fraud convictions and shifting narratives [5][19][21].

## Related Events

### Oust Duterte Matrix
Shortly after the videos went viral, but before Advincula's retraction, Malacañang released an "Oust Duterte" matrix on April 22, 2019. The diagram linked various journalists, media organizations (such as Rappler and the Philippine Center for Investigative Journalism), and lawyers to an alleged plot to overthrow the President [22][23]. The release of the matrix was criticized by the named groups as dangerous and baseless [22].

## Legal Proceedings

The controversy resulted in multiple legal battles involving the administration, the opposition, and Advincula himself.

### Sedition Complaint (2019)
Based on Advincula's sworn statement regarding "Project Sodoma," the PNP-Criminal Investigation and Detection Group (CIDG) filed a complaint in July 2019 against Vice President Robredo, Senator Trillanes, and over 30 others. The charges included sedition, inciting to sedition, cyber libel, and obstruction of justice [20][24].

### DOJ Resolution (2020)
In February 2020, the Department of Justice released its resolution regarding the PNP-CIDG complaint.
*   **Cleared:** The DOJ dismissed the sedition charges against Vice President Leni Robredo, the Catholic bishops, and several other opposition figures due to lack of evidence [4][25].
*   **Indicted:** The DOJ found probable cause to charge Antonio Trillanes IV, Peter Joemel Advincula, and nine others with "conspiracy to commit sedition" [4][26]. The other indicted individuals included Joel Saracho, Boom Enriquez, Yolanda Ong, Vicente Romano III, Fr. Flaviano Villanueva, Fr. Albert Alejo, Jonnel Sangalang, and Eduardo Acierto [25].

### Perjury Conviction of Advincula (2023)
Lawyers from the Free Legal Assistance Group (FLAG)—Jose Manuel Diokno, Lorenzo Tañada III, and Theodore Te—filed a perjury case against Advincula. Advincula had claimed in his affidavit that he met with these lawyers at Ateneo de Manila University on March 4, 2019, to plan "Project Sodoma" [27].

On May 24, 2023, the Manila Metropolitan Trial Court Branch 17 found Advincula guilty beyond reasonable doubt of perjury. The court ruled that Advincula made a "willful and corrupt assertion of a falsehood," as evidence proved the lawyers were not present at the alleged meeting or that the meeting did not occur as described [5][28]. Advincula was sentenced to an indeterminate penalty of three months and one day to one year and one day of imprisonment [27].

### Acquittal in Sedition Case (2023)
The conspiracy to commit sedition case against Trillanes, Advincula, and their co-accused was heard by the Quezon City Metropolitan Trial Court. On September 6, 2023, the court granted the demurrer to evidence filed by the defense, effectively acquitting all accused [6].

The court ruled that the prosecution failed to present sufficient evidence to prove the existence of a conspiracy. Crucially, the prosecution did not present Advincula as a witness to testify on the veracity of his sworn statement. The judge noted that without Advincula's testimony, his affidavit was hearsay and the "essential witness with personal knowledge of the entire incident was demonstrably not presented" [6].

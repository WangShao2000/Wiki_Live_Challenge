# Voyage to the Blue Planet

**Voyage to the Blue Planet** was a concert tour by the American rock band Weezer. Launched in September 2024, the tour commemorated the 30th anniversary of the band's self-titled debut studio album, commonly referred to as the "Blue Album" (1994). The tour was produced by Live Nation and featured support from the alternative rock bands The Flaming Lips and Dinosaur Jr. [1][2].

The concert series was distinguished by a theatrical, sci-fi narrative production in which the band members portrayed astronauts traveling through space to save a dying planet. The setlist was structured to support this narrative, beginning with career-spanning hits, transitioning through a section dedicated to the band's second album, *Pinkerton* (1996), and concluding with a front-to-back performance of the Blue Album [3][4]. The tour received generally positive reviews from critics, who noted the high production values and the enthusiastic audience response to the band's early material [3][5].

## Background and development

Weezer's debut album, *Weezer* (the Blue Album), was released in May 1994. In December 2023, lead vocalist Rivers Cuomo indicated in interviews that the band intended to celebrate the album's 30th anniversary with a "deluxe package" and an "epic tour" [6].

The tour was officially announced on March 11, 2024. The announcement confirmed that the band would play the Blue Album in its entirety, along with other "fan favorites" and "rarities" [2][6]. The tour was scheduled to visit arenas across North America, beginning on September 4, 2024, at the Xcel Energy Center in Saint Paul, Minnesota, and concluding on October 11, 2024, at the Intuit Dome in Inglewood, California [1].

Due to high demand, additional shows were added to the itinerary, including a second night at The Anthem in Washington, D.C., and a final performance at the Acrisure Arena in Palm Desert, California [1][7].

## Concert synopsis

Unlike a traditional rock concert, the *Voyage to the Blue Planet* show was staged as a space opera with a specific plot and scripted segments. The stage design featured a large spacecraft prop named "Betsy 2.0," which lifted off at the start of the show to reveal the band members dressed in blue astronaut flight suits [4][8].

### Narrative arc
The show's storyline followed the band on a mission to save the "Blue Planet," which was described as "dying" and in need of the Blue Album to be restored [4][8]. The performance was divided into three distinct acts:

1.  **Lift Off:** The show began with a countdown and a launch sequence. The band performed various hits from their discography, such as "Hash Pipe," "Beverly Hills," and "Island in the Sun," while a large video screen depicted their travel through space [3][4].
2.  **The Pinkerton Asteroid Belt:** The narrative involved the ship traversing a hazardous region of space dubbed the "Pinkerton Asteroid Belt." During this segment, the band performed songs from their 1996 album *Pinkerton*, including "Getchoo," "Why Bother?," and "Pink Triangle" [3][4]. The ship was attacked by an alien antagonist named "Bokkus," a character originally drawn by drummer Pat Wilson in 1992 during the band's early formation [9].
3.  **Arrival:** Upon "landing" on the Blue Planet, Cuomo planted a flag bearing the Weezer logo into the stage. The band then performed the Blue Album in its entirety, from "My Name Is Jonas" to "Only in Dreams" [3][4].

### Production design
The production utilized extensive lighting, video content, and physical props to convey the sci-fi theme. The lighting design included galactic set pieces and automated pods that moved above the stage [8]. During the finale of "Only in Dreams," confetti cannons were deployed, and the stage lighting turned predominantly blue to match the album's artwork [3][8].

## Reception

The tour received generally positive reviews from music critics. *The Santa Barbara Independent* described the show as a "celebration together of the music that we've explored inside ourselves," highlighting the "raucous, joyous energy" of the audience during the Blue Album performance [3]. *The Mac Weekly* praised the band for balancing their "dorky reputation" with "critically acclaimed widespread appeal," noting that despite the lack of physical movement from the band members themselves, the production managed to "unite thousands of people" [5].

Reviewers often commented on the elaborate stage production. *Ultimate Classic Rock* noted that the "sci-fi-themed tour name wasn't just for show," describing the spacecraft and astronaut costumes as central to the experience [4]. *Inverse* highlighted the inclusion of the deep-cut character "Bokkus" as a nod to the band's history, calling the alien the "ultimate Weezer inside joke" [9].

Some criticism was directed at the scripted nature of the performance. A reviewer for *Ships of Hagoth* noted that while the opening acts were excellent, the "performatively-awkward" stage banter and the inclusion of certain songs like "Across the Sea" were points of contention [10]. However, the same review acknowledged that the Blue Album material was performed with high quality.

Following the conclusion of the North American leg, the band stated on social media that it had been their "best tour ever" [7].

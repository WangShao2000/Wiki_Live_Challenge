# Kingston Line

The **Kingston Line** is a commuter rail line operated by the Massachusetts Bay Transportation Authority (MBTA) in eastern Massachusetts. It runs from South Station in Boston to Kingston, serving the South Shore region. The line is part of the MBTA Commuter Rail system and is operated by Keolis Commuter Services [1][2].

Originally known as the **Plymouth/Kingston Line**, the service was restored in 1997 as part of the Old Colony Lines project, which reactivated passenger rail in the region for the first time since 1959 [1][3]. The line formerly served a second terminus at Plymouth station; however, that station was indefinitely closed in April 2021 due to operational constraints and low ridership, leading to the line's renaming [1][4].

## History

### Old Colony Railroad and Closure
The route of the Kingston Line follows the alignment of the original Old Colony Railroad, which opened between Boston and Plymouth in 1845 [1]. This corridor became a primary rail artery for southeastern Massachusetts. Passenger service on the Old Colony Division continued through the mid-20th century but began to decline with the rise of automobile travel. The New York, New Haven and Hartford Railroad, which had acquired the line, ceased all passenger service on the Old Colony Division in 1959 [1][3].

### Restoration (1997)
In the 1980s and 1990s, the Commonwealth of Massachusetts undertook the Old Colony Railroad Rehabilitation Project to restore commuter rail service to the South Shore. Construction began in 1993 [1]. Service was officially restored on September 29, 1997, operating as the Plymouth/Kingston Line [1][3]. This restoration occurred simultaneously with the reopening of the Middleborough/Lakeville Line. The project involved upgrading tracks, signals, and constructing new stations to modern accessibility standards [3].

### Plymouth Station Closure and Renaming
For the first 24 years of restored service, the line utilized a fork at its southern end. Trains would split after Halifax, with some proceeding to Kingston and others to Plymouth station at Cordage Park. This configuration presented operational challenges, as trains serving Plymouth often had to perform reverse moves or sequential stops (Kingston then Plymouth), which increased travel times [4].

In April 2021, amid service adjustments related to the COVID-19 pandemic and a review of low-ridership stations, the MBTA indefinitely closed Plymouth station [4]. At the time of closure, ridership at Plymouth was significantly lower than at Kingston, which offers direct access to Massachusetts Route 3 and a large parking garage [5][6]. Following the closure, the line was officially renamed the Kingston Line [1].

## Route and Stations

The Kingston Line runs 35.1 miles (56.5 km) from South Station to Kingston [1]. The northern section of the line shares trackage with the Middleborough/Lakeville Line (renamed the Fall River/New Bedford Line in 2025) and the Greenbush Line as far south as Braintree [1][7].

From South Station, trains run south along the Southeast Expressway (Interstate 93), stopping at JFK/UMass and Quincy Center. After Braintree, the Kingston Line splits from the main line used by the Fall River/New Bedford Line and heads southeast through Weymouth, Abington, Whitman, Hanson, and Halifax [1][8]. The line terminates at Kingston station, located near Route 3 [9].

## Operations

### Passenger Service
As of late 2025, the Kingston Line operates seven days a week. Weekday schedules typically feature peak-direction service during rush hours and bi-directional service during the midday and evening. Trains operate in a push-pull configuration using diesel locomotives [1]. The trip from South Station to Kingston takes approximately one hour [10].

### Ridership
In October 2022, the line reported 4,171 average weekday boardings [1]. By late 2025, the MBTA Commuter Rail system as a whole had recovered a significant portion of its pre-pandemic ridership, with over 100,000 daily weekday boardings system-wide [2].

### Freight Service
Freight operations on the line are managed by the **Massachusetts Coastal Railroad** (Mass Coastal). Historically, freight rights were held by the Bay Colony Railroad. In late 2023, Mass Coastal acquired the assets and freight rights of the Bay Colony Railroad, including the Kingston Line [11][12][13]. Freight trains interchange with CSX Transportation at Braintree and serve customers along the line [14].

## Infrastructure

### Kingston Wind Turbine
In 2012, a wind turbine was installed at the Kingston layover facility, located adjacent to the Kingston station. The project, funded by a $2.5 million federal grant, was designed to generate electricity for the station lights and the layover facility, where trains are stored overnight [15][16]. The turbine was projected to cover approximately 65% of the station's energy needs [17][18].

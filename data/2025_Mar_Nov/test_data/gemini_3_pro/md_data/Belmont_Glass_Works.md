# Belmont Glass Works

**Belmont Glass Works**, originally incorporated as the **Belmont Glass Company**, was an American glass manufacturer located in Bellaire, Ohio. Operating from 1866 to 1890, it was the first glass factory established in Bellaire, a town that subsequently earned the nickname "Glass City" due to the proliferation of the industry in the region [1][2]. The company produced a variety of glass products, including lamp chimneys, bar goods, and pressed tableware, and is noted for manufacturing the "Daisy and Button" pattern [3]. The factory closed in 1890 amid economic difficulties and the migration of the glass industry to the natural gas fields of Northwest Ohio [1][2].

## History

### Origins and Establishment
The company was organized in August 1865 under the name **Barnes, Faupel & Company** [2]. It was established as a cooperative by a group of glassmakers, many of whom had gained experience at the J. H. Hobbs, Brockunier and Company glassworks in nearby Wheeling, West Virginia [1][2]. The firm began production in January 1866 [2].

The factory was located in Bellaire, Ohio, situated in Belmont County. The site was selected due to several logistical and resource advantages. Bellaire sat at the intersection of the Ohio River, the National Road, and two major railroads, providing a robust transportation infrastructure for shipping products to markets in Philadelphia, Pittsburgh, and Cincinnati [1][2]. Additionally, the location provided access to the Pittsburgh coal seam, ensuring a steady fuel supply for the furnaces [2].

### Operations and Growth
Although the operating firm was originally Barnes, Faupel & Company, the plant itself was referred to as the Belmont Glass Works [2]. The company was later incorporated as the **Belmont Glass Company** [4]. It maintained this name until approximately 1888, when it became formally known as the Belmont Glass Works until its closure [4].

By 1870, the company had established its own foundry and mold-making shop, allowing it to produce molds for its own use as well as for other manufacturers, including McKee, the Crystal Glass Company, and the Fostoria Glass Company [3][5]. The facility expanded its capacity over time; by 1880, Belmont County ranked sixth among the nation's counties in the value of glass produced, a statistic driven significantly by the activity in Bellaire [1].

In 1884, the factory employed approximately 350 workers, a workforce that included men, women, and minors [2]. By 1887, the company's leadership included M. Sheets as president, H. M. Kelley as secretary, and Harvey Leighton as the factory manager [6].

### Decline and Closure
The late 1880s presented significant challenges for the company. The United States economy experienced multiple recessions between 1882 and 1891, which placed financial strain on manufacturers [2]. Concurrently, the discovery of natural gas in Northwest Ohio led to a "gas boom" in that region. Local municipalities in Northwest Ohio offered incentives such as free land and low-cost fuel to lure energy-intensive industries [1]. This caused a migration of glass manufacturers and skilled labor away from the Ohio River valley [2].

Facing these economic pressures and a lack of profits, the shareholders of Belmont Glass Works voted to cease operations in June 1890 [2]. The company was liquidated in July 1890 [3]. Following the closure, the company's molds were sold to the Crystal Glass Company of Bridgeport, Ohio, and the Central Glass Company [3][5]. The factory buildings were demolished in 1893, and the site was subsequently occupied by the Novelty Stamping Company [2][3].

## Products and Patterns

Belmont Glass Works began production manufacturing lamp chimneys, which are the glass cylinders surrounding the wick of a lantern [2][3]. The company soon diversified into blown glassware and eventually focused heavily on pressed glass tableware, bar goods, and lamps [2][3]. In 1885, the company introduced colored glass into its product lines [3].

### Notable Patterns
Collectors and historians of Early American Pattern Glass (EAPG) have identified several specific patterns produced by Belmont Glass Works:

*   **Pattern No. 100 (Daisy and Button):** This design is considered one of the company's most prominent products. It is a variation of the popular "Daisy and Button" motif, distinguished by its ornate detail [3]. Belmont produced this pattern in crystal as well as colors, including Canary yellow (often referred to as Vaseline glass) [3].
*   **Royal (also known as Royal Lady):** Introduced circa 1881, this pattern features a band with scrolls and cameos, often depicting a princess-like figure [2][7]. Tableware in this line typically featured ball feet and ball finials [2][7]. A variant of this pattern using a skirted base instead of ball feet was named **Venus** [7].
*   **Pattern No. 3 (Dart):** Introduced around 1881, this pattern is characterized by diamond and spear (or dart) motifs [8].
*   **Zipper:** The company produced items, particularly condiment sets and salt shakers, featuring this intricate pattern [2][9].
*   **Dewberry:** This design was patented by Stephen Hipkins Jr. on April 12, 1870. The pattern features ornamentation designed to resemble a dewberry vine, including leaves and fruit [2].
*   **Bell Buoy (Pattern No. 4):** This pattern was used for items such as salt shakers [10].

## Legacy

Belmont Glass Works holds historical significance as the first glass factory in Bellaire, Ohio [1][2]. Its success helped attract other glass manufacturers to the town, eventually leading to Bellaire's designation as "Glass City" during the period of 1870 to 1885 [1].

The company also served as an incubator for talent within the American glass industry. Several key figures and employees left Belmont to establish or manage other prominent glass companies.
*   **Bellaire Goblet Company:** Organized in 1876 by former Belmont employees and officers, including Charles Henry Over, John Robinson, Henry Carr, and William A. Gorby [1].
*   **Fostoria Glass Company:** Henry and Jacob Crimmel, who were involved with Belmont Glass Works, moved to Fostoria, Ohio, in 1887 to assist in the startup of the Fostoria Glass Company, utilizing their expertise and glass recipes [2].

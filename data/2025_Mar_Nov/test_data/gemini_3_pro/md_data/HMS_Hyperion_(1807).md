# HMS Hyperion (1807)

**HMS *Hyperion*** was a 32-gun fifth-rate frigate of the Royal Navy. Built by the private shipbuilder William Gibson at Kingston upon Hull and launched in 1807, she served during the Napoleonic Wars and the subsequent era of colonial independence in South America. The ship was based on the design of the French frigate *Magicienne*.

*Hyperion* saw active service across several stations, including the Mediterranean, the West Indies, and North America. Under the command of Captain Thomas Charles Brodie, the ship was involved in a diplomatic incident in Haiti in 1811. Later, under Captain William Pryce Cumby, she was engaged in fishery protection and convoy duties, notably capturing the American privateer brig *Rattlesnake* in the Bay of Biscay in 1814. Following the Napoleonic Wars, *Hyperion* was deployed to South America where her captain, Thomas Searle, became involved in tensions with Lord Cochrane during the Chilean War of Independence. Her final years were spent as a depot ship for the Coast Blockade service at Newhaven before she was broken up in 1833.

## Design and Construction

*Hyperion* was ordered on 13 June 1805 and was designed by the Surveyor of the Navy, Sir John Henslow. The design was a copy of the French frigate *Magicienne*, which the Royal Navy had captured in 1781 [1] [1]. She was classified as a 32-gun fifth-rate frigate, a type of vessel smaller and faster than a ship of the line, intended for reconnaissance, commerce raiding, and convoy protection [1].

The ship was built by the private shipbuilder William Gibson at his yard in Kingston upon Hull. *Hyperion* was the largest ship constructed by Gibson [1] [1]. She was laid down in February 1806 and launched on 3 November 1807 [1] [1]. Following her launch, she was fitted out at Chatham Dockyard between 9 December 1807 and 23 April 1808 [1]. The total cost of her construction and fitting out was £20,165 [1] [1].

The frigate had a length of 143 feet 9 inches (43.8 m) along the gun deck and 118 feet 7+5⁄8 inches (36.2 m) at the keel. Her beam was 39 feet 4+1⁄2 inches (12.0 m), and she had a depth in the hold of 12 feet 4 inches (3.8 m) [1] [1]. Her tonnage was calculated at 978 34⁄94 tons burthen [1]. The ship had a draught of 10 feet (3.0 m) forward and 13 feet 6 inches (4.1 m) aft [1].

*Hyperion* carried a main battery of twenty-six 18-pounder guns on her upper deck. Her quarterdeck armament consisted of two 9-pounder guns and ten 24-pounder carronades, while her forecastle carried four 9-pounder guns and two 24-pounder carronades [1] [1]. She had a complement of 254 officers and men [1].

## Service History

### Napoleonic Wars

#### Mediterranean and West Indies
*Hyperion* was commissioned in January 1808 under the command of Captain Thomas Charles Brodie [2] [2]. She sailed for the Mediterranean on 30 June 1808 [2] [2]. After serving in that theater for two years, the frigate transferred to the Jamaica Station in 1810 [1] [1].

In early 1811, *Hyperion* was involved in a violent diplomatic incident at Gonaïves, Haiti. The ship had docked to replenish wood and water when an English merchant named Simpson, who had been detained by Haitian authorities for an alleged breach of blockade, appealed to Captain Brodie for protection [2] [2]. Brodie granted this protection, but shortly after Simpson boarded the ship, shore batteries opened fire on *Hyperion*'s boats without warning. Three crew members were killed, and Captain Brodie, along with a master's mate named Dillon and a marine officer named George Pattoun, were taken prisoner by Haitian forces [2] [2].

The following morning, *Hyperion*'s First Lieutenant, James Morgan, navigated the frigate through a difficult channel, anchoring within musket shot of the shore in only four fathoms of water. He positioned the ship to bring her broadside to bear on the shore batteries and a Haitian frigate. Lieutenant George Bissett was sent ashore with an ultimatum: if the captain and his officers were not released within 15 minutes, the town would be destroyed and the Haitian warship seized. The Haitian authorities complied, and the officers were returned to the ship [2] [2]. Captain Brodie died shortly thereafter on 14 March 1811 in Jamaica, reportedly of an illness contracted during these events [1] [3].

#### North America and European Waters
Following Brodie's death, Captain William Pryce Cumby took command in September 1811 [2]. In October 1811, *Hyperion* sailed for Veracruz and Havana before returning to England [2] [2]. After a refit at Portsmouth, she spent the summer of 1812 patrolling the Davis Strait to protect the whale fishery [2] [2]. Later that year, she escorted a convoy from Newfoundland to Barbados. Upon returning to St. John's, Newfoundland, the ship was frozen into the harbor; however, the crew reportedly suffered little from the cold as Captain Cumby had ensured they purchased warm clothing with their arrears of pay while at Portsmouth [2] [2].

In November 1813, *Hyperion* departed Newfoundland escorting a convoy of 37 ships bound for Portugal. The convoy encountered severe storms in the Atlantic. By 25 November, only 16 ships remained with the frigate. Cumby attempted to lead a portion of the convoy to Oporto and then Corunna, but further storms dispersed the remaining vessels. *Hyperion* suffered damage to her upper works and pumps, eventually diverting to Portsmouth [1].

In 1814, *Hyperion* patrolled the English Channel and the Bay of Biscay [2] [2]. On 3 June 1814, while in the Bay of Biscay, she sighted the American privateer brig *Rattlesnake*. commanded by David Moffatt. The *Rattlesnake* was a fast-sailing vessel armed with 16 guns that had already captured numerous British merchant vessels [2] [4] [2]. After a chase lasting 48 hours, *Hyperion* captured the privateer [5] [5] [6] [4].

Captain James Lillicrap took temporary command in January 1815, escorting a convoy from Oporto, Portugal, to England. With the conclusion of the Napoleonic Wars, *Hyperion* was paid off at Portsmouth on 31 August 1815 [2] [2].

### South America Station
The ship was recommissioned in 1818 under Captain Thomas Searle and assigned to the South America Station during the turbulent period of the Chilean War of Independence [1] [2] [2]. *Hyperion* arrived at Rio de Janeiro in October 1819 and Valparaiso in August 1820 [7].

During this deployment, *Hyperion* was present at the port of Callao, Peru, in November 1820. The port was being blockaded by the Chilean Navy under the command of Lord Cochrane (Thomas Cochrane, 10th Earl of Dundonald). On 5 November, Cochrane launched a daring night attack to cut out the Spanish frigate *Esmeralda* from under the guns of the Callao fortress [1] [8]. *Hyperion* and the American frigate USS *Macedonian* were anchored nearby as neutrals.

Relations between Captain Searle and Lord Cochrane were strained. When Cochrane's forces approached, *Hyperion* hailed the boats, potentially alerting the Spanish defenders [8] [9]. During the engagement, Cochrane utilized a ruse de guerre by hoisting lights on the captured *Esmeralda* identical to those displayed by the neutral *Hyperion* and *Macedonian*, causing the Spanish fortress to hold its fire or fire inaccurately to avoid hitting neutral shipping [1]. Searle's hostility toward Cochrane was such that he reportedly "sternly repressed" any signs of admiration from his crew regarding Cochrane's feat, in contrast to the American officers on *Macedonian* who privately wished Cochrane success [8] [9]. Due to the friction between Searle and Cochrane, *Hyperion* was ordered back to Britain [1] [1].

### Later Service and Fate
Captain James Lillicrap resumed command in 1821. *Hyperion* sailed for the Cape of Good Hope in September 1821, carrying Lord Charles Somerset, the Governor of the Cape, as a passenger [2] [7] [2]. While at the Cape in June 1822, *Hyperion* rescued the East Indiaman *Albion*, which had broken its moorings in Simon's Bay during a gale while carrying treasure and passengers. Lillicrap and his crew received financial rewards for the rescue [2] [2].

The ship subsequently operated in the West Indies, combating piracy off Cuba [2] [2]. She returned to Britain in 1824 [1].

In 1825, *Hyperion* was fitted out at Sheerness for service with the Coast Blockade, a naval force tasked with preventing smuggling [1] [1]. She was stationed at Newhaven, Sussex, under the command of Captain William Mingaye [1] [7]. During this period, the ship served as a floating base and depot; the majority of her crew were deployed ashore in Martello towers and along the coast to patrol for smugglers [1]. *Hyperion* remained at Newhaven until the Coast Blockade was disbanded and absorbed into the Coastguard Service. She was towed to Portsmouth in May 1831 and paid off [7] [7].

*Hyperion* was broken up at Portsmouth in June 1833 [1] [1] [7] [2].

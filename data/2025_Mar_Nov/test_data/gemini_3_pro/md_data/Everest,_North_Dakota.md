# Everest, North Dakota

**Everest** is an unincorporated community and ghost town in Cass County, North Dakota, United States [1]. Established in 1881 as a station on the Great Northern Railway, the community experienced a brief period of growth during the late 19th century before declining following the removal of the rail line in 1896 [1][2]. Everest is best known as the birthplace of William Langer, who served as the 17th and 21st Governor of North Dakota and as a United States Senator [3][4].

## History

### Establishment and Name
Everest was platted in 1881 in the northeast quarter of section 15-139-52 of Everest Township [1]. It was established as a station on the Great Northern Railroad, situated approximately two miles south of Casselton [1]. A post office was established in the community on May 24, 1882, with Carl Knuppel serving as the first postmaster [1][2].

There is a noted discrepancy regarding the origin of the community's name. While it is commonly believed to be named "Everest," historical sources indicate it was intended to be named after Charles A. Everitt, the editor of *The Lisbon Star* [1][2]. This naming error has persisted in historical records [2].

### Growth and Decline
During the 1880s and early 1890s, Everest functioned as a local agricultural and transit hub. By 1890, the community reported a population of 175 residents [1]. Archaeological investigations at the site have identified evidence of a business district that included a hotel, saloons, a boarding house, and a blacksmith shop [5][6].

The community's prosperity was closely tied to the railroad. However, the Great Northern Railroad revised its long-range plans for the region, leading to the removal of the station in 1895 [1]. The tracks themselves were taken up in 1896, severing the town's primary commercial artery [1][2].

The community also suffered from structural fires common to the era. Archaeological surveys suggest a significant fire occurred in 1886, which damaged portions of the town [5][6].

Following the removal of the rail infrastructure, the population dwindled. The post office closed on August 13, 1904, briefly reopened on September 17, 1904, and closed permanently on November 30, 1908 [1][2]. Mail service was subsequently routed to Casselton [1]. Despite the loss of the railroad and post office, a small village presence persisted into the mid-20th century, with a reported population of 10 residents as late as 1960 [1][2].

### Contemporary Status
Today, Everest is considered a ghost town [5]. The site is located in a rural area dominated by agriculture. Recent archaeological excavations in the area have uncovered artifacts from the late 19th century, including bottles from the Dakota Territory era, offering physical evidence of the town's former commercial activity [5][7].

## Geography

Everest is located in Everest Township in central Cass County. It is situated approximately two miles south of the city of Casselton and roughly 20 miles west of Fargo [1]. The former townsite is located in the northeast quarter of section 15, township 139 north, range 52 west [1][2].

The surrounding area consists of the flat, fertile plains of the Red River Valley, which is conducive to the region's agricultural economy. The Durbin-Everest Channel, a water management feature, is located in the vicinity [8].

## Demographics

Historical records indicate that Everest's population peaked in the late 19th century.
*   **1890:** 175 [1]
*   **1960:** 10 [1]

As an unincorporated community, Everest does not have its own census data separate from the township. Everest Township had a population of 99 at the 2020 census [9].

## Notable People

*   **William Langer** (1886–1959): A prominent North Dakota politician who served as the state's Attorney General, Governor (1932–1934, 1937–1939), and U.S. Senator (1941–1959). Langer was born in Everest, Dakota Territory, on September 30, 1886 [3][4][10].
*   **Frank J. Langer**: Father of William Langer, Frank Langer was a member of the first North Dakota state legislature and a resident of the Everest area [10].

# Fontaine (Genshin Impact)

**Fontaine** is a fictional region in the open-world action role-playing game *Genshin Impact*, developed by HoYoverse. Released on August 16, 2023, as part of the version 4.0 update, it is the fifth of the seven major nations of Teyvat to be introduced in the game [1][2]. Associated with the element of Hydro (water) and the ideal of "Justice," the region is ruled by the Hydro Archon, Focalors, and governed by a complex legal system centered around the Opera Epiclese [2][3].

The region is culturally and visually inspired by France and the United Kingdom during the Belle Époque and the Industrial Revolution, featuring steampunk technology and Baroque architecture [4][5]. Fontaine introduced significant gameplay expansions, most notably a dedicated underwater exploration system that allows players to traverse submerged landscapes without oxygen limitations [1]. The region serves as the setting for Chapter IV of the game's main storyline, the Archon Quests, which focuses on a prophecy predicting the dissolution of the nation's people into the sea [6].

## Gameplay

### Underwater Exploration
Fontaine introduced the first extensive underwater gameplay mechanics in *Genshin Impact*. Approximately 50% of the region's initial map consists of underwater areas [1]. Unlike traditional underwater levels in video games that often utilize an oxygen meter, the developers implemented a system where players can swim indefinitely without the risk of drowning [1]. A specialized "Aquatic Stamina" bar is used for sprinting (swimming faster), which replenishes automatically when the character stops sprinting or interacts with "Recovery Orbs" found in the environment [7][8].

Combat and interaction underwater differ from land-based gameplay. Characters cannot use their standard elemental skills or bursts; instead, they utilize "Transoceanic Sourcewater" to absorb abilities from Xenochromatic Fontemer Aberrants—local aquatic creatures [8][9]. These abilities include:
*   **Xenochromatic Armored Crab:** Provides a shield and the ability to unleash shockwaves to destroy rocks or block vents [10][11].
*   **Xenochromatic Hunter’s Ray:** Allows players to fire sharp water blades to cut seagrass or damage enemies [10][11].
*   **Xenochromatic Blubberbeast:** Emits sonar waves for detection and area-of-effect damage [10][11].

To facilitate movement, players can utilize "Currents" for rapid travel and perform a "dolphin jump" to breach the water's surface when ascending quickly [8][10].

### Arkhe System
Exclusive to the Fontaine region and its characters is the **Arkhe** system, a combat and puzzle-solving mechanic based on two opposing energies: **Pneuma** (light/gold alignment) and **Ousia** (dark/purple alignment) [12][13]. Every playable character from Fontaine, as well as the Hydro Traveler, possesses an innate alignment to one of these energies [14].

When Pneuma energy meets Ousia energy, an "Annihilation" reaction occurs [13]. This reaction is used to disable specific enemy defenses, particularly those of the "Clockwork Meka" automatons, and to interact with environmental puzzles such as Pneumosia Blocks and Relays [12][15]. The system is designed to function alongside the game's existing elemental reaction system rather than replacing it [14].

## Setting and Design

### Geography
Fontaine is located northeast of the Sumeru desert [2]. The region is characterized by a dichotomy between its terrestrial and aquatic environments. The **Court of Fontaine**, the capital city, is a raised urban center featuring advanced infrastructure, while vast underwater sections contain caverns, marine life, and sunken ruins [1].

Notable locations include:
*   **The Opera Epiclese:** A combined opera house and courthouse where trials and performances take place [3][6].
*   **Fortress of Meropide:** An underwater prison and industrial zone that functions as an autonomous community for exiles [16][17].
*   **Petrichor and the Sea of Bygone Eras:** Areas added in later updates (Version 4.6) that expand on the lore of the previous civilization, Remuria [18].

### Cultural References
The aesthetic of Fontaine is heavily influenced by European culture, specifically France and Great Britain during the late 19th and early 20th centuries [4]. The name "Fontaine" is derived from the French word for "fountain" [4]. The architecture blends Art Deco and Baroque styles with steampunk elements, such as clockwork mechanisms and gears, reflecting the region's status as a hub of technology and arts [4][5].

The region's technology is powered by the Arkhe system, which serves as a renewable energy source for the city's lighting, transportation (such as the Aquabus), and the robotic "Clockwork Meka" that patrol the area [4][12].

## Plot

The storyline in Fontaine, detailed in Archon Quest Chapter IV, revolves around a prophecy stating that "all the people of Fontaine will be dissolved into the water, and only the Hydro Archon will remain, weeping on her throne" [6].

### Synopsis
The Traveler and Paimon arrive in Fontaine and are immediately introduced to the nation's theatrical legal system by the Hydro Archon, Furina, and the Chief Justice, Neuvillette [6]. They become involved in a legal case defending the magician siblings Lyney and Lynette, during which they uncover the existence of "Primordial Seawater," a substance capable of dissolving the people of Fontaine [6].

The investigation leads the protagonists to the Fortress of Meropide, where they discover that the Primordial Sea is rising, threatening to fulfill the prophecy [16]. It is revealed that the people of Fontaine are not "true" humans but were created by the previous Hydro Archon, Egeria, using Oceanids and Primordial Seawater, a sin for which Celestia cursed the nation [19].

In the climax, the "All-Devouring Narwhal," an abyssal monster, threatens to consume the nation [19]. To save the people, Focalors (the divinity of the Hydro Archon) executes a plan to deceive the Heavenly Principles. She separates her divinity from her human spirit, Furina, who plays the role of the Archon for 500 years to maintain appearances [19]. Focalors sacrifices herself to destroy the "Hydro Throne," thereby returning the elemental authority of Hydro to Neuvillette, the reincarnated Hydro Dragon Sovereign [19][20]. Neuvillette then uses this full authority to forgive the "sin" of the Fontaine people, turning them into true humans and preventing their dissolution, although the flood still occurs temporarily [19].

## Characters

Fontaine introduced several playable characters to *Genshin Impact*. The first set of characters released in Version 4.0 included the siblings **Lyney** (Pyro), **Lynette** (Anemo), and **Freminet** (Cryo) [2][21].

Other prominent characters include:
*   **Furina:** The human vessel of the Hydro Archon, known for her dramatic persona [21][22].
*   **Neuvillette:** The Chief Justice of Fontaine and the Hydro Dragon Sovereign [19][21].
*   **Wriothesley:** The administrator of the Fortress of Meropide [16][21].
*   **Navia:** The president of the Spina di Rosula organization [6][21].
*   **Clorinde:** A champion duelist [6][21].
*   **Arlecchino:** A Fatui Harbinger known as "The Knave," who runs the House of the Hearth orphanage [16][18].

## Development and Music

Development of Fontaine began as early as 2021 [4]. The developers utilized soft-body simulation techniques to create realistic underwater currents and aquatic vegetation, aiming to prevent motion sickness and thalassophobia (fear of deep bodies of water) by keeping the underwater environments bright and spacious [1][4].

The original score for Fontaine was produced by **HOYO-MiX** and features orchestral performances by the **London Symphony Orchestra** [23][24]. The music was recorded at Abbey Road Studios and Angel Studios in the United Kingdom [23]. The soundtrack draws inspiration from French impressionist composers such as Maurice Ravel and Camille Saint-Saëns, incorporating instruments like the glass harmonica to evoke the sound of water [4][25].

## Reception

The release of Fontaine received generally positive feedback from critics and players. The underwater exploration mechanics were praised for their fluidity and lack of restrictive oxygen meters, which distinguished the gameplay from typical underwater levels in other games [26][27]. The visual design of the underwater areas was described as "mindblowing" and "beautiful" [26][27].

The storyline, particularly the conclusion in Act V, was highlighted by some reviewers as one of the best narratives in *Genshin Impact* to date, with specific praise directed at the character writing for Furina and Neuvillette [26][28]. However, some criticism was directed at the underwater combat for being overly simplistic compared to the elemental reaction system used on land, with some players feeling it lacked variety [27][29]. Additionally, the heavy reliance on dialogue and exposition in the questlines was noted as a potential drawback for players seeking faster-paced gameplay [29].

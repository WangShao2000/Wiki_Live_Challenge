# Fall River/New Bedford Line

The **Fall River/New Bedford Line** is a commuter rail line operated by the Massachusetts Bay Transportation Authority (MBTA) in southeastern Massachusetts. Running from South Station in Boston to termini in Fall River and New Bedford, the line serves the South Coast region. It was established as part of the South Coast Rail project and officially began passenger service on March 24, 2025 [1], [2].

The line functions as a southern extension of the former Middleborough/Lakeville Line, which was renamed upon the commencement of service to the South Coast [3]. The route utilizes the Middleborough Secondary, the Fall River Secondary, and the New Bedford Main Line to connect the region to the Greater Boston rail network [4]. It is the first time since 1958 that the cities of Fall River and New Bedford have had direct passenger rail connection to Boston [5], [6].

## History

### Early rail service (1845–1958)
Rail service to the South Coast began in the mid-19th century. The Fall River Railroad opened in segments between 1845 and 1846, connecting Fall River to Myricks and eventually linking to the Old Colony Railroad for service to Boston [7]. The New Bedford and Taunton Railroad began operations in 1840 [8]. These lines were eventually consolidated under the Old Colony Railroad and later the New York, New Haven and Hartford Railroad [8].

For over a century, these lines supported both commuter traffic and the "Boat Train," which connected with steamships sailing between Fall River and New York City [7]. However, with the rise of the automobile and the decline of the local textile industry, ridership dwindled in the mid-20th century. The Old Colony Division, which served the region, ceased passenger operations on September 5, 1958, severing the rail link between the South Coast and Boston [5], [9].

### Restoration planning and Phase 1
Efforts to restore rail service to the region began in the 1980s [5]. In 1997, the MBTA restored service as far as Middleborough and Lakeville on the Old Colony Lines, branding it the Middleborough/Lakeville Line [10]. However, the extension to the major South Coast cities faced decades of planning, environmental reviews, and funding debates.

In 2017, the Massachusetts Department of Transportation (MassDOT) split the South Coast Rail project into two phases to expedite service. Phase 1 was designed to provide service via the existing Middleborough/Lakeville line using diesel-powered trains, while Phase 2 proposed a fully electrified route via Stoughton [7], [11].

Construction on Phase 1 began in 2019 [5]. The project involved upgrading 36.1 miles of freight track for passenger use, constructing six new stations, and building two new layover facilities [7]. The project missed initial completion targets of late 2023 and mid-2024 due to construction and testing delays [5], [12].

### Opening
The MBTA officially launched service on the Fall River/New Bedford Line on March 24, 2025 [1]. The opening marked the completion of Phase 1 of the South Coast Rail project. To celebrate the launch, the MBTA offered free fares on the line through the end of April 2025 [3], [13]. Upon the line's opening, the former Middleborough/Lakeville Line was officially renamed the Fall River/New Bedford Line [3].

## Route and stations

The line originates at South Station in Boston and runs southward along the Old Colony Main Line tracks, sharing stops with the Kingston and Greenbush lines as far as Braintree. From Braintree, it follows the alignment of the former Middleborough/Lakeville Line through Brockton and Bridgewater.

South of Bridgewater, the route diverges from the previous alignment. The former Middleborough/Lakeville station was renamed **Lakeville** and removed from the commuter rail schedule; it now serves only the seasonal CapeFLYER [3], [14]. Trains instead proceed to a new **Middleborough** station located at 52 West Grove Street [14].

From Middleborough, the line utilizes the Middleborough Secondary track to reach **East Taunton**. At Cotley Junction, the line splits into two branches [11], [4]:
*   **Fall River Branch:** Diverges onto the Fall River Secondary, serving **Freetown** and terminating at **Fall River Depot**.
*   **New Bedford Branch:** Continues onto the New Bedford Main Line, serving **Church Street** and terminating at **New Bedford**.

## Service and operations

### Schedule
As of the March 2025 launch, the MBTA operates 32 weekday trips between South Station and East Taunton. These split into 15 trips on the Fall River branch and 17 trips on the New Bedford branch [3], [17]. Weekday trains run approximately every 70 minutes. Weekend service consists of 26 trips between South Station and East Taunton, with trains running every two hours [17], [18].

To facilitate connections, the MBTA operates shuttle trains between East Taunton and the branch termini (Fall River and New Bedford) for certain trips, allowing passengers to transfer at East Taunton rather than waiting for a direct train from Boston [1], [13].

### Fares
All stations south of Bridgewater (Middleborough, East Taunton, Freetown, Fall River Depot, Church Street, and New Bedford) are designated as **Zone 8** [3]. As of 2025, the full one-way fare from Zone 8 to South Station is $12.25, with a reduced fare of $6.00 [19], [18].

### Rolling stock
Service on the line is provided by diesel locomotives hauling bi-level coaches, consistent with the non-electrified infrastructure of the Middleborough Secondary and the southern branches [7].

# Muscatine Mall

**Muscatine Mall**, originally known as **Muscatine Plaza**, is an enclosed shopping mall located in Muscatine, Iowa. Opened in 1971, the complex was developed by General Management (later General Growth Properties) and served for decades as a primary retail hub for the region. Throughout its history, the mall has undergone significant changes in tenancy, transitioning from traditional department store anchors such as Montgomery Ward, Woolworth, and JCPenney to a mix of discount retail, furniture stores, and service-oriented facilities.

As of late 2025, the mall's anchor tenants include Slumberland Furniture, Warehouse Bargains, and The Lab, a baseball training facility. The property is owned by Muscatine Mall Management, LLC. In recent years, the site has been the subject of city planning discussions regarding potential redevelopment and mixed-use adaptation following a period of increased vacancy and the departure of national retailers [1][2].

## History

### Development and Opening (1970–1979)
In March 1970, General Management, a Des Moines-based developer that later became General Growth Properties, announced plans to construct a 287,000-square-foot (26,700 m²) enclosed shopping center on Park Avenue in Muscatine [2][3]. The project, initially named Muscatine Plaza, was designed to connect two major anchor stores with an enclosed corridor. Groundbreaking took place on May 17, 1970 [2][3].

The first anchor, Montgomery Ward, opened on November 27, 1970. This 55,000-square-foot (5,100 m²) store replaced a downtown catalog location and included an automotive repair center and a restaurant [2][3]. The second anchor, Woolworth, opened in June 1971 on the north side of the mall. At 72,000 square feet (6,700 m²), it was the chain's largest single-level store at the time [2][3].

The mall officially held its grand opening ceremony on August 5, 1971 [2][3][4]. Early inline tenants included Baskin-Robbins, Kinney Shoes, and a Hy-Vee supermarket [3][4]. In 1975, the mall expanded with the addition of a 48,000-square-foot (4,500 m²) JCPenney store on the east side, bringing the total number of anchors to three [2][3][4]. Following this expansion, the property became widely known as Muscatine Mall.

Changes to the anchor lineup began in the late 1970s. Montgomery Ward closed its location in September 1978, citing the store's small size relative to its full-line locations [2][3]. The space was subsequently remodeled and reopened in August 1979 as Petersen Harned Von Maur (later shortened to Von Maur), a Davenport-based department store [2][3][4]. Around this same period, the mall was sold to Aetna Life Insurance, which retained The Rouse Company to manage the property [2][4].

### 1980s: Anchor Transitions and Walmart
The early 1980s saw significant turnover in the mall's northern anchor space. In 1981, the Woolworth store was converted to the Woolco discount brand. However, the Woolco division proved unprofitable and the store closed in early 1983 [2][4][5].

The vacant Woolco space was leased to Walmart, which opened on July 1, 1983 [2][3]. This location is cited as the first Walmart store to open in the state of Iowa [2][4][6]. Coinciding with Walmart's arrival, the mall underwent renovations that included the subdivision of the former Hy-Vee space—the grocer having moved to a larger peripheral site—into smaller retail units and a new entrance [2][3].

In 1989, The Rouse Company acquired full ownership of the mall from Aetna [2][4]. By 1990, the mall comprised approximately 345,000 square feet (32,100 m²) of retail space and housed roughly 50 stores [2][3].

### 1990s: Departures and Ownership Change
The retail landscape of Muscatine Mall shifted considerably in the late 1990s. In June 1997, Walmart vacated its mall location to relocate to a newly constructed Supercenter nearby [2][4][7]. The following year, in 1998, Von Maur also closed its store at the mall [4][6][8].

To fill the vacancy left by Walmart, the home improvement retailer Menards opened a store in the northern anchor spot in 1998 [4][6][7]. That same year, Rouse sold the mall to Landau & Heyman, a Chicago-based real estate firm [4][5][6].

In an effort to utilize the former Von Maur space, the office supply chain Staples opened a 24,000-square-foot store in a portion of the building in 1999/2000 [4][6][8].

### 2000s–2010s: Decline and Local Ownership
The early 21st century was characterized by high tenant turnover and the gradual loss of national retailers. Staples closed its location in 2002 after a brief tenure [4][6][8]. In November 2003, Elder-Beerman opened a department store occupying the space previously held by Staples and the remainder of the former Von Maur location [2][4][8].

In July 2006, the mall was purchased by Muscatine Mall Management, LLC, a group formed by Quad Cities developers [2][4]. Shortly thereafter, Menards vacated the mall in November 2007 to move to a larger, freestanding location east of town [2][7]. The former Menards space was subsequently filled by Slumberland Furniture in 2010 [2][3][8].

The Elder-Beerman store was rebranded as Younkers in 2008 following a consolidation by parent company The Bon-Ton Stores [2][3][8]. The mall's cinema, Plaza Theatres, which had operated since the mall's early years, closed in May 2013 [2][9].

In January 2014, JCPenney announced it would close its Muscatine Mall location as part of a nationwide restructuring that involved closing 33 underperforming stores. The store closed in May 2014, leaving the mall without one of its longest-tenured anchors [10][11]. The decline of traditional department stores continued when Younkers closed in 2018 as part of the liquidation of The Bon-Ton Stores [3][4][8].

### 2020s–Present: Repurposing and Redevelopment
Following the closure of Younkers and JCPenney, the mall owners sought non-traditional tenants to fill the large vacancies. In March 2019, Compton's Furniture & More opened in the former Younkers space [2][3]. By 2021, this space had transitioned to Warehouse Bargains, a discount furniture and appliance outlet [2][3].

In 2022, the former JCPenney location was repurposed as "The Lab," a training facility for baseball and softball players established by local coaches [2][3][12].

As of 2024 and 2025, the mall retains few inline tenants, with businesses such as Quality Cobbler and Jimmy John's remaining in operation [3][4]. The City of Muscatine has engaged in planning processes to determine the future of the site. In January 2025, during discussions regarding the city's "Comprehensive Plan 2025–2045," residents and officials discussed potential mixed-use redevelopment for the mall property, including the possibility of adding housing [1].

## Architecture and Layout
Muscatine Mall is a single-level enclosed shopping center. The original design featured a central corridor measuring approximately 663 feet (202 m) in length and 36 feet (11 m) in width, connecting the north and south anchor buildings [2][3].

Renovations conducted in the late 1970s and early 1980s introduced brick planters, new lighting, and a mirrored ceiling in the center court [2][5]. Later modifications included the installation of skylights to increase natural lighting within the concourses [3][5]. Following the departure of Menards in 2007, the mall entrance connecting to the north anchor was sealed, removing direct interior access to that portion of the complex [6][7].

## Ownership and Management
The mall was originally developed by General Management [2][3]. It was sold to Aetna Life Insurance in roughly 1980 and managed by The Rouse Company, which later acquired the property outright in 1989 [2][4]. In 1998, ownership transferred to Landau & Heyman [4][6].

Since July 2006, the property has been owned and managed by Muscatine Mall Management, LLC [2][4]. In 2016, the City of Muscatine entered into a settlement agreement with previous ownership groups regarding the termination of a Tax Increment Financing (TIF) development agreement that had been established in 2008 to assist with redevelopment efforts [13].

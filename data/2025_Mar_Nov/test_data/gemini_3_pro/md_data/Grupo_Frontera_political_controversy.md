# Grupo Frontera political controversy

The **Grupo Frontera political controversy** refers to a series of events in early 2025 involving the American regional Mexican band **Grupo Frontera**, following allegations of the group's support for United States President Donald Trump. The controversy originated from a viral video showing the grandmother of vocalist Adelaido "Payo" Solís celebrating Trump's victory in the 2024 presidential election while dancing to the song "Y.M.C.A.", a track frequently associated with Trump's campaign rallies.

The incident sparked significant backlash on social media, particularly among the band's Mexican and Latino fanbase, due to the administration's stance on mass deportations. Consequences included calls for a boycott, a petition to remove the band from the Sueños Music Festival lineup, and a public dispute between members of the Aguilar musical dynasty. The controversy was noted for its contrast with the band's previous public association with former Mexican President Andrés Manuel López Obrador, who had frequently promoted their music during his term. The band subsequently issued statements denying any affiliation with political parties opposed to immigrants.

## Background

### Rise to prominence and political associations
Grupo Frontera, formed in Edinburg, Texas, gained international recognition in 2022. By 2023, the band had established a significant following in Mexico and the United States. During this period, the group developed an indirect association with Mexican politics, specifically through former President Andrés Manuel López Obrador (AMLO).

Throughout 2023, López Obrador frequently played Grupo Frontera's songs, such as "No Se Va" and "un x100to", during his daily morning press conferences ("La Mañanera") [1][2]. The former president cited their music as a positive alternative to *narcocorridos* (drug ballads), which he criticized for glorifying violence [1][3]. This endorsement culminated in Grupo Frontera performing at the Zócalo in Mexico City on September 15, 2023, for the Mexican Independence Day celebrations, an event presided over by López Obrador [4][5]. The band publicly expressed gratitude for the president's support, stating that it helped them transcend their origins in the Rio Grande Valley [2][6].

### Pre-controversy stance
In an interview with *El País* published on January 5, 2025, shortly before the controversy erupted, band members Juan Javier Cantú and Alberto Acosta addressed the political climate following Donald Trump's re-election. When asked about border issues and the proposed border wall, the members stated that they "rarely get involved in politics" and preferred to focus on music. They emphasized their support for immigrants, citing charitable activities such as donating toys to community centers serving deported families [7].

## Inciting incident

In early 2025, following the inauguration of Donald Trump for his second term, a video circulated on social media platforms, including TikTok and X (formerly Twitter). The footage featured the grandmother of lead vocalist Adelaido "Payo" Solís, affectionately known by fans as "La Abuela Frontera" [1][8]. In the video, she was seen celebrating Trump's electoral victory while dancing to the Village People's "Y.M.C.A.", a song that had become a staple of Trump's campaign rallies [8][9][10].

The video was interpreted by many fans as an endorsement of the incoming administration's policies, specifically those regarding mass deportations of undocumented immigrants [11][12][13]. The backlash was exacerbated by allegations that the band had previously posted—and subsequently deleted—a TikTok video of the members themselves dancing to the same song, which critics viewed as a tacit show of support for the political figure [8][9][14].

## Public backlash

### Social media reaction
The viral video triggered an immediate negative response on social media. Users accused the band of betraying their core audience, which consists largely of Mexicans and Mexican-Americans [12][13]. Comments on the band's platforms included labels such as "traitors" and demands that they "never return to Mexico" [12][13]. The criticism was intensified by the political context of early 2025, which involved heightened concerns regarding immigration enforcement and deportations affecting the Latino community [13][15].

### Sueños Music Festival petition
The controversy impacted the band's professional engagements. Following the circulation of the video, a petition was launched on Change.org calling for the removal of Grupo Frontera from the lineup of the 2025 Sueños Music Festival in Chicago [9][16]. The petition garnered over 10,500 signatures [9][16]. Organizers of the festival faced pressure from ticket holders who threatened to boycott the event. Despite the petition and public outcry, the festival organizers did not remove the band from the lineup [16].

### Aguilar family conflict
The controversy precipitated a public dispute between Emiliano and Leonardo Aguilar, sons of the singer Pepe Aguilar. Following the backlash against Grupo Frontera, Emiliano Aguilar, who had recently released a protest song titled "Ilegal" regarding mass deportations, criticized the band on Instagram. He posted a message stating, "Fuck Grupo Frontera. Mexico must be respected," and questioned how a regional Mexican group could support a politician with anti-immigrant rhetoric [8][17][18][19].

Conversely, his brother Leonardo Aguilar expressed support for Grupo Frontera. In the comments section of one of the band's posts, Leonardo wrote that "real fans" would continue to support them and dismissed the critics as people with "nothing to do" [8][20]. This difference in opinion led to a public exchange between the brothers, with Emiliano criticizing Leonardo's stance as disconnected from the struggles of the immigrant community [20][21].

## Band response

For several days following the initial outbreak of the controversy, Grupo Frontera maintained silence on social media [12][13]. On February 7, 2025, the band issued their first official statement regarding the matter via Instagram Stories [12][15].

In the statement, the group denied any political affiliation with parties opposed to the Latino community. The text read, in part: "We want to clarify that Grupo Frontera has NO affiliation nor alliance with any political party that is against immigrants and the Latino community" [9][12][22]. They further clarified that the opinions of family members did not represent the views of the band, emphasizing their own background as immigrants from the border region [12][15][23].

On February 22, 2025, the band released a video to further address the allegations. In this recording, percussionist Julian Peña Jr. and other members reiterated their support for migrants. They specifically addressed the "Y.M.C.A." dance allegations, explaining that they had utilized the song as part of a dance routine since primary school, asserting it was unrelated to political rallies or the Trump campaign [9].

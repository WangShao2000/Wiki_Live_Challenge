# Kaleigh Trace

**Kaleigh Trace** (born 1986) is a Canadian writer, sex educator, and psychotherapist based in Toronto, Ontario. She is known for her work exploring the intersections of sexuality, disability, and queer identity. Her 2014 memoir, *Hot, Wet, and Shaking: How I Learned to Talk About Sex*, won the Evelyn Richardson Memorial Non-Fiction Award. Trace gained internet attention in 2013 for co-creating a viral parody video promoting sexual consent. In addition to her writing, she practices as a registered psychotherapist specializing in relationships and sexuality.

## Early life and education
Trace was born in 1986 [1]. In 1995, at the age of nine, she was involved in a car accident that resulted in a spinal cord injury [1][2]. As a result of the injury, she has paraplegia and uses a wheelchair [1][3]. Trace has stated that her experiences with disability during her adolescence significantly influenced her later work regarding body image and desirability [2][4].

She attended Dalhousie University in Halifax, Nova Scotia, graduating with a Bachelor of Arts in 2009 [1][2]. She later pursued graduate studies at the University of Guelph, where she earned a Master of Science in Couple and Family Therapy [1][5].

## Career

### Sex education and advocacy
Following her graduation from Dalhousie University, Trace began working at Venus Envy, a sex shop and bookstore in Halifax known for its educational focus [2]. She worked there for approximately ten years, eventually serving as the store's education coordinator [5][6]. In this role, she facilitated workshops and discussions on sexual health, aiming to use accessible language to address topics often surrounded by shame [6][7].

During her time at Venus Envy, Trace launched a blog titled *The Fucking Facts* [1][6]. The blog served as a platform for her to discuss sex advice and personal experiences, including her disability and an abortion she had in her early twenties [1][8]. She stated that her goal was to create a resource that was inclusive of disabled people without being clinical [6].

In July 2013, Trace and her colleague Mary Burnet released a music video titled "Ask First," a parody of Robin Thicke's song "Blurred Lines" [1][9]. The video was created in response to criticisms that the original song's lyrics undermined the concept of consent [9]. "Ask First" featured lyrics explicitly promoting consensual sexual encounters and received coverage from Canadian news outlets such as CTV News and *Xtra Magazine* [6][9].

### Psychotherapy
In 2017, Trace moved from Halifax to Toronto to practice as a therapist [1][2]. She is a Registered Psychotherapist with the College of Registered Psychotherapists of Ontario [5]. She operates a private practice where she specializes in couple and family therapy, with a focus on issues related to disability, chronic illness, sexual trauma, and non-monogamy [5][10].

### Writing
Trace's writing has appeared in various Canadian publications, including *The Coast*, *Shameless Magazine*, *Toronto Life*, *GUTS*, and *Plenitude Magazine* [11][12][13].

#### *Hot, Wet, and Shaking*
In 2014, Invisible Publishing released Trace's debut book, *Hot, Wet, and Shaking: How I Learned to Talk About Sex* [1]. The book is a collection of memoir-style essays that chronicle her sexual development and experiences as a queer, disabled woman [1][14]. The text addresses topics such as ableism, body image, and sexual mechanics, often utilizing humor and colloquial language [14][15].

The book received positive critical reception. *The National Post* described it as "honest musings" written in the tone of a "trusted and cheeky friend" [14]. In 2015, the book won the Evelyn Richardson Memorial Non-Fiction Award at the Atlantic Book Awards [1][16]. It was also named the Gold Winner for Best Book by *The Coast* in their "Best of Halifax" awards in 2014 [1]. A German translation, titled *Hot, Wet & Shaking: Wie ich lernte über Sex zu sprechen*, was published by Orlanda Verlag in 2020 [1].

In 2024, a tenth-anniversary edition of the book was published [17]. This revised edition included a new foreword by Trace and an afterword by musician and writer Christa Couture [17][18]. The updated text incorporates essays reflecting on Trace's 2022 cancer diagnosis, shifting the narrative to include themes of mortality alongside sexuality [11][19].

## Personal life
Trace identifies as queer [11][16]. In October 2022, she was diagnosed with terminal bladder cancer [1][2]. Following her diagnosis, she publicly discussed the intersection of "sex and death," noting that both experiences demand vulnerability and attention [19].

In collaboration with Christa Couture, Trace co-founded the "Little Deaths Salon," a performance series launched in 2024 that explores themes of sex and death through art and storytelling [18][20].

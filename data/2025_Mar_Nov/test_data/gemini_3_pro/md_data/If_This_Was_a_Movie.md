# If This Was a Movie

"**If This Was a Movie**" is a song by the American singer-songwriter Taylor Swift. It was originally released on October 25, 2010, as a bonus track on the deluxe edition of her third studio album, *Speak Now* (2010). Swift wrote the song with Martin Johnson, the lead singer of the band Boys Like Girls, and produced it alongside Nathan Chapman. Musically, it is a fast-paced pop rock ballad featuring electric guitars and orchestral elements. Lyrically, the song narrates a plea to a former lover to return, invoking cinematic tropes such as standing in the rain and happy endings to contrast with the reality of a breakup.

The track is notable for being the only song on the standard or deluxe editions of *Speak Now* that was not written solely by Swift. Following its digital release as a promotional single on November 8, 2011, "If This Was a Movie" achieved commercial success, debuting at number 10 on the US *Billboard* Hot 100 and number 17 on the Canadian Hot 100. It also charted in the United Kingdom.

In 2023, Swift released a re-recorded version of the song titled "**If This Was a Movie (Taylor's Version)**". It was released on March 17, 2023, as part of a streaming compilation EP titled *The More Fearless (Taylor's Version) Chapter*. Although originally a *Speak Now* track, the re-recording was grouped with the *Fearless* era to allow the re-recorded version of *Speak Now* to consist entirely of self-written songs. The re-recorded version peaked at number one on the US Country Digital Song Sales chart. Swift performed the song live for the first time in June 2023 during The Eras Tour.

## Background and Release

Taylor Swift wrote her third studio album, *Speak Now* (2010), as a concept album about things she wanted to say to people in her life but never did [1]. While Swift wrote the standard edition of the album entirely by herself, "If This Was a Movie" was co-written with Martin Johnson, the frontman of the American rock band Boys Like Girls [2][3]. The two had previously collaborated on the song "Two Is Better Than One" by Boys Like Girls and "You'll Always Find Your Way Back Home" from the *Hannah Montana: The Movie* soundtrack [3][4].

"If This Was a Movie" was included as the sixteenth track on the deluxe edition of *Speak Now*, which was released exclusively through Target stores in the United States on October 25, 2010 [1][2]. On November 8, 2011, Big Machine Records released the song, along with other bonus tracks from the album, to digital retailers such as iTunes and Amazon as a promotional single [4][5][6].

## Composition and Lyrics

"If This Was a Movie" is a pop rock ballad characterized by a fast tempo [1][2]. The production incorporates a recurring guitar riff, acoustic and electric string instruments, drums, and full orchestration [1][3]. Critics have noted the song's "simple harmonies" in the chorus which support Swift's vocals [3].

Lyrically, the song deals with the aftermath of a breakup. The narrator daydreams about a lost love returning and wishes that real life would mimic the plot of a romantic movie [3][4]. The lyrics reference common cinematic tropes, including a lover waiting outside in the rain and a flashback to happier times [4]. In the chorus, Swift sings: "Come back, come back, come back to me like you would, you would if this was a movie / Stand in the rain outside 'til I came out" [3][4].

The song's title and lyrical content have drawn comparisons to other tracks in Swift's discography. The chorus parallels the *Fearless* track "Breathe," which mentions music playing "like the end of a sad movie," and the *Fearless (Taylor's Version)* vault track "Bye Bye Baby," which contains the line "It wasn't just like a movie" [4]. The song's title uses the subjunctive mood "was" rather than the grammatically traditional "were" (i.e., "If This Were a Movie"), a usage that is common in modern informal English and appears in other popular songs such as Beyoncé's "If I Were a Boy" and Miranda Lambert's "If I Was a Cowboy" [4].

## Critical Reception

Upon its release as a promotional single, "If This Was a Movie" received generally positive reviews. *Billboard* rated the track four out of five stars, stating that it showcased a "more age-appropriate track" for Swift and noted that she was examining "more mature content" by departing from high school fantasy themes [1][3][7].

Retrospective rankings of Swift's discography have placed the song in varying positions. In a 2020 ranking of Swift's songs, *Paste* magazine placed the track at number 115 out of 158, with the reviewer criticizing the phrasing of the line "Come back to me like" [1]. *NME* ranked the song 150th out of 161 tracks in 2020, describing it as "frustratingly repetitive" and stating that it "stutters towards the finish line" [1]. In 2025, Rob Sheffield of *Rolling Stone* ranked the song 266th out of 286 songs [1].

## Commercial Performance

Following its digital release in November 2011, "If This Was a Movie" debuted at number 10 on the US *Billboard* Hot 100 [1][5]. This achievement made it the fourth track from the *Speak Now* era to reach the top ten and extended Swift's record at the time for the most top-ten debuts by any artist [1][5]. The song also reached number three on the US Digital Songs chart, selling approximately 163,000 downloads in its first week [1][8]. Additionally, it peaked at number one on the US Country Digital Song Sales chart [1].

Internationally, the song charted at number 17 on the Canadian Hot 100 and number 10 on the Canadian Digital Song Sales chart [1]. In the United Kingdom, the song reached number 191 on the UK Singles Chart [1].

## Live Performances

Swift did not perform "If This Was a Movie" live during the Speak Now World Tour (2011–2012) [3]. The song made its live debut nearly 13 years after its release during The Eras Tour. Swift performed the song on piano as a "surprise song" during her concert in Minneapolis, Minnesota, on June 23, 2023 [2][3]. Prior to this performance, it was the only song from the *Speak Now* era that had never been performed live [2].

## If This Was a Movie (Taylor's Version)

A re-recorded version of the song, titled "**If This Was a Movie (Taylor's Version)**", was released on March 17, 2023 [1][2]. It was released alongside re-recordings of "Safe & Sound" and "Eyes Open," as well as the previously unreleased track "All of the Girls You Loved Before," in celebration of the commencement of The Eras Tour [1][2].

Although the original song was a track on *Speak Now*, the re-recording was not included on the 2023 re-recorded album *Speak Now (Taylor's Version)*. Instead, it was released as a standalone single and included on a streaming compilation EP titled *The More Fearless (Taylor's Version) Chapter* [2][3]. The artwork for the single utilizes the cover art from *Fearless (Taylor's Version)* [1].

Media outlets and fans noted that this shift in "era" assignment—moving the track from *Speak Now* to *Fearless*—was likely due to Swift's intention for *Speak Now (Taylor's Version)* to be an entirely self-written album [2][3][9]. Because "If This Was a Movie" was co-written with Martin Johnson, its inclusion would have prevented the re-recorded album from having solely Swift's writing credits [2][9]. Consequently, "If This Was a Movie" is the only song in Swift's catalog to change its associated era during her re-recording project [2].

### Production and Reception
"If This Was a Movie (Taylor's Version)" was produced by Swift and Christopher Rowe [2][10]. The re-recording features the same lyrical content and composition as the original. Critics noted Swift's vocals on the re-recording had improved, with *The Times* describing it as "straightforward, innocent, slightly breathy balladry with an orchestral backing" [1].

Commercially, the re-recorded version reached number two on the US Bubbling Under Hot 100 chart and number four on the US Digital Songs chart [1]. It also became Swift's 19th number-one entry on the US Country Digital Song Sales chart, making "If This Was a Movie" the first song to top that specific chart with two different versions [1]. In New Zealand, the song entered the Hot 40 Singles chart at number 12 [1].

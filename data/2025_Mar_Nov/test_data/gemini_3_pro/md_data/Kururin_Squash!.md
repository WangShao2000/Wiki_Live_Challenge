# Kururin Squash!

**Kururin Squash!** (Japanese: くるりんスカッシュ!, Hepburn: *Kururin Sukasshu!*) is an action-puzzle video game developed by Eighting and published by Nintendo for the GameCube. It was released exclusively in Japan on October 14, 2004 [1][2]. It is the third and final installment in the *Kururin* series, following *Kuru Kuru Kururin* (2001) and *Kururin Paradise* (2002) for the Game Boy Advance [1][3].

The game retains the core gameplay of its predecessors, where the player pilots a slowly rotating stick called the Helirin through narrow, maze-like courses without touching the walls. *Kururin Squash!* is the first and only title in the series to feature 3D computer graphics, although the gameplay remains on a two-dimensional plane [1][4]. The game introduces new mechanics such as boss battles, underwater segments, and specialized vehicles that possess unique abilities like punching or shooting projectiles [3][5].

Upon release, *Kururin Squash!* received generally positive reviews from critics, who praised its visual style, music, and gameplay mechanics. However, it faced criticism regarding its difficulty level relative to previous entries and its short length [1][6]. Commercially, the game performed poorly, selling fewer than 20,000 copies in Japan by the end of 2004, making it the lowest-selling entry in the franchise [1]. Despite never being officially localized for international markets, it has been cited as a cult classic and an import-friendly title due to its low language barrier [5][6].

## Gameplay

*Kururin Squash!* is a puzzle game requiring precise timing and navigation. The player controls the Helirin, a vehicle resembling a long, rotating stick. The primary objective is to guide the Helirin from the start of a maze to the goal without colliding with walls or obstacles [7][8].

### Core Mechanics
The Helirin rotates automatically at a constant speed. The player uses the GameCube controller's analog stick to move the vehicle through the stage. The "A" button is typically used to activate special vehicle abilities, while other buttons can increase the movement speed or the rotation speed of the Helirin to navigate tight corners [3][9].

The game utilizes a health system represented by hearts. By default, the Helirin has three hearts. Colliding with a wall or an enemy results in the loss of one heart and a time penalty. If all hearts are lost, the Helirin shatters, and the player must restart the stage or resume from the last checkpoint [3][5]. Blue springs scattered throughout levels reverse the direction of the Helirin's rotation when touched [3].

Unlike the 2D sprite-based graphics of the Game Boy Advance titles, *Kururin Squash!* uses 3D polygonal graphics. This allows for visual depth, such as transparency effects where players can see background elements beneath the maze [4][5].

### New Features and Vehicles
*Kururin Squash!* introduces several new gameplay elements to the series. Coins are scattered throughout each stage; collecting all coins in a level contributes to a "perfect" rating and adds to a total count used to purchase items in the in-game shop [2][4]. Shop items include new Helirin skins, interface designs, and "super play" demonstration videos [4].

The game features "Action Helirins," which are specialized vehicles used in specific stages to overcome unique environmental hazards. These include:
*   **Helibokaan:** Equipped with boxing gloves that extend when the player presses a button, allowing the vehicle to break obstacles and punch enemies [3][4].
*   **Helibashan:** A submarine-like vehicle used in water levels. It can dive underwater to avoid surface obstacles or navigate submerged tunnels [3][5].
*   **Heliburuun:** Capable of generating tornadoes. These tornadoes can defeat enemies, clear smoke, or block fire hazards [3][5].
*   **Helibibyuun:** Features lasers that shoot from the ends of the stick to destroy enemies or trigger switches [3][4].
*   **Heliboboh:** A vehicle that emits flamethrowers to defeat enemies [3].

Boss battles are included at the end of each world. These encounters require the player to utilize the specific Action Helirin introduced in that world to deplete the boss's health meter [3][5].

### Modes
The game features several modes:
*   **Adventure Mode:** The main story campaign where players progress through different worlds, unlocking new stages and vehicles [1].
*   **Challenge Mode:** A mode focused on completing stages as quickly as possible [1].
*   **Multiplayer:** The game supports up to four players in split-screen. "Race Mode" involves players competing to finish a course first, while "Battle Mode" requires players to collect coins and use weapons to attack opponents in an arena setting [1][4][7].

## Plot

The story is set in the fictional Rin Village. The protagonist, Kururin, wins a grand prize in a local lottery: a "4 Nights and 5 Days Family Tour of 4 Countries." Kururin and his extensive family—including his parents, Totorin and Kakarin, and his many siblings—depart for the vacation [2].

The family visits four distinct locations: Sweet Island (a land of sweets), Natural World (forests and seas), Snowscape (a snowy region), and Future Vision (a futuristic city). However, upon returning home, Kururin discovers that only he, his father Totorin, and his mother Kakarin have returned. His siblings are missing. It is revealed that the antagonist, Professor Isogashima, has kidnapped the siblings and created robots to prevent Kururin from interfering with his research [2][3].

Kururin sets out in the Helirin to rescue his siblings across the four worlds. After saving them, he returns home to find that his father, Totorin, has also been kidnapped by Isogashima and taken to the Wonderverse, the final world. Kururin travels to the Wonderverse, defeats the Professor, and rescues his father, reuniting the family [3].

## Development

*Kururin Squash!* was developed by Eighting, a Japanese studio primarily known for fighting games such as *Bloody Roar* and *Battle Stadium D.O.N* [10][11]. It was published by Nintendo. The game was produced by Hiroshi Sato and Masato Toyoshima, with direction by Shinji Hatano and Tomonori Fujisawa [1][12].

The game marks the series' transition from handheld consoles to a home console, utilizing the GameCube's hardware to render 3D graphics while maintaining the 2D gameplay plane. The visual style incorporates a "puppet theater" aesthetic for cutscenes, where characters appear as 2D cardboard cutouts on sticks [4][5].

Yōichi Kotabe returned as the character designer for the game. This was the final title Kotabe worked on in this capacity at Nintendo before leaving the company in 2007 [1][12]. The soundtrack was composed by Atsuhiro Motoyama, who had previously worked on the *Umihara Kawase* series [1][10].

*Kururin Squash!* was officially revealed in *Famitsu* magazine in August 2004 and was released in Japan on October 14, 2004 [1]. Unlike the first game in the series, *Kuru Kuru Kururin*, which saw a release in Europe and Australia, *Kururin Squash!* was never released outside of Japan [1][11].

## Reception

### Critical Response
*Kururin Squash!* received generally positive reviews from import-focused media outlets. Critics praised the game's presentation, particularly the transition to 3D. *Nintendo World Report* highlighted the "fantastic graphics," noting the particle and lighting effects, as well as the unique "puppet on a stick" art style used in cutscenes [5]. The music by Atsuhiro Motoyama was described as "marvelously constructed and quite soothing" [9].

The gameplay was commended for its responsiveness, with the GameCube's analog stick providing "pinpoint accuracy" compared to the D-pad controls of the Game Boy Advance titles [5]. The introduction of new vehicles and boss battles was seen as a welcome expansion of the formula. *Eurogamer* noted that while the basic puzzle levels were the most satisfying, the variations provided by the new vehicles had their moments [6].

However, the game faced criticism regarding its difficulty and content volume. *Eurogamer* stated that the game "doesn't push you hard enough or for long enough," suggesting that the new levels were not as "pure and addictive" as the straightforward puzzles of the predecessors [6]. *Hardcore Gaming 101* noted that while the game is fun, the Adventure mode is relatively short, and the multiplayer battle mode was preferred over the race mode [4].

### Sales
Commercially, *Kururin Squash!* was not successful. In its debut week in Japan, it reached number 18 on the sales charts, selling approximately 5,745 copies [1]. It dropped off the charts quickly, selling a total of 18,562 copies by the end of 2004 [1]. It was the lowest-selling entry in the *Kururin* series and one of Nintendo's lowest-selling first-party titles on the GameCube in Japan [1].

### Legacy
Despite its low sales and lack of international release, *Kururin Squash!* is considered a "cult classic" among GameCube enthusiasts [1][6]. It is frequently recommended as an import title for Western collectors because the game features very little Japanese text that is essential for gameplay, making it accessible to non-Japanese speakers [5][11].

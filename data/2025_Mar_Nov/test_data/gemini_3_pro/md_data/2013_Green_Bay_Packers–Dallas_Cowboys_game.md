# 2013 Green Bay Packers–Dallas Cowboys game

The **2013 Green Bay Packers–Dallas Cowboys game** was a National Football League (NFL) regular season game played on December 15, 2013, at AT&T Stadium in Arlington, Texas. The Week 15 matchup featured two National Football Conference (NFC) teams fighting for playoff positioning. The Green Bay Packers defeated the Dallas Cowboys 37–36, overcoming a 23-point halftime deficit. The victory matched the largest comeback in Packers franchise history, equaling a record set in 1982 [1][2].

The game is notable for the statistical disparity between the two halves and the performance of Packers backup quarterback Matt Flynn, who started in place of the injured Aaron Rodgers. The Cowboys dominated the first half, leading 26–3 at intermission behind the play of quarterback Tony Romo and kicker Dan Bailey [1][3]. However, Green Bay scored touchdowns on five consecutive possessions in the second half [4][5]. The game was decided in the final minutes when Cowboys quarterback Tony Romo threw two interceptions, allowing Green Bay to take the lead on an Eddie Lacy touchdown run with 1:31 remaining [2][6].

The result had significant postseason implications. The victory kept Green Bay's playoff hopes alive, allowing them to eventually win the NFC North division, while the loss contributed to the Cowboys' elimination from playoff contention the following week [1][5]. The game also generated significant media discussion regarding Dallas's play-calling strategy, specifically the decision to pass rather than run while holding a lead late in the fourth quarter, and the early departure of Cowboys receiver Dez Bryant from the field before the game clock had expired [7][8][9].

## Background

Heading into Week 15 of the 2013 NFL season, both teams held middling records but remained in contention for their respective division titles. The Dallas Cowboys entered the game with a 7–6 record, vying for the NFC East title [1]. The Green Bay Packers held a 6–6–1 record and were competing for the NFC North [1].

The Packers were playing without their starting quarterback, Aaron Rodgers, who had suffered a broken collarbone in Week 8 against the Chicago Bears. Following the injury, the Packers struggled, losing or tying five consecutive games under backup quarterbacks Seneca Wallace, Scott Tolzien, and Matt Flynn [1]. Flynn, who had been released by the Oakland Raiders and Buffalo Bills earlier in the 2013 season before returning to Green Bay, was named the starter for the game against Dallas [10].

The Cowboys possessed the league's worst-ranked defense entering the game but were favored to win by four points [1][11]. Both teams were coming off losses in Week 14 [1].

## Game summary

### First half
The first half was controlled by the Dallas Cowboys, who capitalized on offensive efficiency and defensive stops. Dallas opened the scoring with a 47-yard field goal by Dan Bailey. Green Bay responded with a 57-yard field goal by Mason Crosby to tie the game at 3–3 [3].

Following the tie, the Cowboys scored 23 unanswered points. Bailey added field goals of 23, 43, and 50 yards [3]. The Cowboys' offense, led by Tony Romo, found the end zone late in the first quarter when Romo connected with tight end Jason Witten for a 25-yard touchdown pass [3]. In the second quarter, running back DeMarco Murray scored on a 1-yard touchdown run [3].

Green Bay's offense struggled to move the ball, with Matt Flynn completing 10 of 17 passes for only 114 yards and one interception in the first half [12]. At halftime, the Cowboys held a commanding 26–3 lead, and statistical models and commentators viewed a Packers victory as highly improbable [4][12].

### Second half
The momentum shifted immediately in the third quarter. The Packers proceeded to score touchdowns on all five of their second-half possessions [4][7].

Green Bay opened the third quarter with an 80-yard drive, capped by a 13-yard touchdown pass from Flynn to Jordy Nelson [1][3]. Dallas responded with a drive that stalled, resulting in a 50-yard field goal by Dan Bailey, extending their lead to 29–10 [3].

Flynn then led an 80-yard drive ending with a 3-yard touchdown pass to tight end Andrew Quarless, narrowing the deficit to 29–17 [1][3]. Following a defensive stop, the Packers scored again early in the fourth quarter on an 11-yard touchdown pass to running back James Starks, bringing the score to 29–24 [1][3].

The Cowboys offense responded with a 10-play, 80-yard drive. Romo completed a 5-yard touchdown pass to Dez Bryant, who made a leaping catch in the back of the end zone. This extended the Dallas lead to 36–24 with approximately eight minutes remaining [1][3].

Green Bay answered quickly. Flynn directed another 80-yard drive, culminating in a 3-yard touchdown pass to James Jones. This cut the Dallas lead to 36–31 with 4:17 remaining in the game [1][3].

### Final minutes
With under three minutes remaining and holding a five-point lead, the Cowboys took possession. On a second-down play with 2:58 left, the Cowboys lined up to run the ball. However, quarterback Tony Romo checked out of the run play at the line of scrimmage after reading the defensive alignment. He attempted a pass to Miles Austin, but the throw was behind the receiver and was intercepted by Packers cornerback Sam Shields at midfield [2][7][13].

Following the interception, the Packers drove 50 yards in seven plays. Running back Eddie Lacy scored on a 1-yard touchdown run with 1:31 remaining. The Packers attempted a two-point conversion to extend the lead to three points, but the pass failed, leaving the score at 37–36 in favor of Green Bay [1][3].

The Cowboys had one final possession to drive into field goal range. On the second play of the drive, Romo attempted a pass to Cole Beasley. The pass was thrown behind the receiver and intercepted by Packers cornerback Tramon Williams. The play was initially ruled incomplete, but a replay review reversed the call, confirming the interception and sealing the victory for Green Bay [1][6][14].

## Analysis and aftermath

### Historical significance
The 23-point comeback tied the Green Bay Packers' franchise record for the largest deficit overcome to win a game. The record was originally set in 1982 against the Los Angeles Rams [1][2]. Packers head coach Mike McCarthy described the victory as "incredible" and noted the sheer emotion involved in the win [2]. Players compared the atmosphere on the sideline during the comeback to their Super Bowl XLV victory [6].

### Criticism of Dallas play-calling
Following the game, the Cowboys' coaching staff and quarterback Tony Romo faced intense scrutiny regarding their clock management and play selection. Despite leading by five points with under three minutes remaining, and despite running back DeMarco Murray averaging 7.4 yards per carry, the Cowboys opted to pass the ball [16][17].

The interception by Sam Shields occurred on a play where Romo checked out of a called run play into a pass play. Head coach Jason Garrett confirmed post-game that the original call was a run, designed to force Green Bay to use their timeouts. Romo defended the decision to change the play, citing the Packers' defensive alignment which overloaded the box to stop the run. However, Romo admitted he did not protect the ball well enough on the subsequent throw [7][13].

Packers players expressed surprise and relief that Dallas abandoned the running game. Defensive tackle Ryan Pickett stated, "We're just happy they did. We were, like, 'OK, we'll take it,'" noting that the Cowboys' zone run scheme had been highly effective throughout the game [9].

### Dez Bryant early exit
Cowboys wide receiver Dez Bryant attracted media attention for leaving the field with 1:21 remaining on the clock, prior to the game's conclusion. Bryant walked to the locker room after the replay official confirmed the game-sealing interception by Tramon Williams. Bryant later apologized, stating his departure was due to being "too emotional" to watch the Packers kneel out the clock and that he had been crying [18][19]. While former player Deion Sanders criticized the move as "absolutely... wrong," coach Jason Garrett defended Bryant's character while acknowledging he would address the importance of staying on the field [8][20].

### Season impact
The victory improved the Packers' record to 7–6–1, keeping them in contention for the NFC North. Aaron Rodgers returned from injury for the final game of the season, where the Packers defeated the Chicago Bears to win the division and secure a playoff berth [1][2].

The loss dropped the Cowboys to 7–7. Although they remained in contention for the NFC East until the final week of the season, they ultimately finished 8–8 and missed the playoffs for the fourth consecutive year [1][5].

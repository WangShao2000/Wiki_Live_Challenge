# Wish World

"**Wish World**" is the seventh episode of the fifteenth series of the British science fiction television programme *Doctor Who*. It was first broadcast on BBC One on 24 May 2025. The episode was written by showrunner Russell T Davies and directed by Alex Sanjiv Pillai. It serves as the first part of the two-part series finale, concluding with "The Reality War" on 31 May 2025.

The episode stars Ncuti Gatwa as the Fifteenth Doctor and Varada Sethu as his companion Belinda Chandra. In the narrative, the duo finds themselves trapped in a fabricated, heteronormative reality resembling the 1950s, engineered by the antagonist Conrad Clark (Jonah Hauer-King) and the renegade Time Lord known as the Rani (Archie Panjabi). The episode features the return of Millie Gibson as Ruby Sunday and Anita Dobson as Mrs Flood, who is revealed to be an incarnation of the Rani.

"Wish World" received mixed to positive reviews from critics. While the episode was praised for its visual style, social commentary, and the performance of Archie Panjabi, it faced criticism for its heavy reliance on exposition and its function primarily as a setup for the finale. The episode was released in cinemas alongside "The Reality War" in a limited theatrical engagement.

## Plot

In Bavaria in 1865, a woman named Violett gives birth to a son. The Rani (Archie Panjabi) arrives and seizes the child, who is the seventh son of a seventh son of a seventh son. This lineage identifies the infant as an incarnation of Desiderium, a god with the ability to make wishes a reality. The Rani transforms the family into animals and petals before departing with the baby [1][2][3].

In 2025, the Fifteenth Doctor and Belinda Chandra awaken in a fabricated reality. They believe themselves to be a married couple, John and Belinda Smith, living in a suburban, 1950s-style domestic setting with their daughter, Poppy. In this world, the Doctor works for an insurance company, while Belinda is a housewife. The reality is maintained by Conrad Clark (Jonah Hauer-King), who appears on television hosting a children's storytelling programme that reinforces the societal norms of this world [3][4][5].

The stability of the "Wish World" relies on the suppression of doubt. However, imperfections in the fabrication begin to manifest; when characters experience doubt, physical objects, such as coffee mugs, fall through solid tables. The Doctor's suppressed memories and identity threaten to break the illusion [1][3][4].

Outside of this fabricated existence, Ruby Sunday (Millie Gibson) and Shirley Bingham (Ruth Madeley) are part of a resistance movement. They exist on the fringes of society in an encampment of "dispossessed" people, including those with disabilities, whom Conrad's idealized world does not account for and therefore cannot erase completely. They observe the "Wish World" from the outside, aware that reality has been altered [1][3][6].

It is revealed that Conrad Clark, previously seen in the episode "Lucky Day", is utilizing the power of the Desiderium baby to project this fantasy onto Earth. He is manipulated by Mrs Flood (Anita Dobson) and her younger incarnation, the Rani. The Rani's ultimate goal is to use the energy generated by the doubts of the population to tear the fabric of the universe and rescue the ancient Time Lord Omega from an antimatter dimension [1][2][6][7].

As the Doctor begins to regain his agency, the illusion fractures. The episode concludes with the revelation of the Rani's plan and the impending threat of Omega, setting the stage for the finale, "The Reality War" [2][7].

## Production

### Development
"Wish World" was written by showrunner Russell T Davies [2]. The episode was designed as the first half of the Series 15 finale. Davies intended the story to explore themes of fascism, conformity, and the power of storytelling [4][5]. The concept of a "Wish World" allowed the production to utilize a stylized, mid-20th-century aesthetic similar to the Marvel Studios series *WandaVision*, a comparison noted by several critics [4][8].

The episode features the return of the Rani, a Time Lord antagonist who last appeared in the television series in 1987. Ncuti Gatwa had previously expressed a desire for his Doctor to face the Rani [2]. The character is portrayed by Archie Panjabi, with Anita Dobson's character, Mrs Flood, revealed to be a future or alternative incarnation of the same Time Lord [1][2]. The narrative also reintroduces the lore of the "seventh son of a seventh son," a trope previously utilized in fantasy literature and folklore, to explain the magical mechanics of the wish-granting baby [1][9].

### Casting
The episode features the regular cast of Ncuti Gatwa as the Doctor and Varada Sethu as Belinda Chandra. Millie Gibson returns as Ruby Sunday. The guest cast includes Archie Panjabi as the Rani and Jonah Hauer-King as Conrad Clark. Returning recurring characters include Jemma Redgrave as Kate Lethbridge-Stewart, Ruth Madeley as Shirley Bingham, Bonnie Langford as Mel Bush, and Susan Twist as Susan Triad [10][11].

Jonathan Groff makes a cameo appearance as Rogue, a character introduced in the previous series. Carole Ann Ford also appears in a vision as Susan Foreman, the Doctor's granddaughter [2][5].

### Filming
Filming for "Wish World" took place in April and May 2024 [2]. The episode was directed by Alex Sanjiv Pillai, who also directed the subsequent episode "The Reality War" and the 2024 Christmas special "Joy to the World" [2][12]. Interior scenes were shot at Wolf Studios Wales. The opening sequence, set in 1865 Bavaria, was filmed on location in a forest, where production was reportedly disrupted by insects [2].

## Broadcast and Reception

### Broadcast and Ratings
"Wish World" was first broadcast in the United Kingdom on BBC One on 24 May 2025 [2]. It was released simultaneously on BBC iPlayer in the UK and on Disney+ internationally [13].

The episode received an overnight rating of 1.8 million viewers, placing it as the fifth most-watched programme of the day [2][14]. The seven-day consolidated rating was 2.86 million viewers [2][15]. These figures represented a decline from the previous week's episode, "The Interstellar Song Contest". Analysts attributed the lower television ratings to the holiday weekend and the fact that the episode was screened in cinemas as a double bill with the finale, "The Reality War", on 31 May 2025, which may have split the audience [15][16].

### Critical Reception
The episode received mixed to positive reviews. *Popped News* described it as a "bold and unsettling triumph," praising the performance of the cast and the "razor-sharp commentary" on heteronormativity and idealism [4]. The review drew favorable comparisons to *WandaVision*, noting that the episode "weaponises idealism" to create a psychological horror [4].

*Den of Geek* gave a mixed assessment, acknowledging the "brilliant" production design and the fun of the alternate reality but criticizing the episode for feeling like a "rushed info dump" designed to connect disparate plot threads for the finale [7]. The review noted that while the episode contained "tons of ideas," the connective tissue was sometimes lacking [7].

*The Guardian* praised Archie Panjabi's performance, stating she delighted in "playing up the wicked Rani caricature." The review highlighted the chilling nature of the dystopian setting, where individuals could be "disappeared" for being different, drawing parallels to authoritarian regimes [8].

Some criticism was directed at the characterization of the Rani. A review on *The Time Scales* argued that the character had been changed from an amoral scientist into a more generic villain resembling the Master or a "Wicked Witch," lacking the specific scientific motivation of her classic series appearances [17].

*Blogtor Who* called the episode "spectacle and setup," noting that while it worked well as a penultimate chapter, it felt "incomplete as a standalone story." The review praised the social commentary regarding the "dispossessed" but felt the plot became bogged down in Time Lord mythology regarding Omega and the Rani [9].

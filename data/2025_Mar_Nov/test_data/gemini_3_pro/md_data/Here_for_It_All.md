# Here for It All

**Here for It All** is the sixteenth studio album by American singer-songwriter Mariah Carey. It was released on September 26, 2025, through her own imprint, Mariah, in partnership with the media company Gamma. It marks Carey's first studio album in seven years, following *Caution* (2018), and serves as her first release as an independent artist outside of the major label system.

The album features guest appearances by Anderson .Paak, Kehlani, Shenseea, and The Clark Sisters. L.A. Reid served as the executive producer, marking his third collaboration with Carey in this capacity. Musically, *Here for It All* is described as an eclectic record that blends R&B, pop, and hip-hop soul with influences of 1970s funk, disco, and gospel.

Upon release, *Here for It All* debuted at number seven on the US *Billboard* 200 and topped the Independent Albums and Top R&B Albums charts. With this entry, Carey became the third female artist in history to achieve a top-ten album on the *Billboard* 200 in the 1990s, 2000s, 2010s, and 2020s. The album was supported by three singles: "Type Dangerous", "Sugar Sweet", and "Play This Song".

## Background and release

Following the release of her fifteenth studio album, *Caution* (2018), Mariah Carey engaged in various archival projects, including the release of *The Rarities* (2020) and an expanded edition of *The Emancipation of Mimi* [1][2]. The seven-year period between *Caution* and *Here for It All* marked the longest gap between studio albums in Carey's career [3][4].

In 2023, Carey left Roc Nation management and later partnered with Gamma, a media and technology enterprise founded by former Apple Music executive Larry Jackson [4][5]. *Here for It All* is Carey's first album released under this partnership, utilizing her own imprint, Mariah [4][6]. In an interview with *Forbes*, it was confirmed that L.A. Reid served as the album's executive producer, a role he previously held on Carey's albums *The Emancipation of Mimi* (2005) and *Memoirs of an Imperfect Angel* (2009) [5][7].

Carey began teasing the album in June 2025 with social media posts referencing "MC16" [8][9]. The album was officially announced with a release date of September 26, 2025 [3][10]. It was released physically on CD, cassette, and vinyl, as well as on digital streaming platforms [11][12].

## Composition and lyrics

*Here for It All* consists of 11 tracks that incorporate a "retro-modernist" sound, drawing from R&B, pop, gospel, and hip-hop [1][13]. Critics noted the album's sonic references to Black pop music of the 1970s and 1980s, distinguishing it from the stricter contemporary R&B sound of her previous album, *Caution* [4].

### Songs
The album opens with "Mi", a track described as having "SZA-esque atmospherics" and serving as a continuation of the mood established on *Caution* [1][12]. The lead single, "Type Dangerous", is a hip-hop soul track that samples "Eric B. Is President" (1986) by Eric B. & Rakim [8][14]. Lyrically, the song discusses an attraction to partners who represent trouble [8][14].

"Sugar Sweet", featuring Shenseea and Kehlani, is a dancehall-influenced pop and R&B song characterized by "skittering drums and sultry synthesizers" [15]. "Play This Song", a duet with Anderson .Paak, was noted for its 1970s soul and Motown influences, featuring live drums and "twinkling chimes" [16][17].

The album includes a cover of Paul McCartney and Wings' 1973 song "My Love", which Carey reinterprets with a gospel-tinged R&B arrangement [1][17]. "Jesus I Do" features the gospel group The Clark Sisters; the track is described as a "Sly and the Family Stone style romp" that blends traditional gospel with funk elements [10]. The album concludes with the title track, "Here for It All", a ballad that addresses themes of resilience, fame, and personal history [10][18].

## Singles

"Type Dangerous" was released as the album's lead single on June 6, 2025 [14]. The song debuted and peaked at number 95 on the US *Billboard* Hot 100, marking Carey's 50th entry on the chart [14][19]. It also reached number one on the *Billboard* Adult R&B Airplay chart [2][14].

"Sugar Sweet", featuring Shenseea and Kehlani, was released as the second single on July 25, 2025 [15]. The track peaked at number 17 on the US Hot R&B Songs chart and number two on the R&B Digital Song Sales chart [15][20].

"Play This Song", featuring Anderson .Paak, was released as the third single on September 26, 2025, coinciding with the album's release [16].

## Critical reception

*Here for It All* received generally positive reviews from music critics. *PopMatters* praised the album for its "affectionate and charming look at Black pop music of the 1970s and 1980s," noting that it felt "charmingly retro without feeling stale" [4]. The *Daily Bruin* commended Carey for "constructing an LP that manages to simultaneously represent a step forward in her sonic progression while also keeping her changing voice at center stage," highlighting the track "Play This Song" as a standout [17].

*Soul in Stereo* offered a mixed review, describing the album as a "mixed bag" where some tracks felt like "copy/paste versions of superior hits," while others, such as the title track and "Jesus I Do," effectively tweaked Carey's formula [10]. *Slant Magazine* noted influences of soul, disco, and gospel throughout the record [6]. Reviewers consistently highlighted the vulnerability and "earnest sensitivity" of the title track and "Nothing Is Impossible" [1].

## Commercial performance

In the United States, *Here for It All* debuted at number seven on the *Billboard* 200 with 47,000 album-equivalent units, of which 39,000 were pure album sales [21][22]. This marked Carey's 19th top-ten album on the chart [21]. With this debut, Carey joined Madonna and Shania Twain as the only women to have released new top-ten albums in the 1990s, 2000s, 2010s, and 2020s [21][23].

The album debuted at number one on the US Top Album Sales, Top R&B Albums, and Independent Albums charts [21][24]. Internationally, the album charted at number 31 on the UK Albums Chart and number 18 on the Scottish Albums Chart [6].

# LIV (nightclub)

**LIV** is a nightclub brand with locations in Miami Beach, Florida, and Las Vegas, Nevada. The original venue, **LIV Miami**, is located within the Fontainebleau Miami Beach hotel and was founded in 2008 by hospitality entrepreneur David Grutman [1]. The club is owned and operated by Groot Hospitality, a company in which Live Nation Entertainment acquired a majority stake in 2019 [1][2].

The venue is known for its high revenue generation, consistently ranking among the top-grossing nightclubs in the United States [3][4]. In late 2023, the brand expanded with the opening of **LIV Las Vegas** at the Fontainebleau Las Vegas, which subsequently underwent a significant expansion in 2025 [5]. The name "LIV" is derived from the Roman numerals for 54, referencing the year 1954 when the original Fontainebleau hotel opened, as well as serving as an homage to the New York City nightclub Studio 54 [1][6].

## History

### Miami Beach
LIV Miami was established in 2008 as part of a $1 billion renovation of the Fontainebleau Miami Beach hotel by the Soffer family [7][8]. The space occupied by the nightclub was historically known as the **Tropigala Lounge**, and prior to that, the **La Ronde Room** in the 1950s and 1960s, which hosted performers such as Frank Sinatra, Sammy Davis Jr., Elvis Presley, and Tony Bennett [9].

The nightclub officially opened in November 2008. Its launch events included the Victoria's Secret Fashion Show after-party, attended by models such as Adriana Lima, Heidi Klum, and Miranda Kerr, as well as musical performances by Usher [10][11][12]. David Grutman, who had previously worked at Miami venues such as Tantra and the Opium Group, was brought in by Jeff Soffer to manage the venue [3][6].

In 2017, ahead of its tenth season, LIV Miami underwent a $10 million renovation overseen by the design firm ICRAVE [9][13]. The renovation was completed in nine weeks during the summer, a period during which the club temporarily closed [13]. The redesign focused on technological upgrades, including the installation of a dynamic LED structure nicknamed "The Spider" [13][14].

### Las Vegas
LIV Las Vegas opened in December 2023 inside the Fontainebleau Las Vegas [1][15]. The launch weekend featured performances by John Summit, Myke Towers, and Metro Boomin [15].

Following its opening, management determined that the venue required expansion to meet demand. Specifically, during the initial performance by resident DJ John Summit, the venue reportedly turned away over 1,000 patrons [5]. Consequently, the club underwent an "eight-figure" renovation and expansion, reopening in March 2025 [5]. This project merged the indoor nightclub with the adjacent dayclub, **LIV Beach**, creating a unified indoor-outdoor venue spanning over 80,000 square feet [5][16]. The renovation included lowering the stage, expanding the dance floor, and adding a new "GA+" ticketing option that provides general admission guests with access to a backstage area and private bar [5][16].

## Architecture and Design

### LIV Miami
LIV Miami encompasses over 18,000 square feet of space [9][17]. The venue's design preserves the historic "bones" of the original theater, including its domed ceiling [9]. The interior features a "stadium" style layout with VIP tables surrounding the dance floor and mezzanine skyboxes offering views from above [13][18].

A central feature of the Miami location, introduced during the 2017 renovation, is the kinetic lighting installation known as "The Spider" (also referred to by staff as "Lola") [14]. This structure consists of a steel truss equipped with 410 individual LED screens, robotic motors, and fans [13][14]. The apparatus is capable of moving up and down, rotating, and creating various geometric shapes such as an orb or chandelier, choreographed to the music [13][14]. The 2017 redesign also replaced white seating with brown leather and added projection mapping capabilities to the venue [9][14].

### LIV Las Vegas
The Las Vegas venue, following its 2025 expansion, is designed to function as a "festival-like" environment [5]. The combined 80,000-square-foot space connects the indoor nightclub with the outdoor pool deck [5]. The venue features two distinct entrances: a second-floor entrance for general admission and a "field-level" entrance on the pool deck for VIP table holders [5][16]. The design prioritizes sightlines to the DJ booth and includes an updated mixology program [5].

## Name and Branding
The name "LIV" is a direct reference to the year 1954 (represented by the Roman numerals L, I, and V), which is the year the original Fontainebleau Miami Beach hotel opened [6][8]. Founder David Grutman has also stated that the name serves as a reference to the Manhattan nightclub Studio 54, to which he has compared the venue's atmosphere and cultural relevance [1][19].

## Financial Performance
LIV has consistently been ranked among the highest-grossing nightclubs in the United States.
*   **2011:** The club generated approximately $35 million in revenue [6].
*   **2012:** Revenue was estimated between $35 million and $45 million, ranking it fifth in the country [1][4].
*   **2013:** Revenue was estimated at over $35 million [20].
*   **2014:** The venue generated between $40 million and $45 million, maintaining its position as the fifth highest-grossing club in the U.S. and the highest-grossing venue outside of Las Vegas [21][22].
*   **2016:** Annual revenues were reported at roughly $45 million [3].

## Entertainment and Programming
LIV is known for its mix of electronic dance music (EDM) and hip-hop programming. The venue hosts a weekly party known as **"LIV on Sunday,"** which focuses on hip-hop and has been described as a significant cultural event within the genre [1]. The event is often referenced in rap lyrics; for example, the song "Hustle Hard" by Ace Hood mentions the venue, and Meek Mill's "Amen" references the club in its chorus [1].

The club features residencies and performances by high-profile DJs and artists. Notable past and present performers include:
*   **DJs:** Tiesto, David Guetta, Calvin Harris, Deadmau5, Steve Aoki, and John Summit [5][20][23].
*   **Hip-Hop/Pop:** Jay-Z, Kanye West, Drake, Travis Scott, and Post Malone [20][21][22].

In 2025, David Guetta announced an exclusive residency at the Fontainebleau Las Vegas, performing at both LIV and LIV Beach [24].

## Controversies and Legal Issues

### Trademark Dispute with LIV Golf
In 2023, the owners of LIV nightclub filed a Notice of Opposition with the U.S. Patent and Trademark Office to block the trademark registration of **LIV Golf**, a professional golf tour financed by the Public Investment Fund of Saudi Arabia [25][26].

The nightclub's legal filing argued that the trademarks were "visually, phonetically, and aurally similar" and that the golf tour's use of the name would cause consumer confusion and dilute the nightclub's brand [26][27]. Both entities utilize the Roman numeral "LIV" (54) for their names, though for different reasons: the nightclub references the year 1954, while LIV Golf references the 54 holes played in its tournaments [19][28]. The nightclub's opposition highlighted that it had been using the trademark for 15 years prior to the golf tour's formation [26]. As of late 2024, the opposition filing was listed as "pending" [27].

### Security and Legal Incidents
*   **Tory Lanez Lawsuit (2020):** In September 2020, a club promoter and artist sued rapper Tory Lanez, alleging that Lanez assaulted him inside LIV Miami in November 2019 [29]. The plaintiff claimed he was punched and attacked by Lanez and his entourage following a dispute involving an Instagram post [29].
*   **Door Policy and Pricing:** The venue has faced consumer complaints regarding its door policies and pricing transparency. In 2016, reports surfaced of patrons being charged unexpected cover fees, with one incident involving a bill of nearly $2,000 for entry and drinks that the patrons claimed was not clearly disclosed [30].
*   **Discrimination Allegations:** While not exclusively specific to LIV, the venue has been mentioned in broader discussions regarding discriminatory door policies in high-end nightclubs, where entry is often subjective based on appearance or gender ratios [31][32].

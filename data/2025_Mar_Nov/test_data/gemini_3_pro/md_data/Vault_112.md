# Vault 112

**Vault 112** is a fictional underground fallout shelter appearing in the 2008 action role-playing video game *Fallout 3*, developed by Bethesda Game Studios. Located in the Capital Wasteland (the ruins of Washington, D.C. and the surrounding area), the facility is distinct among the game's vaults for containing a virtual reality simulation known as "Tranquility Lane." The vault is overseen by Dr. Stanislaus Braun, a high-ranking scientist from the Vault-Tec Corporation and the creator of the Garden of Eden Creation Kit (G.E.C.K.).

In the narrative of *Fallout 3*, Vault 112 serves as a critical plot location where the player character, the Lone Wanderer, must travel to rescue their father, James. Unlike many other vaults in the franchise which have fallen into disrepair or chaos, Vault 112 remains functionally intact, maintained by robotic assistants and housing its residents in suspended animation pods. The location explores themes of simulated reality, unchecked authority, and the psychological effects of indefinite isolation.

## Background and Construction

According to in-game lore, Vault 112 was one of the last vaults commissioned by the Vault-Tec Corporation before the Great War of 2077. Construction of the facility began in November 2068 and was completed in June 2074 [1], [2]. The vault was designed with a relatively small capacity compared to other shelters, built to house only 85 occupants [2], [3].

The facility was constructed specifically to the specifications of its Overseer, Dr. Stanislaus Braun. Braun was a prominent scientist within the *Fallout* universe, serving as the Director of Vault-Tec's Societal Preservation Program and the head of Future-Tec [4]. He is credited with the invention of the Garden of Eden Creation Kit (G.E.C.K.), a terraforming device intended to restore the post-nuclear environment [5].

Unlike standard vaults designed for physical survival and social experiments within a physical space, Vault 112 was equipped with advanced "Tranquility Loungers" (also referred to as Visiontron loungers). These pod-like devices were designed to place occupants into a state of suspended animation while their consciousness was uploaded into a shared virtual reality simulation [1], [2]. The facility is powered by geothermal energy and maintained by "Robobrain" robots, allowing it to operate indefinitely without active human maintenance [3], [2].

## Location and Layout

Vault 112 is located in the western portion of the Capital Wasteland. The entrance is concealed beneath the floor of an abandoned automotive repair shop known as Smith Casey's Garage [6], [7]. The garage is situated west of the Evergreen Mills raider camp and southeast of Little Lamplight [3], [7].

To access the vault, one must activate a switch within the garage, which opens a hydraulic trapdoor in the floor, revealing the blast door entrance [8], [9]. Upon entering the vault, visitors are greeted by a Robobrain sentry. In the events of *Fallout 3*, the robot informs the player that they are "202.3 years behind schedule" and issues them a Vault 112 jumpsuit [2], [2].

The interior of the vault differs significantly from others in the game; it lacks the typical signs of violent conflict, radiation, or infestation by mutants. The main chamber houses the Tranquility Loungers arranged in a circle around a central computer mainframe [2]. An Overseer's office is located on an upper level, containing a personal terminal and a designated lounger for Dr. Braun [8].

## The Experiment

The stated purpose of Vault 112 was to preserve a select group of individuals in a "perfect" virtual society, protecting them from the ravages of nuclear war and the subsequent collapse of civilization. The residents were placed in the loungers with the expectation of living in a utopia for the duration of the experiment [1], [1].

However, the experiment was designed to give Dr. Braun absolute control over the simulation. Once the residents entered the pods, they were unable to leave voluntarily. Braun retained the ability to manipulate the virtual world, the laws of physics within it, and the memories of the inhabitants [1], [1].

Over the course of two centuries, Braun grew bored with the utopian simulations. To alleviate his boredom, he began to use the residents as "playthings" for his own amusement. He would subject them to psychological torture and death within the simulation, only to resurrect them and wipe their memories, restarting the cycle in a new setting [10], [1]. Previous iterations of the simulation included scenarios such as "Toucan Lagoon" (where residents were eaten by sharks) and a winter-themed "Slalom Chalet" [11], [12].

## Tranquility Lane Simulation

At the time of the player's arrival in 2277, the active simulation is "Tranquility Lane," a monochrome, idealized depiction of a 1950s American suburban neighborhood [10], [13]. The environment consists of a cul-de-sac with pristine houses, manicured lawns, and cheerful music, standing in sharp contrast to the destroyed reality of the Capital Wasteland outside the vault.

### Residents and Avatars
Dr. Braun inhabits the simulation using the avatar of a young girl named "Betty." Through this guise, he rules over the neighborhood with a sadistic demeanor [14], [4]. The other residents appear as ordinary suburbanites, largely unaware that they are in a simulation or that they have been trapped for centuries.

Notable residents include:
*   **Old Lady Dithers:** An elderly resident who is the only inhabitant other than Braun to realize that Tranquility Lane is a simulation. She is aware of the "failsafe" mechanism and attempts to warn the newcomer [14], [14].
*   **The Neusbaums, Rockwells, Hendersons, and Simpsons:** Families subjected to Braun's torment.
*   **Doc:** A dog roaming the neighborhood. This is revealed to be the avatar of James, the protagonist's father, whom Braun has trapped in the simulation [14], [4].

### The Quest
Upon entering an empty lounger, the Lone Wanderer appears in Tranquility Lane as a child. To escape the simulation and rescue James, the player must interact with Betty (Braun). Betty refuses to release James unless the player engages in a series of sadistic pranks and murders against the other residents [14], [15].

These tasks include:
*   Making a child resident, Timmy Neusbaum, cry (options include striking him or telling him his parents are divorcing) [14].
*   Breaking up the marriage of the Rockwells [15].
*   Arranging the death of Mabel Henderson (e.g., by tampering with a chandelier or a kitchen appliance) [14], [15].
*   Finally, donning a mask and knife (the "Pint-Sized Slasher" costume) to massacre all residents of the simulation [15].

### The Failsafe
Alternatively, the player can bypass Betty's tasks by activating an emergency failsafe program. This option is hinted at by Old Lady Dithers [14]. The failsafe terminal is hidden inside the "Abandoned House" within the simulation. Accessing the terminal requires interacting with a specific sequence of objects (Radio, Pitcher, Gnome, Pitcher, Cinder Block, Gnome, Bottle) that play musical tones corresponding to the simulation's background music [16], [17].

Activating the failsafe initiates the "Chinese Invasion" program. This simulation spawns Chinese soldiers who kill the residents of Tranquility Lane. Unlike the deaths orchestrated by Betty, the deaths caused by the failsafe are permanent; the safety protocols are disengaged, killing the residents in the real world as well [14], [17]. This action breaks the cycle of torture, permanently ending the simulation. Dr. Braun is left alive but trapped alone in the empty virtual world, unable to exit or reset the program [10], [4].

## Role in Plot

Vault 112 is a mandatory destination in the *Fallout 3* main questline "Scientific Pursuits" (or "The Waters of Life," depending on quest progression). James travels to the vault seeking Dr. Braun, hoping to obtain information or technology related to the G.E.C.K. to repair the Project Purity water purification system [14], [18].

Upon arriving, James is tricked by Braun and imprisoned in the simulation. When the Lone Wanderer frees James (either by appeasing Braun or triggering the failsafe), the two reunite in the physical vault. James reveals that he did not find the G.E.C.K. in Vault 112 but learned that it could be found in Vault 87 [5], [18]. They subsequently leave Vault 112 to return to Rivet City.

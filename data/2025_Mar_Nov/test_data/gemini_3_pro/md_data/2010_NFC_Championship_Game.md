# 2010 NFC Championship Game

The **2010 NFC Championship Game** was a National Football League (NFL) postseason game played on January 23, 2011, to determine the National Football Conference (NFC) champion for the 2010 NFL season. The visiting Green Bay Packers defeated the Chicago Bears by a score of 21–14 at Soldier Field in Chicago, Illinois. With the victory, the Packers advanced to Super Bowl XLV, where they subsequently defeated the Pittsburgh Steelers [1][2].

The game was notable for being only the second postseason meeting in the history of the Bears–Packers rivalry, which is the oldest rivalry in the NFL [1][3]. The contest was characterized by a defensive struggle, the controversial exit of Bears starting quarterback Jay Cutler due to injury, and a late-game rally led by Chicago's third-string quarterback, Caleb Hanie [1][4].

## Background

### Rivalry Context
The Green Bay Packers and Chicago Bears have played each other since 1921. Despite meeting over 180 times prior to this game, the two franchises had faced each other in the NFL playoffs only once before: the 1941 Western Division playoff, which the Bears won 33–14 [1][3][5]. The 2010 NFC Championship Game marked the 182nd overall meeting between the clubs [3].

### 2010 Season
The **Chicago Bears**, led by head coach Lovie Smith, finished the 2010 regular season with an 11–5 record, securing the NFC North division title and the No. 2 seed in the NFC playoffs [1]. The Bears earned a first-round bye and defeated the Seattle Seahawks 35–24 in the Divisional Round to advance to the conference championship [1][6]. Their defense was statistically strong, ranking in the top five for points allowed and second against the run [1].

The **Green Bay Packers**, coached by Mike McCarthy, finished the regular season with a 10–6 record, earning the No. 6 seed as a Wild Card entrant [1]. To reach the NFC Championship, the Packers defeated the Philadelphia Eagles 21–16 in the Wild Card Round and the top-seeded Atlanta Falcons 48–21 in the Divisional Round [1]. By reaching the conference title game, the Packers became the first NFC No. 6 seed to advance to the Super Bowl [2].

## Game Summary

### First Half
The game began with the Green Bay Packers executing an efficient opening drive. Quarterback Aaron Rodgers completed passes of 22 and 26 yards to wide receiver Greg Jennings, followed by a 22-yard completion to Jordy Nelson. Rodgers capped the 84-yard drive with a 1-yard rushing touchdown, giving Green Bay an early 7–0 lead [1][7].

The remainder of the first quarter and much of the second quarter was defined by defensive play, with both teams exchanging punts. The Bears' offense, led by quarterback Jay Cutler, struggled to move the ball effectively against the Packers' defense. In the second quarter, Green Bay mounted a 5-play, 49-yard drive that concluded with rookie running back James Starks scoring on a 4-yard touchdown run, extending the Packers' lead to 14–0 [1][7].

Near the end of the first half, the Bears attempted to score before the intermission. However, a deep pass from Cutler intended for Johnny Knox was intercepted by Packers cornerback Sam Shields, preserving Green Bay's 14-point lead at halftime [1][3].

### Second Half
The complexion of the game changed in the third quarter when Jay Cutler left the game with a knee injury. He was replaced by backup quarterback Todd Collins, who was ineffective, failing to complete a pass during his brief appearance [1][8]. The Bears then turned to their third-string quarterback, Caleb Hanie [1].

Entering the fourth quarter trailing 14–0, Hanie led the Bears on a 67-yard drive. Running back Chester Taylor scored on a 1-yard touchdown run to cut the deficit to 14–7 [1][7].

With approximately six minutes remaining in the game, the Bears possessed the ball deep in their own territory. On a zone blitz called by Packers defensive coordinator Dom Capers, nose tackle B.J. Raji dropped into coverage and intercepted a short pass from Hanie. Raji returned the interception 18 yards for a touchdown, performing a celebratory dance in the end zone. The score extended Green Bay's lead to 21–7 [1][9][10].

The Bears responded quickly. Hanie connected with wide receiver Earl Bennett for a 35-yard touchdown pass with 4:43 remaining, bringing the score to 21–14 [1][7]. After the Bears' defense forced a punt, Chicago regained possession with a chance to tie the game. Hanie drove the offense to the Packers' 29-yard line. However, on a fourth-down play with under a minute remaining, Hanie threw a pass intended for Johnny Knox that was intercepted by Sam Shields—his second interception of the game—effectively sealing the victory for Green Bay [1][3].

## Jay Cutler Injury Controversy

A significant aftermath of the game involved the reaction to Jay Cutler's exit. Cutler left the game in the third quarter with a knee injury and did not return, though he was seen standing on the sideline for the remainder of the contest. This led to immediate criticism from fans, media, and fellow NFL players on social media platforms like Twitter, who questioned his toughness and dedication [4][13].

Notable players such as Maurice Jones-Drew, Darnell Dockett, and Deion Sanders posted critical comments during the game. Jones-Drew tweeted, "All I'm saying is that he can finish the game on a hurt knee... I played the whole season on one," while Sanders wrote, "Folks i never question a players injury but i do question a players heart" [4][13].

Following the game, the Bears organization and teammates defended Cutler. Head coach Lovie Smith stated, "He was hurt and he couldn't go. The trainers and doctors ... made that decision" [13]. Teammate Brian Urlacher called Cutler "tough as hell" and dismissed the critics as "jealous people" [4][14]. Medical evaluations the following day revealed that Cutler had suffered a Grade II sprain of his medial collateral ligament (MCL) [14][15].

## Aftermath

The Green Bay Packers advanced to Super Bowl XLV, played on February 6, 2011, at Cowboys Stadium in Arlington, Texas. They defeated the Pittsburgh Steelers 31–25 to secure the franchise's fourth Super Bowl title and 13th overall NFL championship [1][2]. Aaron Rodgers was named the Super Bowl MVP [1].

For the Chicago Bears, the loss marked the end of their 2010 season. The game remained a point of contention regarding Cutler's legacy in Chicago, although he returned to lead the team in subsequent seasons [1][16]. The Bears did not return to the playoffs until the 2018 season [1].

# Lux (Doctor Who)

"**Lux**" is the second episode of the fifteenth series of the British science fiction television series *Doctor Who*. It was first broadcast on BBC One and Disney+ on 19 April 2025. The episode was written by showrunner Russell T Davies and directed by Amanda Brotchie.

The episode stars Ncuti Gatwa as the Fifteenth Doctor and Varada Sethu as his companion, Belinda Chandra. It features a guest appearance by Alan Cumming, who provides the voice for the antagonist, Lux Imperator (also known as Mr. Ring-a-Ding). Set in 1952 Miami, the story follows the Doctor and Belinda as they investigate a cinema where patrons have mysteriously disappeared, leading to a confrontation with a living cartoon character who is revealed to be a member of the Pantheon of Discord.

"Lux" is notable for its extensive use of 2D animation within a live-action format, paying homage to the rubber hose animation style of the 1930s.

## Plot

The Fifteenth Doctor and Belinda Chandra attempt to pilot the TARDIS back to 24 May 2025, but the ship instead lands in Miami, Florida, in 1952. While exploring the era, they discover the Palazzo Cinema, a movie theater that has been chained shut following the unexplained disappearance of fifteen people over the preceding months [1]. Despite the closures, the projector continues to run nightly.

Inside the cinema, the travelers encounter Reginald Pye, the projectionist, who is grieving his late wife. It is revealed that months prior, a beam of moonlight reflected off a spoon and struck a film strip of the cartoon character "Mr. Ring-a-Ding," bringing the animation to life [2][3]. The entity, initially appearing as the jovial cartoon character, is actually Lux Imperator, the God of Light and a member of the Pantheon of Discord [2][4]. Lux has trapped the missing cinema patrons inside individual frames of film strips [5]. He has coerced Reginald into feeding him light by playing movies; in exchange, Lux uses his powers to manifest a living projection of Reginald's deceased wife [5][6].

When the Doctor intervenes, Lux drops his "Mr. Ring-a-Ding" persona and reveals his true nature. He attacks the Doctor and Belinda, trapping them within the film reel. The pair are transformed into two-dimensional animated characters and transported into a surreal, cartoon version of Miami [5][7]. Within this 2D world, they struggle with "two-dimensional brains" until they share a moment of emotional vulnerability; this emotional depth renders them three-dimensional, allowing them to break free from the cartoon logic [5][8].

In a metafictional sequence during their escape, the Doctor and Belinda briefly breach the "fourth wall," entering a living room where they encounter three *Doctor Who* fans watching the events unfold on television [8][9]. The fans—Lizzie, Hassan, and Robyn—assist the protagonists, realizing that within the context of the trap, they themselves are fictional constructs created by Lux [8][9].

Returning to the live-action world, the Doctor realizes Lux intends to consume his Time Lord regeneration energy to construct a permanent physical body [5]. Reginald Pye, encouraged by the projection of his wife, sacrifices himself to stop the entity. He ignites the highly flammable nitrate film reels, causing an explosion that blows the roof off the cinema [1][10]. The sudden exposure to direct sunlight overwhelms Lux. As the God of Light, he absorbs the solar energy uncontrollably, expanding infinitely until he dissipates into the universe [1][10].

With Lux defeated, the trapped victims are released from the film strips and returned to the real world [6]. As the Doctor and Belinda depart, the recurring character Mrs. Flood appears, breaking the fourth wall to address the audience regarding the TARDIS's departure [10][11].

## Production

### Development
"Lux" was written by showrunner Russell T Davies [10]. Davies had harbored the concept of an episode featuring a living cartoon for over two decades but was previously unable to produce it due to budgetary constraints [10]. In earlier iterations of the idea, the antagonist was conceived as a hologram to reduce technical demands, but the increased budget of the fifteenth series allowed for full 2D animation integration [10].

The character of Lux Imperator was established as a member of the Pantheon of Discord, a group of god-like entities introduced in the show's 2023 specials. Lux joins other members of the Pantheon such as the Toymaker (the God of Games), Maestro (the God of Music), and Sutekh (the God of Death) [2][12]. The episode explores themes of light and entertainment, with Davies describing the villain as the "dazzle at the heart of the Pantheon" [2].

### Casting
Ncuti Gatwa and Varada Sethu returned as the Fifteenth Doctor and Belinda Chandra, respectively [2]. The episode features Alan Cumming as the voice of Mr. Ring-a-Ding/Lux Imperator [2]. This marked Cumming's second appearance in the *Doctor Who* franchise; he previously portrayed King James I in the Series 11 episode "The Witchfinders" (2018) [12]. Davies stated that while he hesitated to cast the same actor twice, the voice-over nature of the role made it feasible [10].

Linus Roache guest-starred as the projectionist Reginald Pye [11]. The cast also included Anita Dobson, reprising her recurring role as Mrs. Flood [11]. The trio of fans in the meta-textual sequence were played by Bronté Barbé, Samir Arrian, and Steph Lacey [9].

### Animation and Effects
The animation for Mr. Ring-a-Ding was produced by the visual effects studio Framestore, led by creative director Ian Spendloff [13][14]. The production team aimed for authenticity to the 1930s "rubber hose" animation style associated with Fleischer Studios [5][13].

To achieve this look, the animation was created using traditional hand-drawn techniques at 25 frames per second, rather than using 3D models rendered to look like 2D [13]. The animators utilized TVPaint software to digitize the hand-drawn process [13]. The character design for Mr. Ring-a-Ding included a pig-like nose and blue skin, selected from approximately thirty different sketches drafted by Spendloff [10]. The visual effects team also created the sentient film strips and the transformation sequences where the lead actors become cartoons [13].

## Broadcast and Reception

### Broadcast
"Lux" premiered on 19 April 2025 [7]. It was broadcast on BBC One in the United Kingdom and released simultaneously on Disney+ in international markets [7].

### Critical Reception
The episode received positive reviews from critics, who praised its visual creativity and the performance of Alan Cumming. *Doctor Who Magazine* highlighted the authenticity of the animation, noting the "painstaking" work by Framestore [13]. *Screen Rant* described the episode as an "instantly classic outing" that provided a "step-up in fun and storytelling," praising the chemistry between Gatwa and Sethu [15].

Reviewers noted the episode's tonal similarities to the previous season's "The Devil's Chord," citing the period setting and the presence of a theatrical, god-like villain [16][17]. *Den of Geek* called it an "energetic, inventive follow-up," though noted the risk of diminishing returns with the recurring Pantheon motif [16]. *Outfront Magazine* commended the episode for utilizing the series' increased budget effectively, describing the villain as an "Art Deco menace" and praising the balance between "wacky self-parody" and "metaphysical" stakes [18].

The metafictional scene involving the *Doctor Who* fans drew specific attention. *Bleeding Cool* noted that the sequence "gently satirizes but ultimately celebrates" the fandom, rather than mocking it [9].

# Tony Hawk's Pro Skater 2 (Game Boy Advance video game)

**Tony Hawk's Pro Skater 2** is a skateboarding video game developed by Vicarious Visions and published by Activision for the Game Boy Advance (GBA). It was released as a launch title for the system in North America on June 11, 2001, and in Europe on June 22, 2001 [1][2]. It is a handheld adaptation of the console game of the same name, which was originally developed by Neversoft.

Distinct from its home console counterparts, which utilize a fully three-dimensional (3D) third-person perspective, the Game Boy Advance version employs an isometric viewpoint with pre-rendered backgrounds and 3D character models [1][2]. The game tasks players with controlling a professional skateboarder to perform tricks, complete objectives, and achieve high scores within a time limit.

Upon release, the game received critical acclaim, with reviewers praising its faithful translation of the console gameplay mechanics to a handheld format, its fluid controls, and its technical performance [3][4]. It is often cited as one of the highest-rated games in the Game Boy Advance library [5].

## Gameplay

*Tony Hawk's Pro Skater 2* on the Game Boy Advance retains the core gameplay formula of the series, requiring players to perform skateboarding tricks to earn points and complete level-specific goals. Unlike the PlayStation and Nintendo 64 versions, which feature a behind-the-back camera, this version utilizes a fixed isometric perspective [2][6].

### Controls and Mechanics
The game uses a control scheme adapted for the GBA's limited button layout. Players use the D-pad to steer the skater, the "B" button to jump (ollie), and the "A" button to grind on rails or edges. Flip tricks and grab tricks are mapped to the "L" and "R" shoulder buttons combined with directional inputs [7][8].

A significant mechanic introduced in *Tony Hawk's Pro Skater 2* is the "manual," a trick that allows players to balance on two wheels while riding on flat ground. This mechanic enables players to link aerial tricks and grinds together into extended combinations, or "combos," which exponentially increase the player's score [6][9]. The game also features a "Special Meter" that fills as the player successfully lands tricks. When the meter is full, the player can execute character-specific "Special Moves" that yield high scores [7][8].

### Modes
The game features three primary modes of play:
*   **Career Mode:** The main progression mode where players must complete specific objectives across various levels. Goals include achieving high scores, collecting the letters S-K-A-T-E, finding a hidden "Secret Tape," and performing specific tricks on level geometry [2][10]. Completing goals earns cash, which can be used to purchase stat points to improve the skater's abilities (such as speed, balance, and hangtime) or to buy new skateboard decks and tricks [11][12].
*   **Single Session:** A two-minute run on any unlocked level where the player attempts to set a high score without the specific checklist of Career Mode goals [2][8].
*   **Free Skate:** A practice mode with no time limit, allowing players to explore levels and practice tricks without pressure [2][13].

Due to hardware limitations and cartridge storage constraints, the GBA version omits the "Create-A-Skater" and "Park Editor" modes found in the console versions [14][15]. Multiplayer modes are also absent [4].

### Levels
The game contains seven levels. Most are adaptations of stages from the console version of *Tony Hawk's Pro Skater 2*, though the layouts were modified to accommodate the isometric viewpoint [16]. The game also includes a level from the first *Tony Hawk's Pro Skater* and a GBA-exclusive level.

The levels included are:
*   Hangar (Texas)
*   School II (Southern California)
*   Marseille (France)
*   NY City (New York)
*   Skatestreet (Ventura)
*   Warehouse (Troy, NY) – Originally from *Tony Hawk's Pro Skater* [4][10].
*   Rooftops (Boston) – An exclusive level not found in the console versions [10][17].

## Characters

The game features a roster of 13 professional skateboarders, mirroring the lineup of the console release. Each skater possesses different starting statistics and a unique set of special moves [7][18].

The playable professionals are:
*   Tony Hawk
*   Bob Burnquist
*   Steve Caballero
*   Kareem Campbell
*   Rune Glifberg
*   Eric Koston
*   Bucky Lasek
*   Rodney Mullen
*   Chad Muska
*   Andrew Reynolds
*   Geoff Rowley

In addition to the professional roster, the game includes unlockable characters. Spider-Man appears as a hidden skater, consistent with his inclusion in the console versions, developed using the same engine as Activision's *Spider-Man* game [2][9]. Another unlockable character is Mindy, who replaces the characters Officer Dick and Private Carrera found in other versions [9][19].

## Development

The Game Boy Advance version of *Tony Hawk's Pro Skater 2* was developed by Vicarious Visions, a studio based in Albany, New York [1][16]. Development began in August 2000 and concluded in April 2001, lasting approximately eight months [16].

### Concept and Engine
Prior to this project, Vicarious Visions had worked on the Game Boy Color version of *Spider-Man*. When Nintendo provided the studio with Game Boy Advance development kits in April 2000, the team brainstormed potential launch titles. While 2D side-scrollers were the standard for handhelds, studio CEO Karthik Bala proposed an isometric adaptation of *Tony Hawk's Pro Skater 2* [1][16].

To secure the project, the team created a technical demonstration. Lead artist Andy Lomerson produced mockups to visualize the game on the handheld hardware. Karthik Bala presented these concepts to Activision executives and Tony Hawk personally at E3 2000, leading to the project's approval [1][20].

The developers created a custom 3D engine for the game. Unlike the Game Boy Color ports, which were 2D side-scrollers, the GBA version utilized an isometric perspective. The skater is rendered as a real-time 3D polygonal model composed of over 300 polygons, moving within pre-rendered, static environments [2][11]. This approach allowed the team to replicate the physics and trick system of the console versions more accurately than a 2D sprite-based engine would allow [1][8]. The developers ported a significant portion of the original PlayStation code to the GBA to ensure the controls felt authentic [8].

### Audio
Due to the limited storage capacity of the Game Boy Advance cartridge, the licensed soundtrack featuring bands like Rage Against the Machine and Papa Roach could not be included [2][4]. Instead, the game features an original instrumental soundtrack composed by Manfred Linzner of Shin'en Multimedia. The music was created using the GAX sound engine and was designed as a pastiche of the skate-punk and rock genres associated with the series [2].

## Reception

*Tony Hawk's Pro Skater 2* for the Game Boy Advance received "universal acclaim" upon release, according to review aggregator Metacritic, where it holds a score of 95 out of 100 [5]. It is tied with *The Legend of Zelda: A Link to the Past* as the highest-rated game on the platform [5].

Critics praised the game as a technical achievement for the handheld system. *IGN* awarded the game a 9.5 out of 10, calling it a "technical marvel" and noting that it provided the first true polygonal gaming experience on the GBA [4][21]. The review highlighted the fluidity of the character animation and the responsiveness of the controls [4]. *GameSpot* gave the game a 9.3 out of 10, describing it as a "superb" translation of the console experience [22].

The control scheme was widely lauded for successfully mapping the complex trick system of the console game to the GBA's limited buttons. Reviewers noted that despite the lack of analog input, the digital controls were precise and allowed for complex combos [8][11].

However, some criticism was directed at the isometric perspective. Reviewers noted that the fixed camera angle could occasionally make it difficult to judge distances or line up jumps with ramps, particularly when the view was obstructed by foreground objects [3][23]. The omission of the "Create-A-Park" and multiplayer modes was also noted as a drawback compared to the home console releases [4][15].

### Awards and Accolades
The game received an Editor's Choice award from *IGN* [21]. It was recognized by various publications as one of the best launch titles for the Game Boy Advance and has appeared on multiple lists of the greatest games for the system [4][24].

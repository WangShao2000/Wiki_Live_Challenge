# Banshee (collection)

**Banshee** is the Autumn/Winter 1994 fashion collection by British designer Alexander McQueen. It was the designer's fourth collection and his second professional runway show. Staged at the Café de Paris in London on February 26, 1994, the collection is noted for its exploration of Irish folklore, specifically the myth of the banshee, and for establishing the dark, romantic, and often controversial aesthetic that defined McQueen's career. The collection was revisited and referenced significantly by Seán McGirr, the creative director of the Alexander McQueen brand, for his Spring/Summer 2025 collection.

## Background and Inspiration

Following his graduation from Central Saint Martins and his "Taxi Driver" (Autumn/Winter 1993) and "Nihilism" (Spring/Summer 1994) collections, McQueen continued to develop his signature style with **Banshee**. The collection was presented during London Fashion Week in February 1994 [1].

The primary inspiration for the collection was the banshee (Irish: *bean sí*), a female spirit in Irish folklore whose wailing is said to foretell the death of a family member [2][3]. McQueen, who had Irish ancestry, drew upon stories he had heard regarding banshees wailing after shipwrecks [3][4]. The designer sought to channel the "pent up anger and injustice" often associated with his work into a narrative about female power and resilience [5].

The collection also drew cinematic inspiration, specifically from the 1967 Luis Buñuel film *Belle de Jour*, exploring themes of repressed and liberated sexuality [4].

## Design and Aesthetics

**Banshee** blended McQueen's sharp tailoring skills, honed during his apprenticeship on Savile Row, with experimental and gothic elements [4][6]. The collection featured a mix of "gothic, romance, and history," establishing a template for the house's future aesthetic [6].

### Garments and Materials
The clothes combined severe tailoring with delicate, distressed materials. Key elements included:
*   **Tailoring:** Frock coats and naval dress jackets featuring military braiding, reflecting a subversion of traditional uniforms [4][7].
*   **"Bumsters":** The collection continued McQueen's use of "bumster" trousers—pants with an extremely low waistband designed to elongate the torso—which had caused a sensation in his previous shows [6][7].
*   **Fabrics:** The designer utilized sheer black mousseline, chiffon, and wool [7][8]. Garments were often distressed, painted, or printed with bleached-out brush-stroke effects [2].
*   **The Plaster Corset:** A significant piece in the collection was a white plaster cast corset moulded on chicken wire. It was modeled by Rebecca Lowthorpe, who later described the piece as rigid and heavy, leaving welts on her skin [7].

### Imagery
The collection's imagery was dark and foreboding. A rare tailcoat from the collection, sold at auction, was described as being made of dark brown faille with bleached effects, inspired by the legend of the banshee washing the blood-stained clothes of those about to die [2].

## Runway Show

The runway presentation for **Banshee** took place at the Café de Paris, a nightclub in London [2][9]. The production was described by participants as "ad hoc" and chaotic, characteristic of McQueen's early, low-budget presentations [7].

### Casting and Atmosphere
McQueen cast a mix of professional models and friends. Notable appearances included:
*   **Isabella Blow:** The fashion editor and McQueen's muse walked in the show wearing knickers and an Admiral's coat with military braiding [7][8].
*   **Pregnant Model:** In a moment that became widely cited in fashion history, a heavily pregnant woman (often described as a skinhead) walked the runway wearing a sheer black mousseline gown [4][7][10].
*   **Styling:** The models' hair was slicked flat, and the name "McQueen" was stenciled in silver spray paint onto their heads [4][7][8].

### Production
There were no fittings prior to the show; McQueen decided on the outfits immediately before the models went out [7]. The atmosphere backstage was intense, with McQueen instructing models not to smile to maintain a sombre mood [7]. The soundtrack featured the sounds of wailing banshees [8][11].

## Reception and Legacy

While early McQueen shows were often met with shock, **Banshee** helped cement his reputation for combining technical virtuosity with theatrical storytelling. The collection is considered the foundation for the "Alexander McQueen" brand aesthetic, merging history with raw emotion [6].

Garments from the collection are held in museum archives. For example, the National Gallery of Victoria (NGV) holds a dress from the **Banshee** collection made of a wool blend and plastic thread [12].

### 2025 Tribute
The **Banshee** collection received renewed attention in September 2024, when Seán McGirr, the creative director of Alexander McQueen, presented his Spring/Summer 2025 collection in Paris [11]. McGirr explicitly cited the 1994 **Banshee** collection as his primary inspiration [9][13].

McGirr's tribute reinterpreted the folklore and the 1994 show's themes:
*   **Themes:** McGirr described the banshee as a "guiding force" and a figure of "strident self-expression" rather than just a harbinger of death [3][13].
*   **Design References:** The SS25 collection featured "twisted" tailoring, shredded organza, and "banshee hair" embroidery designed to look like wind-whipped hair [13].
*   **Atmosphere:** The show notes included a quote from McQueen about the banshee, and the runway was styled with fog and mist to evoke the liminal spaces of Irish mythology [9][14].

# Erik Campbell (Final Destination)

**Erik Campbell** is a fictional character in the *Final Destination* film franchise. He serves as a main character in the 2025 horror film *Final Destination: Bloodlines*, the sixth installment in the series. Portrayed by Canadian actor Richard Harmon, Erik is introduced as a tattoo artist and the eldest sibling in the Campbell family.

The character's storyline centers on the "Campbell bloodline," which becomes a target for Death following a premonition experienced by his grandmother, Iris Campbell, in the 1960s. Erik is notable within the franchise for a plot twist regarding his parentage, which initially excludes him from Death's design, and for his eventual demise involving a magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) machine, which critics and filmmakers have cited as a standout sequence in the series.

## Character arc
### Background
Erik Campbell is introduced as a resident of Cloverdale, New York, working as a tattoo artist [1]. He is the son of Brenda Campbell and the older brother of Bobby and Julia Campbell. He is also the cousin of the film's protagonist, Stefani Reyes, and her brother Charlie [2]. The family history is defined by Erik's grandmother, Iris Campbell, who in 1969 had a premonition of a collapse at the Sky View restaurant. Her intervention saved her life and the lives of others, but inadvertently placed her future descendants on Death's list to correct the imbalance [3].

### *Final Destination: Bloodlines*
In *Final Destination: Bloodlines*, Erik is initially presented as a skeptic regarding his cousin Stefani's warnings about the family curse. He possesses a rebellious personality, characterized by a sarcastic sense of humor and an alternative fashion style featuring numerous tattoos and piercings [2]. Following the death of his stepfather, Howard Campbell, via a lawnmower accident, Erik is believed to be the next target in the family lineage based on age hierarchy [4].

Erik survives a near-fatal incident at his tattoo parlor. While closing up, a chain from a ceiling fan becomes entangled with his nose ring, nearly dragging him into a fire caused by spilled cleaning fluid. He survives the fire largely due to the protection offered by his leather clothing [5]. Shortly thereafter, his sister Julia is killed by a garbage truck, disrupting the expected order of death [5].

This anomaly leads to the revelation that Erik is not the biological son of Howard Campbell. His mother, Brenda, discloses that Erik was the product of an extramarital affair with a man named Jerry Fenbury [2]. Consequently, Erik is not a blood descendant of Iris Campbell and was never originally part of Death's design [6].

Despite his safety from the lineage-based curse, Erik intervenes to save his younger half-brother, Bobby, who is next on the list. The family consults William Bludworth, a recurring franchise character, who explains that the design can be broken if a victim dies and is resuscitated [5]. Erik attempts to facilitate this by intentionally triggering Bobby's severe peanut allergy in a hospital setting, planning to revive him with an EpiPen immediately after clinical death [7].

The plan fails when they enter a room containing an active MRI machine. The machine malfunctions, and its powerful magnetic field rips Erik's facial and body piercings from his skin. Subsequently, a metal wheelchair is pulled across the room by the magnet, impaling Erik and crushing him against the machine, folding his body in half [7]. His death is attributed to the franchise rule that interfering with Death's design leads to unforeseen and brutal consequences [4].

## Development
### Casting
Erik Campbell is portrayed by Richard Harmon. Harmon, a self-described lifelong fan of the *Final Destination* franchise, learned of the production through his agent and auditioned for both the role of Erik and the character's grandfather, Paul [1]. Due to the 2023 SAG-AFTRA strike, the casting process was delayed, and Harmon did not receive confirmation of his role for eight months after his audition [1].

### Characterization and original concepts
Directors Zach Lipovsky and Adam Stein stated that they aimed to create characters with "layers" rather than one-dimensional victims [1]. In early drafts of the screenplay, the character of Erik was written as a video game streamer rather than a tattoo artist. In this original iteration, he was scripted to die while playing a virtual reality game during a live stream [1].

The filmmakers utilized the character to explore the theme of Death's "playfulness." The sequence in the tattoo parlor, where Erik narrowly escapes death, was designed as a "misdirect." Director Adam Stein compared Death in this scene to a fisherman who might "hook the fish and then throw 'em back," toying with the victim before the final kill [8].

## Reception
### Critical response
Richard Harmon's performance was generally well-received, with some critics identifying him as a "scene-stealer" who provided effective comic relief [1]. The character has been described as a "fan favorite" following the film's release [1]. Media outlets noted similarities between Erik and the character Ian McKinley from *Final Destination 3*, citing their shared skepticism and alternative aesthetic [9].

### Analysis of death scene
Erik Campbell's death scene involving the MRI machine garnered significant attention from critics and audiences. *Dexerto* described the sequence as "metal" and noted the "cruel irony" that Erik, who was not originally on the list, suffered one of the film's most gruesome fates due to his piercings and the magnetic force [7]. *Screen Rant* characterized it as the "most brutal death" in the film, highlighting the narrative implication that "messing with Death" results in messy consequences [4]. The scene was praised for its sound design and the visceral nature of the practical effects [7].

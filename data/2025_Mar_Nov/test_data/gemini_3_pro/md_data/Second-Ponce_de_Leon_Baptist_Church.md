# Second-Ponce de Leon Baptist Church

**Second-Ponce de Leon Baptist Church** (often abbreviated as **SPdL**) is a Baptist congregation located at 2715 Peachtree Road in the Buckhead district of Atlanta, Georgia [1]. The church was established in November 1932 through the merger of two historic Atlanta congregations: Second Baptist Church (founded 1854) and Ponce de Leon Baptist Church (founded 1904) [1][2].

The church is noted for its Georgian Colonial architecture, its role in the establishment of Mercer University's Atlanta campus, and its dual affiliation with both the Southern Baptist Convention (SBC) and the Cooperative Baptist Fellowship (CBF) [1][3]. As of late 2025, the church is in a period of pastoral transition following the tenure of the Rev. Dr. Dock Hollingsworth [4][5].

## History

The history of Second-Ponce de Leon Baptist Church spans back to the mid-19th century through its predecessor organizations. The modern entity was formed to survive the economic hardships of the Great Depression [1].

### Predecessor Churches
**Second Baptist Church** was founded on September 1, 1854, in downtown Atlanta [1][2]. It was the second Baptist congregation established in the city, following First Baptist Church of Atlanta (1848) [1]. During the American Civil War, the church building was spared from destruction during the Burning of Atlanta in 1864. This preservation is attributed to the intercession of Father Thomas O'Reilly, a Catholic priest at the nearby Immaculate Conception Church, who persuaded Union forces to spare several houses of worship [2][4].

**Ponce de Leon Baptist Church** was established on October 6, 1904, with 115 charter members, many of whom transferred from First Baptist and Second Baptist [1]. Originally located at the corner of Ponce de Leon Avenue and Piedmont Avenue, the congregation served the growing population in the northern section of the city [6]. By 1929, as the city expanded northward, the congregation purchased property on Peachtree Road in Buckhead for a new sanctuary [1].

### Merger (1932)
By the early 1930s, both Second Baptist and Ponce de Leon Baptist faced declining attendance in their downtown locations and financial strain exacerbated by the Great Depression [1]. To ensure their survival and continued ministry, the two congregations voted to merge. The merger was officially completed in November 1932 [1][2]. The consolidated congregation adopted the name Second-Ponce de Leon Baptist Church and occupied the property on Peachtree Road previously purchased by Ponce de Leon Baptist [2][6].

### Mid-20th Century Growth
The church experienced significant growth during the mid-20th century, particularly under the leadership of Monroe F. Swilley Jr., who served as pastor starting in 1945 [1]. By 1954, the congregation had grown to approximately 3,600 members [1].

During this era, the church played a central role in the expansion of Baptist higher education in Atlanta. In the late 1940s and 1950s, Swilley and the congregation were instrumental in the founding of Atlanta Baptist College. Swilley left the pastorate in 1969 to become the college's first president. This institution later merged with Mercer University in 1972 to become the university's Atlanta campus [3].

### Late 20th Century to Present
In the 1970s, the church was led by Russell H. Dilday. During this period, the Southern Baptist Convention experienced internal conflict between conservative and moderate factions. Second-Ponce de Leon became a spiritual home for approximately 200 members, including several deacons, who left First Baptist Church of Atlanta following the appointment of Charles Stanley as pastor [1][7]. Dilday later served as the president of the Southwestern Baptist Theological Seminary [8].

In 2021, the church announced it was considering a redevelopment plan for a portion of its 12-acre property to create a missions and ministry endowment, although the historic sanctuary would remain preserved [9].

## Architecture

The current sanctuary, dedicated on October 10, 1937, was designed by architect George Harwell Bond [1]. The building is constructed in the Georgian Colonial and Neocolonial styles, drawing inspiration from historic colonial meeting houses and churches in New England [1].

### Exterior and Interior
The exterior features a white-painted brick facade and a prominent steeple that rises 160 feet (49 meters) [1]. The church bell, originally installed in the Second Baptist Church building in downtown Atlanta, was moved to the new location and installed in December 1939 [1].

The sanctuary auditorium measures 68 feet by 87 feet, with a ceiling height of 36 feet. It features balconies on both sides and originally had a seating capacity of approximately 1,274 [1]. In 1990, an addition designed by the firm Peter Hand and Associates was completed to expand the church's facilities [1].

### Organ
The sanctuary houses a pipe organ built by the M. P. Möller Pipe Organ Company. Identified as Opus 9185, the instrument was installed in 1959 [10].

## Ministry and Affiliations

### Denominational Affiliation
Second-Ponce de Leon Baptist Church maintains a dual affiliation with the Southern Baptist Convention (SBC) and the Cooperative Baptist Fellowship (CBF) [1]. This dual alignment reflects the church's history during the theological shifts within the Baptist denomination in the late 20th century. While the church is listed in the SBC directory [11], it also participates in CBF life, hosting the CBF General Assembly in 2021 [1].

### Community and Missions
The church operates the **Community Wellness Center (CWC)**, a fitness and recreational facility open to the public, which serves as a ministry for physical and spiritual health [12].

Historically, the church has been active in establishing new congregations. In 1954, it established a mission church that became Wieuca Road Baptist Church (now Church at Wieuca) in North Buckhead [9][13]. The church also has a long-standing media ministry, having begun radio broadcasts in the 1930s and televised services in 1962 [4].

## Notable People

### Pastors
*   **Monroe F. Swilley Jr.** (served 1945–1969): A key figure in the establishment of Mercer University's Atlanta campus [3].
*   **Russell H. Dilday** (served 1970s): Later president of Southwestern Baptist Theological Seminary [8].
*   **Dock Hollingsworth** (served 2013–2024): A Mercer University alumnus and former faculty member who led the church for over a decade [5].

### Members
The congregation has included several prominent figures in Georgia politics and society:
*   **Joseph E. Brown**: 42nd Governor of Georgia (1857–1865) and U.S. Senator, was a member of the predecessor Second Baptist Church [1][14].
*   **Joseph Mackey Brown**: Governor of Georgia, son of Joseph E. Brown [1].
*   **Henry Dickerson McDaniel**: Governor of Georgia (1883–1886) [1].
*   **Joseph M. Terrell**: Governor of Georgia (1902–1907) [1].
*   **Carl Sanders**: Governor of Georgia (1963–1967), was a long-time member of the church [15][16].
*   **H. Ross Arnold**: Former FBI agent and attorney who helped found The Galloway School [17].

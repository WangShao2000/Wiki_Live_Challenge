# The Well (Doctor Who)

"**The Well**" is the third episode of the fifteenth series of the British science fiction television series *Doctor Who*. It was first broadcast on BBC One and Disney+ on 26 April 2025. The episode was co-written by showrunner Russell T Davies and Sharma Angel-Walfall, and directed by Amanda Brotchie.

The episode features Ncuti Gatwa as the Fifteenth Doctor and Varada Sethu as his companion, Belinda Chandra. In the narrative, the pair arrive on a mining colony situated on a planet 500,000 years in the future. They join a military rescue squad investigating a loss of contact with the base, only to discover a sole survivor, Aliss Fenly (Rose Ayling-Ellis), and a hostile force that kills anyone who stands directly behind its host. The episode serves as a direct sequel to the 2008 episode "Midnight", featuring the return of the Midnight Entity.

"The Well" received generally positive reviews from critics, with particular praise directed at the atmospheric tension and the performance of guest star Rose Ayling-Ellis. However, some reviewers expressed mixed opinions regarding the decision to demystify the antagonist of "Midnight" by featuring it in a second story.

## Plot

The Fifteenth Doctor and Belinda Chandra, attempting to return to Earth in 2025, instead land the TARDIS 500,000 years in the future on a barren world designated Planet 6-7-6-7. They encounter a platoon of troopers led by Captain Shaya Costallion, who have been deployed to investigate a silence from the planet's mining colony, Colony Base 15.

Upon entering the facility, the group finds the mining crew dead; evidence suggests they turned on one another in a violent frenzy. The only survivor is the base's cook, Aliss Fenly, who is deaf. Aliss is found in a state of extreme distress, warning the group not to stand behind her. The Doctor determines that Aliss is being shadowed by an invisible, parasitic entity. The entity is identified as the same creature the Tenth Doctor encountered on the planet Midnight; Planet 6-7-6-7 is revealed to be Midnight, moved and renamed over the millennia.

The entity's modus operandi has evolved since its previous encounter. Rather than mimicking speech, it now physically attaches itself to a host and kills anyone who steps into the space directly behind them. This mechanic forces the troopers to maintain a constant, fearful line of sight on Aliss. The Doctor attempts to trap the entity using a pool of mercury to create a 360-degree reflection, theorizing the creature cannot bear to see itself. However, the plan fails to contain the creature permanently.

As the situation deteriorates and troopers are killed, Shaya Costallion takes command of the situation. Recognizing that the creature requires a living host to anchor itself, Shaya creates a diversion. She shoots Belinda, bringing her near death to lure the creature away from Aliss, and then sacrifices herself by throwing herself—and the attached entity—into the colony's deep mining well.

The Doctor is able to resuscitate Belinda using future medical technology. Believing the threat neutralized, the Doctor and Belinda depart. However, in a closing scene aboard the troop transport ship, a surviving soldier, Mo Gilliben, is shown reporting to her superior, Mrs Flood, via video link. As the call ends, a trooper notices a presence behind Mo, implying the entity survived and escaped the planet.

## Production

### Development and Writing
"The Well" was co-written by Russell T Davies and Sharma Angel-Walfall [1][2]. It was the first episode of the 2025 series to feature a co-writer alongside the showrunner [3]. Angel-Walfall was suggested to Davies by executive producer Jane Tranter, having previously worked on other Bad Wolf productions [1].

The script originally featured a different antagonist. Ncuti Gatwa had expressed a desire to explore stories involving Orishas, spirits from the Yoruba religion of West Africa [4]. Davies and Angel-Walfall initially developed the script to feature these deities. However, during pre-production, Davies became concerned that adapting religious figures into *Doctor Who* "monsters" might be disrespectful to the faith and difficult to execute with appropriate cultural sensitivity [3][4]. Consequently, the concept was abandoned late in the process. Davies noted that the behavior of the antagonist in the draft—specifically its unseen and psychological nature—paralleled the creature from his 2008 script "Midnight", leading to the decision to rework the episode into a sequel [3][5].

### Casting
The episode stars Ncuti Gatwa as the Doctor and Varada Sethu as Belinda Chandra. The guest cast includes Caoilfhionn Dunne as Captain Shaya Costallion and Christopher Chung as Cassio Palin-Paleen [1][6].

Rose Ayling-Ellis was cast as Aliss Fenly, the deaf survivor of the colony. Ayling-Ellis, known for her role in *EastEnders* and for winning *Strictly Come Dancing*, is deaf and communicates using British Sign Language (BSL) [7][8]. The role was not originally written for a deaf actor; however, following a suggestion by casting director Andy Pryor, the script was adjusted to incorporate BSL [1]. A deaf consultant was present on set to ensure the authenticity of the sign language used by the cast, and the cast underwent BSL training [1][9].

### Filming
Filming for "The Well" took place during the third production block of Series 15, alongside the episode "Lux" [1]. Location shooting occurred at Pant-y-Ffynnon Quarry in Bridgend, Wales, in February 2024 [1]. The location had previously been used for the 2022 special "The Power of the Doctor" [1]. The shoot was described by the cast as physically demanding due to sub-zero temperatures and night shoots extending until 2:00 a.m. [1].

## Broadcast and Reception

### Broadcast
"The Well" premiered on 26 April 2025 [1][10]. In the United Kingdom, it was released on BBC iPlayer at 00:00 BST and broadcast on BBC One later that day. Internationally, it was released simultaneously on Disney+ [10][11].

### Critical Reception
The episode received generally positive reviews, with critics praising its atmosphere and tension. *Blogtor Who* commended the episode for building dread organically rather than rushing into action, noting that it "shows that *Doctor Who* can still deliver proper scares when needed" [12]. The review highlighted the performance of Ncuti Gatwa, stating that he conveyed "centuries of accumulated trauma" when realizing he had returned to Midnight [12].

Rose Ayling-Ellis's performance was widely acclaimed. *Omnibus* described her performance as "truly amazing," noting she delivered "just the right amount of doubt as to whether she really is the innocent victim or an enemy with something to hide" [9]. The integration of disability into the narrative was also praised as a step forward for representation, with the Doctor communicating fluently in BSL [9].

The decision to make the episode a sequel to "Midnight" drew mixed responses. *Den of Geek* called it a "risky decision" to follow up a "stone-cold classic," but acknowledged the episode as a "meaty drama with real chills" [13]. Conversely, a reviewer for *Doctor Who TV* felt the episode was "dragged down" by the comparison, arguing that the original's horror relied on the unknown, whereas "The Well" turned the entity into a "generic horror monster" that possessed brute strength [14]. *The Doctor Who Companion* offered a counterpoint, suggesting that keeping the creature largely unseen and mysterious helped it serve as a "shining example of how to bring a past adversary back" [15].

## Merchandise

A novelisation of the episode, written by Gareth L. Powell, was released by BBC Books as part of the *Target Collection* on 10 July 2025 [16][17]. The novelisation expands on the televised story by including background profiles for the troopers and providing greater insight into the internal monologues of the characters, including the Doctor [16][18]. An audiobook version, narrated by Caoilfhionn Dunne (who played Shaya in the episode), was released simultaneously [17][19].

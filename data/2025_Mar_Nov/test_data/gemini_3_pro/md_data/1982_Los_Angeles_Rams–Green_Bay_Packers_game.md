# 1982 Los Angeles Rams–Green Bay Packers game

The **1982 Los Angeles Rams–Green Bay Packers game** was a National Football League (NFL) regular season matchup played on September 12, 1982, at Milwaukee County Stadium in Milwaukee, Wisconsin. The game, which took place during the opening week of the 1982 NFL season, is notable for the Green Bay Packers overcoming a 23-point halftime deficit to defeat the Los Angeles Rams by a score of 35–23 [1][2].

Trailing 23–0 at halftime after committing five turnovers, the Packers scored 35 unanswered points in the second half [1][3]. This performance established a franchise record for the largest comeback victory, a mark that stood alone for 31 years until it was tied during the 2013 season [4][5]. The game marked the beginning of a strike-shortened season in which the Packers qualified for the playoffs for the first time since 1972, while the Rams finished with a losing record, leading to the dismissal of their head coach [3][6].

## Background

Entering the 1982 season, the two franchises were in different phases of development. The Los Angeles Rams, coached by Ray Malavasi, had been a dominant force in the NFC West throughout the 1970s, including an appearance in Super Bowl XIV. However, they finished the 1981 season with a 6–10 record, missing the playoffs for the first time in eight years [2][3]. In an effort to revitalize their offense, the Rams acquired veteran quarterback Bert Jones, who had previously played for the Baltimore Colts [3].

The Green Bay Packers were coached by Bart Starr, the former quarterback who had led the team to five NFL championships as a player. Entering his eighth season as head coach, Starr faced pressure to produce a winning season; the team had not reached the postseason since 1972 [3][6]. The Packers finished the 1981 season with an 8–8 record, missing a playoff berth on the final day of the season [2]. The Packers' offense was led by quarterback Lynn Dickey and featured Pro Bowl wide receivers James Lofton and John Jefferson [7].

The game was played at Milwaukee County Stadium, which served as the Packers' secondary home venue from 1953 to 1994 [1]. The attendance for the game was 53,694 [8].

## Game summary

### First half
The first half was dominated by the Los Angeles Rams, who capitalized on multiple Green Bay turnovers. The Packers turned the ball over five times in the first two quarters, including fumbles on their first three possessions [3]. The Rams opened the scoring in the first quarter with a 4-yard touchdown run by running back Wendell Tyler, followed by a 32-yard field goal from kicker Mike Lansford [2][3].

In the second quarter, the Rams continued to build their lead. Quarterback Bert Jones connected with tight end Mike Barber for an 8-yard touchdown pass, extending the lead to 17–0 [2]. Lansford added two more field goals from 20 and 28 yards out before the intermission [2]. The Packers' offense failed to score, and the team left the field at halftime trailing 23–0, having gained only 66 net yards [1].

### Second half
The momentum shifted in the third quarter as the Packers' defense began to hold the Rams, forcing "three-and-outs" on Los Angeles' first two possessions of the half [1]. Green Bay's offense, led by quarterback Lynn Dickey, began to execute scoring drives. The Packers scored their first points on a 4-yard touchdown pass from Dickey to tight end Paul Coffman [9]. Later in the quarter, running back Eddie Lee Ivery scored on a 3-yard run, cutting the deficit to 23–14 entering the fourth quarter [3][9].

Early in the fourth quarter, the Rams attempted a 51-yard field goal to extend their lead, but Lansford's kick went wide left [1][10]. Taking over at their own 34-yard line, the Packers drove down the field, capping the possession with a 15-yard touchdown pass from Dickey to James Lofton [1]. The extra point narrowed the Rams' lead to 23–21.

The turning point of the game occurred on the ensuing kickoff. Rams returner Robert Alexander fumbled the ball, which was recovered by Packers linebacker Guy Prather at the Los Angeles 10-yard line [10]. On the very next play, Dickey threw a 10-yard touchdown pass to Coffman, giving Green Bay its first lead of the game at 28–23 [2][10].

The Rams attempted to respond, but their comeback hopes were extinguished by the Packers' defense. Linebacker George Cumby intercepted a pass from Bert Jones, halting a Rams drive [1][3]. With approximately three minutes remaining, Eddie Lee Ivery broke free for a 27-yard touchdown run, securing the victory [1][3]. Linebacker John Anderson recorded another interception late in the game to seal the win [3]. The Packers scored 35 unanswered points in the second half to win 35–23.

## Statistics

Green Bay quarterback Lynn Dickey completed 17 of 27 passes for 237 yards, three touchdowns, and three interceptions [3][7]. His primary targets were John Jefferson, who caught 6 passes for 116 yards, and Paul Coffman, who recorded two touchdown receptions [3]. Running back Eddie Lee Ivery rushed for 109 yards on 17 carries and scored two touchdowns [3].

For the Rams, quarterback Bert Jones completed 17 of 31 passes for 202 yards, one touchdown, and two interceptions [3]. He was sacked four times by the Packers' defense [3]. Running back Wendell Tyler rushed for 57 yards and a touchdown [3].

The Packers outgained the Rams in total offensive yards (377 to 271) and achieved more first downs (22 to 12) [3]. The game was marred by turnovers, with Green Bay committing six (including five in the first half) and Los Angeles committing three [3].

## Aftermath

Following this game, the 1982 NFL season was interrupted by a 57-day players' strike, which reduced the schedule to nine regular-season games [6]. The Packers finished the shortened season with a 5–3–1 record, qualifying for the playoffs for the first time in the Bart Starr era and the first time overall since 1972 [6]. They defeated the St. Louis Cardinals 41–16 in the first round of the playoffs before losing to the Dallas Cowboys in the second round [11].

The Rams finished the season with a 2–7 record, placing them at the bottom of the NFC [3]. Following the season, head coach Ray Malavasi was fired [2].

The 23-point deficit overcome by the Packers stood as the largest comeback victory in the team's history for over three decades [1]. This record was tied on December 15, 2013, when the Packers rallied from a 26–3 halftime deficit (23 points) to defeat the Dallas Cowboys 37–36 [4][12].

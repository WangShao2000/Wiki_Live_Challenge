# Diego García Miravete

**Diego Leonardo García Miravete** is a Mexican American football coach and former player. He is best known for his tenure as the head coach of the Cóndores UNAM, a representative team of the National Autonomous University of Mexico (UNAM), where he won nine national championships between 1976 and 1993 [1][2]. His nine titles tie him with Roberto "Tapatío" Méndez for the most championships won by a head coach in the history of the Organización Nacional Estudiantil de Fútbol Americano (ONEFA) [1].

García Miravete has also served as the head coach for the Auténticos Tigres of the Autonomous University of Nuevo León (UANL), the Borregos Salvajes of the Monterrey Institute of Technology and Higher Education (ITESM) at the Toluca and Santa Fe campuses, and the Badalona Dracs in Spain [1][3]. Additionally, he coached the Mexican junior national team to victories in the NFL Global Junior Championship [1]. He was inducted into the Mexican American Football Hall of Fame (*Salón de la Fama del Futbol Americano de México*) in 2017 [1][4].

## Early life and education

Diego Leonardo García Miravete was born in Mexico City, Mexico [1][5]. His introduction to American football occurred at a young age; in 1952, at the age of seven, he attended the inauguration of the Estadio Olímpico Universitario with his father, where he witnessed the *Clásico Poli-Universidad* rivalry game [1][6].

He attended the Escuela Nacional Preparatoria 5 for high school. At age 15, he joined the school's football team, the *Gallos* (Roosters). However, he suffered a fractured clavicle during his first tackling drill and missed his freshman season [6]. He later enrolled at the National Autonomous University of Mexico (UNAM) to study industrial engineering [1]. During his university studies, he worked for the Cervecería Moctezuma brewery as a packaging designer [5].

## Playing career

García Miravete played college football as a defensive end, although he initially aspired to play quarterback [6][7]. His coaches, including Manuel Neri, determined his position based on his physical attributes [5].

He played for the Pumas UNAM from 1966 to 1969 [1]. During this period, the team was known as "The Invincibles" (*Los Invencibles*) for their dominance in the league [5]. Following a restructuring of the UNAM football program in 1970, which split the Pumas into three separate teams, García Miravete joined the newly formed Cóndores UNAM [1][6]. He played for the Cóndores in 1970 and 1971, serving as team captain in his final season [1]. He was selected for the *Selección Puma* (the university's all-star selection) and the Mexican national team [4][5].

## Coaching career

### Cóndores UNAM (1976–1993)
García Miravete began his coaching career as an assistant for the Cóndores UNAM under head coach Manuel Neri from 1973 to 1975 [1][5]. When Neri left the program in 1976, García Miravete was appointed head coach [5].

Under his leadership, the Cóndores established a dynasty in Mexican college football. García Miravete implemented a disciplined, organizational approach that he likened to running a company [6][7]. During his 18-year tenure, the team won nine National Championships in the ONEFA league. The championship years were 1978, 1979, 1980, 1983, 1984, 1985, 1986, 1990, and 1991 [1][2].

His success with the Cóndores is often cited as the "Golden Era" of the team, characterized by a strong rivalry with the Águilas Blancas of the Instituto Politécnico Nacional (IPN) [8][9]. He left the program following the 1993 season [10].

### Auténticos Tigres UANL (1995–1999)
After a brief stint coaching the Cardinals Palermo in Italy in 1995, García Miravete returned to Mexico to take charge of the Auténticos Tigres at the Autonomous University of Nuevo León (UANL) [1][5]. He served as head coach from 1995 to 1999 [1]. His tenure involved rebuilding the program, though he faced challenges with the team's performance and internal culture during this period [3][11].

### Borregos Salvajes Toluca (2000–2005)
In 2000, García Miravete was hired as the head coach of the Borregos Salvajes (Wild Rams) at the ITESM Toluca campus, shortly after the team was promoted to the major conference (*10 Grandes*) [1]. He led the team to five consecutive semifinal appearances between 2001 and 2005 [1][12]. Despite consistent postseason appearances, the team did not reach the championship game during his tenure, and he resigned after the 2005 season [1].

### International coaching
García Miravete has coached several teams in Europe.
*   **Cardinals Palermo (Italy):** He coached this team in 1995, leading them to the playoff semifinals in the second division [1].
*   **Badalona Dracs (Spain):** In 2000, he served as the head coach for the Badalona Dracs (often referred to as "Drags" in some sources) in the Spanish league [1][13]. Under his direction, the team competed in the European Football League [1].
*   **Corsari Palermo (Italy):** He returned to Italy in 2006 to coach the Corsari Palermo in the Serie A2, leading the team to a derby victory shortly after his arrival [1].

### Later career
García Miravete served as an assistant coach for the Linces UVM (Universidad del Valle de México) from 2007 to 2009 [1]. In 2010, he became the head coach of the Borregos Salvajes at the ITESM Santa Fe campus, a position he held until 2014 [1].

In 2018, he was invited by the Autonomous University of Aguascalientes (UAA) to establish a football program [14]. He led the university's flag football teams and initiated recruitment for a Liga Mayor team, although the full football program was reportedly discontinued shortly after its inception [1][15].

As of 2024 and early 2025, García Miravete serves as the head coach for the Sharks Aguascalientes, a team competing in the FADEMAC (Football Americano del Estado de México A.C.) league [16][17].

## National team coaching

García Miravete coached the Mexican junior national team at the NFL Global Junior Championship. He led the team to gold medals in 1997 (New Orleans) and 1998 (Chula Vista), and a silver medal in 1999 (Fort Lauderdale) [1][18]. He also served as the head coach for the Mexican All-Stars in the Aztec Bowl (*Tazón Azteca*) on multiple occasions, including 1984, 1986, 1990, and 1991 [1].

## Awards and recognition

Diego García Miravete is considered one of the most successful coaches in the history of Mexican college football.
*   **National Championships:** He won 9 national titles as a head coach, tying the record held by Roberto "Tapatío" Méndez [1].
*   **Coach of the Year:** He was named ONEFA Coach of the Year ten times [1].
*   **Hall of Fame:** He was inducted into the *Salón de la Fama del Futbol Americano de México* in 2017 [1][4].
*   **Premio Luchador Olmeca:** In 2010, he was awarded this honor by the Confederación Deportiva Mexicana [1].

## Personal life

García Miravete married in 1968 and has children, including sons Diego and César, whom he coached during his career [1][19]. He holds a degree in industrial engineering from UNAM [1]. He is known by the nicknames "Lapizote" and "Flaco" [1].

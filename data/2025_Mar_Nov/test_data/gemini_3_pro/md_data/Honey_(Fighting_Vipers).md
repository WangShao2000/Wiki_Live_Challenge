# Honey (Fighting Vipers)

**Honey** (Japanese: ハニー, Hepburn: *Hanī*) is a fictional character from the *Fighting Vipers* video game series developed by Sega AM2. Created by character designer Masahiro Sugiyama, she made her debut in the 1995 arcade game *Fighting Vipers*. In North American and European localizations of the original game, her name was changed to **Candy**, though subsequent releases and crossovers have largely reverted to her original Japanese name.

Within the series, Honey is depicted as a fashion student who enters fighting tournaments to promote her self-designed clothing line. She is distinguished by her red, plastic-coated armor which resembles a stylized fairy or magical girl costume. Beyond the *Fighting Vipers* series, the character is notable for the existence of **Honey the Cat**, an anthropomorphic variant hidden within the code of *Sonic the Fighters* (1996) who was later made officially playable in the game's 2012 high-definition re-release.

Honey was received as the "standout" character of *Fighting Vipers*, achieving status as a "virtual idol" in Japan and generating significant cosplay enthusiasm. While her design was praised for its distinctiveness and appeal, it also drew criticism regarding the sexualization of a character depicted as a minor in the original game.

## Conception and Development

### Design and Creation
Honey was created by Masahiro Sugiyama, a character designer for Sega AM2 [1][2]. During the development of *Fighting Vipers*, the team sought to create a cast that differed from the more serious martial artists found in their previous hit, *Virtua Fighter*. Director Hiroshi Kataoka stated that while Honey's design was a departure from the studio's typical output, the development team immediately took a liking to her, bypassing the usual trial-and-error process other characters underwent [2].

Sugiyama designed Honey to serve as a "cute" counterpart to the game's other female characters, Grace and Jane, who were portrayed with more serious or rugged aesthetics [1][2]. Her visual identity centers on her role as a fashion designer; she wears a red, rubberized dress with white frills, red-and-black stiletto boots, and distinctive wings on her back [3]. Producer Yu Suzuki remarked on the character's immediate popularity during development, noting that the team had put visible passion into her creation [2].

For the game's sequel, *Fighting Vipers 2* (1998), the character designs were handled by the artist Imai Toonz. Honey's appearance was updated to a more futuristic aesthetic, featuring a dark blue dress with large openings along the sides, replacing the red ensemble of the first game [2][4].

### Localization
For the original *Fighting Vipers* release in North America and Europe, Sega changed the character's name from Honey to **Candy** [5][6]. This change was exclusive to the first game's Western ports; the anthropomorphic version in *Sonic the Fighters* and the human character in the PAL release of *Fighting Vipers 2* retained the name Honey [7][8].

### Sonic the Fighters Connection
During the development of *Sonic the Fighters* (1996), which was built upon the *Fighting Vipers* engine, Sugiyama modeled an anthropomorphic cat version of Honey. This character was not intended for the final arcade release and was hidden within the game's code [1][9]. According to director Hiroshi Kataoka, Sugiyama likely "snuck" the character into the data during development [1].

The character was rediscovered years later by hackers investigating the arcade board's data [10]. This discovery surprised producer Tohru Murayama, who noted the character had generated a significant amount of fan art in the West despite being inaccessible [1]. Consequently, when *Sonic the Fighters* was ported to Xbox 360 and PlayStation 3 in 2012, AM2 officially completed the character and added her to the playable roster as an unlockable secret [1][11].

## Appearances

### Video Games
**Fighting Vipers Series**
In the original *Fighting Vipers* (1995), Honey is introduced as a 16-year-old student and aspiring fashion designer [12]. She enters the game's tournament to showcase her original fashion line, wearing her self-made rubber dress as armor [5][7]. In *Fighting Vipers 2* (1998), she returns as an 18-year-old trainee stylist, continuing to fight to promote her brand [12].

**Fighters Megamix**
Honey appears in the 1996 crossover title *Fighters Megamix*, which pits characters from *Fighting Vipers* against those from *Virtua Fighter*. In this game, she has a designated rivalry with Pai Chan [1][13]. This title also introduced an alternate costume dubbed "New Honey," a schoolgirl-style outfit featuring belt accessories, which was designed by Sugiyama [2].

**Sonic the Fighters**
The anthropomorphic variant, **Honey the Cat**, appears in the 2012 HD release of *Sonic the Fighters*. To unlock her, players must highlight Amy Rose on the character select screen and press the "Start" button [14]. She retains the red dress and wings from the original *Fighting Vipers* design [10].

**Other Appearances**
Honey appears as a playable character in the arcade game *Fighting Vipers* minigame included within the *Yakuza* spinoff titles *Judgment* and *Lost Judgment* [15][16].

### Print Media
**Archie Comics**
Following the 2012 re-release of *Sonic the Fighters*, Honey the Cat was integrated into the *Sonic the Hedgehog* comic series published by Archie Comics. She debuted in the "Champions" story arc (Issues #268–271) following the "Super Genesis Wave" continuity reboot [11]. In this narrative, she is the founder and CEO of "Honey Brand Clothes and Accessories" and enters the Chaos Emerald Championship to promote her designs [11][17]. The comics establish that she designed the clothes for other characters, including Amy Rose [11].

**IDW Publishing**
Honey has made cameo appearances in the *Sonic the Hedgehog* comics published by IDW. She appears in the "30th Anniversary Special" and the "Free Comic Book Day 2021" issue [18][19].

## Gameplay
Honey's fighting style is officially described as "Cat Punch" [2][3]. Her moveset was designed to be accessible to casual players, featuring a low skill ceiling with fast attacks [3]. Key techniques include the "Cat Uppercut" and the "Bootie Bop" (a hip attack) [2][12].

A central mechanic of *Fighting Vipers* is the ability to break an opponent's armor. Honey wears upper and lower body armor; if her lower armor (skirt) is destroyed, she continues to fight in her remaining clothing [20]. In the Japanese version of the original game, a specific cheat code allowed players to remove her skirt armor before the match began, a feature that was removed from Western localizations [20][21].

In *Sonic the Fighters*, Honey the Cat's gameplay is largely derived from the character Knuckles the Echidna, utilizing his gliding ability (visualized with her wings) but incorporating unique animations based on her *Fighting Vipers* counterpart, such as the "Cat Punch" [19][22].

## Reception and Cultural Impact
Honey is widely considered the most popular character in the *Fighting Vipers* series [2]. Following the game's release, she saw a dramatically higher usage rate in arcade location tests compared to other characters [2][3]. The Japanese publication *2D Idol Encyclopedia* cited her as a prime example of a "virtual idol," comparing her rapid rise in popularity to characters like Jill Valentine and Sakura Kasugano [2].

The character became a frequent subject of fan works and cosplay. *Game Charge* magazine noted a high presence of Honey cosplayers at gaming events in 1996, attributing this to the simplicity and distinctiveness of her costume [2].

Honey's reception has also been tied to her sex appeal. Critics and retrospective reviews have noted that her "risque" promotional imagery and character design contributed significantly to the arcade game's success [2][5]. However, this aspect of her character also drew criticism. The sexualized depiction of a character identified as a minor (16 years old in the first game) was a point of contention, with some retailers reportedly refusing to stock home ports of the title due to the nature of the imagery [2]. Retrospective analysis has described her as a "pioneer" for the "fighting game babe" archetype, paving the way for the design philosophies seen in later franchises such as *Dead or Alive* [5].

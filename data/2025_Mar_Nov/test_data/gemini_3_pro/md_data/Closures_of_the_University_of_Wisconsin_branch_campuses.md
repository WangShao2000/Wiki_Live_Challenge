# Closures of the University of Wisconsin branch campuses

The **closures of the University of Wisconsin branch campuses** refer to a series of discontinuations of in-person instruction and facility closures at two-year satellite campuses within the Universities of Wisconsin (formerly the University of Wisconsin System). Beginning in 2023 and continuing through 2025, the system moved to shutter or repurpose multiple branch campuses, citing significant declines in enrollment, shifting demographics, and financial unsustainability [1][2][3].

Historically, the University of Wisconsin Colleges operated 13 two-year campuses across the state, designed to provide accessible liberal arts education and associate degrees. In 2018, a system-wide restructuring merged these colleges as branch campuses under seven of the system's four-year universities [3][4]. Despite this consolidation, enrollment at the two-year campuses continued to fall, dropping by approximately 60% system-wide between 2010 and 2023 [3].

The wave of closures began with the University of Wisconsin–Platteville Richland in July 2023 [2]. Following a directive from Universities of Wisconsin President Jay Rothman in October 2023 to assess the viability of the remaining branches, several other campuses were slated for closure or transition to online-only models [2][5]. By late 2025, six campuses had closed or announced imminent closure, including branches in Washington County, Fond du Lac, Marinette, Waukesha, the Fox Cities, and Baraboo [6][7][8]. Additionally, the Wausau campus announced a relocation of operations to a local technical college [9].

## History and context

### Establishment and mission
The two-year campuses, formerly known as the University of Wisconsin Colleges, were established to provide affordable access to higher education in rural and suburban communities throughout Wisconsin. These institutions primarily offered Associate of Arts and Science degrees, serving as transfer points to the system's four-year universities [10][11]. The campuses were typically owned by the local county governments, which maintained the facilities, while the University of Wisconsin System provided the staffing, curriculum, and operational funding [12][13].

### 2018 restructuring
In response to early signs of enrollment decline and budgetary pressures, the UW System Board of Regents approved a restructuring plan in 2018. This plan dissolved the separate UW Colleges institution and merged the 13 two-year campuses into seven four-year universities as branch campuses [3][4]. For example, the campus in Waukesha became the University of Wisconsin–Milwaukee at Waukesha, and the campus in Richland Center became the University of Wisconsin–Platteville Richland [14][15]. Critics of the merger argued that it diverted resources away from the branch campuses and contributed to their continued enrollment struggles [4].

### Financial and demographic challenges
The closures were driven by a combination of "market forces," including a demographic decline in traditional college-aged students and a shift in student preference toward four-year institutions, technical colleges, or online learning [2][16][17].

*   **Enrollment Decline:** Enrollment at the two-year campuses dropped precipitously. For instance, the Waukesha campus saw a decline of over 65% from 2014 to 2023 [17]. The Richland campus enrollment fell to approximately 60 students in its final year [13].
*   **Funding:** The Universities of Wisconsin faced financial constraints due to a long-standing freeze on in-state tuition and reductions in state funding. By 2024, 10 of the system's 13 four-year universities were facing a combined structural deficit of $18 million [18][19].
*   **Competition:** In some regions, the branch campuses faced direct competition from the Wisconsin Technical College System. The expansion of associate degree offerings at technical colleges, such as Waukesha County Technical College (WCTC), created redundancy in educational services [3][17].

## Closures and transitions

As of December 2025, the following campuses have been closed, have ended in-person instruction, or have announced plans to do so.

### UW–Platteville Richland
The University of Wisconsin–Platteville Richland, located in Richland Center, was the first of the branch campuses to close during this period. On November 22, 2022, the UW System announced that in-person classes would end due to declining enrollment, which had fallen to 64 degree-seeking students [2][20][21].

Despite efforts by community leaders to save the campus, including town halls and petitions, the campus officially ceased operations on July 1, 2023 [6][21]. The closure marked the first time a UW campus had closed since the University of Wisconsin–Medford ceased operations in 1980 [20][21]. Following the closure, the Universities of Wisconsin vacated the premises entirely in 2024, returning control of the facilities to Richland County [13][21].

### UW–Milwaukee at Washington County
On October 17, 2023, Universities of Wisconsin President Jay Rothman announced the end of in-person instruction at the University of Wisconsin–Milwaukee at Washington County, located in West Bend [1][5]. Enrollment at the campus had declined from 1,054 students in 2020 to fewer than 300 by late 2022 [19][22].

The campus closed on June 30, 2024 [5][12]. In the wake of the closure, UW-Milwaukee merged its operations with Moraine Park Technical College (MPTC). Under this partnership, MPTC assumed the primary role for associate degrees in the region, while UWM maintained a presence to offer bachelor's degree completion programs [5][12].

### UW–Oshkosh, Fond du Lac
Concurrent with the Washington County announcement, President Rothman announced on October 17, 2023, that the University of Wisconsin–Oshkosh, Fond du Lac campus would end in-person instruction by June 2024 [1][16]. Enrollment at the campus had dropped to 196 students by the fall of 2023 [19].

The campus officially closed in June 2024 [23][24]. Following the closure, Fond du Lac County Executive Sam Kaufman proposed repurposing the campus facilities for county government offices, senior housing, and a community performing arts center [23].

### UW–Green Bay, Marinette
In January 2024, the University of Wisconsin–Green Bay announced the suspension of in-person classes at its Marinette campus, effective at the end of the Spring 2024 semester [25][26]. Enrollment had fallen to 213 students, with only 99 being undergraduates and the remainder being high school students taking college credits [18].

Unlike other closures where the university vacated the property entirely, UW-Green Bay stated it would maintain a presence in Marinette. The university shifted to an online-only delivery model for the location while continuing to utilize the Herbert L. Williams Theatre and Fine Arts Gallery for community events [20][27].

### UW–Milwaukee at Waukesha
The University of Wisconsin–Milwaukee at Waukesha, the largest of the branch campuses to close, announced its closure on March 11, 2024 [14][28]. The campus closed following the conclusion of the Spring 2025 semester [17][29].

Enrollment at the Waukesha campus had declined by over 65% between 2014 and 2023, falling to 686 students [17]. UWM Chancellor Mark Mone cited the duplication of services with the nearby Waukesha County Technical College (WCTC) as a primary factor, noting that WCTC had begun offering associate of arts and science degrees [3][17]. Following the closure, UWM established a "University Center" at WCTC to offer bachelor's degree completion and graduate programs [29][30].

### UW–Oshkosh, Fox Cities
On June 13, 2024, the University of Wisconsin–Oshkosh announced that its Fox Cities campus in Menasha would close on June 30, 2025 [8][31]. An analysis by the university projected that enrollment, which stood at 473 in 2024, would drop to fewer than 100 students by 2032 [8][32].

The decision drew criticism from local officials, including Outagamie County Executive Tom Nelson, who expressed disappointment in the lack of communication regarding the closure [8][31]. The campus facilities, including the Barlow Planetarium and Weis Earth Science Museum, are owned by Winnebago and Outagamie counties, which began negotiations for the future use of the property in 2025 [33][34].

### UW–Platteville Baraboo Sauk County
On October 17, 2025, the University of Wisconsin–Platteville announced that it would close its Baraboo Sauk County campus [6][35]. Operations at the campus are scheduled to cease on May 22, 2026 [7][36].

The decision followed a period of financial uncertainty for the campus. In early 2024, Sauk County had agreed to cover maintenance costs to keep the campus open, but enrollment continued to struggle, with only 126 students enrolled in the fall of 2025 compared to a capacity of 350 [6][7]. Chancellor Tammy Evetovich stated that the decision was based on financial concerns and low enrollment [6].

### UW–Stevens Point at Wausau (Relocation)
In September 2025, the University of Wisconsin–Stevens Point announced a major restructuring of its Wausau branch. Rather than closing outright, the university announced plans to relocate its operations from the existing Wausau campus to the campus of Northcentral Technical College (NTC) [9][37].

The relocation is scheduled to take effect in the fall of 2026 [38]. Under the new partnership, UW-Stevens Point will offer bachelor's and master's degree programs at the NTC facility, while NTC will continue to provide associate degrees [37]. The move was driven by a 73% decline in enrollment at the UW-Stevens Point Wausau campus between 2015 and 2024 [9]. The university plans to vacate its current facilities, which are owned by Marathon County, by December 2026 [39].

## Status of remaining branch campuses

As of December 2025, several branch campuses remain operational, though some face continued enrollment challenges.

*   **UW–Eau Claire – Barron County:** Located in Rice Lake, this campus remains open and continues to admit students for the Fall 2025 and Spring 2026 semesters [40][41].
*   **UW–Whitewater at Rock County:** Located in Janesville, this campus remains open. The university has planned construction and maintenance projects for the facility extending through 2025 [42][43].
*   **UW–Green Bay, Manitowoc and Sheboygan:** These campuses remain operational. UW-Green Bay Chancellor Michael Alexander noted in early 2024 that these locations were on "positive trajectories" compared to the Marinette campus [25]. Commencement ceremonies for these campuses were scheduled for December 2025 [44][45].
*   **UW–Stevens Point at Marshfield:** While no closure has been officially announced as of December 2025, the campus has experienced significant enrollment declines similar to those at Wausau and other closed campuses [46][47].

## Disposition of facilities and redevelopment

The closure of these campuses has left county governments, which own the land and buildings, with the responsibility of repurposing the properties. The State of Wisconsin made grants of up to $2 million available to counties affected by closures to assist with redevelopment efforts [26][27].

*   **Richland County:** Following the departure of UW-Platteville, Richland County took full control of the campus. The county explored various uses for the buildings, including selling assets to the local school district [21].
*   **Waukesha County:** In January 2025, the Waukesha City Council approved a land use plan changing the designation of the 71-acre UWM at Waukesha campus to residential. This change allows Waukesha County to sell the property to developers for potential single-family and multi-family housing projects [48][49].
*   **Fond du Lac County:** The former campus is being repurposed into a "Fond du Lac County Campus," housing county offices, a senior housing complex, and community amenities [23].
*   **Winnebago and Outagamie Counties (Fox Cities):** As of late 2025, the future of the Fox Cities campus remained under negotiation. Proposals included selling the property or converting it for use by the Menasha Joint School District. The counties expressed a desire to preserve community assets such as the planetarium and museum [33][50].

## Reactions and impact

The closures have elicited mixed reactions from state officials, university administrators, and local communities.

**University administration:** Universities of Wisconsin President Jay Rothman and various chancellors have consistently described the closures as necessary responses to "market realities" and financial unsustainability [2][16]. They have emphasized that the status quo was no longer viable given the sharp declines in enrollment and have advocated for new models, such as partnerships with technical colleges [2][5].

**Community and political reaction:** Local officials in affected counties have often expressed disappointment and concern regarding the economic impact of the closures. Richland County Board Chair David Turk described the closure of the Richland campus as a "gut punch" to the community [13]. In the Fox Cities, County Executive Tom Nelson criticized university leadership for the decision to close the campus [8].

**Student impact:** The closures have forced current students to transfer to main campuses, other branch campuses, or online programs to complete their degrees. While universities have offered transition assistance, students have expressed concerns about the loss of affordable, close-to-home education options and the personal disruption caused by the closures [10][16][51].

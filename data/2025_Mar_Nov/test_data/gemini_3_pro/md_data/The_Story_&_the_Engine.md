# The Story & the Engine

"**The Story & the Engine**" is the fifth episode of the fifteenth series of the British science fiction television series *Doctor Who*. It was first broadcast on BBC One in the United Kingdom and released on Disney+ internationally on May 10, 2025. The episode was written by playwright and poet Inua Ellams and directed by Makalla McPherson.

In the episode, the Fifteenth Doctor (Ncuti Gatwa) and his companion Belinda Chandra (Varada Sethu) travel to Lagos, Nigeria, in 2019. The Doctor visits an old friend who runs a barbershop, only to discover the establishment has been commandeered by a mysterious entity known as "The Barber" (Ariyon Bakare). The shop, which exists simultaneously in Lagos and in a pocket dimension on the back of a giant spider, is revealed to be an engine powered by storytelling. The Barber intends to use this engine to destroy ancient gods who he claims stole his stories without credit.

The episode is notable for being the first *Doctor Who* story set primarily in Nigeria and for its exploration of West African mythology, specifically the figure of Anansi. It features a guest cast including Sule Rimi, Michelle Asante, and a cameo appearance by Jo Martin as the Fugitive Doctor. The episode received generally positive reviews from critics, who praised its cultural specificity, visual concepts, and performances, though some criticized its pacing and heavy reliance on dialogue.

## Plot

The Fifteenth Doctor and Belinda Chandra land the TARDIS in Lagos, Nigeria, in 2019. The Doctor intends to visit Omo Esosa, an old friend who owns a local barbershop called "Omo's Palace," while Belinda explores the city. Upon entering the shop, the Doctor discovers that Omo and several regular customers are being held captive by a mysterious figure known only as "The Barber."

The Doctor attempts to leave but finds the shop is trapped within a dimensional anomaly. While the interior appears to be a standard barbershop, the exterior exists in a void, resting on the back of a colossal mechanical spider traversing a web in space. The Barber reveals that the shop functions as a "Story Engine," a machine powered by the narrative energy of its captives. The men are forced to tell stories to fuel the engine, which the Barber is using to navigate the "Nexus," a web of storytelling that connects all existence.

Belinda, realizing the Doctor is in trouble, attempts to intervene but is also drawn into the trap. The Barber explains his motivation: he is a primordial storyteller who provided narratives to ancient gods and deities, including Anansi, but was cast aside and denied credit for his creations. He plans to travel to the center of the Nexus to sever the gods' connection to humanity, effectively killing them and destabilizing human culture. He is assisted by a woman named Abby, who is revealed to be Abena, the daughter of Anansi.

The Doctor confronts Omo, who admits he betrayed the Doctor to the Barber in exchange for safety, a revelation that deeply hurts the Doctor. However, as the engine nears its destination, the captives begin to resist. Abena, conflicted by the Barber's destructive nihilism and the Doctor's appeals to the importance of stories, switches sides. She braids a map of the Nexus into the Doctor's hair, allowing him to navigate the engine's systems.

To stop the Barber, the Doctor interfaces directly with the engine. He overloads it by feeding it his own life story—a narrative so complex and "never-ending" that the machine cannot process it. During this sequence, the Doctor briefly manifests as the Fugitive Doctor, confusing the Barber. The surge of energy causes the spider and the engine to destabilize.

The Doctor offers the Barber a chance to escape, but the entity refuses to abandon his vengeance. The Doctor, Belinda, Omo, and the other captives escape through the shop's front door back into Lagos just as the engine collapses. In the aftermath, Omo apologizes to the Doctor, and they reconcile. The Doctor and Belinda depart, with the Doctor reflecting on the power of stories and the necessity of giving credit where it is due.

## Production

### Development and Writing
"The Story & the Engine" was written by Inua Ellams, a Nigerian-born poet and playwright known for his play *Barber Shop Chronicles*. The episode marks Ellams' first contribution to *Doctor Who* [4][8]. The concept for the episode originated from lead actor Ncuti Gatwa's desire to have a story set in Africa [5]. Showrunner Russell T Davies approached Ellams to write the script, which explores themes of storytelling, ownership, and the cultural significance of barbershops as safe spaces for Black men [3][8].

Ellams incorporated elements of West African folklore into the script, specifically the figure of Anansi, the spider trickster god of stories [3]. The writer stated that the antagonist, the Barber, was conceived as a foil to Anansi—a figure who created stories but received no credit, driving him to vengeance [9]. To promote the episode, Ellams wrote an exclusive prequel story titled "What I Did On My Holidays By Omo Esosa," which was published on the *Doctor Who* website. The prequel details the first meeting between a young Omo and the Doctor during a forest fire in Etsako [10].

### Filming
The episode was directed by Makalla McPherson and produced by Vicki Delow [1]. It was filmed as part of the series' fourth production block, alongside the following episode, "The Interstellar Song Contest" [1]. While the majority of the episode was filmed at Wolf Studios Wales in Cardiff, establishing shots and drone footage were recorded on location in Lagos, Nigeria, to ensure the setting appeared authentic [1][8].

The production utilized a "Volume" style video wall to create the backgrounds for the scenes where the barbershop travels through the Nexus, allowing for dynamic lighting and environmental effects [11].

### Cast Notes
The episode features a predominantly Black cast [12]. Ariyon Bakare, who plays the Barber, previously appeared in the *Doctor Who* universe as the lion-like alien Leandro in the 2015 episode "The Woman Who Lived" [2]. Jo Martin's uncredited cameo as the Fugitive Doctor was kept secret prior to broadcast; Russell T Davies stated that her inclusion was important to acknowledge her incarnation within a story that explicitly explores the Doctor's Black identity [1][13].

## Broadcast and Reception

### Broadcast and Ratings
"The Story & the Engine" was first released on BBC iPlayer in the United Kingdom and Disney+ globally on May 10, 2025. It was subsequently broadcast on BBC One later that evening [1].

The episode received an overnight rating of 1.59 million viewers. The consolidated seven-day viewing figure was 2.70 million viewers [1][14]. This represented a slight decrease from the previous episode, "Lucky Day," and a 20.1% decrease year-on-year compared to the Series 14 episode "Dot and Bubble" [14].

### Critical Reception
The episode received generally positive reviews, with critics praising its ambition, cultural setting, and thematic depth, though some found fault with its pacing.

*Den of Geek* gave the episode a positive review, describing it as "poetic" and "conceptually daring." The review highlighted how the episode celebrated the Doctor's Black identity as an "unambiguous positive," contrasting it with previous episodes like "Lux" that focused on the trauma of racism. The reviewer praised the chemistry between Gatwa and the guest cast, particularly the sense of community within the barbershop [8].

*GamesRadar+* called the episode "one of the most original and ambitious episodes this show has produced in years," praising its mix of mythology and science fiction. The review noted that while the plot was somewhat "baffling" upon first viewing due to its high-concept nature, it succeeded as a "bottle episode" driven by strong performances [15].

*The Doctor Who Companion* offered a mixed assessment. While the reviewer appreciated the "refreshing take" and the long-overdue African setting, they criticized the episode for being "very talky" and feeling like a "television adaptation of a children's book." The review also noted that the set design for the barbershop and the TARDIS felt limited by the production constraints [16].

*Doctor Who TV* provided a more negative critique, labeling the episode "dull" and arguing that it "reaches for mythic depth but rarely finds the structure, pace, or emotional drive to support its ambition." The reviewer felt that the betrayal by Omo lacked emotional weight because the friendship between him and the Doctor had not been sufficiently established on screen [17].

*Critical Popcorn* praised the script's "humanoid drama" and the "dramatic weight" of the dialogue, noting that while it might be "too talky for some," it offered a welcome change of pace for the season. The review highlighted Ariyon Bakare's charismatic performance as the Barber [18].

# Fall_River/New_Bedford_Line


The Fall River/New Bedford Line is a commuter rail line of the Massachusetts Bay Transportation Authority (MBTA) Commuter Rail system in southeastern Massachusetts, United States. It serves southeast Massachusetts by connecting Boston to Taunton, Fall River, and New Bedford. The line is part of the South Coast Rail project and has undergone significant improvements.

## History

Most of the line originated as the Fall River Railroad, which opened in segments between Fall River and South Braintree via Middleborough in 1845 and 1846. The railroad was built as part of the Old Colony Railroad, which connected Boston to New York City. Over the years, the line has undergone numerous changes, including the addition of branches to Fall River and New Bedford.

## Routes

The Fall River/New Bedford Line runs south from Boston to Taunton, where it splits into branches to Fall River and New Bedford. It has 10 intermediate stations on the combined section. The line has been extended as part of the South Coast Rail project, which added five new stations to the line, extending west to Taunton.

## Operators

The Fall River/New Bedford Line is operated by the Massachusetts Bay Transportation Authority (MBTA). The line is part of the MBTA's Commuter Rail system, which connects various cities and towns in Massachusetts.

## Notable Aspects

The Fall River/New Bedford Line has undergone significant improvements in recent years, including the extension of the line to Taunton, Fall River, and New Bedford. The line plays a crucial role in connecting southeastern Massachusetts to the state capital, Boston. It also offers fare-free service from opening day until the end of March, providing a convenient and affordable transportation option for passengers.

# Muscatine_Mall


Muscatine Mall, located in Muscatine, Iowa, is a shopping and entertainment destination that opened in 1971. Initially, the mall featured Woolworth, Montgomery Ward, and JCPenney as its anchor stores. Over the years, the mall has undergone changes, including the closure of some of its anchor stores, such as Woolworth. Currently, the mall is anchored by Slumberland Furniture, Warehouse Bargains, and Lab. The mall also offers a variety of businesses and eateries, creating a vibrant shopping and entertainment experience for families and individuals.

## History

Muscatine Mall was originally built as an open-air mall in the early 1960s. It predates the opening of Greengate Mall and South Hills Village by a few years. The mall was developed by Des Moines-based General Growth Properties and opened in 1971. In the 1990s, the mall enjoyed relative success, but it began to decline in the late 1990s. One owner hiked up the rent around the turn of the century, trying to get stores to leave so they could de-mall the property. This was only partially successful, as several stores remained. The mall remained open until about 2001, when it became largely abandoned.

## Features

Muscatine Mall offers a diverse shopping experience, featuring outlet stores, unique shops, and delightful dining options. The mall is anchored by Slumberland Furniture, Warehouse Bargains, and Lab. Other notable stores include The Lab, Slumberland Furniture, and Warehouse Bargains. The mall also has an outdoor area with various in-line spaces and interior mall suites available. In the past, the mall was known for its winter concourse, which had a view down into the ice rink, and its groundbreaking closed circuit TV system to keep visitors informed.

## Notable Events

Throughout its history, Muscatine Mall has been featured in various media, including documentaries and videos. It was featured in Dead Malls Season 3 Episode 17 and Dead Malls Episode 10. The mall was also the subject of a YouTube video titled "A Visit to Muscatine Mall" and a Facebook post titled "Muscatine Mall's history and anchor changes." The mall has been described as the "jewel of the Mississippi," a reference to its location along the Mississippi River. Muscatine Mall has been largely abandoned since around 2001, but it remains a significant landmark in the area.

# 2011_Flat_Rock–Trenton_tornado


## Lead

The 2011 Flat Rock–Trenton tornado was a devastating EF4 tornado that occurred on April 27, 2011, as part of the 2011 Super Outbreak. The tornado directly killed 15 people, injured at least 50 others, and impacted hundreds of structures. It had a path length of 41 miles, reaching a maximum width of 1.3 miles. The event highlights the destructive power of tornadoes and serves as a significant event in the history of the region.

## Occurrence

The tornado began its life as an EF0 to EF1 tornado near Section, Alabama, with peak wind speeds of 70 to 90 mph. It then intensified to an EF4, with maximum winds of 190 mph and a diameter of up to 1.3 miles. The tornado remained on the ground for approximately 41 miles, affecting areas in both Alabama and Georgia.

## Impact

### Flat Rock, Alabama
In Flat Rock, entire homes were obliterated, and thousands of trees were snapped. A propane tank was hurled 100 yards, and 19 cattle perished. The tornado also caused significant damage to infrastructure, including Friendship Baptist Church.

### Trenton, Georgia
In Trenton, the tornado leveled at least two mobile homes and swept their remains hundreds of yards downwind. The tornado also caused extensive damage to structures and infrastructure in the area.

## Aftermath

The 2011 Flat Rock–Trenton tornado was part of the 2011 Super Outbreak, which was the largest single outbreak of tornadoes on record. The event resulted in 324 fatalities, over 3,100 injuries, and estimated damages exceeding $12 billion. In the aftermath, emergency services provided aid and support to the affected communities.

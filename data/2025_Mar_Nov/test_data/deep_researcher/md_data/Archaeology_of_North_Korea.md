# Archaeology_of_North_Korea


The archaeology of North Korea has a rich history that spans from the earliest human presence on the peninsula to the present day. Major discoveries include ancient tombs, temples, and fortresses. Current research is focused on understanding the ancient past of the region, with a particular emphasis on the state ideology of Juche. Despite challenges posed by the country's isolation, collaborative efforts between North and South Korea have led to significant advancements in archaeological knowledge.

## History of Archaeology in North Korea

The history of archaeology in North Korea is closely tied to the nation's complex political and social history. The first major archaeological discoveries of the newly independent North Korea were made in the 1940s, with the excavation of a shell midden in Unggi County. In the 1960s, a rare joint Chinese-North Korean archaeological excavation took place in China's Liaoning Province at the site of a massive Gojoseon royal palace. The field of archaeology in North Korea has been influenced by the state ideology of Juche, with excavations prioritized as a means to study the ancient past of the region.

## Major Archaeological Discoveries

North Korea has a rich collection of archaeological sites, including major tombs, fortresses, temples, and other tangible sites. Notable discoveries include an ancient Buddha statue dating back to the early 10th century and a 1.7-meter-tall Buddha statue discovered in Yakjon-ri, South Pyongan Province. The site of Manwoldae, home to the Koryo dynasty's royal palace, is currently being excavated by North and South Korean researchers. Additionally, there are 193 sites designated as National Treasures in North Korea.

## Current State of Archaeological Research

Despite the challenges posed by the country's isolation, collaborative efforts between North and South Korea have led to significant advancements in archaeological knowledge. Recent research has focused on the state ideology of Juche and the investigation of prehistoric and protohistoric sites. LiDAR technology has been used to identify the shapes of remains or relics located in remote places. There are ongoing efforts to resume joint archaeological surveys with North Korea at historic palace sites.

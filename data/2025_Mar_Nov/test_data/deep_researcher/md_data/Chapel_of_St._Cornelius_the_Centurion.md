# Chapel_of_St._Cornelius_the_Centurion

Chapel of St. Cornelius the Centurion
The Chapel of St. Cornelius the Centurion is an Episcopal chapel located on Governors Island in New York Harbor. It is part of the parish of Trinity Church in Manhattan. The chapel, named after St. Cornelius the Centurion, is known for its Gothic Revival architecture. It has a rich history dating back to the 19th century, with the original chapel being built in 1846 and the current structure being dedicated in 1906. The chapel is a significant landmark in the area and is open to the public for various events and gatherings.

The chapel is located on Governors Island, which is situated in the New York Harbor, approximately one-half mile from Battery Park in Lower Manhattan and one-quarter mile from Brooklyn. The island has a rich history, having been used as a military facility by British and American forces for over 200 years. In 1794, construction began on Fort Jay, a fort on high ground in the center of the Island. In 1800, New York transferred the Island to the United States government for military purposes, and during the American Civil War, it was used for recruitment and as a prison for captured Confederate soldiers. Throughout World Wars I and II, the Island served as an important supply base for Army ground and air forces.

The chapel played a significant role in the religious life of the island's community. The original chapel on Governors Island dates back to 1846 when the Rev. John McVickar, D.D., was appointed chaplain for the Army servicemen and their families on the island. Services were held under the trees in fair weather, and in the post headquarters at other times. Dr. McVickar soon campaigned for an adequate post chapel, but the Government was not accustomed to building chapels, nor was it willing to make an appropriation for the purpose. Dr. McVickar counted among his friends the Commander-in-Chief of the Army, General Scott, and shortly the original chapel was built.

The current chapel, built in 1906, is a one-story masonry structure with a basement of approximately 9,400 gross square feet. It is a cruciform structure with a massive tower, nave, transepts, chancel, and side chapel. The chapel is part of the National Historic Landmark District and is listed in the National Register of Historic Places. It is open to the public for various events and gatherings, including religious ceremonies and community events.

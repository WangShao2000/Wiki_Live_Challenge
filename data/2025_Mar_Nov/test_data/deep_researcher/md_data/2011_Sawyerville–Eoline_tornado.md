# 2011_Sawyerville–Eoline_tornado


## Lead

On the afternoon of April 27, 2011, a large, long-tracked, and destructive low-end EF3 tornado, known as the Sawyerville–Eoline tornado, moved across Central Alabama. The tornado caused significant structural damage consistent with an EF-3 rating and winds of 145 mph, resulting in seven fatalities and 52 injuries. It touched down in far southwest Greene County near the Tombigbee River and moved through sparsely-populated rural areas of Greene and Hale counties, affecting Eoline and other nearby areas. The tornado's path extended 71.3 miles with a maximum width of one mile, and it was part of the largest tornado outbreak in modern history, which occurred on April 27, 2011.

## The Tornado

The Sawyerville–Eoline tornado was rated as an EF-3 with maximum wind speeds of 145 mph. It touched down in far southwest Greene County near the Tombigbee River just west of Tishabee on County Road 69 near Head Drive. The tornado moved through Eoline and caused significant structural damage, consistent with the EF-3 rating, impacting hundreds of structures and affecting the surrounding areas. The tornado's path extended 71.3 miles with a maximum width of one mile, making it one of the longest-tracked tornadoes during the 2011 Super Outbreak.

## The Outbreak

April 27, 2011, was one of the deadliest and most destructive days in the history of tornadoes in the United States. The outbreak produced destructive tornadoes in Alabama, Arkansas, Georgia, Mississippi, North Carolina, Tennessee, and Virginia, and affected a broad area, from Choctaw County in South Alabama to the state's mountainous upper regions. The combined tornado damage path length in Central Alabama was 691.02 miles. Sixteen of the tornadoes had damage path lengths over 10 miles long, and eight of the tornadoes had damage path lengths over 25 miles long.

## Notable Aspects

The Sawyerville–Eoline tornado was one of the most significant tornadoes during the 2011 Super Outbreak. This strong and long-tracked EF3 wedge tornado touched down in extreme southwest Greene County, moving through sparsely-populated rural areas and causing extensive damage. The tornado's path extended for 71.3 miles, resulting in seven fatalities and 52 injuries. Notable areas affected by the tornado include Eoline and other nearby communities.

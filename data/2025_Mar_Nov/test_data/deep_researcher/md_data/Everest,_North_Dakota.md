# Everest,_North_Dakota

Everest is an unincorporated community in Everest Township, Cass County, North Dakota, United States. It is located south of Casselton and was platted in 1881. Once a prosperous town with over 200 residents, the population has since declined to around 100 residents. The town was the birthplace of North Dakota governor William Langer and his family.

The climate in the area is characterized by a warm-summer humid continental climate, with large temperature variations and irregular precipitation. The geography of the region includes the Durbin-Everest Channel, an intermittent stream, flowing south of the town. Notable landmarks and attractions in the area include the Painted Canyon Overlook and the Enchanted Highway.

Everest was once a bustling community along a railroad line, which provided transportation and economic benefits. However, the removal of the rail line and closure of the post office led to the town's decline. Today, the townsite is mostly characterized by the railroad tracks, if they still exist.

The town has a rich history and has been home to notable figures, such as William Langer, who was a significant political figure in North Dakota. However, the town itself is not currently a major center for business or industry, and its main features are historical and cultural.

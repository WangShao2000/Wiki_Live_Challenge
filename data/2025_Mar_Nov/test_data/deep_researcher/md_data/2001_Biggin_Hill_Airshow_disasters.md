# 2001_Biggin_Hill_Airshow_disasters


The 2001 Biggin Hill Airshow was held in Biggin Hill, Kent, United Kingdom. Unfortunately, the airshow was marred by two fatal aircraft crashes on the second day of the event. 

## Background
The 2001 Biggin Hill Airshow was scheduled to be a spectacular event with various aircraft displays and performances. However, the show faced several challenges, including low cloud and intermittent rain, which affected the initial day's weather conditions.

## Incidents
On the first day of the airshow, an incident occurred when a De Havilland Venom made a wheels-up landing. Fortunately, this incident did not result in any major damage or injuries. 

The airshow continued on the second day, but it was overshadowed by two tragic incidents involving fatal aircraft crashes. 

### De Havilland Vampire Crash
On the morning of June 3, 2001, a formation flight of two De Havilland Vampire aircraft was scheduled to perform a display. While the Vixen aircraft successfully completed its portion of the display, the Vampire suddenly lost control and crashed into a wood at the end of runway 21. The aircraft was being flown by Captain Jonathan Kerr and Sir Ken Hayr, both experienced pilots. Despite scanning the sky, no parachutes were seen, and the outcome was fatal.

### Bell P-63A Kingcobra Crash
Later in the day, the TFC's Bell P-63A Kingcobra crashed on the airfield during its display. The pilot, Guy Bancroft-Wilson, a captain with British Airways and ex-Red Arrows team member, was killed in the crash. This accident was a repeat of the previous day's tragedy, adding to the sorrow and disbelief of the attendees.

## Investigation
Both crashes were investigated by the Air Accidents Investigation Branch (AAIB). The investigation into the Bell P-63A Kingcobra crash reported that the pilot had incorrectly positioned the aircraft during a turn, leading to a loss of control and subsequent crash.

## Conclusion
The 2001 Biggin Hill Airshow is remembered for these tragic events. While the show itself was organized well, the loss of four pilots in two fatal crashes left a lasting impact on the aviation community and the families of the pilots involved. The airshow remains a somber reminder of the risks associated with air displays and the importance of safety measures.

# Meléndez–Quiñónez_dynasty


The Meléndez–Quiñónez dynasty (Spanish: dinastía Meléndez-Quiñónez) was a period in El Salvador's history from 1913 to 1927 when the Salvadoran government was controlled by the Meléndez and Quiñónez families. The dynasty began in 1913 when Carlos Meléndez became president after Manuel Enrique Araujo's assassination. Carlos resigned in 1914 to run in the 1915 presidential election, which he won. He was succeeded by his brother, General José María Meléndez, in 1918. The dynasty continued until 1927 with José María Meléndez, who was succeeded by another brother, General Maximiliano Quiñónez.

During the Meléndez–Quiñónez dynasty, the state began to centralize its authority. The dynasty is also known for the creation of the National Democratic Party (Partido Nacional Democrático), a national organization that undermined the traditional power of the liberal and conservative parties. The dynasty's rule was marked by an increase in political repression and the suppression of opposition.

In 1927, the dynasty came to an end with the election of Maximiliano Hernández Martínez, who began a long period of authoritarian rule in El Salvador. The dynasty is considered a crucial period in El Salvador's modern history, as it marked the beginning of the country's transition to a more centralized and authoritarian state.

# Edwardsville_Amazon_warehouse_collapse

On the evening of December 10, 2021, a powerful tornado struck the DLI4 delivery facility in Edwardsville, Illinois, an Amazon warehouse that oversees delivery logistics for the Greater St. Louis Metropolitan Area. The tornado caused the collapse of the warehouse, resulting in the tragic deaths of six workers and injuries to several others. This article will provide an overview of the event, its causes, consequences, and the subsequent investigation and response.

## Cause of the Collapse
The cause of the collapse was a tornado with estimated wind speeds of 150 mph. According to the Occupational Safety and Health Administration (OSHA), the collapse was due to improper anchoring of support columns to the floor, which were not built to code (OSHA shares findings after tornado hit Edwardsville, 2022).

## Consequences of the Collapse
The collapse of the warehouse had significant consequences. Six workers were killed, and several others were injured. The incident raised questions about the company's workplace safety policies and led to calls for improved safety measures in the workplace (Amazon warehouse collapse in Edwardsville, 2021).

## Investigation and Response
OSHA opened an investigation into the building collapse at the Amazon Warehouse in Edwardsville, IL. The investigation found that the warehouse was not built to code, and Amazon's decision to rebuild the warehouse without key safety improvements was under scrutiny (OSHA is investigating deadly Amazon warehouse collapse, 2021).

## Aftermath and Rebuilding
In the aftermath of the collapse, the Amazon warehouse in Edwardsville reopened nearly three years later. The incident raised questions about the company's commitment to worker safety and led to calls for improved safety measures in the workplace (Edwardsville Amazon warehouse reopens nearly 3 years after collapse, 2024).

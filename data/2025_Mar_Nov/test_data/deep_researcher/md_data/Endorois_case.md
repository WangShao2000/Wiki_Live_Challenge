# Endorois_case

The Endorois case is a landmark legal case against the Kenyan government, representing the first legal recognition of an African indigenous peoples' rights over traditionally owned land. The case began in 1973 when the Kenyan government forcibly evicted the Endorois people, a traditional pastoralist community, from their ancestral land near Lake Bogoria to create a game reserve for tourism. The Endorois people, represented by the Centre for Minority Rights Development (Kenya) and Minority Rights Group International, challenged the eviction in court. 

In 2002, the African Commission on Human and Peoples' Rights ordered the Kenyan government to restore the Endorois to their historic land and to compensate them. The government was found culpable of violating six Articles of the African Charter on Human and Peoples' Rights. However, as of 2023, the Endorois have been granted limited land access and revenue sharing, but not full ownership.

The case has had significant implications for indigenous land rights in Africa, highlighting the importance of respecting the rights of indigenous peoples to their traditional lands and ways of life.

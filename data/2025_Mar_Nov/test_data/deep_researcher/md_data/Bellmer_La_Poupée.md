# Bellmer_La_Poupée

Hans Bellmer, a German artist known for his surreal and disturbing artworks, created 'Bellmer La Poupée', a series of photographs of life-sized dolls. These dolls were constructed by Bellmer and posed in nightmarish tableaux, often with mutilated and reassembled parts. The series explores themes of sexuality, the human body, and the base condition of humanity. The first doll was created in 1933, and Bellmer constructed several dolls, all of which had moveable parts that could be put together in different configurations. His second doll, 'La Poupée', was created in 1935-6 and is his most widely known doll. It was made from modular, jointed body parts and was a response to the fascist celebration of the perfect body as an ideal of Aryan supremacy. In 1934, Bellmer included the photographs of the first doll in his book 'Die Puppe'. In 1936, he published the book 'La Poupée', which included ten photographs documenting the dolls.

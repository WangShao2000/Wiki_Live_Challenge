# Ring_of_Nestor

Ring of Nestor
The Ring of Nestor is a significant gold signet ring discovered in a Mycenaean tholos tomb near the ancient site of Pylos in Messenia, Greece. Discovered in 1924 during excavations led by Sir Arthur Evans, the ring has been the subject of much debate and scholarly discussion regarding its authenticity and historical significance.

The ring measures 3.29 cm by 2.18 cm and is the fifth largest Aegean signet ring. It features an oval bezel with a tub-like reverse and two tongues or projections on each side. The ring's hoop inner diameter is 1.4 to 1.56 cm, and the hoop band is 0.82 cm by 0.35 cm.

The historical significance of the Ring of Nestor lies in its potential to provide insights into Minoan religion and cultural practices. Sir Arthur Evans considered it to show scenes from the underworld and to illustrate connections between Minoan belief and ancient Egyptian religion. However, its authenticity has been debated by scholars, with some viewing it as a forgery.

Further research and analysis have confirmed the ring's authenticity, but the debates surrounding its origin and historical context continue to this day. The Ring of Nestor remains an important artifact in the study of Aegean antiquities and the cultural connections between Minoan and Mycenaean civilizations.

# Assassination_of_George_Lincoln_Rockwell

George Lincoln Rockwell (March 9, 1918 – August 25, 1967) was an American neo-Nazi activist who founded the American Nazi Party (ANP) in 1959 and later renamed it the National Socialist White People's Party (NSWPP). His life ended violently on August 25, 1967, when he was assassinated in Arlington, Virginia.

### The Assassination

On the afternoon of August 25, 1967, just before noon, Rockwell was shot and killed in the parking lot of the Dominion Hills shopping center (a strip mall) on Wilson Boulevard in Arlington, Virginia. As the 49-year-old Rockwell slid into the driver's seat of his fading blue-and-white 1958 Chevrolet, gunshots rang out from the roof of the shopping center. The assassin was John Patler (born John Patsalos), a former high-ranking member of Rockwell's organization who had served as the party's "Minister of Propaganda" and publication editor.

### Motive and Background

Patler had been expelled from the American Nazi Party in April 1967 after Rockwell accused him of having "Bolshevick leanings" following ideological disagreements and internal conflicts within the organization. The assassination was an act of revenge stemming from this bitter estrangement and Patler's expulsion from the party. Patler fired two shots into Rockwell's chest from the window of a laundromat (Econowash) as Rockwell drove past.

### Aftermath

Patler was convicted of first-degree murder in December 1967 and sentenced to 20 years in prison. He was paroled in 1975, though he reportedly returned to prison later for parole violations. Rockwell's assassination did not end the American Nazi movement, but it marked the violent conclusion of his nearly decade-long tenure as America's most prominent neo-Nazi leader.

Rockwell's death occurred just months after he had visited college campuses to give speeches promoting his extremist ideology, including an appearance at the University of Wisconsin–Eau Claire in February 1967. Despite being a U.S. Navy veteran who had sought burial at Arlington National Cemetery, his request was denied following his assassination.

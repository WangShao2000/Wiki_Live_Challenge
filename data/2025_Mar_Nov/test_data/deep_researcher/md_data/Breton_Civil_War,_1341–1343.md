# Breton_Civil_War,_1341–1343

Breton Civil War, 1341–1343

The Breton Civil War, 1341–1343, was a dynastic dispute between two claimants to the Duchy of Brittany. It broke out after the death of Duke John III in 1341. The conflict was further complicated by the involvement of the French and English monarchs, leading to a larger conflict during the Hundred Years' War. The war ended in 1343 with the victory of Jean de Montfort, leading to a period of relative stability in the region.

The Breton Civil War was primarily caused by a dispute over the succession to the Duchy of Brittany. Duke John III died in 1341 without a male heir, leaving his wife Joanna of Montfort as the regent for their young son John. This led to a power struggle between two claimants: John of Montfort and Charles de Blois. The conflict was further fueled by the intervention of the French and English monarchs, each supporting a different claimant. John of Montfort was backed by Edward III of England, while Charles de Blois was supported by John II of France.

The key figures in the Breton Civil War include:
- John of Montfort: The English-backed claimant to the Duchy of Brittany.
- Charles de Blois: The French-backed claimant to the Duchy of Brittany.
- Jean de Montfort: The son of John of Montfort, who took command after his father's capture.
- Joanna of Montfort: The regent for her son John, who took command of the field army.
- Edward III of England: The English monarch who supported John of Montfort.
- John II of France: The French monarch who supported Charles de Blois.

The Breton Civil War ended in 1343 with the victory of Jean de Montfort and his English allies over Charles de Blois. This led to a period of relative stability in the Duchy of Brittany, with Jean de Montfort becoming the Duke.

The Breton Civil War was significant as it highlighted the complex political and military dynamics of the Hundred Years' War. It also demonstrated the influence of external powers, such as England and France, on the internal affairs of Brittany. The conflict further strained the relationship between the two countries and contributed to the ongoing tensions during the Hundred Years' War.

# Brave Bunnies
The Bunny family as depicted in Brave Bunnies, with siblings Bop and Boo (center) accompanied by their parents and baby siblings.
Brave Bunnies is a children's animated television series aimed at preschoolers (ages 2–5)[1], created by Ukrainian producer Olga Cherepanova and co-produced with the Spanish animation studio Ánima[2]. The show follows two curious bunny siblings, Bop and Boo, who travel with their family in a "Bunny Bus" to meet new animal friends and learn about diversity and friendship[3]. The series premiered on December 5, 2020, on Ukraine’s PlusPlus channel[4] and has since been distributed internationally, airing in over 80 countries by 2023[1]. In 2022, amid disruptions caused by the war in Ukraine, Canadian media company WildBrain partnered with the original producers to continue the franchise, co-producing a second season and expanding global distribution[5].
## Premise
Brave Bunnies centers on a family of adventurous rabbits. The main characters are Bop and Boo, a brother-and-sister pair of young bunnies, along with their parents (known as Bunny Ma and Pa) and four baby bunny siblings[6]. Together they travel across their imaginative "Bunny World" in a colorful camper van called the Bunny Bus, embarking on a road-trip adventure. In each episode, Bop and Boo encounter a new friend belonging to a different animal species and create a fun game or activity to share[3]. Through these adventures, the series highlights diversity and models social skills, showing the bunny characters learning to appreciate differences in others while making friends through play[3]. The tone of the show is lighthearted and educational, mixing preschool-friendly comedy and catchy songs with gentle lessons. Brave Bunnies explicitly aims to teach young children early socialization skills – encouraging them to accept others’ differences and be curious and courageous in exploring the wider world[7].
## Production and Development
Brave Bunnies was developed by the Ukrainian creative studio Glowberry, with Olga Cherepanova as the creator and creative producer of the series[2]. The project was first conceived in the late 2010s and gained early recognition as a pitch finalist at the 2018 Cartoon Forum animation conference in France[2]. Production of the series began as a co-production between Glowberry and Ánima’s Spanish animation team, with support from Ukraine’s FILM.UA Group and funding from the Ukrainian State Film Agency and the Ministry of Youth and Sports of Ukraine[2]. The show’s visual style was crafted by art director Anna Sarvira, a Ukrainian illustrator, to achieve a bright and kid-friendly 2D look[6]. The creative team also included Tim Fehrenbach from the UK as the series director and John van Bruggen from Canada as head writer[4].
The first season of Brave Bunnies consists of 52 episodes, each about 7 minutes long[3]. Before its public debut, the series attracted industry attention at MIPJunior 2020, where a preview of Brave Bunnies was among the most-viewed screenings at that market’s animation showcase[6]. The series is produced in 2D digital animation, featuring simple shapes and vibrant colors designed to appeal to young children[6].
## Release and Broadcast
The world premiere of Brave Bunnies took place on December 5, 2020, on the PlusPlus children’s TV channel in Ukraine[4]. The show began rolling out internationally in early 2021. Aardman Animations, the British studio known for animated hits, served as the global distributor for Season 1[3], helping secure broadcast deals in numerous territories. Brave Bunnies debuted on broadcasters around the world, including on Nick Jr. and Milkshake! in the United Kingdom, ABC Kids in Australia, Rai Yoyo in Italy, Super RTL (TOGGO) in Germany, YLE in Finland, and HOP! in Israel[3][2]. By October 2021, the series had aired in at least a dozen countries across Europe, Asia, and Oceania[6]. Additional launches followed in Latin America and other regions as the show’s popularity grew[7].
Over the next two years, Brave Bunnies continued to expand its reach. By late 2023, the series was available in over 80 countries worldwide through various broadcast partners and streaming platforms[1]. For example, the show was picked up by the Canal+ Group’s TeleTOON+ channel in Poland, added to Channel 5’s Milkshake! preschool lineup in the UK, and made available on the Stan streaming service in Australia[1]. In the United States, Brave Bunnies became accessible via the WildBrain+ on-demand channel on Comcast’s Xfinity platforms[1]. These international deals reflect the global appeal of the brand, bolstered by support from major partners in the children’s media industry[1].
## Season 2 and WildBrain Partnership
Production of Brave Bunnies was disrupted in 2022 by the outbreak of the Russian invasion of Ukraine, which impacted the Kyiv-based Glowberry studio. In response, Glowberry entered a partnership with Canadian entertainment company WildBrain to secure the series’ future[5]. Announced in late 2022, this agreement ensured that WildBrain would oversee Brave Bunnies going forward. WildBrain became the majority stakeholder in the Brave Bunnies intellectual property, with Glowberry and Ánima remaining as minority stakeholders[5]. Under the arrangement, WildBrain took over global distribution and merchandising of the series and committed to co-produce new content, thereby ensuring the continuation of the show despite the challenges in Ukraine[5].
Under the new partnership, WildBrain’s animation studio in Vancouver collaborated with Ánima’s team in Europe to produce a second season of Brave Bunnies[1]. Season 2, comprising another 52 seven-minute episodes (bringing the total episode count to 104), was completed in 2023[1]. The second season continues the travels of Boo and Bop as they revisit some of their “Best Bunny Friends” from Season 1 and meet new characters in different locales, each with their own family and culture. With WildBrain’s backing, the series also gained further international deals for its new episodes. In 2023, UK broadcaster Channel 5’s Milkshake! and Italy’s Rai Yoyo acquired both Season 1 and 2 for their preschool lineups, and Poland’s TeleTOON+ signed on to air the series as well[1]. WildBrain has additionally leveraged its digital network to promote Brave Bunnies on online platforms, including a dedicated Brave Bunnies YouTube channel that posts new content for young viewers[1]. The three-way partnership between WildBrain, Glowberry, and Ánima aimed to preserve the show’s Ukrainian creative vision while expanding its global audience and franchise potential[5].
## Merchandise and Publications
As Brave Bunnies grew in popularity, it expanded into books and toys to extend its preschool fanbase beyond the TV screen. In late 2021, the first tie-in books were published in Italy by De Agostini’s DeA Planeta Libri imprint, introducing storybooks and activity books based on the Brave Bunnies characters (this launch included titles such as Incredible Friends and Around the World)[6]. This Italian book release was followed by an international publishing deal with Penguin Random House, which planned to roll out Brave Bunnies books in additional markets starting in 2022[6].
The franchise’s toy line was spearheaded by Spin Master, a Canadian toy company that Glowberry named as the global master toy licensee for Brave Bunnies[6]. Spin Master developed a range of Brave Bunnies toys – including figures, playsets, plush dolls, games, and puzzles – which began rolling out in stores in 2022[7]. The initial products were launched in markets such as the United Kingdom, Italy, Germany, Spain, and Australia, with a broader global release by late 2022[7]. These toys allow children to reenact the characters’ adventures and reinforce the show’s themes through play. Additional licensed merchandise, from apparel to educational games, has been planned as part of the Brave Bunnies brand growth, managed by licensing agencies like WildBrain’s CPLG (worldwide) and Glowberry’s local partners in Ukraine and elsewhere[1].
## Reception
Brave Bunnies has been received positively within the children’s entertainment industry. In its development stage, the concept was singled out at Cartoon Forum 2018 as a noteworthy project, signaling strong potential interest from international broadcasters[2]. Upon its market debut, a preview of Brave Bunnies at MIPJunior 2020 generated significant attention – it was reported as one of the most-watched screenings among new preschool series at that event[6]. Early impressions from industry experts praised the show’s charming animation style and its emphasis on inclusion and friendship[3].
As the series reached audiences, it built a steady following in multiple countries. While preschool programs typically do not garner extensive critical reviews, broadcast partners have noted the series’ broad appeal. WildBrain’s distribution team, for example, cited strong demand from global buyers, crediting Brave Bunnies’ “catchy songs, adorable characters and big adventures” that engage young viewers everywhere while fostering values of curiosity and acceptance[1]. The fact that Brave Bunnies was airing in over 80 territories by 2023 is seen as a testament to its international success as a preschool franchise[1]. The show’s ability to continue production and find a worldwide audience despite challenging circumstances (such as the war in Ukraine) has also been highlighted as an inspirational success story for the Ukrainian animation industry[5].

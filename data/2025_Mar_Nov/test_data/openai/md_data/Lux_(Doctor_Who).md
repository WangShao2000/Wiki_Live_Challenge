# Lux (Doctor Who)
Lux is the second episode of the fifteenth series of the British science fiction television programme Doctor Who (marketed globally as season 2 of the revived series). It was first broadcast on BBC One on 19 April 2025, and made available earlier that day on BBC iPlayer[1][2]. The episode was written by Doctor Who showrunner Russell T Davies and directed by Amanda Brotchie (in her Doctor Who directorial debut)[3][4]. It stars Ncuti Gatwa as the Fifteenth Doctor and Varada Sethu as his companion, Belinda Chandra. The story is set in 1952 Florida, where the Doctor and Belinda investigate mysterious disappearances at a Miami cinema and encounter a whimsical yet sinister cartoon character, Mr. Ring-a-Ding, who is not what he seems[1]. The character — voiced by actor Alan Cumming — is ultimately revealed to be an otherworldly being called Lux Imperator, a self-proclaimed “God of Light”[5][6]. Lux is notable for integrating traditional 2D animation with live action, featuring an animated antagonist within the episode – a first in the history of the programme[7][5].
## Plot
In 1952, the Fifteenth Doctor and Belinda arrive in Miami, Florida, intending to make a brief stop. They find an old movie theater where 15 people recently vanished during a late-night cartoon screening[8]. The Doctor’s curiosity is piqued by the locked, abandoned cinema and its lone caretaker, Reginald Pye. Entering the theater, they discover a cheery black-and-white cartoon character on the screen named Mr. Ring-a-Ding. The cartoon’s signature catchphrases (like “Don’t make me laugh”) and an eerie musical jingle recur as Mr. Ring-a-Ding seemingly interacts with them[9]. Before long, the animated figure breaches reality, stepping out of the screen into the physical world[7]. The Doctor realizes this is no ordinary cartoon – Mr. Ring-a-Ding is a disguise for Lux Imperator, an ancient cosmic entity. Lux Imperator proclaims himself the “God of Light” and a member of the Pantheon of Discord, a powerful clique of chaos-loving godlike beings (which the Doctor has encountered before)[10][11].
The missing moviegoers, the Doctor learns, have been trapped as images on film reels by Lux Imperator’s power[12]. Reginald Pye admits he has been helping the entity: each night he runs film projections to “feed” Lux, after being promised a personal reward – a magical film reel that allowed him one last dance with his late wife, Helen[12]. Like other members of the Pantheon, Lux Imperator forces the Doctor into playing by certain “rules” of a game or challenge[9][10]. When provoked, Lux transforms the Doctor and Belinda into cartoon figures as well, pulling them into an animated “reel” reality under his control[13][14]. The Doctor and Belinda must use their wits to escape the cartoon realm – exploiting genre tropes and even breaking the fourth wall. They momentarily encounter a trio of apparent Doctor Who fans watching their adventure unfold on a screen, prompting the Doctor to question whether he and Belinda might be fictional characters themselves[13][15]. This surreal meta-fictional interlude, however, turns out to be another of Lux’s nested reality traps. The Doctor gains the upper hand when he deduces Lux’s weakness: as a being of light, Lux Imperator can be defeated by overwhelming him with an excess of light[16][17].
As dawn approaches, the Doctor signals Reginald Pye to ignite all the highly flammable film stock in the projection room. The resulting blaze and explosion of sunlight flood the theater[18][19]. Lux gorges on the sudden surge of pure light – far more energy than he can contain – causing him to expand exponentially. In a climactic moment, Lux Imperator ascends into the sky and dissipates, becoming “everything and nothing” among the starlight[20][21]. With the villain gone, the human victims trapped in film are restored to reality unharmed[16]. Belinda, moved by the adventure and her shared vulnerable moments with the Doctor, decides to continue traveling with him a while longer before returning to her own time[18][22]. After the Doctor and Belinda depart in the TARDIS, a mysterious older woman, Mrs. Flood, appears at the scene. Observing the confused survivors, Mrs. Flood ominously remarks that the Doctor’s “show” is a limited run set to end on 24 May 2025, hinting at greater events to come[22].
## Production
Writing and concept: Lux was written by Russell T Davies. As showrunner, Davies conceived the episode’s blend of animation and live-action, drawing inspiration from meta-fictional films like The Purple Rose of Cairo (1985) and Last Action Hero (1993), in which characters move between cinematic worlds and reality[23]. The central idea of a 1930s-style cartoon character coming to life had been “bubbling around” in Davies’s mind for some time, but only materialized once the show had sufficient budget and technical resources in its new era[24][3]. Lux marks the first time the televised Doctor Who series depicts its lead characters as traditional hand-drawn cartoons within an episode’s narrative, an ambitious novelty made possible by advanced visual effects and co-production funding[7]. Davies described the episode as a mix of “sci-fi, action and humour” that also “plays with the rules of narrative and genre in unexpected ways,” pushing the boundaries of the show’s format[25][26].
Casting: Ncuti Gatwa and Varada Sethu headlined the episode as the Doctor and Belinda, respectively, continuing their roles from the series opener. Guest cast includes Scottish actor Alan Cumming as the voice of Mr. Ring-a-Ding (the Lux Imperator). This was Cumming’s second Doctor Who appearance – he had previously portrayed King James I on-screen in a 2018 episode, and he expressed excitement at returning in a voice role[27][28]. Cumming’s vocal performance for the devious cartoon villain was recorded remotely, as he never met Gatwa or Sethu in person during production[29]. Director Russell T Davies specifically sought out Cumming for the part, emailing him the offer; Cumming, a lifelong Doctor Who fan, eagerly accepted[30]. Another prominent guest star is Linus Roache, who appears as Reginald Pye, the haunted cinema caretaker. Veteran actress Anita Dobson makes a brief cameo in Lux as the enigmatic Mrs. Flood, continuing a recurring story arc that threads through the series[31]. The episode’s other supporting roles (such as the trapped cinema patrons) were filled by a cast including Lewis Cornay, Lucy Thackeray, and others[32].
Filming and design: Lux was directed by Amanda Brotchie, known for her work on Gentleman Jack and Picnic at Hanging Rock, making this her first time directing Doctor Who[4]. Principal photography took place in January 2024. To recreate 1950s Miami on screen, production was staged in Penarth, Wales – including location shoots on Penarth Pier – despite challenging winter conditions[33][34]. Brotchie noted that the crew endured two severe winter storms during filming, all while trying to evoke a warm, tropical Miami night setting[33]. The episode’s animated elements were produced in collaboration with the visual effects team at Framestore. Animators meticulously crafted Mr. Ring-a-Ding in the style of a 1930s Fleischer Studios cartoon character, complete with era-authentic expressions and motions[35]. On set, a life-sized standee of Mr. Ring-a-Ding was used as a reference so actors could interact with the correct eyelines and framing for the CGI character[36]. Notably, some of the animators who brought Mr. Ring-a-Ding to life had previously worked on the acclaimed live-action/animation hybrid film Who Framed Roger Rabbit (1988)[37]. Murray Gold composed the musical score for Lux, continuing his role as series composer; his soundtrack ranged from whimsical cartoon motifs (for Mr. Ring-a-Ding’s scenes) to dramatic orchestration during the episode’s suspense and emotional moments.
## Broadcast and reception
“Lux” premiered in the UK on Saturday 19 April 2025 in Doctor Who’s regular evening slot, with a scheduled broadcast at 7:15 p.m. on BBC One[1]. In a departure from past practice, the episode was also released for streaming on BBC iPlayer on the morning of the broadcast day (at 8 a.m.), as part of the BBC’s strategy to draw online viewership[2]. Overnight ratings for “Lux” were 1.58 million viewers on BBC One, which represented the lowest overnight audience in the programme’s long history[38]. This figure, however, did not account for those who watched via iPlayer and other catch-up platforms; consolidated multi-platform ratings published later showed higher overall viewership once streaming was included[2]. The relatively low initial television figure was attributed to fierce competition from other channels and the shift in viewing habits toward streaming[39][40].
Despite the ratings dip, critical and fan reception to Lux was largely positive. Viewers and reviewers praised the episode’s inventive premise and its bold stylistic choices, such as the integration of an animated villain. The Radio Times reported that fans on social media reacted warmly – some lauding Mr. Ring-a-Ding’s animation and calling Lux “hands down the best episode of the new era” of Doctor Who[41][42]. Critics likewise received the episode enthusiastically. Den of Geek described “Lux” as “a visual delight of an episode that has a ball playing with the possibilities of its premise,” highlighting the faithful Fleischer-era cartoon aesthetic of Mr. Ring-a-Ding and the episode’s playful bending of reality[43][44]. Reviewer Stefan Mohamed noted that while the villain Lux Imperator delivered “loopy visuals and reality-based rug-pulling” on par with other recent Doctor Who cosmic antagonists, the character’s resolution was unexpectedly poetic – with the Doctor allowing Lux to achieve an apotheosis rather than brutally defeating him[45][17]. Film Stories praised the story as “a clever, witty outing for Ncuti Gatwa and Varada Sethu”, remarking that the episode showcased Russell T Davies “firing on all cylinders” with its mix of heartfelt moments and genre-bending fun[46][47]. Some criticism was directed at aspects like pacing and an overly sentimental musical score in a couple of scenes[48], but overall Lux was regarded as a standout installment of the 2025 series. Several commentators highlighted the mid-episode meta-fiction sequence – wherein the Doctor and Belinda meet Doctor Who fans within the story – as a memorable and audacious breaking of the fourth wall, done in an affectionate spirit toward the show’s fandom[15][49].

# 2025 World Seniors Championship
The 2025 World Seniors Championship (officially the 2025 JenningsBet World Seniors Championship) was a professional snooker tournament held from 7 to 11 May 2025 at the Crucible Theatre in Sheffield, England[1][2]. It featured 16 senior players from nine nations[2]. England’s Alfie Burden won the title, defeating Jersey’s Aaron Canavan 8–4 in the final[3]. Canavan had previously won the tournament in 2018, while Burden had been runner-up in 2023[4]. All matches were broadcast live in the UK on Channel 5[5].
## Participants and format
Defending champion Igor Figueiredo of Brazil was the top seed. Other invited players included former ranking event winners such as four-time Seniors champion Jimmy White and 1997 World Champion Ken Doherty[6], as well as Joe Perry, Dominic Dale, Malta’s Tony Drago, England’s Tony Knowles, and 2018 champion Aaron Canavan[7]. The remaining eight spots were filled through qualifying: four players (Andrew Norman, Gerard Greene, Wayne Townsend, and Aaron Canavan) advanced via the “Race to the Crucible” series[8], four were regional seniors champions (Craig Steadman of Europe, Hassan Kerde of Asia-Pacific, Charl Jonck of Africa, and Fabio Luersen of the Americas)[9], and the final place was won by Alfie Burden in a ‘Golden Ticket’ playoff[10].
The tournament adopted an extended format: early rounds through the semi-finals were best-of-7 frames, the semi-finals were best-of-11, and the final was best-of-15[5]. (Two breaks were played in each of the first two sessions of the final.) In the UK all matches were shown live on Channel 5[5]. The event was sponsored by JenningsBet, hence its official name.
## Results
#### Quarter-finals
Dominic Dale defeated Joe Perry 4–1[11].
Alfie Burden defeated Tony Drago 4–3 (after trailing 0–3)[12].
Hassan Kerde defeated Charl Jonck 4–2[13].
Aaron Canavan defeated Wayne Townsend 4–2[14].
#### Semi-finals
Alfie Burden 6–2 Hassan Kerde[15]. Burden raced to a 4–0 lead and won comfortably 6–2.
Aaron Canavan defeated Dominic Dale (deciding frame)[16]. Canavan trailed 4–5 but won the final frame to prevail 6–5.
#### Final
The best-of-15 final was contested by Alfie Burden and Aaron Canavan. Canavan won the opening frame, but Burden then won four in a row to lead 4–1 after the first session[17]. In the second session Burden made breaks of 65, 109 and 80 to extend his lead to 7–2[18], and he eventually closed out the match 8–4[3]. The opening session of the final was refereed by veteran official Michaela Tabb, marking her last match at the Crucible[19].
With this victory Burden captured his first World Seniors title.

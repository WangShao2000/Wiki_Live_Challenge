# Second-Ponce de Leon Baptist Church
Second-Ponce de Leon Baptist Church is a historic Baptist church located in the Buckhead district of Atlanta, Georgia, United States. The congregation was established in 1932 through the merger of three churches: Second Baptist Church (founded 1854), Ponce de Leon Avenue Baptist Church (founded 1904), and Buckhead Baptist Church (founded 1911)[1][2]. With roots dating back to the mid-19th century, Second-Ponce de Leon is one of Atlanta’s oldest Baptist congregations and remains a prominent church in the city. Its landmark building on Peachtree Road was designed in a Georgian Colonial Revival style and has long been a Buckhead architectural fixture[3].
## History
Origins: The earliest predecessor was Second Baptist Church of Atlanta, organized in 1854[1]. During the Civil War, Second Baptist’s downtown building was one of the few churches spared in General Sherman’s burning of Atlanta in 1864 – reportedly saved due to the intervention of Father Thomas O’Reilly of the Immaculate Conception Catholic Church[4][5]. By the late 19th and early 20th centuries, Atlanta’s growth prompted the creation of new Baptist congregations. In 1904, members from First and Second Baptist churches helped found Ponce de Leon Avenue Baptist Church to serve the city’s northern neighborhoods[6]. The new Ponce de Leon church was built at the corner of Ponce de Leon Avenue and Piedmont Avenue in Midtown Atlanta[7]. A third congregation, Buckhead Baptist Church, was established in 1911 to serve the then-suburban Buckhead community.
Mergers and Formation: By the 1920s, Ponce de Leon Avenue Baptist Church had outgrown its original facilities. In 1929, it merged with Buckhead Baptist Church and sold the Midtown property to a local Congregational church[8]. The combined congregation purchased land at Peachtree Road and East Wesley Road in Buckhead and began construction of a larger church campus[8]. The initial phase of the new building was completed in 1930[8], and the congregation worshipped there as Ponce de Leon Baptist Church. A few years later, on November 18, 1932, Atlanta’s Second Baptist Church officially merged into this congregation[9]. The unified church adopted the compound name Second-Ponce de Leon Baptist Church, honoring the heritage of both the Second Baptist and Ponce de Leon congregations[1]. This merger brought together the histories of all three predecessor churches under one organization.
Growth and Ministry: After 1932, Second-Ponce de Leon Baptist Church became a significant presence in Atlanta’s religious and civic life. The church experienced growth in the mid-20th century, expanding its programs and facilities to serve a growing membership. In 1954, Second-Ponce de Leon sponsored the launch of a new mission congregation in north Buckhead, purchasing property on Wieuca Road to establish Wieuca Road Baptist Church[10]. Wieuca Road Baptist grew rapidly and became an influential church in its own right, reflecting Second-Ponce’s commitment to church planting and outreach. Second-Ponce de Leon also supported various community ministries and maintained an active role in the Atlanta Baptist Association.
During the 1960s, Second-Ponce de Leon was involved in the founding of a Baptist college in Atlanta. Senior pastor Monroe F. Swilley Jr., who served at the church from 1945 to 1969, was a key advocate for establishing Atlanta Baptist College. In 1969 he resigned from the pastorate to become the first president of that new college[11][12]. Under Swilley’s leadership, the church had been a major financial supporter of the college’s creation[12]. (Atlanta Baptist College later merged into Mercer University in 1972, and Mercer’s Atlanta campus library was named in honor of Dr. Swilley[12].) Over the decades, many of the church’s clergy and members have been involved with Mercer University and other Baptist institutions, contributing to Second-Ponce’s reputation as an important church in Georgia Baptist life[11][13].
By the early 21st century, Second-Ponce de Leon Baptist Church remained a vibrant congregation. In 2004, the church marked the 150th anniversary of its earliest founding (1854–2004) and published a comprehensive history titled Second to None: A History of Second-Ponce de Leon Baptist Church by historian C. Douglas Weaver[14]. The church continues to adapt its ministries to serve both its longstanding membership and the wider community in Buckhead.
## Architecture
The church’s building at 2715 Peachtree Road NE was constructed in the early 1930s and is noted for its elegant Georgian Colonial Revival architecture. The design was created by Atlanta architect George Harwell Bond, and the main sanctuary was completed and formally dedicated in 1937[3]. The red-brick edifice features classical elements inspired by American colonial churches. Both the front and rear façades have large columned porticoes, and atop the sanctuary roof sits a distinctive white cupola crowned with a gilded dome[15][16].
The campus has expanded over time to include additional facilities, but the sanctuary and chapel retain their historic character. The four-story sanctuary structure contains worship space, classrooms, and a music suite, while adjacent buildings house a fellowship hall, family life center, and educational programs[17][18]. Modern renovations – such as a major refurbishing of the sanctuary and chapel completed in 2009 – have updated the interiors and added amenities while preserving the building’s traditional architectural style[19][20]. The Second-Ponce de Leon church building remains a landmark in Buckhead, contributing to the area’s architectural heritage. It is a contributing property in Atlanta’s Garden Hills Historic District, recognized for its historical and architectural significance[21].
## Affiliations and Congregational Life
Second-Ponce de Leon Baptist Church has long been associated with the Southern Baptist Convention (SBC) through the Georgia Baptist Convention, reflecting its roots in the Southern Baptist tradition[22]. At the same time, the church is known for its moderate theology and inclusive approach. It actively partners with the Cooperative Baptist Fellowship (CBF), a network of Baptist churches formed by moderates in the 1990s. In fact, Second-Ponce de Leon has been identified as one of the prominent Atlanta Baptist congregations supporting the CBF since its early years[23]. The church embraces historic Baptist principles such as local church autonomy, soul freedom, and the ordination of women, aligning it with the more progressive wing of the Baptist faith[24][25].
Within the congregation, there are ministries spanning all ages, including worship services, music programs, missions, and community outreach. Second-Ponce de Leon operates a Community Wellness Center on its campus that offers recreation, health, and fitness programs open to the public[26][27]. The church’s worship style is often described as traditional but warm, featuring a choir and orchestra, and the congregation highly values education and fellowship. Over the years, many of its members have been civic leaders, educators, and professionals in Atlanta, contributing to the church’s active role in the community. Second-Ponce de Leon Baptist Church continues to “share God’s love in the world,” consistent with its mission, by blending its rich heritage with ongoing service and ministry in contemporary Atlanta[28][29].

# LIV (nightclub)
LIV is a prominent nightclub located in Miami Beach, Florida, inside the historic Fontainebleau Miami Beach hotel. Opened in 2008 by nightlife entrepreneur David Grutman, the club quickly became one of the highest-grossing and most popular nightclubs in the United States[1][2]. It is known for its upscale ambiance, celebrity clientele, and frequent live performances by top DJs and artists. The name "LIV" represents the Roman numeral 54, a nod to 1954 – the year the Fontainebleau opened – reflecting the venue’s link to the hotel’s legacy[3]. LIV has expanded beyond its original location, including a VIP club presence at Hard Rock Stadium in Miami and a second nightclub at the Fontainebleau Las Vegas resort (opened in late 2023)[4][5].
## History
LIV was established as part of a $1 billion renovation of the Fontainebleau Miami Beach and held its grand opening in late 2008, coinciding with the Victoria’s Secret Fashion Show after-party[6]. The club was created and operated by David Grutman under his company (then Miami Marketing Group, later Groot Hospitality). From its outset, LIV cultivated an exclusive image and attracted high-profile guests, helping revive Miami Beach’s nightclub scene. In 2010, owner Stephen Ross of the Miami Dolphins partnered with Grutman to bring the LIV experience to professional sports – Club LIV opened at the team’s Sun Life Stadium as a 10,000-square-foot luxury lounge and nightclub space in the west end zone[7]. This venue, featuring DJ booths, cabanas and bottle service, allowed VIP fans to party during games, aligning with Ross’s vision of infusing South Beach nightlife into stadium entertainment[8]. (Hard Rock Stadium continues to host this “LIV at the Stadium” club for Dolphins games and events.)
By the early 2010s, LIV was regularly ranked among the top nightlife venues in the U.S. by revenue. In 2011, industry estimates placed LIV among the five highest-grossing clubs nationally, with annual revenue around $40–45 million[2]. The club’s success led Grutman to expand his portfolio (he opened a second Miami nightclub, Story, in 2012) and eventually form Groot Hospitality in 2018 as an umbrella for his ventures. In October 2019, Live Nation Entertainment acquired a 51% stake in Groot Hospitality, which included LIV, as part of a deal to fuel the brand’s expansion[9][10]. Grutman retained operational control and stated the partnership would provide “firepower” to grow concepts like LIV in new markets[11]. Plans for a Las Vegas iteration of LIV were subsequently realized: a LIV Las Vegas nightclub opened inside the newly built Fontainebleau Las Vegas hotel in December 2023, 15 years after the Miami original[5]. The Las Vegas outpost, also owned by Grutman in collaboration with Fontainebleau’s developers, marked the brand’s first expansion outside Florida and features a similar luxury club atmosphere on a larger scale.
Over the years, LIV has periodically upgraded to stay at the forefront of nightlife trends. In mid-2017, the Miami club closed for about two months to undergo a major renovation (timed with its 9th anniversary). The $10 million overhaul, led by design firm ICRAVE, added new flooring, bars and VIP sections, and installed an innovative light and sound system[12][13]. Over 400 moving LED panels were embedded in the domed ceiling to create a kinetic light show nicknamed “The Spider,” dramatically enhancing the venue’s visual effects and 360-degree immersion[13][14]. LIV celebrated its grand re-opening in September 2017 with performances by artists like Travis Scott, Skrillex and Lil Wayne, unveiling the high-tech amenities to guests[15]. The club continued to thrive into the late 2010s, solidifying its status as a South Florida nightlife institution.
Like many hospitality venues, LIV was impacted by the COVID-19 pandemic in 2020. The nightclub shut down in March 2020 amid statewide closures and remained closed for over a year. It finally reopened on April 16, 2021, as Miami Beach lifted restrictions, hosting a weekend of events headlined by dance music acts (such as Alesso) to mark the return of nightlife[16][17]. By 2022, LIV had fully resumed its regular operation and continued to attract celebrity-packed crowds. In 2023, the club drew attention for a legal dispute with the newly formed LIV Golf tour – Grutman’s company filed a trademark opposition, arguing the Saudi-backed golf league’s use of “LIV” could cause confusion with the well-known nightclub brand[1]. Meanwhile, the opening of the Las Vegas location in late 2023 extended the LIV brand’s reach and heralded a new chapter, with Grutman also planning a dayclub (LIV Beach) and other ventures under the LIV name in that resort[18][5]. As of 2025, LIV in Miami remains a centerpiece of the city’s nightlife, while the LIV Las Vegas venue has begun establishing itself on the Vegas Strip scene.
## Location and Design
LIV is situated in the lobby level of the Fontainebleau Miami Beach, a renowned hotel at 4441 Collins Avenue that has a rich entertainment history. The nightclub occupies the space of the hotel’s former Tropigala lounge (also known as the La Ronde Room), where icons like Frank Sinatra and Elvis Presley once performed in the mid-20th century[19]. The design of LIV pays homage to this legacy while featuring modern, state-of-the-art club architecture. The venue encompasses approximately 18,000 square feet of floor space arranged in a multi-tiered layout[12]. At the center is a sunken rectangular dance floor and stage, above which soars a high domed ceiling – a signature element retained from the Fontainebleau’s original design[19].
Encircling the dance floor are several tiers of VIP tables, booths, and bars. A pair of grand staircases curves around the DJ booth and connects the ground floor with the mezzanine-level balconies and private skyboxes[20]. This configuration provides sightlines of the stage and dance floor from nearly every corner of the club. The décor is characterized by a blend of elegant materials (marble, rich fabrics) and cutting-edge lighting technology. During the 2017 renovation, LIV installed a steel truss lattice in the dome ceiling to support hundreds of programmable LED screens – the system known as “The Spider” can lower and move to create enveloping light displays above the crowd[14]. The venue also upgraded its audio to an advanced L-Acoustics sound system, ensuring top-quality acoustics for both live performers and DJs[21].
LIV’s layout includes multiple full-service bars, elevated DJ platforms, and exclusive VIP sections such as the stage-side banquettes and several roped-off lounge areas for celebrity guests. The official capacity is around 1,100 patrons[22], though on major event nights the club often fills beyond that with standing-room crowds. A strict upscale dress code is enforced, aligning with the club’s luxury branding. The Fontainebleau hotel location also means club-goers have access to valet parking, and many guests stay on the property when attending events. In popular imagination, the opulent design and atmosphere of LIV have drawn comparisons to storied nightclubs like Manhattan’s Studio 54 – both for its architectural grandeur and its see-and-be-seen social scene[23].
The new LIV Las Vegas mirrors many design elements of the Miami original while adapting to its casino resort setting. Located in the Fontainebleau Las Vegas, the Vegas club is larger in scale, featuring multi-level spaces and an adjacent dayclub. After a post-opening expansion in 2025, the combined LIV Nightclub and LIV Beach in Las Vegas span over 80,000 square feet, integrating an indoor nightclub with an outdoor pool party area[24][25]. The Las Vegas club emphasizes an “immersive, festival-like” layout, with two-story LED walls, expansive dance areas, and VIP bungalows overlooking the pool[26][27]. Despite the grand size, the operators aimed to replicate LIV’s signature intimacy – in Las Vegas, no table or guest is far from view of the DJ, keeping the energy focused on the music and performance[28][29]. Both the Miami and Las Vegas locations feature contemporary, extravagant design elements intended to provide a high-end nightlife experience that blends a nightclub’s energy with the hospitality of an elite lounge.
## Notable Events and Celebrity Presence
Since its inception, LIV has been synonymous with celebrity nightlife in Miami. The club’s weekly hip-hop party, “LIV on Sunday,” has become especially famous. Established around 2009, LIV on Sunday transformed Sunday nights into a must-attend event for hip-hop artists, athletes, and entertainers. The party regularly sees live performances or guest appearances by rap superstars, and its reputation has been immortalized in song lyrics – artists such as Lil Wayne, Drake, Rick Ross, and Meek Mill have all name-dropped “LIV on Sunday” in their tracks[30]. Media outlets have described LIV’s Sunday bash as a “legendary hip-hop haven” where “the stars come out” to party after big concerts or award shows[31]. It is not uncommon for figures like Diddy, Lil Wayne, DJ Khaled, or Drake to be spotted partying or even spontaneously performing on a Sunday night at LIV. Rapper Lil Wayne, a Miami resident for many years, was known to have a reserved table as a regular, and DJ Khaled famously recorded part of his music video “I’m On One” inside LIV, further cementing the club’s cultural cachet in the hip-hop world.
Beyond its weekly parties, LIV has hosted a plethora of high-profile events and celebrations. In the music industry, the club is a go-to venue for album release parties, tour after-parties, and artist birthdays. One notorious example was Jay-Z’s album release party for Watch the Throne in August 2011, which took place at LIV. That night, Jay-Z reportedly set a club record by spending $250,000 on bottle service, including a 15-liter “Ace of Spades” champagne bottle valued around $100,000[32]. This extravagance was an attempt to outdo Dallas Mavericks owner Mark Cuban, who had run up a roughly $110,000 tab at LIV earlier (after his NBA team won the 2011 championship)[32]. According to reports, the club’s cover charge during Jay-Z’s party was as high as $300 for women and $500 for men, underscoring the demand to attend such star-studded events[33]. Other hip-hop moguls have held major celebrations at LIV as well – for instance, Cash Money Records co-founder Birdman celebrated his birthday at the club with a lavish affair, and Drake has used LIV for after-parties following Miami concerts.
LIV is also a favorite spot for athletes, especially after championship victories or significant sports events in Miami. Perhaps the most famous sports celebration at LIV occurred in June 2012, when the Miami Heat – led by LeBron James, Dwyane Wade, and Chris Bosh – partied at LIV after winning the NBA Finals. The Heat’s championship party at the club became urban legend: the team and their friends reportedly amassed a $147,000 bill in one night[34]. LeBron James was seen drinking straight from a huge 15L bottle of Brut Ace of Spades champagne (a bottle estimated at $75,000) as confetti fell and the trophy was passed around[35]. The celebration lasted until dawn, with players and celebrities dancing on stage – and notably, the Heat’s bash outdid the Mavericks’ own LIV celebration from the previous year by tens of thousands of dollars in champagne and service[36]. The club’s role as the unofficial victory party venue for Miami sports teams continued in subsequent years; for example, the University of Miami’s 2021 football recruiting social was held at LIV, and other championship teams visiting Miami have been known to buy out tables at the club.
In addition to athletic triumphs, LIV has hosted numerous fashion, film, and corporate events. During the annual Art Basel Miami Beach festival, LIV often holds exclusive after-parties with internationally known DJs. The club has been the site of product launch events and model showcases – including serving as a venue for Victoria’s Secret after-parties beyond just the 2008 opening. World-famous DJs such as Tiesto, David Guetta, Calvin Harris, and Skrillex have headlined nights at LIV, either as part of Miami’s Winter Music Conference/Ultra Music Festival week or on regular tour stops. Pop stars and Hollywood actors also make appearances; it’s not unusual to see the likes of Justin Bieber (who performed impromptu at LIV in 2015)[37], or celebrities like Kim Kardashian, Jennifer Lopez, and Floyd Mayweather enjoying a night at the club’s VIP areas. Media coverage frequently captures these celebrity moments, contributing to LIV’s image as a glitterati playground.
Despite the party-hard reputation, LIV’s management maintains tight security and controlled entry to protect the exclusivity of the experience. The club has occasionally made news beyond entertainment: for example, in early 2021 it offered on-site COVID-19 vaccination pop-ups for patrons and staff as part of reopening safely[38]. Overall, the parade of famous faces and headline-making parties at LIV over the years have cemented its status as a nightlife icon in Miami. The phrase “Livin’ at LIV” has even entered local vernacular to denote a lavish night out, and images of champagne sparklers and celebrity DJs at the club have become emblematic of Miami’s glamor.
## Cultural Impact
LIV’s influence extends beyond just being a nightclub – it has become a cultural reference point for luxury nightlife and Miami’s party scene. In the hip-hop and pop culture lexicon, “LIV on Sunday” signifies the pinnacle of nightlife indulgence. Numerous songs and music videos have referenced LIV, reinforcing its reputation nationally. For instance, rapper Drake famously alluded to partying at LIV in his lyrics, and Lil Wayne’s frequent shout-outs to LIV (and appearances there) helped popularize the club among fans. The Miami New Times noted that Miami is woven into hip-hop storytelling “from clubs like LIV to neighborhoods like Carol City,” underlining that LIV has become part of the city’s identity in music[30]. Being mentioned alongside locales like South Beach and Ocean Drive, LIV symbolizes the allure of Miami’s glamorous after-hours culture.
The club has also garnered recognition in industry circles. In the annual DJ Mag Top 100 Clubs rankings (a global poll of nightlife venues), LIV has perennially made the list, reflecting its international renown. In the 2010s it climbed into the upper tier of the rankings, and even as of 2025, DJ Mag listed LIV Miami among the world’s notable clubbing destinations (citing its signature dome and LED “Spider” installation as unique features)[19]. Such accolades place LIV in company with legendary European superclubs and Las Vegas mega-clubs, indicating its far-reaching reputation. Nightlife media often compare LIV to iconic clubs of the past: for example, coverage in 2017 described it as “already an iconic event space… reminiscent of Ibiza or Studio 54” in atmosphere[23]. This comparison highlights how, within less than a decade, LIV achieved a mythos akin to those historic venues, at least in terms of prestige and vibe.
Another aspect of LIV’s cultural impact is its synergy with Miami’s image. The club has been featured on reality TV and in celebrity news items, helping shape perceptions of Miami Beach as a playground for the rich and famous. Episodes of shows like Keeping Up with the Kardashians and Miami Vice (revival) have included scenes at Fontainebleau and LIV, showcasing its distinctive interiors. The club itself partnered with brands and events; for instance, during Super Bowl LIV (which fortuitously matched the club’s name in numeral form) in Miami in 2020, LIV hosted multiple high-profile parties for athletes and entertainers in town[39]. The coincidence of Super Bowl “LIV” further boosted the nightclub’s visibility that year, and the club capitalized by aligning promotions with the event.
The LIV brand’s expansion into other domains also illustrates its cultural footprint. The presence of “LIV at Hard Rock Stadium” brought nightlife culture into sports in an innovative way, a concept that has since been emulated by other stadiums looking to enhance the fan experience. The launch of LIV in Las Vegas in 2023 likewise signals Miami’s influence reaching a broader entertainment capital. David Grutman’s growing celebrity as the “king of Miami nightlife”[10] – due in large part to LIV’s success – has led to collaborations with global stars (he’s partnered with singer Pharrell Williams on restaurants, for example). In this sense, LIV has elevated its founder and Miami’s hospitality scene onto an international stage.
In popular discourse, LIV often represents the height of Miami’s lavish party lifestyle, for better or worse. It has been the subject of both glamourous praise and occasional criticism (such as debates about pricey bottle service or strict door policies). Nevertheless, the club’s longevity and continued prominence suggest it successfully navigates trends and maintains its cachet. As one of the longest-running major nightclubs in South Beach still in operation since the 2000s, LIV holds a legacy comparable to previous Miami hot-spots like Club Space or Mansion, but with a sustained mainstream appeal. In summary, through music, media, and its own trendsetting, LIV has made a lasting cultural impact as an emblem of Miami Beach nightlife luxury and entertainment.

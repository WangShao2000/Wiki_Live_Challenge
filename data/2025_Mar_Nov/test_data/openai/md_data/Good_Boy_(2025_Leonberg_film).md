# Good Boy (2025 film)
Good Boy is a 2025 American supernatural horror film directed by Ben Leonberg in his feature directorial debut[1]. Leonberg co-wrote the screenplay with Alex Cannon[1], and the film stars Leonberg’s own dog, Indy (a Nova Scotia Duck Tolling Retriever), alongside Shane Jensen, Arielle Friedman, and Larry Fessenden[2]. Presented almost entirely from the dog’s perspective, the story follows a loyal pet who must protect his owner from a malevolent supernatural force after they move into an isolated family home[3][4]. The film premiered at the 2025 South by Southwest (SXSW) Film & TV Festival in March 2025 and was released theatrically in the United States on October 3, 2025, by IFC Films[5][6]. Made on an ultra-low budget of around $70,000 and filmed over three years[7], Good Boy became a sleeper hit – it opened to $2.2 million and went on to gross over $8.5 million worldwide[8] – and earned praise from critics for its inventive concept and emotional storytelling[9][10].
## Plot
Todd, a young man recovering from a serious illness, decides to leave the city and retreat to his late grandfather’s remote cabin in the countryside, bringing along his beloved dog, Indy[11][12]. Upon arriving at the old house, Indy immediately senses something is wrong – the loyal Nova Scotia Duck Tolling Retriever is wary of the creepy, long-vacant home and begins noticing strange phenomena that Todd cannot perceive[3]. Indy explores empty rooms and dark corners where he detects an invisible presence, and he experiences eerie signs that only he can understand, including phantom scents, unexplained noises, and even ghostly warnings from a long-dead dog that once lived on the property[4][13].
As days pass, Todd’s health and behavior deteriorate under the influence of an unseen malevolence lurking in the house[14][15]. Through Indy’s eyes and ears, the threat manifests as ominous sights and sounds: shadowy figures flicker in the periphery, doors creak with no one there, and a gaunt apparition stalks the halls at night. Indy also encounters the spirit of Bandit, the grandfather’s deceased dog, whose forlorn cries echo from behind a locked cellar door[16]. With Todd growing increasingly sick and acting erratically – at times resembling a man possessed – Indy realizes that a dark force is trying to drag his best friend toward death[17][18]. In a series of frightening encounters, the courageous dog attempts to intervene: he trails the invisible intruder, alerts Todd to danger, and even faces off against a sinister ghoul that emerges from the shadows. In the climax, as supernatural darkness closes in, Indy engages in a desperate struggle to save Todd from the entity. The ordeal builds to a tense and melancholic finale, as Indy’s unwavering loyalty is tested against a threat he can barely comprehend[19]. (In the end, Indy survives the nightmare, though the outcome for Todd is tragic, underscoring the sorrowful devotion of a dog who never leaves his owner’s side[20][19].)

(Indy the dog is credited as the film’s lead “actor,” and Bandit, the ghost dog, is a spectral presence important to the story. Liptu Das provides vocal sounds for a dog referred to as “Hero,” an element of the film’s auditory perspective[26][27].)
## Production
Ben Leonberg conceived Good Boy as a horror story told entirely through a dog’s point of view, inspired by the genre trope that “the dog knows” when something evil is present[28][18]. Leonberg co-wrote the script with Alex Cannon, crafting a minimalistic scenario with few characters and one primary location to accommodate the perspective-driven concept and a limited budget[29][11]. The film was shot over the span of three years on a shoestring budget of approximately $70,000, with Leonberg using his own trained pet dog, Indy, as the star[7]. This ultra-low-budget, largely self-financed approach allowed the filmmakers to work at the dog’s pace and comfort level. Leonberg and his wife (producer Kari Fischer) were the only people on set with Indy during filming, creating a calm environment to elicit the dog’s natural behavior[30][31].
To capture the canine point of view, the production employed innovative techniques. Cinematographer Wade Grebnoel often shot from a low height at Indy’s eye level, and many scenes are framed so that human faces (even Todd’s) are obscured or off-screen, mirroring what Indy sees[32][33]. The filmmakers avoided using CGI for the dog; instead, they relied on practical tricks and patient training to get the needed reactions[34][35]. For example, when Indy appears “paralyzed with fear” at an unseen presence, in reality he was fixated on a treat or tennis ball just off camera[36]. Likewise, Indy’s whimpers and yelps of “distress” were crafted in post-production from audio of him happily playing fetch, ensuring the dog was never actually frightened on set[36]. Any intense action involving Indy was carefully staged: some sequences were filmed in reverse and later reversed in editing to make it look like Indy was approaching danger, and other shots used subtle speed ramps, camera shake, and sound design to convey peril without upsetting the animal actor[36]. The production even created dog-safe fake blood (a mixture of edible red foods) for certain scenes[36]. Leonberg’s gentle directing and Indy’s strong bond with him enabled the film to capture a range of expressive canine “performances” through editing. Critics noted that Indy’s reactions – his attentive stares, cautious steps, and defensive posture – feel remarkably genuine and help sell the film’s scares[37][38].
Principal photography primarily took place in rural locations in upstate New York, using an actual old farmhouse and its surrounding woods to achieve an authentic atmosphere (the exact shooting location was not widely publicized, but the setting is implied to be in the northeastern U.S.)[39][40]. As a result of the prolonged shooting schedule (working around Indy’s training and comfort), Good Boy was only completed in early 2025. The final cut runs a taut 73 minutes[41][42], reflecting the filmmakers’ effort to keep the story lean and focused on the dog’s experience. Leonberg described the film as a personal project born from his love of horror and dogs; he has cited the emotional bond between pets and owners as the core of the narrative, using the supernatural elements as an allegory for illness and mortality[21][43].
## Release
Good Boy had its world premiere on March 8, 2025, at the SXSW Film and TV Festival in Austin, Texas, where it screened as part of the festival’s Midnighters section[5]. The premiere reportedly sold out, and Indy’s performance received an enthusiastic response from the audience (the dog was even honored with a novelty “Howl of Fame” award for Best Canine Performance at the festival)[3][44]. Following SXSW, the film toured several genre and independent film festivals, including the Overlook Film Festival in New Orleans and international fests like the Bucheon International Fantastic Film Festival in South Korea, building up buzz through word-of-mouth from horror enthusiasts.
In the spring of 2025, streaming service Shudder (owned by AMC Networks) acquired the distribution rights to Good Boy for key territories including North America, the U.K., and Australia[45][44]. Shudder partnered with IFC Films to give the movie a theatrical release in the United States, initially planning a limited rollout. However, in August 2025, the film’s trailer went viral online – amassing over 2 million views within days – and internet searches for “Does the dog die in Good Boy?” spiked dramatically as curious viewers took interest[46][47]. Buoyed by this surge of attention and positive festival reviews, IFC shifted strategy and expanded Good Boy to a wide release. The film opened nationwide on October 3, 2025 (just in time for the Halloween season), across approximately 1,650 theaters in the U.S. and Canada[48][49]. It was rated PG-13 for its horror elements and had a brisk runtime of 1 hour 13 minutes[50].
On its opening weekend, Good Boy earned $2.34 million, debuting at #9 at the domestic box office[49]. This marked the second-best opening ever for an IFC Films release, and one of the strongest starts for a Shudder-affiliated film, despite Good Boy’s micro-budget and lack of A-list stars[47][51]. The film exceeded expectations and benefited from a “viral marketing” effect – audiences were intrigued by the concept of a horror movie from a dog’s POV, and horror fans turned out to experience it in a communal setting[52][49]. Good Boy remained in the top 10 in its second weekend with a modest drop, grossing about $1.36 million in the sophomore frame and bringing the 10-day total to roughly $4.8 million[53][54]. The theatrical run continued through late October, with the film playing in theaters for about three weeks. By the end of its domestic run, Good Boy had grossed $6.62 million in the United States and Canada[55][56]. Internationally, the release was more limited – the movie rolled out to a few overseas markets (such as Spain, Mexico, and Australia) around late October to capitalize on Halloween interest[57][58]. These engagements added another ~$2 million to the tally, bringing the total worldwide gross to approximately $8.68 million[59]. This outcome made Good Boy one of the most financially successful original films in Shudder’s history and a major return on investment given its tiny budget. Industry observers noted that the film’s performance was comparable to studio horrors with far larger budgets, underlining how strongly the premise resonated with dog lovers and genre audiences[47][60].
After the theatrical window, Good Boy was released to digital platforms. It became available for paid video-on-demand rental and purchase on October 24, 2025[61], allowing those who missed it in cinemas to watch at home. The film then made its subscription streaming debut on Shudder (and sister service AMC+) on November 21, 2025, as part of Shudder’s November lineup[62][63]. This streaming release was timed to arrive just before the U.S. Thanksgiving holiday, with Shudder marketing it as a unique horror experience to share with family (noting tongue-in-cheek that Good Boy is one scary movie where animal lovers don’t have to fear for the dog)[64]. Upon hitting Shudder, the film continued to find a wider audience and spurred discussions about its emotional impact and innovative storytelling on social media and horror forums. A Blu-ray and DVD release has been announced for early 2026, which will include behind-the-scenes featurettes showcasing how Leonberg worked with Indy to achieve the film’s distinctive perspective.
## Reception
### Critical response
Good Boy received generally positive reviews from critics, with particular praise for its originality and the canine lead’s performance. On Rotten Tomatoes, the film holds a 91% approval rating based on over 180 reviews, with an average critical consensus describing Good Boy as a “visually striking, emotionally devastating horror film” that offers a “uniquely haunting” experience by subverting genre conventions[10][9]. Metacritic assigned the film a score of 73 out of 100, indicating “generally favorable” reviews, based on a sample of critic opinions[65].
Reviewers often noted that the concept – a haunted house tale seen entirely through a dog’s eyes – could have easily come off as a gimmick, but most agreed that Good Boy transcends its high concept. Benjamin Lee of The Guardian called the film “crafty [and] ingenious” in how it finds fresh perspective in a familiar story, likening it to a “haunted-house riff” led by a truly heroic dog[66][42]. Lee wrote that Leonberg turns a potentially gimmicky premise into “something surprisingly impactful,” portraying Indy as “a sort of confused child” who remains steadfastly loyal even as his owner undergoes a Jack Torrance-like descent into darkness[15][20]. The Guardian review highlighted the film’s emotional undercurrents and praised Indy (both the character and the real dog) for anchoring the horror with genuine heart, although it noted that even at 73 minutes the film “can feel stretched” due to a thin story, with some repetitive sequences of the dog investigating bumps in the night[67][68]. Nevertheless, the review concluded that Good Boy delivers a “fittingly melancholic finale” and serves as a “punchy announcement” of Leonberg’s talent, predicting “big things lie ahead for him” in the genre[69].
Robert Daniels of RogerEbert.com also commended the film’s ambition, stating that Good Boy “could easily devolve into a mere gimmick, but [the] dialogue-light script is aiming for more – a movie about heart, loyalty, and friendship”[70]. Daniels was impressed by sequences that truly put the viewer into the dog’s subjective experience, such as a brief, “ingenious” dream scene that takes us “inside the mind of a dreaming dog”, calling these moments “transportive”[26]. His review noted the film’s effective use of sound design and camerawork to convey Indy’s heightened senses – the way Indy’s ears catch distant thuds and creaking doors, or how his vision scans shadowy corners – all of which create suspense from ordinary dog reactions[71][72]. Like other critics, Daniels felt the narrative sagged in the middle with some monotony (he argued the concept might have worked just as well in a 40-minute short), but he believed the last act “sticks the landing” with an “intense race to save Todd” that pays off emotionally[73]. He lauded Indy as “an expressive, wonderful pup” who keeps the audience invested even when the film’s tricks become familiar[74]. “By the end, Indy and this movie deserve a treat,” the review quipped, emphasizing that the film’s true triumph is making the viewer care so deeply about a dog’s devotion[43].
Other critics echoed these sentiments. Meagan Navarro of Bloody Disgusting wrote that Good Boy is an “emotionally affecting haunted house tale” that doubles as a heartbreaking metaphor – the evil haunting the house reflects the creeping threat of illness and death, with Indy’s unconditional love representing hope and resistance[21][75]. Variety’s reviewer (Dennis Harvey) highlighted the film’s technical cleverness, observing that the filmmakers “visualize how a dog might see the world” with claustrophobic framing and distorted sounds, making familiar horror beats feel fresh and unpredictable[76][77]. At the same time, some critics felt the film’s simplicity was a double-edged sword. The Hollywood Reporter praised the concept and Indy’s performance but cautioned that viewers expecting a conventional horror narrative might find the film “slight” – as the runtime is short and the plot minimal, the movie is more focused on atmosphere and sensation than on complex story developments (the humans are deliberately underdeveloped, since we only know what the dog knows)[78][11]. Nonetheless, even mixed reviews acknowledged that Good Boy offers a rare and compelling point of view. Wendy Ide of The Observer (UK) described the film as “both hair-raising and heartbreaking,” noting that it “manages to be both a crowd-pleasing genre exercise and a genuinely poignant exploration of a dog’s unconditional loyalty”[9][43].
Audiences, especially dog lovers, responded strongly to the film. The CinemaScore-style viewer polls were not widely reported (given the film’s indie status), but anecdotal reports indicate many viewers were deeply moved – some to tears – by Indy’s bravery and the bond depicted on screen. Online discussions saw horror fans calling Indy “the best dog actor ever”, and the film sparked debate about whether animal actors should be eligible for major awards[79][80]. A minority of horror aficionados felt the movie was overhyped – on forums like Reddit, a few viewers argued that aside from the unique perspective, the scares were traditional and the plot predictable, with one commenter quipping that they “only finished it because the dog was cute”[81]. However, such critiques were in the minority compared to the overwhelmingly positive buzz in the horror community.
Notably, Good Boy received recognition from an animal welfare perspective as well. PETA (People for the Ethical Treatment of Animals) praised the production for its humane treatment of its four-legged star. In October 2025, PETA honored the film with a “Good Boy, Great Filmmaking Award,” citing the filmmakers’ extraordinary care to ensure Indy’s safety and comfort on set[53][82]. PETA’s award announcement highlighted that Leonberg and Fischer used their own dog rather than a trained animal actor from a supplier (some of which have faced cruelty allegations), and they “pulled out all the stops” to make the experience positive for Indy[30]. Lauren Thomasson, PETA’s Animals in Film director, applauded Good Boy for “showing Hollywood how to tell animal stories in a compassionate and ethical way,” saying the film’s message reinforces that to a pet, “their human family is everything,” so humans should pay attention to what animals are trying to tell us[83][84].
### Box office and impact
Given its minimal budget and unconventional concept, Good Boy’s box office success was seen as remarkable. Earning over $6.6 million domestically[55] (and $8.7 million globally[59]), the film achieved a profit multiplier rarely seen in modern cinema – more than 100 times its production cost. It became IFC Films’ third-highest grossing opening weekend in the company’s history (behind only two other horror titles) and helped boost parent company AMC/Shudder’s confidence in theatrical releases for niche horror projects[47]. Industry analysts noted that Good Boy tapped into a crossover audience: horror aficionados were drawn by the positive reviews and festival buzz, while many general viewers were intrigued by the promise that, unlike most horror movies with dogs, this one does not harm the canine (a fact that spread via social media and even the site “DoesTheDogDie.com”)[85][29]. The “dog doesn’t die” selling point, combined with the novel POV, turned Good Boy into a must-see event for pet owners who might usually avoid horror. IFC’s marketing leaned into this, even releasing tongue-in-cheek promo materials encouraging audiences to “trust your dog’s instincts” and highlighting Indy’s performance as a selling point.
By the end of 2025, Good Boy had set a new benchmark for Shudder: it became the highest-grossing film to date that was branded as a Shudder original (surpassing the 2024 thriller In a Violent Nature which had earned around $4.2M)[86][87]. This success story has drawn comparisons to other recent micro-budget horror breakouts and has been cited in discussions about expanding awards recognition to animal performers. The film’s popularity even spurred a lighthearted awards campaign: some fans and commentators mused that Indy the dog should be considered for an Academy Award (or that a special Oscar category for animal actors ought to exist), though the Academy has no such provision. While an Oscar nod was never realistic, Good Boy did make awards history on another front. In late 2025, the film received two nominations at the 9th Astra Film Awards – one for Best First Feature (recognizing Ben Leonberg’s direction) and another for Best Performance in a Horror or Thriller for Indy the dog[88][89]. Indy’s nomination marks the first time an animal actor has ever been nominated in a major film acting award category, a fact widely noted in entertainment media as a groundbreaking moment[89][80]. (The Astra Film Awards are an emerging awards body that honors genre cinema; although not as prestigious as Oscars, they garnered attention for opening their acting categories to non-human performers.)
Overall, Good Boy’s critical and commercial performance has positioned it as a cult favorite of 2025 and a notable debut for Ben Leonberg. The film’s success demonstrated that innovative storytelling – even using an unorthodox protagonist – can resonate with audiences and that there is appetite for fresh ideas in horror. It also sparked conversations about the depth of emotion in pet-human relationships, all within the framework of a spooky crowd-pleaser. As one reviewer put it, “Good Boy is not just a horror movie with a dog; it’s a moving reminder of why we don’t deserve dogs.” Indy’s soulful presence earned the film a unique place in the genre, and future projects with animal points-of-view will likely be measured against the high bar set by this very good boy.

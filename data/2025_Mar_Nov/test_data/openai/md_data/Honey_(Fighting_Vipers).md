# Honey (Fighting Vipers)
Honey (Japanese: ハニー, Hepburn: Hanī) is a fictional character in Sega’s Fighting Vipers series of fighting video games. She first appeared as a playable fighter in the original Fighting Vipers (1995), which was developed by Sega’s AM2 studio[1][2]. In international releases of that game, the character’s name was changed to “Candy,” although later installments restored the original name[3]. Honey is portrayed as a teenage fashion designer who enters fighting tournaments to promote her self-made clothing line[4][5]. The character is known for her distinctive attire – a bright red vinyl dress with angel-wing accessories – and a moveset themed around cat-like attacks (e.g. clawing and scratching strikes)[6]. Honey’s design and marketing emphasized sex appeal; one notorious promotional image titled “A Taste of Honey” was deemed too risqué and removed from the Western release of a Sega game[7]. An anthropomorphic version of the character called Honey the Cat appears as a secret fighter in Sonic the Fighters (1996), reflecting Honey’s legacy beyond the Fighting Vipers franchise[8].
## Concept and design
Within the game’s storyline, Honey is a 16-year-old fashion student from the fictional Armstone City (aged 18 in Fighting Vipers 2)[9]. She is normally shy and quiet, but her personality changes when she suits up in the flashy rubber dress that she designed for battle[4]. Donning this costume transforms her into a bold “radical viper” persona, and she uses the underground fighting competition as a venue to show off her fashion creations[10]. Honey’s outfit is a red latex dress with a short frilled skirt, complemented by white boots, gloves, and decorative angel wings on her back and in her hair[6]. Many of her combat moves have feline names – such as “Cat Punch” and “Cat Scratch” – underscoring a playful cat motif in her fighting style[6]. The character was designed by Masahiro Sugiyama, the character artist for Fighting Vipers (and later Sonic the Fighters)[11].
One distinctive gameplay feature of the Fighting Vipers series is armor destruction: characters wear breakable armor that can be smashed off. When Honey’s armor is fully broken during a match, her dress shatters – leaving her wearing only a leather bra, mini-skirt, boots and gloves as her “underwear” outfit[12]. This revealing state, along with Honey’s provocative poses, made her one of the early examples of overt fanservice in 3D fighting games[13]. Sega’s promotional artwork for the character was likewise suggestive. Notably, the Japanese version of Fighters Megamix featured a pin-up style illustration of Honey called “A Taste of Honey,” which was removed from the North American release due to its sexualized content[7]. (The image was included as an unlockable gallery item in Japan, but replaced in the Western version.) In the console port of Fighting Vipers 2, an alternate “naughty nurse” costume for Honey was added as a hidden bonus; players can unlock this risqué nurse uniform by completing special objectives in the game[14].
## Appearances
### Fighting Vipers (1995)
Honey (known as Candy in the West) is one of the original playable characters in Fighting Vipers, an arcade fighting game later released on the Sega Saturn. In the game’s story, she is a young amateur fashion designer who enters the “City of Armstone” fighting tournament to advertise her custom-made outfits[4][15]. Her stage in the game is an urban street area (Bayside Park), which notably contains a piece of graffiti that reads, “In memory of Honey, you will always be remembered… R.I.P.” – a tongue-in-cheek Easter egg referencing the fact that the name “Honey” was dropped in the international version[16]. Honey’s fighting style in Fighting Vipers emphasizes speed and agility. She is one of the few characters in the game with an air-throw technique (reflecting her acrobatic, flying kicks)[6]. When her armor is damaged by opponents, pieces of her red dress break off until she’s left in a skimpy bra top and skirt, which became an iconic visual element of the character[12]. Despite the localized name change to “Candy,” the character’s portrayal and moveset remained the same across all regions.
### Fighting Vipers 2 (1998)
Honey returns as a playable character in Fighting Vipers 2, released in arcades in 1998 and later on the Dreamcast. The sequel’s storyline is set two years after the first game, with Honey now 18 years old and continuing to pursue her fashion career[9]. In FV2, a crackdown on the illegal fights (ordered by the city authority, B.M.) leads to an event called the “Viper Hunt,” but Honey still sees it as the perfect opportunity to show off her newest dress designs[17]. Her personality and fighting style remain consistent with the first game – she is still depicted as a cheerful, style-conscious brawler using fast cat-like attacks. Notably, in Fighting Vipers 2 the character’s original name “Honey” was retained in English releases (the European PAL version), effectively reversing the earlier name change[18]. The Dreamcast version of FV2 also features additional costumes for Honey as unlockable content. By fulfilling certain in-game conditions (such as witnessing all special “Super KO” moves), players can unlock Honey’s bonus outfits – most famously a revealing nurse uniform – as alternate attire for the character[14]. This extra costume further highlights the character’s sexualized theme, as it resembles a stylized naughty nurse outfit complete with a short white dress and stockings. Aside from the new costumes, Honey’s role in FV2 stays much the same, and she remains one of the franchise’s most recognizable characters.
### Other appearances and crossovers
Honey (Candy) is also featured in Fighters Megamix (1996) on the Sega Saturn, a crossover title that mixes characters from Fighting Vipers and Virtua Fighter. She appears on the Fighters Megamix roster (wearing her standard red outfit) as a selectable fighter representing the Fighting Vipers side. As mentioned, an art image of Honey was included in the Japanese Fighters Megamix gallery but was censored for the game’s Western release due to its provocative nature[7]. Beyond the Fighting Vipers series itself, Honey’s most notable cameo is in Sonic the Fighters – a 1996 arcade fighting game featuring characters from the Sonic the Hedgehog universe (also developed by Sega AM2). In that game, an anthropomorphic cat character named Honey the Cat appears as a hidden opponent. According to developer interviews, Honey the Cat was directly inspired by (and modeled after) the Fighting Vipers character: the development team inserted Honey’s character design into the Sonic game engine as a test, since Sonic the Fighters was built on the same engine as Fighting Vipers[19][20]. The feline Honey shares the original Honey’s visual style (including a similar red dress and twin-tail hairstyle) and even uses some of her signature moves (renamed with Sonic-themed twists). In the original arcade release of Sonic the Fighters, Honey the Cat was not normally playable – her data remained hidden in the code and was discovered by hackers years later[8]. However, when the game was re-released in 2012 for modern platforms, Sega officially added Honey the Cat as a fully playable character on the roster[21]. This crossover appearance gave the Honey character a second life in the Sonic franchise. (The Honey the Cat version also went on to appear in Sonic comic books, reinforcing the character’s cult popularity outside of Fighting Vipers lore.)
## Reception and legacy
Although Fighting Vipers was a relatively niche series, Honey/Candy emerged as its breakout character and has since gained a minor cult following. Critics and fans often regard Honey as an emblematic figure of the franchise’s flamboyant, 1990s style. For example, the gaming site GamingHell noted that Honey “symbolizes everything about Fighting Vipers – it is totally over-the-top” in terms of design and attitude[22]. Her exaggerated fashion-model persona and revealing costumes have drawn both notoriety and nostalgic affection. In retrospective commentary, Honey has been described as one of the most overtly sexualized female characters of her era in fighting games – effectively a pioneer of the “sexy fighter” archetype that later became more common[13]. This aspect has made the character memorable; Retro Gamer magazine pointed out that Honey “pushed the envelope” for character design in Sega’s mid-’90s roster, predating the likes of Dead or Alive’s fan-service elements (a sentiment echoed by The Fighters Generation reviewer)[13].
Honey’s unique look also resonated with the cosplay community. During the late 1990s, it was not uncommon to see fans cosplay as Candy/Honey at gaming conventions – her colorful outfit and wings made her a popular choice for costume play[23]. This fan interest, combined with her appearance in Sonic the Fighters, has kept the character in the public eye to some degree despite the Fighting Vipers series being dormant after 2001. Commentators have called Honey one of the more memorable and iconic characters from Sega’s 3D fighting game lineup of the 1990s[24][22]. While she never reappeared in new games beyond cameos, the character remains a curiosity in Sega’s history – often remembered for her stylish concept and the cross-franchise quirk of having a Sonic-universe doppelgänger. Overall, Honey (Candy) is regarded as a distinctive creation that embodied the playful, fashion-forward and over-the-top spirit of Fighting Vipers during its time[22].

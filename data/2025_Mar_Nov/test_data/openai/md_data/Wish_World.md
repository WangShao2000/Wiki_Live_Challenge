# Wish World
## Lead
"Wish World" is the seventh and penultimate episode of the fifteenth series of the British science fiction television series Doctor Who[1]. It was first broadcast in the UK on BBC One on 24 May 2025, and made available the same day on BBC iPlayer (in the UK) and via Disney+ internationally[2]. The episode was written by showrunner Russell T. Davies and directed by Alex Pillai[3]. It stars Ncuti Gatwa as the Fifteenth Doctor, alongside Varada Sethu as his companion Belinda Chandra[3]. "Wish World" serves as the first half of a two-part series finale (followed by "The Reality War"), and is notable for reintroducing the renegade Time Lord villain known as the Rani – a character from the classic Doctor Who era, now portrayed by Archie Panjabi in her revival-series debut[4][5].
## Plot
The Doctor (Ncuti Gatwa) and Belinda (Varada Sethu) return home to find themselves in an idyllic but uncanny alternate reality – the so-called "Wish World" – living as a married couple with an infant daughter named Poppy[6]. In truth, this false world has been imagined into being by the Doctor’s former ally Conrad Clark (Jonah Hauer-King) and made real by the Rani (Archie Panjabi), who harnesses the power of a wish-granting entity called Desiderium (“the God of Wishes”) as part of her scheme[7]. The Rani orchestrated this elaborate illusion so that the Doctor’s inherent Time Lord doubts would eventually crack open the fabric of reality, allowing her to locate Omega – an ancient, long-lost Time Lord – hidden in a realm beneath the universe[8]. As the Doctor begins to question the perfect life around him, the illusion starts to destabilize. He gradually regains his true memories and, aided by a timely message from his ally Rogue (Jonathan Groff) breaking through the charade, is able to shake off the brainwashing effect of the Wish World[9]. In the climax, the Rani deliberately shatters the fabricated world – unleashing giant skeletal creatures across London – and sends the Doctor hurtling off the edge of this unreal universe into a void[10]. The episode ends on a cliffhanger, with reality collapsing, directly setting up the events of the finale "The Reality War".
## Production
"Wish World" was written by executive producer Russell T Davies and directed by Alex Pillai[11]. It was produced as part of the two-part finale for the 2025 series (Series 15) of Doctor Who, paired with the subsequent episode "The Reality War"[12]. The episode features the return of the Rani, a renegade Time Lord scientist character who originally appeared in the 1980s Doctor Who serials (portrayed then by Kate O’Mara)[4]. In the revived series, the Rani is revealed to have been hiding in the guise of “Mrs Flood” (played by Anita Dobson) before fully re-emerging in "Wish World" – with Archie Panjabi debuting as the Rani’s new incarnation[4]. The story also alludes to the classic villain Omega as the ultimate antagonist, marking that character’s first on-screen involvement since the 1980s (albeit via brief voiceover cameos)[13].
The principal cast of the episode includes Ncuti Gatwa as the Fifteenth Doctor and Varada Sethu as Belinda Chandra, the Doctor’s companion[3]. In addition, "Wish World" brings back several recurring characters from the series: Millie Gibson appears as former companion Ruby Sunday, Bonnie Langford reprises her classic-era role as Melanie “Mel” Bush, Jemma Redgrave portrays UNIT leader Kate Stewart, and Ruth Madeley plays Shirley Bingham – all of whom are integrated into the story’s alternate-universe setting with altered roles[14][15]. Actor Jonathan Groff makes a brief return in this episode as Rogue, a time-traveling character introduced earlier in the 2020s series, who delivers a crucial message to the Doctor within the Wish World narrative[9]. The episode also includes a cameo by original 1960s series actress Carole Ann Ford, reprising her role as the Doctor’s granddaughter Susan Foreman in a surprise appearance that connects back to the Doctor’s early history[16][17].
## Broadcast
"Wish World" premiered in the UK on BBC One on Saturday 24 May 2025, airing in an early evening slot (6:50 pm)[1]. In line with the release strategy for the 2025 series, the episode was also made available to stream on BBC iPlayer on the morning of its broadcast, and via Disney+ in international markets on the same day[2]. The initial overnight viewership for the BBC One telecast was approximately 1.8 million viewers in the UK[18]. (This figure does not include viewers from streaming platforms; the episode’s early iPlayer availability meant many fans watched via catch-up services, and final consolidated ratings were higher after a week of additional viewings[19][20].) Notably, "Wish World" and its follow-up "The Reality War" were screened together as a double-bill in over 450 cinemas across the UK and Ireland on 31 May 2025, giving fans a chance to watch the two-part finale on the big screen[21].
## Reception
Critical reception. "Wish World" received a mixed critical response. Reviewers praised the episode’s ambitious concepts, imaginative visuals and the performances of the cast – for example, Varada Sethu’s portrayal of Belinda earned positive notice for convincingly depicting the character in an unsettling new context[22]. The Guardian’s recap described the episode as a “Doctor Who fairytale” scenario akin to WandaVision, with the Doctor trapped in a sugary dystopia that gradually cracks to reveal a darker reality[23][24]. However, several critics felt that the story attempted to juggle too many ideas and pieces of Doctor Who lore at once. Stefan Mohamed of Den of Geek noted the “excellent production values” but argued that there were “more ideas than comfortably fit into its runtime,” ultimately calling the penultimate episode “a mixed bag” overall[25]. William Tucker of But Why Tho? was more negative in his assessment, writing that "Wish World" is "difficult to settle into, and the lack of energy makes it uncomfortable" to watch, with a sluggish pace despite its intriguing premise[26]. Some commentary also criticized the climactic info-dump and reliance on obscure continuity – the reveal of Omega (a villain from the 1970s/80s era) as the endgame threat was seen by certain reviewers as underwhelming or too backward-looking after the build-up[13]. At the same time, the return of the Rani and other past characters was appreciated by long-time fans, and the episode’s closing moments were noted for effectively raising the stakes heading into the finale.

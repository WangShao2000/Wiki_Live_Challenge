# The Well (Doctor Who)
The Fifteenth Doctor faces an unseen threat in "The Well". The Well is an episode of the British science fiction television series Doctor Who. It is the third episode of the 2025 series (the programme’s fifteenth series since its 2005 revival, marketed as "Season 2" in some regions)[1][2]. The episode was co-written by showrunner Russell T Davies and Sharma Angel-Walfall, and directed by Amanda Brotchie[3][4]. It first aired on BBC One in the UK on 26 April 2025, and was also released on BBC iPlayer that morning (with international streaming on Disney+ in parallel)[5][6]. Ncuti Gatwa stars as the Fifteenth Doctor, alongside Varada Sethu as companion Belinda Chandra[7]. Guest actors include Rose Ayling-Ellis as Aliss Fenly – a character whose deafness plays a key role in the story – and Caoilfhionn Dunne as platoon leader Shaya Costallion[8][9]. "The Well" sees the Doctor and Belinda investigating a distant-future mining colony with a lone survivor, where they encounter a terrifying invisible entity previously seen in the 2008 episode “Midnight” from the David Tennant era[1][10].
## Plot
In the 5020th century, the Doctor and Belinda arrive on planet 6767, a desolate world poisoned by radiation. They join a unit of armed Lombardic troopers deploying to Colony Base 15, a mining outpost from which all contact was lost[11][12]. On the base, they find that nearly all of the 35 colonists have died violently – some shot with lasers, others with their bones broken[13]. Every mirror on the base has been shattered. The only survivor is Aliss Fenly, the colony’s cook, who is found frightened and injured. Aliss, who is deaf, communicates with the Doctor via sign language and a speech-to-text visor built into the team’s spacesuits[14][15]. She claims the crew went insane and that she had “no choice” but to kill her friend in self-defense[16]. The Doctor discovers a large, deep well at the mining control center and realizes from various clues (such as a dead X-tonic star and a surface once made of diamonds) that the planet is actually Midnight, a world he visited long ago[17][18]. Shaya confirms that 400,000 years in the past the planet was known as Midnight, infamous for an unseen hostile lifeform[18].
The troopers grow suspicious that something invisible is lurking behind Aliss, as anyone who steps directly behind her is instantly hurled to their death by an unseen force[19]. The Doctor deduces that an entity is hiding just out of sight behind Aliss and has been possessing and killing the colonists. The creature whispers to its host to induce paranoia, but Aliss’s inability to hear renders her relatively immune to its influence[20]. The entity remembers its last encounter with the Doctor on Midnight and even whispers the Doctor’s secret name to unsettle him[21]. To expose the creature, the Doctor pours liquid mercury on the floor, creating a reflective surface; upon seeing its own faint reflection, the creature flings Aliss aside, breaking its hold on her[22]. As the survivors attempt to evacuate, the entity generates a kinetic blast that knocks everyone down. Believing the alien has jumped into Belinda’s body, Shaya shoots Belinda to expel it, then deliberately allows the entity to transfer into herself[23]. Shaya flees and sacrifices herself by leaping down the deep mineshaft (the “well”) to trap the creature in the planet’s depths once again[24]. The Doctor and a recovering Belinda depart, but in the aftermath, the remaining soldiers witness a final ominous sign – the surviving trooper Mo now appears to have something unseen standing right behind her, suggesting the entity has survived Shaya’s effort to destroy it[25][26].
## Production
Davies conceived "The Well" as a spiritual sequel to “Midnight” (2008), reviving the same mysterious monster faced by the Tenth Doctor[10]. According to Doctor Who’s official behind-the-scenes notes, the script was not initially intended as a Midnight follow-up; however, during development the need for an unseen, whispering antagonist became apparent, and Davies proposed bringing back the unnamed Midnight entity as the story’s foe[27]. Co-writer Sharma Angel-Walfall—one of several new writers in the series—worked with Davies to craft the episode’s suspenseful “base-under-siege” narrative. Rose Ayling-Ellis, a deaf actress best known from EastEnders, was cast as Aliss Fenly. Her character was not originally written as deaf, but the script was revised to incorporate Ayling-Ellis’s deafness into the plot in a natural way[9]. The episode’s director, Amanda Brotchie, utilized claustrophobic framing and the conceit of an ever-present unseen threat to evoke horror, in an homage to the atmosphere of “Midnight”. Production of "The Well" featured expansive industrial set designs for the mining base, enabled by a larger budget under the show’s new co-production deal with Bad Wolf and Disney+ – in contrast to the single-bus set of the original “Midnight” episode[28]. Principal photography took place at Bad Wolf Studios in Wales, with additional visual effects used to depict the irradiated planet surface and the invisible creature’s impact (such as objects being thrown by an unseen force).
## Broadcast and reception
“The Well” premiered in the UK on BBC One on 26 April 2025 in a 6:50 pm time slot[5]. On the same day, it was made available to stream on BBC iPlayer ahead of the television broadcast, as part of a new release strategy for the series[29]. Internationally, the episode was distributed via Disney+ (day-and-date in the United States, among other markets)[6]. The initial overnight viewership in the UK was 1.9 million viewers, which marked a notable increase from the previous week’s episode (“Lux” at 1.58 million, the lowest Doctor Who overnight rating on record)[5]. Consolidated ratings over seven days (including time-shifted viewing and online streaming) rose to 3.24 million for “The Well”[30]. It was the 25th most-watched programme of the week on British television and the highest-rated BBC One show on the day of its broadcast[31]. Over a 28-day period, the episode’s total reach expanded further to around 3.6 million viewers[32][33], roughly doubling its audience after the initial airing.
Critical response to “The Well” was largely positive. Reviewers welcomed the episode’s return to horror-thriller territory and its connection to the fan-favorite “Midnight” episode. Den of Geek described “The Well” as “a meaty drama with real chills,” praising the sharp writing by Davies and Angel-Walfall and the strong performances of the cast[34]. In particular, Rose Ayling-Ellis’s portrayal of Aliss earned acclaim; The Guardian wrote that the episode “belonged to [Ayling-Ellis’s] performance,” highlighting how she conveyed both vulnerability and quiet menace in the role[35]. Caoilfhionn Dunne was also singled out for her impactful turn as Shaya, bringing unexpected depth to the hardened commander character[36][37]. Critics noted that the creature’s unseen nature and the tense base-under-siege setup successfully delivered suspense, even as the story paid homage to (and expanded upon) the premise of “Midnight”. Some commentators observed that the expansive setting and higher budget meant the sequel lacked a bit of the original story’s claustrophobic intensity[38][39]. Nevertheless, the episode was widely regarded as one of the standout entries of the season. Phenixx Gaming lauded “The Well” as “one of the best episodes of Doctor Who in years,” calling it a “genuinely fantastic” slice of sci-fi horror that invited only minor nitpicks[40][41]. Overall, “The Well” was seen as a success, with reviewers commending the show for delivering an atmospheric, scary adventure that balanced nostalgic callbacks with fresh character-driven stakes.

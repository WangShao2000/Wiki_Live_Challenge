# Fontaine (Genshin Impact)
Fontaine is a fictional region in the action role-playing game Genshin Impact, developed by the Chinese studio HoYoverse. It is one of the seven nations in the game's fantasy world of Teyvat, associated with the Hydro element and the ideal of Justice[1]. Fontaine is often referred to as the "Nation of Justice" and is ruled by the Hydro Archon, Focalors (also known in-game as Lady Furina), who presides over its elaborate judicial system[2]. The region is characterized by its abundant water bodies, advanced technology, and vibrant culture of art and innovation[2]. Fontaine was introduced into Genshin Impact on August 16, 2023, as the game's fifth major nation added via the Version 4.0 update "As Light Rain Falls Without Reason"[3]. This update notably added new gameplay features such as underwater exploration and combat, a first for Genshin Impact[4]. The aesthetics and world design of Fontaine draw inspiration from 19th-century European industrial era art and architecture, giving it a steampunk-flavored, French-inspired style[5]. The name "Fontaine" itself means "fountain" in French, reflecting the region’s water-centric theme[6].
## Setting and Inspiration
In the lore of Genshin Impact, Fontaine is located in the central part of Teyvat – west of the nation of Liyue and northwest of Sumeru[7]. True to its Hydro element, the landscape of Fontaine features sprawling rivers, lakes, and waterfalls, and its main city is built around and above water. Fontaine is frequently likened to a “city on the water,” with some design influence from real-world Venice in its canal-like environs[8]. As the Nation of Justice, Fontaine’s society places great emphasis on law, order, and public trials. The Hydro Archon Focalors (Furina) is revered as the God of Justice, and under her influence, courtroom trials in Fontaine are dramatized as grand public spectacles often held in an opera house setting[2]. This unique blend of legal proceedings and theatrical performance is a defining cultural aspect of Fontaine.
Fontaine is the most technologically advanced nation in Teyvat’s setting[9]. Its level of industry and innovation is often compared to the steam-powered era of the real 19th-century Industrial Revolution[9]. The nation’s architecture showcases industrial materials like metal and gears alongside elegant ornamentation, creating a Belle Époque-meets-steampunk aesthetic[5]. In the game’s lore, many modern inventions originate from Fontaine – for example, the Kamera, a photograph-taking device, and other mechanical gadgets were developed by Fontainian inventors[9]. The people of Fontaine are depicted as stylish and cultured; characters from Fontaine often wear fashions inspired by late 19th-century European attire and contribute to fields like art, technology, and journalism within the game’s world.
## Gameplay Features and Design
Fontaine’s introduction in Genshin Impact brought significant new gameplay mechanics and content. Most notably, Version 4.0 allowed players to dive underwater and explore submerged areas of the map[4]. Underwater exploration is seamless in Fontaine’s regions – the player’s characters do not require oxygen and cannot drown as they would in other areas; instead, a special aquatic stamina system is used for swimming and diving actions[4]. While diving, combat is also transformed. Rather than wielding their usual weapons, characters can absorb Xenochromatic abilities from aquatic creatures to interact with undersea enemies and objects[4]. For example, the Traveler (player character) can obtain a charged water projectile attack while underwater, used to solve puzzles or defeat foes. This made Fontaine the first region in Genshin Impact to feature true three-dimensional underwater gameplay, adding a new dimension to exploration.
The region is divided into multiple zones, including both surface and underwater environments. Upon its initial release, Fontaine opened with areas such as the Court of Fontaine (the capital city) and surrounding wilderness regions (e.g. Beryl and Belleau regions). Subsequent updates expanded the map: Version 4.1 in September 2023 unlocked the northern part of Fontaine, including an underwater prison complex known as the Fortress of Meropide[10], and Version 4.2 in November 2023 extended the map eastward to places like the Erinnyes Forest and the Morte Region[11]. These areas introduced new environmental puzzles (some involving Fontaine’s unique energy mechanics called Arkhe powers, Pneuma and Ousia) and new enemies to encounter.
Fontaine’s enemies and bosses reflect the region’s dual themes of water and technology. For instance, the Version 4.0 release added the Icewind Suite, a boss fight against a pair of clockwork meka automatons that dance in battle like performers, and the Emperor of Fire and Iron, a gigantic armored crab (nicknamed the "Crablord") that combines Pyro attacks with heavy mechanical defenses[12]. Version 4.1 introduced the Millennial Pearl Seahorse, an elegant marine creature that strikes with Electro-charged attacks underwater, and an Experimental Field Generator, a malfunctioning research device that manipulates gravity during its encounter[10]. In Version 4.2, as the storyline climaxed, players face the All-Devouring Narwhal, an enormous primordial sea beast risen from the depths, and the Hydro Tulpa, a formidable water entity that can enhance itself in combat[13]. These battles are designed to test the player’s adaptability to Fontaine’s new mechanics, whether it be underwater combat or puzzle-like boss gimmicks.
## Storyline
Chapter IV of Genshin Impact’s Archon Quest (the game’s main story questline) takes place in Fontaine and is centered on a prophecy of calamity hanging over the nation. According to Fontainian legend, one day a great flood of Primordial Sea waters will engulf the land, “drowning the people and leaving the Hydro Archon alone, weeping on her throne”[14][13]. This prophecy of an apocalyptic flood—and the sins of the nation that might bring it about—forms the backdrop of Fontaine’s story arc.
When the Traveler (the protagonist) arrives in Fontaine, they find themselves amid a spectacular public trial orchestrated by Lady Furina, the Hydro Archon[2]. The nation’s court system turns out to be a grand spectacle: the Archon uses courtroom drama to entertain the populace and reinforce her authority. Early in the story, a trial involving a famous magician from Fontaine (Lyney) serves as a key introduction to this judicial culture. The Traveler, with their companion Paimon, intervenes to help uncover the truth in the case, drawing the attention of Furina and Chief Justice Neuvillette. Neuvillette, known as the Iudex of Fontaine, is the highest judge of the nation’s Supreme Court and a close aide in administering justice. He maintains an air of stern fairness and carries a mysterious aura, later revealed to be tied to the ancient Dragon of Water in Fontaine’s lore.
As the narrative progresses through updates 4.1 and 4.2, the Traveler and their Fontaine allies investigate the dark underpinnings of the prophecy. They explore sites like the Fortress of Meropide – an underwater prison where dissenters and criminals are kept – and the Institute of Natural Philosophy, a research facility that had been studying the Primordial Sea. Clues from these places reveal that Fontaine’s scholars and leaders have long been seeking a way to prevent the prophesied flood[14]. For example, in the depths of the Fortress of Meropide, the party discovers experiments and plans (such as a massive Ark engine project) intended to help some of the population survive a possible deluge[10][14]. The plot also involves the Fatui (a scheming external faction) – notably, the Fatui Harbinger Childe (Tartaglia) arrives in Fontaine and is arrested after a mysterious incident, prompting the Traveler to search for him as part of uncovering the larger mystery[15].
In “Masquerade of the Guilty” (Version 4.2), the Fontaine storyline reaches its climax. The long-feared catastrophe nearly comes to pass as the Primordial Seawater begins surging across Fontaine, threatening to submerge the entire nation[13]. The Hydro Archon Furina, who until then had struggled with her waning divine power and public confidence, joins forces with the Traveler and Neuvillette to confront this crisis. The campaign leads to the Tower of Ipsissimus, an ancient tower containing the truth behind the Primordial Sea and the source of the floodwaters[16]. In the final battle, massive aquatic creatures like the All-Devouring Narwhal emerge for the player to defeat, symbolizing Fontaine’s sins coming to the surface[13]. Through these trials, it is revealed that Furina’s true role and the identity of the actual Hydro Archon are not what they initially seemed – a twist that ties into Fontaine’s past and the Hydro Dragon’s legacy. Ultimately, the disaster is averted and the prophecy is nullified, but not without sacrifice and revelations that change the governance of Fontaine. By the end of Fontaine’s chapter, the people of the region gain a renewed hope, and the Traveler prepares to depart for the next nation, having resolved the crisis of the Hydro nation.
## Characters
Fontaine is home to a large cast of characters, many of whom become playable in Genshin Impact. The region’s Archon, Furina (Focalors), is introduced as a theatrical and capricious figure who secretly grapples with the heavy burden of the prophecy. In gameplay, Furina became playable in Version 4.2 as a five-star Hydro-aligned character wielding a sword[11]. Another central character is Neuvillette, the Chief Justice of Fontaine, who upholds the nation’s laws. Stoic and just, Neuvillette was released in Version 4.1 as a five-star Hydro Catalyst user, notable for his unique chargeable attacks and his association with Fontaine’s ancient dragon※. Alongside Neuvillette in Version 4.1, Wriothesley was introduced – he is the warden of the Fortress of Meropide (holding the title “Duke” of the prison) and became playable as a five-star Cryo Catalyst user with a pugilistic fighting style[17].
The first Fontaine characters were added to the game with the 4.0 update. These include Lyney, Lynette, and Freminet, three siblings who hail from Fontaine’s performing arts community[18]. Lyney is a famed magician and illusionist (a 5★ Pyro archer) whose stage partner is his younger sister Lynette (a 4★ Anemo swordswoman)[18]. Freminet, their younger brother, is a diver and explorer often found salvaging underwater relics; he fights as a 4★ Cryo claymore wielder and specializes in physical damage[19]. These characters play roles in Fontaine’s storyline (with Lyney and Lynette involved in the initial court case) and reflect the region’s mix of artistry and technology – for instance, Lyney’s magic tricks and Freminet’s diving gear both rely on Fontainian gadgetry.
Subsequent updates expanded the roster of Fontaine characters. Notable figures include Navia, the warm-hearted leader of the Spina di Rosula detective agency (introduced as a Geo claymore user in Version 4.3), and Clorinde, a renowned Champion Duelist of Fontaine (an Electro swordswoman, released in a later update), both of whom assist the Traveler during the Fontaine quests. The region also features characters like Charlotte, a journalist from the Steambird newspaper, who appears in the story investigating the events in Fontaine and became playable as a 4★ Cryo catalyst wielder in Version 4.2[11]. In addition, Fontaine’s narrative includes several NPC figures such as Chief Justice Neuvillette’s aides, members of the Fontaine Research Institute, and even a Fatui Harbinger (Arlecchino) operating behind the scenes. Together, the characters of Fontaine showcase the nation’s identity – from its elegant nobility and artists to its industrious engineers and secretive conspirators.
## Development and Release
Fontaine was first officially teased in mid-2023 as the next major region in Genshin Impact following Sumeru. Hints about Fontaine had appeared in the game’s lore and through non-player characters much earlier – for example, NPCs in other nations mentioned Fontaine’s advanced technology and fashion. A short teaser at the end of Version 3.8’s special program (livestream) gave players a first glimpse of Fontaine’s environment and the Hydro Archon[20]. On August 16, 2023, Fontaine was launched as part of the Version 4.0 update, marking the fifth of Teyvat’s seven nations to become explorable in-game[3]. The update, titled "As Light Rain Falls Without Reason", opened the Court of Fontaine and its surrounding regions to players. HoYoverse released a trailer and promotional media showcasing Fontaine’s unique features such as underwater diving, the majestic architecture of the Court, and the new characters Lyney, Lynette, and Freminet who accompanied the release[21][19]. The Fontaine update also coincided with Genshin Impact’s third anniversary, and was celebrated with in-game events and rewards for the player community[22].
Following the initial release, Fontaine’s world content was expanded through the regular patch cycle of Genshin Impact. Version 4.1, titled "To the Stars Shining in the Depths," was released on September 27, 2023, and it extended Fontaine’s explorable map into the northern underwater areas[22]. This patch introduced new story quests that delved deeper into Fontaine’s history and the prophecy, as well as two high-profile Fontaine characters (Neuvillette and Wriothesley) as playable units[17]. Version 4.2 ("Masquerade of the Guilty") arrived on November 8, 2023, bringing the finale of Fontaine’s Archon Quest arc and adding two new sub-regions in the east of Fontaine (the Erinnyes Forest and Morte area)[11]. Furina (the Hydro Archon) and Charlotte were made playable in 4.2, aligning with their prominence in the concluding story[11]. These updates were accompanied by time-limited events (such as a Fontaine-themed film festival in Version 4.3[23]) and new gameplay activities highlighting Fontaine’s culture, like dance challenges, mechanical puzzles, and photography contests.
## Reception
Fontaine’s release was met with enthusiasm from players and commentators, rejuvenating interest in Genshin Impact with its fresh mechanics and atmosphere. In particular, the addition of underwater exploration and combat in Fontaine was widely praised as an innovative enhancement to the game[24]. Critics noted that the underwater areas were beautifully designed and offered a relaxing, immersive experience distinct from the rest of Teyvat. Polygon, in a preview of Version 4.0, highlighted that the swimming and diving mechanics “feel great” and add a new layer of verticality to exploration[24]. The region’s artistic direction and music – a blend of elegant classical motifs and whimsical steampunk flair – also received positive mention, with many players appreciating Fontaine’s Belle Époque-inspired visuals and the dynamic soundtrack that adapts when diving underwater.
On the other hand, some aspects of Fontaine’s content drew mixed reactions. The extensive focus on courtroom drama in the early Archon Quests was novel, though a few players felt the pace slowed during legal procedural scenes. However, the Fontaine storyline’s climax in Version 4.2 was widely regarded as dramatic and emotionally impactful, tying up the prophecy plot in a satisfying way. The character Furina (Hydro Archon) became especially popular for her personality and design, and her playable kit’s unique mechanics (tied to Fontaine’s Pneuma/Ousia energy system) were noted as complex but rewarding. Overall, Fontaine has been considered one of Genshin Impact’s most ambitious region updates, successfully combining gameplay innovation with rich world-building. Its success has set high expectations for subsequent regions (such as the pyro-themed nation of Natlan) in future updates.

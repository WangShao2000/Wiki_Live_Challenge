# The Interstellar Song Contest
“The Interstellar Song Contest” is the sixth episode of the fifteenth series of the British science-fiction television programme Doctor Who. It was first released on 17 May 2025, via BBC iPlayer in the morning and broadcast on BBC One that evening at 7:10 pm, scheduled to lead into coverage of the Eurovision Song Contest final[1][2]. The episode was written by Juno Dawson and directed by Ben A. Williams, and features Ncuti Gatwa as the Fifteenth Doctor alongside Varada Sethu as companion Belinda Chandra[3]. Set in the year 2925, the story sees the Doctor and Belinda arrive at a space station hosting the 803rd Interstellar Song Contest – a galactic take on Eurovision – which is soon hijacked by alien terrorists. The episode incorporates musical spectacle and dark sci-fi elements, culminating in major revelations. It features guest appearances by British TV personality Rylan Clark (playing himself as the contest’s host) and original 1960s Doctor Who star Carole Ann Ford (reprising her role as Susan Foreman)[4][5]. The climactic twist reveals that the mysterious character Mrs Flood (portrayed by Anita Dobson) is actually the renegade Time Lord the Rani, marking the character’s first onscreen return since the 1980s; Dobson’s Rani subsequently regenerates into a new incarnation played by Archie Panjabi in the episode’s closing moments[6][7].
## Plot
In 2925, the Doctor and Belinda land the TARDIS on Harmony Arena, a space station where the 803rd Interstellar Song Contest is underway. The galaxy-wide music competition – hosted by a cryogenically preserved Rylan Clark – promises an evening of camp entertainment[8]. As various alien performers take the stage, events take a deadly turn when a group of militant aliens from the planet Hellia infiltrate the arena. Led by a horned extremist known as Kid (played by Freddie Fox), these “Hellions” seize control of the show. Kid announces his plan to exact vengeance for the genocide of his people: he sabotages the arena’s force shields, exposing the audience to the vacuum of space, and uses a mind-link device to threaten the billions watching the broadcast across the galaxy[9][10].
As chaos erupts, the audience members are blown out into space. The Doctor manages to extend a protective “mavity” (gravity) field around the floating crowd, suspending them in life-saving stasis instead of instant death. With Belinda’s help – and assistance from contest crew and attendees hiding off-stage – the Doctor works to foil Kid’s mass-murder scheme. They restore life support and prevent the lethal signal from killing the remote viewers. Confronting Kid and a fellow Hellion conspirator, the Doctor’s temper flares upon learning the full scale of their intended atrocity (three trillion victims). Enraged, he briefly tortures Kid in retaliation before Belinda intervenes and reminds him of his principles, prompting the Doctor to show mercy[11][12]. Kid and his accomplice are handed over to authorities, and the Interstellar Song Contest resumes. One contestant, Cora (Miriam-Teak Lee), an undercover Hellion who survived the attack, delivers a defiant final song honoring her lost homeworld – a banned act of protest that nevertheless earns applause.
Throughout the turmoil, the Doctor is haunted by sudden psychic visions of his long-lost granddaughter Susan Foreman. At one critical moment, the spectral image of Susan appears inside the TARDIS, addressing the Doctor as “Grandfather” and urging him to come find her[5]. This mysterious hallucination inexplicably helps revive the Doctor after he is nearly asphyxiated in space. Unnerved and astonished, the Doctor realizes Susan (played by Carole Ann Ford) may somehow still be alive. In the aftermath, the Doctor and Belinda leave the arena, their quest to return Belinda to 2025 taking an ominous turn – especially after the shocking events that follow at the episode’s end.
In a mid-credits scene, the elderly observer Mrs Flood, who had been appearing throughout the Doctor’s recent travels, is revealed to be a disguised incarnation of the Rani – a renegade Time Lord and former adversary of the Doctor. Fatally injured during the Hellion attack, Mrs Flood undergoes a “bi-generation” regeneration, transforming into a new, younger Rani (portrayed by Archie Panjabi) before ominously declaring her return[6][13]. This revelation concludes the episode on a cliffhanger, directly setting up the series’ two-part finale to follow.
## Production
### Conception and Writing
Showrunner Russell T Davies conceived “The Interstellar Song Contest” as a playful genre mashup, pitching it to writer Juno Dawson with the brief “Eurovision meets Die Hard.” Dawson, a longtime Doctor Who fan and author, eagerly developed the idea[14]. The premise combined a musical extravaganza – inspired by the real Eurovision Song Contest – with a high-stakes disaster movie scenario in the vein of an action thriller. Dawson later explained that she expanded Davies’s “bare-bones concept” into a full script largely unchanged from her initial outline: “What would happen if an enemy faction planned an attack on the Eurovision Song Contest…in space?”[14] The episode’s narrative balances camp pageantry with darker themes (terrorism, genocide) in line with this pitch.
The production embraced the Eurovision parody wholeheartedly. A fictional song contest was devised as a far-future counterpart to the famed Earth competition, complete with over-the-top performances and a tongue-in-cheek tone. Multiple original alien songs were created for the episode, and real-world TV presenter Rylan Clark – himself a familiar face to Eurovision fans – was cast as the Interstellar Contest’s host, essentially playing a far-future version of himself. (In the story’s lore, Rylan has been cryogenically stored so he can continue hosting well into the 30th century, a comedic nod to his enthusiasm for Eurovision[15].) Writer Juno Dawson described the finished episode as “camp… but also action-packed,” calling it a “high-octane” romp that nonetheless carries an emotional core and social commentary[16].
### Casting and Characters
Ncuti Gatwa leads the cast as the Fifteenth Doctor, with Varada Sethu co-starring as new companion Belinda Chandra. The guest cast features several notable names and surprise appearances. British presenter Rylan Clark appears as himself, hosting the in-universe song contest[4]. Clark’s participation tied in with the Eurovision theme and provided a humorous crossover with real-world pop culture. Actor Freddie Fox guest-stars as the antagonist “Kid,” the charismatic Hellion terrorist leader. Stage actress Miriam-Teak Lee portrays Cora, while Kiruna Stamell and Iona Anderson play another contestant duo (Nina and Wynn). The episode also includes cameos by media personalities Graham Norton (as a holographic commentator) and Davina McCall, further enhancing the Eurovision atmosphere (these cameos were uncredited Easter eggs acknowledged by the showrunners in interviews).
A major talking point of “The Interstellar Song Contest” was the brief return of actress Carole Ann Ford as Susan Foreman – the Doctor’s granddaughter who was last seen in the series in the 1983 anniversary special The Five Doctors. Ford’s cameo, kept secret before broadcast, occurs as a vision of Susan inside the TARDIS, marking her first on-screen appearance in the show in over four decades[5]. The moment was designed to surprise long-time fans and tease an unresolved plot thread regarding the Doctor’s original companion. Ford, who was part of Doctor Who’s very first episode in 1963, filmed the cameo in secrecy; her one-scene return was widely noted as a historic nod to the show’s origins.
Another significant casting twist centered on the character Mrs Flood, played by veteran actress Anita Dobson. Throughout Series 15, Dobson had appeared as the enigmatic Mrs Flood in multiple episodes, sparking much fan speculation. In “The Interstellar Song Contest,” Mrs. Flood’s true identity is dramatically revealed to be the Rani – a classic Doctor Who villain (a Time Lord scientist) not seen since the 1980s. This reveal had been heavily foreshadowed, but the production kept it officially unconfirmed until broadcast. In the episode’s climax, Dobson’s Rani undergoes a regeneration (referred to in-story as “bi-generation”), allowing a new actress to take over the role[6]. Archie Panjabi, an Emmy-winning actress, was introduced as the new incarnation of the Rani in a surprise mid-credits scene[7][13]. Panjabi and Dobson appear briefly together during the transformation, symbolically “passing the baton.” This marked the first time the Rani had been portrayed on-screen by someone other than the late Kate O’Mara (who originated the role in 1985). Both actresses would continue as the Rani in the subsequent two-part series finale. The return of the Rani – a notable Doctor Who foe – after roughly thirty years was heavily promoted by the BBC once the spoiler had aired, and it signaled Russell T Davies’s intent to reintroduce elements of Doctor Who lore to new audiences.
### Filming and Effects
Production of the episode posed unique challenges, as it required blending large-scale musical set-pieces with action and VFX-heavy sequences. Ben A. Williams, the director, described “The Interstellar Song Contest” as one of the most technically complex episodes of the series to film[17][18]. Scenes of the concert crowd being blown into space, for example, combined practical wire-work (for suspended actors) with extensive CGI rendering of a zero-gravity catastrophe. The colorful performances by alien singers were filmed with elaborate costumes, prosthetics, and dance choreography, mimicking the flamboyance of a real Eurovision stage show albeit with extraterrestrial flair. Composer Murray Gold created several original songs and pastiche jingles for the contest sequences, in addition to the episode’s dramatic score. Notably, one satirical song titled “Dugga Doo” (attributed in the story to the planet Grimbald) was released online after the episode aired, as a playful homage to Eurovision novelty entries[19][20].
Filming took place at Bad Wolf Studios in Wales for the interiors of Harmony Arena, with a live audience of extras used to simulate the concert crowd. The production team employed volumetric stage screens and extensive post-production effects to depict the massive interplanetary audience and the moment the arena’s roof ruptures. For scenes where the Doctor and others float in space, Gatwa and the actors were shot against green-screen with harness rigs, and visual effects artists later added starfields, debris, and the zero-g physics. The mix of tones – from camp musical scenes to dark suspense – was also a focus in editing, as the team had to balance comedic timing with the buildup of the episode’s more serious plot elements.
## Broadcast and reception
“The Interstellar Song Contest” debuted on 17 May 2025, in what British media dubbed a “Super Saturday” of television programming[21]. Uniquely, the episode’s release was coordinated with the Eurovision Song Contest 2025 final airing that same evening. The BBC made the Doctor Who episode available to stream on BBC iPlayer at 8:00 a.m. that day, followed by a BBC One television broadcast in a 7:10 p.m. slot – immediately before the live Eurovision coverage at 8 p.m.[1][2]. (This scheduling was so tight that contingency plans were in place: if an overrunning sports event – the FA Cup Final – threatened to delay programming, Doctor Who’s linear broadcast would have been skipped, with viewers directed to iPlayer[22].) The stunt paid off in ratings: overnight figures indicated that The Interstellar Song Contest drew a consolidated viewership of around 6.8 million in the UK, benefiting from the Eurovision lead-in, and it trended on social media alongside the song competition.
Critical and fan reaction to the episode was sharply divided. Many viewers enjoyed the exuberant, self-parodic premise and praised the installment as a bold celebration of Eurovision’s camp spirit transposed into sci-fi[23]. The musical numbers, visual imagination, and Rylan Clark’s tongue-in-cheek cameo were frequently highlighted as entertaining high points. Additionally, long-time fans reacted with excitement to the surprise appearances of Susan and the Rani, which added a sense of Doctor Who history and continuity to the otherwise standalone adventure.
However, “The Interstellar Song Contest” also faced notable criticism for its jarring shifts in tone and what some reviewers perceived as an overstuffed narrative. Detractors argued that the episode struggled to decide what it wanted to be, oscillating between light-hearted pastiche and grim drama without fully satisfying either goal[24]. The central plot – in which a massacre is narrowly averted – was seen by some as oddly undermined by the need to keep the story upbeat. Den of Geek, for instance, commented that while the outrageous Eurovision-in-space concept “broadly works” on its own terms, the script “might have hung together more cohesively” had it stuck to that tone, instead of trying to also serve as a pivotal arc episode with weighty lore and character turns[25][26]. Ncuti Gatwa’s performance drew a mixed response in this regard: though praised in general, his portrayal of the Doctor’s uncharacteristic vengeful outburst towards Kid raised eyebrows, with some feeling the dark turn was not properly set up by the preceding episodes or was resolved too quickly to have lasting impact[12][27].
On the other hand, a segment of the fandom appreciated the ambitious blend of genres, acknowledging that Doctor Who has always balanced eclectic storytelling modes. They defended the episode as intentionally “big, silly, populist spectacle” that still delivered plot developments crucial to the series narrative[28][26]. The return of the Rani was met with enthusiasm, though tempered by debates over the execution of the reveal (some felt it was rushed as a short epilogue rather than built into the main story). Likewise, Susan’s cameo, while applauded for its sentimental value, left viewers speculating about its payoff in future episodes since it played no direct role in this story’s resolution.
Despite the mixed reactions, “The Interstellar Song Contest” succeeded in generating substantial buzz. Media outlets noted that the episode “divided the fanbase” but unquestionably got people talking about the show’s direction[29]. It stands as one of the most unusual and talked-about Doctor Who adventures of the 2020s – a mashup of glittery musical fandom and sci-fi peril that could only occur in Doctor Who’s expansive universe. Subsequent episodes and interviews clarified some of the lingering questions from this installment, and in retrospect, The Interstellar Song Contest is often remembered as a bold experiment that pushed the boundaries of the show’s format, for better or worse, in the lead-up to the series 15 finale.

# Joy Laking
Joy Snihur Wyatt Laking (born 1950) is a Canadian visual artist known for her realist landscape and still-life paintings, especially scenes of Nova Scotia[1]. Based in Portapique, Nova Scotia, she has been a professional painter since the early 1970s[2]. Laking works in watercolour, oil, and acrylic media[3], and her artwork has been exhibited nationally and internationally – including a solo show at the Art Gallery of Nova Scotia[3]. She has received honors such as the Halifax Women of Excellence Award (2009) and the Queen Elizabeth II Diamond Jubilee Medal (2012) for her contributions to the arts[4]. In addition to painting, Laking wrote and produced a play about domestic abuse and has published two books featuring her art and stories[5][6].
## Early life and education
Laking was born in 1950 in Owen Sound, Ontario[7]. She was raised in an artistic family – her mother was a professional artist and her father painted as a hobby[8]. Surrounded by art from a young age, Laking developed an early appreciation for drawing and painting. She earned a Bachelor of Arts degree with a major in Fine Art from the University of Guelph, graduating in 1972[9]. Shortly after graduation, she moved to rural Nova Scotia in 1972 to pursue a career as a full-time artist[10].
## Career
Upon moving to Nova Scotia, Laking settled along the Bay of Fundy and began painting the local landscapes. She held her first solo exhibition in 1982 at the Mount Saint Vincent University Art Gallery in Halifax[11]. In the following years, she had major solo shows at venues such as the Royal Botanical Gardens in Burlington, Ontario, and the Art Gallery of Nova Scotia[11]. Her 1989 exhibition at the Art Gallery of Nova Scotia, titled "Winter Works," was subsequently toured around the province for a year[12].
Laking’s paintings are firmly rooted in representational realism. She initially became known for her watercolour paintings and printmaking, then later expanded into working with acrylics and oil paints[13]. Many of her works depict the maritime scenery of Nova Scotia – from coastal villages and Bay of Fundy tides to rural farms – as well as detailed still lifes and portraits of local life. She frequently paints en plein air (on location) to capture natural light and atmosphere, only retreating to her studio during cold winter months[14]. Although Nova Scotia’s environment is her primary inspiration, Laking has also painted scenes around the world, including in Europe, Asia, Africa, and South America[15]. These travel experiences, such as painting trips to India and Ghana, help refresh her perspective on her familiar Nova Scotian subjects[15].
In addition to creating art, Laking has been active in promoting the visual arts community. She is an elected member of the Society of Canadian Artists and was a founding member of PLANS (Professional Living Artists of Nova Scotia), an organization supporting local artists[16]. Laking has also served on the boards of several cultural and community organizations – among them the Art Gallery of Nova Scotia’s Board of Governors, the Robert Pope Foundation, the Ship’s Company Theatre in Parrsboro, and a regional women’s shelter (Third Place Transition House) – reflecting her engagement beyond her own artwork[17]. In 2014, she was an instigator and participant in Capture 2014: Nova Scotia Realism, a juried exhibition at Dalhousie University Art Gallery that highlighted realist painters in the province[12].
## Other works
Beyond her painting career, Joy Laking has pursued other creative projects in writing and theatre. She wrote a theatrical play titled Invisible Prisons, which consists of a series of monologues about domestic abuse. The play was first produced in Truro, Nova Scotia in 2013 (with a repeat production in 2014)[16]. Invisible Prisons has since been performed in other regions; in 2020, for example, it was presented as a virtual production by the Zucker School of Medicine in New York as part of an educational program[5]. Laking has made the play’s script available to schools, community groups, and police departments to help raise awareness about domestic violence.
Laking is also the author of two books. Colours in Winter (2019) is a whimsical children’s picture book that she wrote and illustrated[18]. It follows a child’s imaginative wish to see bright colors in winter’s snow, reflecting Laking’s interest in sharing art with young audiences. In 2020 she published The Painted Province: Nova Scotia Through an Artist’s Eyes, a glove-compartment-sized art book featuring over 200 of her landscape paintings of Nova Scotia[19]. Each section of the book focuses on a different region of the province, accompanied by Laking’s personal anecdotes, “word-paintings,” and even GPS coordinates for the painting locations[20][21]. The Painted Province invites readers to explore Nova Scotia’s scenery through the eyes of an artist, and it has gone through multiple printings due to its popularity[22].
## Awards and honours
Over the course of her career, Laking’s contributions have earned her several notable awards. In 2009, she received the Halifax Women of Excellence Award, recognizing her achievement in arts and culture[23]. She was later awarded the Queen Elizabeth II Diamond Jubilee Medal in 2012, in honor of her significant public service and artistic contributions[23]. In 2016, Laking was selected for an artist residency in Port Bickerton, Nova Scotia[23], allowing her to work and mentor in that coastal community. Her lifelong dedication to the arts was further recognized with a Lifetime Achievement Award from the Cobequid Arts Council, which was presented at the Marigold Cultural Centre in 2022[2].

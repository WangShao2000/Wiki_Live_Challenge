# Here for It All
Here for It All is the sixteenth studio album by American singer-songwriter Mariah Carey, released on September 26, 2025 through her own imprint MARIAH in partnership with the independent media company Gamma[1][2]. It is Carey’s first studio album in seven years, following Caution (2018)[3], and marks her debut project under a multi-album deal with Gamma, a company launched by former Apple Music executive Larry Jackson[1]. The album was executive-produced by veteran record executive L.A. Reid and showcases collaborations with Anderson .Paak, Jamaican dancehall artist Shenseea, R&B singer Kehlani, and the gospel group the Clark Sisters[4][5]. Here for It All was preceded by the singles “Type Dangerous” and “Sugar Sweet,” and upon release it received generally favorable reviews from music critics[6]. The album debuted at number seven on the US Billboard 200 chart, becoming Carey’s nineteenth top ten album, and topped multiple Billboard genre charts including Top R&B Albums[7][8].
## Background and Development
After completing her contract with Epic Records following Caution, Carey sought a new creative direction and business model for her next album. In 2023, she signed a multi-album partnership with the newly formed entertainment company Gamma, founded by Larry Jackson[1]. This made Here for It All her first album released independently of major labels, via her own MARIAH imprint with distribution by Gamma[2]. The project reunited Carey with producer Antonio “L.A.” Reid, who had overseen two of her mid-2000s albums, and this reunion marked Reid’s return as executive producer[9].
Carey began recording the album over several years at studios in Los Angeles, New York, Atlanta, and other locations, often referring to it by the codename “MC16” before the title was revealed. She collaborated with a mix of new and familiar writers and producers. Notably, she did not work with her longtime past producers like Walter Afanasieff or Babyface, instead partnering with contemporary talent. Anderson .Paak co-wrote multiple tracks and appears as a featured artist, while Carey's musical director Daniel Moore II contributed significantly to songwriting and production[10]. Additional producers on the album include the production team The Stereotypes and producer Bernard “Harv” Harvey[4]. Carey described the album’s concept as celebrating every era of her sound “with a little twist,” blending her classic songwriting approach with modern influences[11]. In interviews, she noted that writing her 2020 memoir had made her more introspective and “vulnerable” in her music, an aspect she leaned into during the songwriting for Here for It All.
## Music and Composition
Here for It All is an R&B and pop album that incorporates a diverse mix of genres, including elements of gospel, hip hop, and disco[12]. Critics noted that Carey uses the album to pay homage to the Black pop and soul music of the 1970s and 1980s that influenced her. According to PopMatters, the record has a “charmingly retro” feel reminiscent of that era, yet “without feeling stale or derivative”[13][14]. The production features live instrumentation on several tracks: for example, “Play This Song,” featuring Anderson .Paak, channels a ’70s soul duet vibe with live drums and a mellow groove[15]. In contrast, the lead single “Type Dangerous” is a hip hop-soul track built around a sample of Eric B. & Rakim’s 1986 classic “Eric B. Is President,” giving it a 1980s street flair[16][17].
The album’s songs range from lush balladry to upbeat R&B jams. “Nothing Is Impossible” and the title track “Here for It All” are grand, sentimental ballads that spotlight Carey’s vocal range and convey themes of resilience and romance[18]. In these, Carey delivers emotive vocal runs and whistle notes that hark back to her signature style. On the other hand, tracks like “Confetti & Champagne” and “I Won’t Allow It” take a more playful, uptempo approach. These songs incorporate disco-funk rhythms and feature defiant, sometimes humorous lyrics addressed to an ex-lover[19][20]. Carey infuses tongue-in-cheek humor into her lyrics; on “Confetti & Champagne,” she toasts to herself in celebration of moving on, while on the disco-tinged “I Won’t Allow It” she pointedly rebukes a former partner’s flaws in a sassy send-off[20].
Gospel influences are present through the track “Jesus I Do,” a collaboration with the legendary Clark Sisters. That song merges gospel with a danceable beat, echoing the disco-gospel style of the late ’70s[21]. Carey’s lifelong love of gospel shines as she trades powerful vocals with the Clark Sisters in an uplifting praise anthem. The album also includes a cover of Paul McCartney & Wings’ 1973 ballad “My Love,” reinterpreted by Carey. Her version of “My Love” strips back some of the original’s soft rock sheen in favor of a soulful delivery, which one critic noted brings a raw emotional edge to the melody[22][23]. Throughout the album, reviewers observed that Carey’s vocals, while still showcasing her famed range, have a more hushed and unpolished texture at times. Rather than layering on heavy studio gloss, Carey embraces a “no-makeup” vocal sound – allowing a slight rasp and the natural wear of her voice to show, which adds poignancy to the songs’ emotional content[24][22]. This candid approach to her singing was highlighted as a bold artistic choice for an artist long celebrated for vocal perfectionism.
## Release and Promotion
On July 21, 2025, Mariah Carey officially announced Here for It All and its release date of September 26, 2025[1]. The announcement confirmed the album’s title and noted it would be her first release under the Gamma partnership, with L.A. Reid executive-producing[9]. In the lead-up to the album, Carey used social media to tease new music: she hinted at the project by posting a series of videos labeled “MC16,” and previewed lyrics from songs like “Sugar Sweet” before the track list was made public. On August 27, 2025, she revealed the album’s 11-song track listing via Instagram Stories, confirming the titles and guest features. Various limited edition physical formats (vinyl, CD, and cassette) were made available for pre-order, including retailer-exclusive editions with alternate cover art. A deluxe digital edition of the album, sold through Carey’s official store, included a bonus track: a solo version of “Sugar Sweet” without the guest artists[25][26].
Carey launched the album’s promotional campaign with high-profile live performances. The lead single “Type Dangerous” was released on June 6, 2025[27], and Carey gave the song its television debut at the 2025 BET Awards on June 9, 2025. During that award show (where she received the BET Ultimate Icon Award), she performed “Type Dangerous” live in a show-stopping set, which included a surprise appearance by rapper Rakim – paying homage to the song’s classic hip-hop sample[28][29]. Later that month, on June 15, 2025, Carey headlined the Capital Summertime Ball concert at London’s Wembley Stadium and included “Type Dangerous” in her setlist, introducing the new era to an international audience.
The album’s second single, the uptempo track “Sugar Sweet,” featuring guest vocals from Shenseea and Kehlani, was released on July 25, 2025[27]. “Sugar Sweet” is a breezy, summer-themed R&B song with a dancehall influence, produced by Carey and Harv[5][30]. To promote the single, Carey shared a lyric video that highlighted a playful candy-inspired visual theme, and the song garnered media attention for uniting three female artists on one track. “Sugar Sweet” achieved some chart success on release – it debuted at number 2 on Billboard’s R&B Digital Song Sales chart in early August 2025[31][5], signaling a warm reception from R&B audiences (Carey’s previous single “Type Dangerous” had debuted at No. 1 on the same chart in June)[32].
In the weeks leading up to the album, Carey engaged in further promotion. She performed a medley of her hits and new material at a 10th anniversary event for Apple Music in Culver City on June 30, 2025, where she discussed the forthcoming album and confirmed it was nearly complete. As the release date approached, Carey used social media to count down to Here for It All’s debut, sharing nostalgic clips of her past album eras to build anticipation. On September 26, 2025, coinciding with the album’s release, Carey issued “Play This Song” (featuring Anderson .Paak) as a focus track. A high-budget official music video for “Play This Song” premiered the same day, showcasing Carey and .Paak in a retro-themed visual that matched the song’s soulful style. A few days later, Carey appeared on The Tonight Show Starring Jimmy Fallon to further promote the album. She was interviewed on the show and, along with Anderson .Paak and The Roots, performed “Play This Song” live on the September 29, 2025 episode[33][34]. Critics noted that Carey’s Tonight Show performance demonstrated her vocal prowess and stage charisma, reinforcing the positive buzz around her return.
## Critical Reception
Here for It All received a generally positive reception from music critics. On review aggregator Metacritic, the album earned a score of 73/100, indicating “generally favorable reviews,” based on eight professional critiques[6]. Reviewers often highlighted Carey’s blend of nostalgia and contemporary R&B on this album, as well as her vocal approach. PopMatters praised Here for It All as “a winning return” for Carey, describing the album as “an affectionate and charming look at Black pop music of the ’70s and ’80s” that manages to feel retro without being dated[13]. The review emphasized that Carey’s “spectacular voice” remains “miraculously intact” and that she successfully adapts to the modern musical landscape as an independent artist[35]. NME similarly commended the record’s polished execution, noting that while Here for It All “doesn’t exactly shake things up,” it is “pretty [and] polished” and finds Carey “sitting comfortably on top of her sonic throne” as a veteran artist still in control of her craft[36].
Several critics pointed out that the album’s strength lies in its consistency and clever nods to different eras of Carey’s career. Pitchfork reviewer Rich Juzwiak gave Here for It All a 7.0/10 score, highlighting the project as “thoughtfully constructed and surprisingly candid about the present condition of her voice”, with an understated production that he characterized as a “no-makeup sound” for an artist devoted to glamour[24]. The Pitchfork review appreciated Carey’s willingness to embrace a more unvarnished vocal style and found the songs “consistently enjoyable” even if none were positioned as instant chart-topping hits[37][38]. Pitchfork did note that in the context of Carey’s hit-filled discography, the album feels like a more minor, intimate work, given the seven-year gap since her last album[37]. AllMusic (which rated the album 3.5 stars out of 5) echoed that sentiment, observing that Here for It All is “surprisingly concise” and recalls the focused brevity of Caution (2018), even as it touches on a broad array of styles from gospel to disco[39].
Some reviews were more mixed, arguing that the album plays it safe. Rolling Stone opined that the 11 tracks “mostly play it safe and stay in [Carey’s] pocket,” though the magazine also acknowledged “there’s no denying her voice is still intact” and powerful throughout[40]. Slant Magazine was somewhat critical, comparing the album unfavorably to Carey’s previous effort: Slant wrote that the songs on Here for It All “aren’t as fresh or tightly consistent as the ones on 2018’s Caution and suggested that in trying to cover “all” styles, the album “ends up falling short”* in cohesion[41]. Despite these reservations, Slant still noted moments where Carey’s personality and vocal skill shine, especially on the ballads. Across reviews, there was consensus that Carey’s songwriting and voice remain strong. The Independent (UK) gave the album 4 out of 5 stars, summing up the general outlook: Here for It All may not reinvent Carey’s wheel, but it delivers a “pretty, polished affair” that reaffirms Mariah Carey’s place atop pop/R&B royalty[36].
## Commercial Performance
In the United States, Here for It All debuted at number 7 on the Billboard 200 chart for the week of October 12, 2025[8]. This entry earned Mariah Carey her nineteenth top ten album on the Billboard 200 over the course of her career. It also continued her streak of all of her studio albums reaching the top ten in the U.S.[42]. In its first week, Here for It All earned 47,000 equivalent album units, according to data reported by Billboard[7]. Of that sum, a strong portion came from pure album sales, aided by Carey’s dedicated fanbase (“lambily”) purchasing physical editions and collector versions of the album. The debut at No. 7 tied Here for It All with Carey’s 2001 soundtrack album Glitter as one of her lowest-peaking studio albums on the main chart[43], yet it underscored her longevity: Carey became only the third female artist in history to achieve at least 19 top-ten albums in the U.S., alongside Barbra Streisand and Madonna.
Beyond the main chart, Here for It All performed impressively on several Billboard genre and component charts. It opened at No. 1 on the Top R&B Albums chart, giving Carey her fourth number-one on that tally since its inception in 2012[7]. The album also debuted at No. 1 on Billboard’s Top Independent Albums chart and at No. 1 on the Top Album Sales and Top Current Albums charts (which rank pure sales), reflecting the robust sales by an independently released project[2]. Additionally, Here for It All entered at No. 3 on the Vinyl Albums chart, thanks to strong demand for its various vinyl editions[8]. In Carey’s other key markets, the album saw moderate success. In the United Kingdom, Here for It All debuted at number 31 on the UK Albums Chart (and reached No. 1 on the UK R&B Albums sub-chart)[44]. It also charted within the top 40 in countries like Australia and Canada, though without matching the higher peaks of Carey’s ’90s releases.
Despite not producing a major pop hit single, Here for It All benefitted from Carey’s promotional efforts and the loyalty of her fanbase. The singles found success on R&B formats: “Type Dangerous” became a top-five hit on U.S. Adult R&B radio (peaking at No. 2 on the Adult R&B Airplay chart)[45], and “Sugar Sweet” also gained traction on R&B/Hip-Hop digital song charts[5]. By the end of 2025, Here for It All had sold approximately 85,000 album-equivalent units in the U.S. and earned praise for demonstrating an iconic artist’s ability to adapt to the changing music industry. The solid commercial showing of the album—combined with its positive critical reception—reinforced Mariah Carey’s status as a enduring force in pop and R&B, even as Here for It All ushered in a new independent chapter of her career.

# Champions of the Future
Champions of the Future (COTF) is an international kart racing series launched in 2020 by the RGMMC Group, a Swiss-based motorsport promoter. The competition is run under FIA Karting (CIK-FIA) rules and typically comprises multiple rounds each season at major European circuits. Initially covering the CIK OK-Junior and OK classes (and homologated 60 Mini karts), it has since expanded to include gearbox classes (KZ and KZ2) as well. COTF events mirror the FIA Karting calendar, often using the same tracks just weeks before official FIA rounds. The series functions as both a standalone championship and a developmental platform: it provides young drivers with high-level international experience and is often described as a “proving ground” aligned with professional standards[1]. Notably, several Drivers who have won or competed in COTF have gone on to higher levels of motorsport, including single-seater racing[2].
COTF’s inaugural season was held in 2020, with three rounds at Wackersdorf (Germany), Zuera (Spain) and Kristianstad (Sweden). According to RGMMC President James Geidel, the series was created to give teams “another chance to get into the CIK classes and try out OK” karts without the expense of larger FIA events. The first championship included OK-Junior (60cc), OK (125cc), KZ2 (125cc gearbox) and the new 60 Mini category. In subsequent years the calendar expanded, with later seasons featuring around five European rounds per year. For example, by 2025 events had been held in countries such as Spain, Portugal, Italy and Germany. RGMMC – the organizer of COTF – is the same company that runs the FIA Karting World and European Championships, and Champions of the Future is listed among its major international series. A 2025 report noted that COTF “aligns closely with the FIA Karting calendar, confirming its role as the perfect dress rehearsal for the sport’s major international events”.
## Competition format
COTF races use international karting classes. The OK-Junior and OK categories (single-speed, 125cc karts) have been featured in every season, with full FIA-style qualifying heats and finals. The series also introduced a Shifter (gearbox) segment: events including KZ and KZ2 classes were held as part of COTF (for example, a 2025 round at Franciacorta, Italy featured KZ/KZ2 categories). Each championship is run on a points system over the season. Events are professionally organized – with live timing, scrutineering, parc fermé, and other standards – to give drivers and teams the same environment as top-level FIA races.
Prizes and titles are awarded in each class for season standings. (For instance, in 2021 British driver Freddie Slater won the OK-Junior title[3].) COTF has become a prestigious series in the karting community, attracting factory-backed teams and talented junior drivers. As one karting commentator noted, in just a few years it has become “one of the most exciting and respected karting series in Europe”.
## Champions of the Future Academy Program
In 2023 RGMMC launched the Champions of the Future Academy Program to develop younger karting talent. This is an arrive-and-drive international series for mixed-gender categories: Mini 60 (ages 8–12), OK-National Junior (OK-N Junior, ages 11–14) and OK-National Senior (OK-N Senior, ages 14–17). All competitors use equalized equipment (identical chassis and engines) and cost-control measures – for example, limited entry fees and a random draw for gear – are applied to keep expenses low. Academy rounds are typically four-day events (practice on Thursday-Friday, qualifying and heats on Saturday-Sunday).
The Academy series is explicitly international in scope. Its calendar spans multiple continents; for example, the 2026 schedule includes events in Europe (Valencia ESP, Thessaloniki GRE, Viterbo ITA) as well as the Middle East (Muscat OMA, Al Ain UAE, Al Forsan UAE). The Academy Program often partners with initiatives like F1 Academy’s “Discover Your Drive” to support female karters – selecting young women to compete alongside others. By providing a well-structured, low-barrier entry to karting, the COTF Academy series aims to nurture the “next generation” of racers.
## Expansion: Champions of the Future America
In December 2025 RGMMC announced that COTF would expand to North America. In partnership with K1 Speed (a major U.S. kart and motorsport company), Champions of the Future America was unveiled as a new 10-round series for 2026[1]. This U.S. series will run from late January through September, primarily at K1’s outdoor tracks in California and Nevada, and will follow FIA Karting technical regulations[1][4]. The goal is to give American kart drivers “access to the same professional standards” as the European series[1], and to create pathways into international competition. (The announcement emphasized that America is the world’s largest karting market and that this initiative “bridges the gap between Europe and the United States”[5].)

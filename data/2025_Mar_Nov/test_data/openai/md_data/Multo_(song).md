# Multo (song)
"Multo" (lit. "Ghost") is a song by the Filipino alternative pop band Cup of Joe. It was released on September 14, 2024, by Viva Records as the lead single from the band’s debut album Silakbo (2025)[1]. The song was co-written by Cup of Joe’s vocalist Raphaell “Rapha” Ridao together with his older brother Immanuel Ridao during the COVID-19 lockdown in 2020[2]. Produced by Shadiel Chan (with co-production by Jovel Rivera), "Multo" is an emotional synth-driven pop-rock ballad about the lingering “ghost” of a past love and the pain of unresolved grief[3][4].
Upon its release, "Multo" gradually became a viral hit and a breakthrough for Cup of Joe, garnering widespread popularity on streaming and social media platforms. In the Philippines, it topped the national music charts for a record-setting run of 27 consecutive weeks in 2025[5][6]. The track also gained international traction: it entered the Billboard Global 200 chart in April 2025 – marking the first ever entry by a Filipino act – and eventually peaked at No. 80 on that global chart[7][8]. The song reached as high as No. 28 on Spotify’s daily Global Top 50, the highest position for any Filipino song to date[9]. By May 2025, "Multo" had accumulated over 60 million streams on Spotify[10], and it continued to trend internationally through countless TikTok and Instagram videos featuring its theme of love and heartbreak[11][12].
Background and release: Cup of Joe is a five-member pop-rock band from Baguio, Philippines. In mid-2020, during pandemic quarantine, lead singer Raphaell Ridao and his brother began writing "Multo" as a way to channel their feelings of loneliness and loss[13]. According to Ridao, they poured all their longing and sadness into the song, finding “a sense of relief” after doing so[14]. The song’s lyrics, in the Filipino language, describe how one is “haunted” by memories of a lost love – no matter where one goes or how much time passes – until those emotions are finally confronted[15][3]. The title "Multo", which means ghost, symbolizes the persistent emotional shadow left by a past relationship. Musically, the track marked a stylistic shift for Cup of Joe; it is a melancholic synth-pop ballad with pop-rock elements, contrasting with the upbeat pop sound of some of their earlier works[16]. The band members have noted that "Multo" was more experimental and ambitious in sound, featuring a poignant chorus and atmospheric hook that immediately resonated with listeners[17][18].
Cup of Joe announced "Multo" unexpectedly, giving fans only a few hours’ notice on the day of release. The single was surprise-revealed on the afternoon of September 13, 2024, and officially released later that night[19]. It was issued as the first single from Silakbo, the band’s debut studio album which would arrive in January 2025[6]. Silakbo is a concept album themed around the five stages of grief, and "Multo" appears as the seventh track, representing the “depression” stage of the album’s narrative arc[20]. Upon release, the song initially received modest attention, but its heartfelt theme and relatable lyrics quickly struck a chord with listeners. By late 2024, Cup of Joe’s prior singles like “Misteryoso” and “Tingin” had charted locally[21], and "Multo" built on this momentum. The song’s popularity snowballed in early 2025 through word of mouth and social media – especially TikTok, where users set personal videos and breakup stories to the "Multo" soundtrack, making the song a viral audio for themes of heartbreak and “ghosting”[11][22]. This organic online exposure helped propel "Multo" to the top of streaming charts in the Philippines and eventually onto international charts[23][24].
Music and lyrics: "Multo" is characterized by its lush synthesizer-driven sound and emotive pop-rock instrumentation. The production (handled by Shadiel Chan, with Jovel Rivera) blends gentle keyboards and ambient guitar work with a slow-building rhythm, creating a haunting, melancholic atmosphere. The band’s co-lead vocalists, Raphaell Ridao and Gian Bernardino, deliver an impassioned performance, conveying the song’s themes of regret and lingering heartache. Lyrically, "Multo" is written entirely in Filipino, filled with poetic imagery of darkness and haunting to depict the aftermath of a painful breakup[4][25]. Critics and the band members have noted that the song’s central metaphor – being followed by a “ghost” of one’s feelings – encapsulates the difficulty of moving on from lost love[15][4]. In one notable line, “Hindi na ba mamamayapa?” (translated as “Am I never going to be at peace?”), the narrator expresses frustration at the thought that they may never find emotional closure[25]. The chorus — “Pasindi na ng ilaw / Minumulto na ’ko ng damdamin ko” (“Turn on the lights / I am being haunted by my emotions”) — vividly conveys the feeling of being trapped in a darkness by unresolved feelings[25]. These candid, heartfelt lyrics resonated strongly with listeners who had undergone similar experiences. Billboard Philippines described "Multo" as an “emotional, synth-driven track” that delivers a poignant exploration of “the ghosts of our past” and how one is “constantly haunted by the memory of lost love”[17][3]. The song’s style and subject matter marked a new musical chapter for Cup of Joe, showcasing more mature songwriting. Rolling Stone later called "Multo" an “artistic awakening” for the band and evidence of their growth as “serious pop storytellers,” highlighting the song’s ability to linger in the listener’s mind after one full listen[26][18].
Music video: The official music video for "Multo" was released on March 6, 2025, about six months after the single. Directed by Kris Ulrich Cazin, the video features Filipino actors Miles Ocampo and Elijah Canlas portraying a couple torn apart by heartbreak[27][28]. In the cinematic narrative, Ocampo’s character is shown grappling with bittersweet memories of a past romance — the “ghosts” of her former love — which manifest in scenes throughout their old home. Canlas appears as the lost lover in flashbacks and as a phantom-like presence in the empty rooms, symbolizing how the memories continue to haunt her. The video’s somber, artful visuals (lit with chiaroscuro lighting to evoke an eerie, nostalgic mood) reinforce the song’s themes of love and loss. As the song builds to its chorus, Ocampo’s character is seen turning on lights in a dark house, directly mirroring the lyric “Pasindi na ng ilaw” (“light up the lights”) as she seeks relief from the multo of her emotions. Cup of Joe themselves make cameo appearances performing the song with subdued passion, intercut with the storyline. The music video’s release was highly anticipated by fans — the band had teased the project in late February 2025 and even held a special launch event for it in Quezon City, which sold out within an hour[29]. Upon release, the video was praised for its storytelling and the performances of Ocampo and Canlas, who are both noted young actors. Critics remarked that the visual treatment added depth to the song, effectively translating the “hauntingly beautiful” quality of "Multo" into a short film about love, grief, and acceptance[30][31]. The music video quickly garnered millions of views on YouTube, further fueling the song’s popularity. (By September 2025, the various uploads of "Multo" – including official and lyric videos – had accumulated nearly 20 million combined views on YouTube[32].)
Commercial performance: "Multo" became one of the most successful OPM (Original Pilipino Music) songs of the mid-2020s. In the Philippines, it achieved massive chart dominance. The single reached No. 1 on the inaugural Billboard Philippines Hot 100 in April 2025 and remained at the top spot for 27 consecutive weeks[6][5]. This streak set a record for the longest-running number-one song since the chart’s establishment, solidifying "Multo" as a fixture on Filipino airwaves and streaming playlists throughout 2025[5][33]. Even as numerous other hits emerged, "Multo" maintained its lead, outperforming both local releases and popular international tracks in the country over several months[5][34]. The song also topped Billboard’s Top Philippine Songs weekly chart for the same duration, underscoring its cross-platform popularity[5]. By year’s end, "Multo" was officially the most-streamed song of 2025 in the Philippines on Spotify[35][36]. The band Cup of Joe was likewise named Spotify’s top local artist of the year, largely propelled by the song’s performance[35].
Internationally, "Multo" marked a breakthrough for Filipino music on the global stage. Thanks to strong streaming numbers and virality, the track entered the Billboard Global 200 chart (which ranks the world’s most popular songs) in the issue dated April 22, 2025[7]. It debuted at No. 181 on that chart, making Cup of Joe the first Filipino act in history to appear on the Global 200[7][37]. In the same week, "Multo" also charted on the Billboard Global Excl. U.S. listing at No. 74[38] – the first time a Filipino song had reached the top 100 of that ranking. Following its debut, "Multo" continued to rise in global popularity; it eventually peaked at number 80 on the Billboard Global 200 in the subsequent weeks[8]. This achievement was hailed as a significant milestone for OPM, as no Filipino-language song had ever climbed so high on an international chart before. On Spotify’s worldwide charts, "Multo" performed impressively as well. In mid-2025, it climbed into the Top 30 of Spotify’s Global daily songs chart, reaching around the #28 position at peak[9]. The song also amassed a large international listenership on platforms like YouTube and Apple Music, aided by its inclusion in various global playlists and algorithmic recommendations. By late 2025, Cup of Joe reported having over 10 million monthly listeners on Spotify – the highest for any Filipino artist at the time – attributable in large part to the runaway success of "Multo"[39][40].
Accolades and impact: "Multo" earned Cup of Joe critical acclaim and several honors. In October 2025, the song won “Song of the Year” at the inaugural Filipino Music Awards, held at the Mall of Asia Arena[41]. This award recognized "Multo" for its impact and popularity, and the band also took home “Album of the Year” for Silakbo during the same event[42]. Earlier in 2025, "Multo" had been nominated in various local award programs and was consistently lauded by media for revitalizing OPM’s presence on the global scene. The song’s commercial and streaming feats led to Billboard Philippines honoring Cup of Joe with a special recognition plaque in October 2025, commemorating "Multo"’s record-breaking 27-week reign at No. 1 on the charts[43][44]. Beyond awards, "Multo" became a cultural phenomenon in the Philippines. It inspired countless covers, renditions, and social media trends – from acoustic covers by other OPM artists to dance challenge videos (despite being a ballad) interpreting the song’s emotional story. The track was also used in popular Filipino TV shows, web series, and short films as an anthem for heartbreak. Notably, fans of a prominent teen drama pairing (actor Will Ashley and actress Bianca de Vera) adopted "Multo" as an unofficial “theme song” for the couple, further spreading the song’s exposure online[10].
Industry observers credit "Multo" with helping usher in a new wave of global interest in Filipino music. The band has expressed gratitude that their very Filipino-specific song — sung entirely in Tagalog and rooted in local sentiment — resonated with a worldwide audience[12][45]. Cup of Joe’s members noted that the song’s success proved that OPM could “make waves internationally” and opened doors for more Filipino acts to be recognized abroad[45][46]. By the end of 2025, "Multo" had firmly secured its place as one of the most iconic OPM songs of the decade, with many Filipino listeners considering it an anthem of heartbreak and healing. Its enduring popularity was reflected in Spotify’s 2025 Wrapped report, where "Multo" ranked as the #1 Local Song of the Year in the Philippines[36]. The song’s emotional message, combined with its record-breaking achievements, have positioned "Multo" as a modern classic in Philippine pop music, often being referred to in media as a “ghostly hit” that transcended its humble beginnings to haunt charts and hearts worldwide[8][44].

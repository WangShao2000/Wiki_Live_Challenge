# Diego García Miravete
## Early life and playing career
Diego Leonardo García Miravete is a Mexican former gridiron football coach and player. He played college football at the National Autonomous University of Mexico (UNAM) in the 1960s, on the Pumas UNAM team. Although García Miravete aspired to play as a quarterback, the head coach Manuel Neri assigned him to another role on the team[1]. He was part of the UNAM program during a period when the university’s football team was reorganized into multiple squads (such as Cóndores, Guerreros Aztecas, and Águilas Reales) around 1970. García Miravete’s experience as a player in that era laid the groundwork for his future coaching career.
## Coaching career
### UNAM Cóndores era (1978–1993)
García Miravete joined the coaching staff of Cóndores UNAM, one of the university’s Liga Mayor teams, and became head coach in 1978. This marked the beginning of a dominant era for UNAM football: under García Miravete’s leadership, the Cóndores won nine national college championships between 1978 and 1991[2]. These titles came in the seasons 1978, 1979, 1980 (a three-peat), 1983, 1984, 1985, 1986, 1990, and 1991[2]. His 18-year tenure as head coach of the Cóndores produced one of the most successful records in Mexican college football history, tying legendary coaches Roberto “Tapatío” Méndez and Jacinto Licea for the most national championships (nine) by a head coach[3][4]. During this period, García Miravete also coached the Selección Pumas (an all-star UNAM selection) in the annual rivalry games against the Politécnico’s all-star team, winning seven of those “Clásico” encounters[5]. His coaching style emphasized continuous improvement; he famously instilled the motto “no puedo dejar de ganar” (“I cannot stop winning”) to motivate his team to pursue excellence every season[6].
By the early 1990s, García Miravete had become one of the most respected figures in Mexican college football. He was recognized with multiple Coach of the Year awards during his UNAM coaching stint, reflecting his consistent success[5]. After the 1991 championship, he continued coaching Cóndores until the mid-1990s, before the UNAM teams were eventually consolidated into a single Pumas team in 1998.
### Other coaching roles in Mexico and abroad (1994–2009)
After leaving the UNAM Cóndores program, García Miravete took on a variety of coaching positions both internationally and in other Mexican institutions. In 1994, he went to Italy to coach the Cardenales de Palermo, leading a European club team for two seasons[7]. He then returned to Mexico to become head coach of the Auténticos Tigres at the University of Nuevo León (UANL) from 1995 to 1999[7]. In 2000, García Miravete had a stint in Europe with the Dragones de Barcelona (Barcelona Dragons) in NFL Europe[7]. Upon returning, he joined the coaching staff of the Monterrey Institute of Technology and Higher Education (ITESM), where he served as head coach of the Borregos Salvajes Toluca (Tec de Monterrey Campus Toluca) from 2000 to 2005[8]. He briefly went back to Italy in 2006 to once again coach the Palermo football club[9].
From 2007 to 2009, García Miravete worked with the Linces of Universidad del Valle de México (UVM) as a coordinator of football development[10]. In 2009, he was officially introduced as the new head coach of the ITESM Campus Santa Fe collegiate team in Mexico City[11]. This appointment was part of ITESM’s expansion of its college football program, and it added another chapter to García Miravete’s coaching résumé, now spanning multiple universities. He coached the Santa Fe Borregos program in the late 2000s, helping to lay the foundations for that young team.
Throughout these roles, García Miravete remained a highly regarded mentor. He was frequently called upon to lead Mexican national select teams in international competitions. He served as head coach of Team Mexico in the Aztec Bowl (a postseason all-star game) four times, guiding squads of top college players in matches against international opponents[5]. He also coached Mexico’s junior national team in the NFL Global Junior Championship (an international youth tournament) on three occasions[5]. By 2009, his coaching career spanned 34 years and included over 300 Liga Mayor games (with 218 victories)[12], making him one of the most experienced coaches in Mexico.
## Later years and legacy
In 2018, after decades in coaching, García Miravete took on a new challenge by joining the Autonomous University of Aguascalientes (UAA) to develop its American football program[13]. At UAA, he was tasked with building a flagship college team (eventually nicknamed the “Sharks” of Aguascalientes) and starting a flag football (tochito bandera) initiative. University officials noted his arrival, emphasizing that he “has been considered the most successful coach in the history of American football in the country.”[14] With 20 years at the helm of UNAM’s team, 18 more years coaching other top teams in Mexico (including ITESM programs), and 2 years in Europe, his experience was seen as a major asset to the young UAA program[13]. Under his guidance, UAA formed its first Liga Mayor squad and a women’s flag football team, aiming to compete in national university championships.
Even in his 70s, García Miravete remained involved in nurturing new talent. He stayed in Aguascalientes to assist a youth team known as the Sharks de Aguascalientes, working with a former player to coach the next generation. In 2019, he led the Sharks (a FADEMAC juvenile team) in regional competitions[15]. Opposing coaches noted that despite his legendary status and wealth of experience, García Miravete was still on the field coaching and mentoring young players[16]. The Sharks program benefitted from his expertise as he helped establish a winning culture there.
García Miravete’s legacy in Mexican American football is significant. He is widely regarded as an icon of collegiate coaching, having tied the all-time record for national titles and influenced the game across multiple generations[2][17]. He has been honored with at least ten Coach of the Year recognitions in Mexico[5]. Additionally, two training fields at UNAM are named after him (and Manuel Neri), reflecting his stature in the university’s sports history[18]. After a coaching journey spanning over four decades, Diego García Miravete is celebrated as one of the architects of modern college football in Mexico, known for his strategic acumen, his emphasis on discipline and excellence, and a coaching record that remains unmatched by most of his peers[14][19].

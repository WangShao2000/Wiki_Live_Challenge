# 1982 Los Angeles Rams–Green Bay Packers game
The 1982 Los Angeles Rams–Green Bay Packers game was a National Football League regular-season game played on September 12, 1982, at Milwaukee County Stadium. It was the season opener of the strike-shortened 1982 NFL season for both the Los Angeles Rams and the Green Bay Packers. In the game, the Packers overcame a 23–0 halftime deficit to defeat the Rams 35–23[1][2]. This dramatic comeback – scoring 35 unanswered points in the second half – is the largest comeback victory in Packers franchise history (tied with a 2013 game)[3][4]. The game is remembered as a highlight of Packers head coach Bart Starr’s tenure, helping propel Green Bay to its first playoff appearance since 1972[5][6].
## Background
Going into the 1982 season, the Green Bay Packers had not reached the postseason in a decade (their last playoff berth was in 1972) and frustration was growing among fans and team management[5]. Bart Starr, the Hall of Fame former quarterback, was in his eighth year as Green Bay’s head coach and had yet to lead the Packers to the playoffs[5]. The team had shown promise with an 8–8 record in 1981 (just missing a Wild Card spot), which earned Starr a contract extension and raised expectations for 1982[7]. The Packers’ offense was built around a big-play passing attack featuring quarterback Lynn Dickey throwing to star wide receivers James Lofton and John Jefferson, along with Pro Bowl tight end Paul Coffman[8]. However, there were concerns about the running game—running back Eddie Lee Ivery had struggled with knee injuries—and the defense’s consistency, putting added pressure on the offense to perform[8]. Starr’s future with the team was widely believed to hinge on breaking the playoff drought in 1982[9].
The Los Angeles Rams, meanwhile, were a team in transition. After being perennial contenders throughout the 1970s (including a Super Bowl appearance in the 1979 season), the Rams had fallen to a 6–10 record in 1981, their first losing season since 1972[10]. In response, the Rams sought to revitalize their roster by acquiring veteran talent. Notably, they traded for former NFL MVP quarterback Bert Jones, who had starred for the Baltimore Colts before injuries derailed his career[11]. Los Angeles also picked up tight end Mike Barber from the Houston Oilers to bolster the receiving corps[12]. Under head coach Ray Malavasi, the Rams aimed to rebound in 1982 using these new additions. The Week 1 matchup against Green Bay was played at the Packers’ Milwaukee County Stadium (their second home field at the time), with 53,694 fans in attendance[13]. Anticipation was high for both teams at this opening game, which kicked off shortly before an NFL players’ strike would put the season on hold two weeks later.
## Game summary
### First half
The Rams dominated the first half, capitalizing on numerous Green Bay mistakes. The Packers’ offense was plagued by turnovers from the outset – Green Bay fumbled on each of its first three possessions, all in their own territory, setting up Los Angeles with favorable field position[14]. In total, the Packers committed five turnovers (four by quarterback Lynn Dickey on interceptions and fumbles) before halftime[15][16]. The Rams took full advantage of these miscues to build a 23–0 lead by the break[16].
Los Angeles opened the scoring in the first quarter after a Green Bay fumble, with running back Wendell Tyler capping a short drive on a 4-yard touchdown run[17][16]. Soon after, following another turnover, Rams kicker Mike Lansford added a 32-yard field goal to make it 10–0[14][16]. In the second quarter, Los Angeles extended the lead when Bert Jones led a 51-yard drive, ending in an 8-yard (approximately 10-yard, according to some sources) touchdown pass to tight end Mike Barber[17][18]. Lansford kicked the extra point and went on to add two more field goals before halftime – from 29 yards and 28 yards – after additional Green Bay turnovers stalled the Rams’ drives[19]. As the Packers headed to the locker room at halftime down 23–0, the home crowd showered them with boos in frustration at the team’s flat performance[20].
### Second half
During the halftime intermission, Bart Starr opted not to bench Lynn Dickey despite the quarterback’s first-half struggles (three interceptions and a lost fumble)[21]. Starr later explained that he had faith Dickey could rebound, and that a veteran quarterback can “all of a sudden find it” even after a poor start[22]. That decision proved crucial, as the momentum swung dramatically when the second half began.
On the opening drive of the third quarter, the Green Bay defense forced a Los Angeles punt – a sign of the reversal to come[23]. Dickey and the Packers offense then came out rejuvenated. Green Bay’s first scoring drive covered 55 yards and ended with Dickey throwing a 4-yard touchdown pass to tight end Paul Coffman, putting the Packers on the board at 23–7 after kicker Jan Stenerud’s extra point[24][25]. The Packers’ defense stepped up its play, shutting down the Rams’ attack; Los Angeles did not manage a single first down in the entire third quarter (and only four first downs in the second half)[26][27]. Gaining confidence, Green Bay struck again before the quarter ended: running back Eddie Lee Ivery powered into the end zone on a 3-yard touchdown run, cutting the deficit to 23–14 after the point-after kick[28][29].
Early in the fourth quarter, the Packers continued their comeback. Wide receiver John Jefferson hauled in a deep pass from Dickey for a 50-yard gain, reaching the Rams’ red zone[30][31]. (Jefferson injured his hamstring on the play and would miss the remainder of the game, but not before making six catches for 116 yards in a huge effort[31][32].) A few plays later, Dickey found James Lofton in the end zone for a 15-yard touchdown strike, drawing Green Bay to within two points at 23–21[30][33]. With the County Stadium crowd now roaring, the Packers quickly seized an opportunity to take the lead. On the ensuing kickoff, Jan Stenerud executed a squib kick that Rams return man Robert Alexander mishandled; Green Bay reserve linebacker Guy Prather hit Alexander and recovered the fumble at the Los Angeles 10-yard line[34][35]. On the very next play, Dickey capitalized on the turnover, firing a 10-yard touchdown pass to Paul Coffman – Coffman’s second TD catch of the game – to give the Packers their first lead, 28–23, with just over nine minutes remaining[35]. In a span of roughly 20 minutes of game time, Green Bay had scored 28 unanswered points.
The stunned Rams tried to respond, and quarterback Bert Jones put together a drive into Green Bay territory mid-fourth quarter. Los Angeles reached the Packers’ 31-yard line after Jones completed three passes[36]. However, the Green Bay defense stiffened: two short runs gained only one yard, and on third down Jones was sacked for an eight-yard loss, pushing the Rams out of field goal range[36]. Los Angeles had to punt, and time was beginning to dwindle. With about 6:00 left in the game, the Rams got one more chance after their defense forced a Green Bay punt. But any hopes of a Rams comeback were snuffed out by another big play from the Packers’ defense – linebacker George Cumby intercepted a Bert Jones pass at the Green Bay 30-yard line, stopping the drive cold[36].
Green Bay then put the game out of reach. Eddie Lee Ivery delivered an insurance touchdown when he broke free on a draw play up the middle for a 27-yard rushing score late in the fourth quarter[37]. Stenerud’s extra point made it 35–23 in favor of the Packers. The Rams’ final possessions resulted in turnovers as well. With 2:58 remaining, Los Angeles advanced to the Green Bay 32, only for Packers linebacker John Anderson to intercept Jones for the team’s third pick of the quarter, effectively sealing the outcome[37]. The clock ran out with the Green Bay Packers completing a stunning comeback victory, 35–23, after trailing 23–0 at halftime.
### Statistical summary
The statistics from the game reflected the tale of two halves. Green Bay outgained Los Angeles in total yardage 377–271 and led in first downs 22 to 12[38]. The Packers’ offensive surge overcame their early mistakes – despite turning the ball over six times in the game (to the Rams’ three turnovers), Green Bay still prevailed[38]. Quarterback Lynn Dickey finished 17 of 27 passing for 237 yards with 3 touchdown passes and 3 interceptions[39]. After his shaky start, Dickey posted a nearly flawless second half, distributing the ball to his playmakers. Eddie Lee Ivery led all rushers with 109 yards on 17 carries and scored two touchdowns on the ground[39]. In the air attack, John Jefferson had 6 receptions for 116 yards to top the receiving stats (most of his production came before his fourth-quarter injury)[32]. Tight end Paul Coffman caught 4 passes for 66 yards, including two touchdown receptions[32]. James Lofton added 59 yards receiving on 4 catches and scored one touchdown[32]. For the Rams, Bert Jones went 17 of 31 for 202 yards, with 1 touchdown and 2 late interceptions[40]. Los Angeles’ top rusher was Wendell Tyler with 57 yards and a TD, and their leading receiver was Willie Miller with 78 yards on three catches[40]. The Packers’ defense sacked Jones four times (while Green Bay’s offensive line allowed two sacks on Dickey)[38].
## Aftermath and legacy
The come-from-behind win over Los Angeles proved to be a turning point that set the tone for the Packers’ season. “That’s the greatest comeback I’ve ever witnessed,” an elated Bart Starr said afterward, praising his team’s resilience[41]. Indeed, the victory gave Green Bay a 1–0 start and a surge of confidence. The following week, the Packers defeated the New York Giants on Monday Night Football to improve to 2–0[42]. However, the season was soon interrupted by a players’ strike that halted play for two months. When the NFL resumed action with a reduced schedule, Green Bay continued to play well. The Packers finished the 1982 regular season with a 5–3–1 record, placing third in the NFC under the unusual one-time playoff seeding format (divisions were suspended that year)[6]. They qualified for the expanded postseason tournament, marking Green Bay’s first playoff berth in ten years[6]. In the first round of the 1982–83 playoffs, the Packers defeated the St. Louis Cardinals (41–16) to advance[43][6]. Their season ultimately ended with a loss to the Dallas Cowboys in the second round of the NFC playoffs[6]. Still, 1982 was the only playoff season of Bart Starr’s nine-year coaching tenure in Green Bay, and the comeback against the Rams became one of the signature moments of his coaching career[44].
For the Los Angeles Rams, the collapse in Milwaukee was an ill omen in a disappointing season. The Rams never recovered momentum after the Week 1 loss. Hampered by a lack of success on the field and the abbreviated schedule, Los Angeles finished the season with a 2–7 record, near the bottom of the NFC[45]. Head coach Ray Malavasi was fired following the 1982 campaign, and the team hired John Robinson as his replacement in 1983. Quarterback Bert Jones’ comeback attempt was short-lived as 1982 turned out to be his final NFL season; he retired the following year due to chronic shoulder injuries.
The 23-point rally by Green Bay on September 12, 1982, remains one of the largest comebacks in NFL history and is still the biggest comeback victory in Green Bay Packers franchise history (tied with a 2013 game in which the Packers similarly overcame a 23-point deficit)[3][4]. It is often cited as a classic example of a “tale of two halves” in pro football. Decades later, Packers fans and historians remember the game as a remarkable demonstration of perseverance; the team’s ability to score 35 unanswered points in one half under pressure has secured this contest’s place among the most memorable games in Green Bay’s storied history[3].

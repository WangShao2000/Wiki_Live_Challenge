# Love Me Forever (Pinkshift album)
Love Me Forever is the debut studio album by American rock band Pinkshift. It was released on October 21, 2022, through Hopeless Records[1]. The album, produced by veteran punk engineer Will Yip, showcases Pinkshift’s blend of pop-punk, punk rock and post-hardcore influences with elements of grunge[2]. Its 12 tracks channel raw emotion and social frustration, supported by a high-energy sound and Ashrita Kumar’s powerful vocals. Love Me Forever was met with positive reviews from music critics, who praised its intense energy and heartfelt songwriting. It was highlighted in several publications’ year-end lists, with Alternative Press calling it “an impeccable debut”[3], and Kerrang! ranking it among the 50 best albums of 2022[4].
## Background and Recording
Pinkshift formed in 2018 in Baltimore, Maryland, and built an early following through a string of singles and tours with bands like Pup and Mannequin Pussy[5]. After releasing the Saccharine EP in 2021, the trio began working on their first full-length album[6]. Singer Ashrita Kumar, guitarist Paul Vallejo, and drummer Myron Houngbedji put aside their original plans (such as attending medical and engineering school) to focus on the band[7]. In 2021–2022, Pinkshift signed with Hopeless Records, a label known for pop-punk acts[8]. They recorded Love Me Forever in early 2022 with producer Will Yip, known for his work with bands like Turnstile and The Wonder Years[1]. According to the band, the album sessions captured a “full force and fury,” as they aimed to deliver passionate, unapologetic punk songs with a message[1][9].
## Release and Promotion
On June 1, 2022, Pinkshift debuted the album’s first single, “Nothing (In My Head)”, coinciding with the announcement of their signing to Hopeless Records[8]. This track introduced the band’s raucous pop-punk style to a wider audience. The album itself was officially announced on July 14, 2022, alongside the release of the second single “I’m Not Crying, You’re Crying.” That song — which opens the album — was accompanied by a lyric video and described by Kumar as a burst of “raw and unfiltered emotion” kicking off the record with “panicked denial”[10][11]. In late August 2022, Pinkshift released “Get Out” as the third preview track from Love Me Forever, a fast, thrashing punk anthem. During live shows that summer, the band dedicated “Get Out” to protesting the U.S. Supreme Court’s curtailing of rights, as the song is, in Kumar’s words, “a blatant attack against the white supremacist and patriarchal colonizers of our bodies, homes, and ways of life”[12]. Each single was accompanied by a music video or visualizer, generating buzz in the punk/alternative music scene.
Love Me Forever was released in full on October 21, 2022[1]. To promote the album, Pinkshift embarked on a North American tour in the fall of 2022. The tour kicked off with an album release show in their hometown of Baltimore on October 20, 2022, and continued through early December with dates across the U.S. and Canada[13]. The band’s energetic live performances and the album’s growing critical acclaim helped solidify Pinkshift as a notable new act in punk rock by the end of 2022.
## Music and Lyrics
Musically, Love Me Forever is rooted in pop-punk but frequently pushes into heavier and darker territory. Reviewers noted the album’s “mix of punk, post-hardcore, grunge, and pop” influences, which coalesce into what one critic called “a stunning burst of influences and experiences” fueled by anger[14]. Fast, aggressive rhythms and whirlwind guitar riffs drive many songs, while Kumar’s vocals range from melodic hooks to outright screams[15]. Kerrang! wrote that Pinkshift combines “sugar-rush melody and electric energy” with moments that verge on hardcore aggression, showcasing a versatility beyond typical pop-punk conventions[16][17]. The production by Will Yip was praised for its clarity and punch, giving the band a sharp, cathartic sound without diluting its raw edge[18].
Lyrically, the album balances deeply personal themes with broader social commentary. Several tracks explore mental health, heartbreak, and trauma from a first-person perspective. For example, the song “Trust Fall” addresses the emotional fallout of an abusive relationship, laying bare feelings of insecurity and loss of self[19]. On “I’m Not Crying, You’re Crying,” the narrator frantically denies their pain when asked “are you okay?”, an opening that immediately sets a tone of turmoil and vulnerability[20]. “Cherry (We’re All Gonna Die)”, despite its upbeat tempo, carries an ironic nihilism about impending doom[21]. The track “The Kids Aren’t Alright” bluntly voices generational anxiety about the future (at one point observing that “the world is ending all the time”)[22], reflecting young people’s despair amid constant crises.
Other songs turn their focus outward to critique social injustices. “Burn the Witch” reimagines historical witch trials to comment on modern groupthink and misogyny, condemning how society can demonize women[23]. In its chorus, Kumar pointedly challenges listeners with the line, “You’ve got two feet, now won’t you stand for something?”[24]. “Get Out” is an overtly political track; Kumar has described it as an anthem demanding space and autonomy, born from pure “unapologetic rage” against oppressive forces[25]. The title track, “Love Me Forever,” along with the closing acoustic-inflected song “Dreamer,” bring a more introspective angle. Kumar explained that the album’s title encapsulates a plea for unconditional self-acceptance – to “love me forever” even in moments of anger and sadness, as one must learn to love oneself fully in order to persevere[26]. This theme of finding empowerment in one’s own vulnerability ties the album together, even as it traverses from fury to hope.
Despite the often heavy subject matter, Love Me Forever interjects breathing room and dynamics. Mid-album, “In a Breath” stands out as a gentle piano-driven ballad that provides a moment of quiet reflection[27]. Its inclusion highlights the band’s willingness to strip back the noise and reveal a fragile side before launching back into the high-octane finale. Overall, the album’s songwriting toggles between defiance and despair, but ultimately delivers a message of resilience. As one reviewer observed, Pinkshift “walk [the] fraying edge between frenzied confessions and righteous fury” throughout Love Me Forever, yet emerge “unbowed, unbroken, and powerful” by the record’s end[28].
## Critical Reception
Love Me Forever received generally positive reviews from critics, especially within the punk and alternative rock press. Nick Ruskell of Kerrang! awarded the album a 4 out of 5 rating, praising it as “a bold first proper kick from a band already brimming with promise.” He wrote that the record is accomplished and “wise beyond [the band’s] years,” delivering an infectious one-two punch with its opening tracks and maintaining that momentum throughout[29][30]. Kerrang! highlighted Pinkshift’s ability to incorporate hardcore influences – noting, for instance, that songs like “Cherry (We’re All Gonna Die)” hit with a Turnstile-like intensity – while still crafting big hooks and energetic punk anthems[17].
In Flood Magazine, Mischa Pearlman hailed Love Me Forever as a standout debut. He described the album as “a stunning burst of influences and experiences, all of which coalesce in a swirling swathe of anger and injustice”, yet underscored by genuine vulnerability[14]. Pearlman noted the significance of Pinkshift’s perspective as people of color in a scene often dominated by white male voices, calling the album “phenomenal” and “utterly indispensable” for how it channels personal and political rage into cathartic music[14]. Under the Radar magazine’s Caleb Campbell also lauded the record, giving it a score of 7.5 out of 10. Campbell wrote that Pinkshift “conjure the cathartic magic of punk” on a collection of songs that explore “the nihilism of a generation growing up with an uncertain future, the wounds of trauma and abuse, and the alienation of living unapologetically in a hostile world.” Despite these heavy themes, he concluded that the band emerges “unbroken, and powerful,” delivering a “stellar punk debut” that positions Pinkshift among the next generation of the genre[28][31].
Several publications cited Love Me Forever as one of the notable rock albums of 2022. Alternative Press included the album in its list of “the 55 best albums of 2022,” praising its “honest lyrics, fast punk beats, and shredding guitars” and calling Love Me Forever “an impeccable debut” for the band[3]. Kerrang! ranked it at No. 19 on their year-end list of the top 50 albums of 2022[4], a strong endorsement given the magazine’s international profile in rock music. The album also appeared on year-end lists from several niche music sites and critics; for example, The Alternative and Chorus.fm both placed Love Me Forever in their top albums of 2022[32]. Critics commonly noted that beyond the hype of Pinkshift’s early viral success, the album fulfilled and solidified the band’s potential. Consequence (which featured Pinkshift as an “Artist to Watch” in October 2022) commended Love Me Forever as “pop punk for the end of the world,” highlighting the way the record’s urgent riffs and sincere fury captured the mood of a turbulent era[33].
In summary, Love Me Forever established Pinkshift as a fresh and formidable voice in modern punk. Reviewers praised the album’s balance of visceral punk aggression and emotional authenticity, as well as Pinkshift’s efforts to push for inclusivity in their genre. From its blistering guitar-driven tracks to its moments of melodic release, the album was received as a confident debut that fulfilled the band’s promise and left many excited for what Pinkshift would do next[34].

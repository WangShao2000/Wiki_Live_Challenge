# 2010 NFC Championship Game
The 2010 NFC Championship Game was the National Football Conference (NFC) title game for the 2010 NFL season, played on January 23, 2011, at Soldier Field in Chicago. It pitted the Green Bay Packers against the Chicago Bears, two long-time divisional rivals, with a trip to Super Bowl XLV on the line. The visiting Packers won the game 21–14, earning the NFC title and a berth in the Super Bowl[1]. Green Bay jumped out to an early lead and eventually held off a late Bears rally led by a third-string quarterback after Chicago’s starter Jay Cutler exited with an injury. Packers quarterback Aaron Rodgers scored an early rushing touchdown and later made a crucial tackle to prevent a defensive score, while defensive tackle B.J. Raji returned an interception for a touchdown in the fourth quarter[1][2]. Chicago’s offense struggled for much of the game; when Cutler left due to a knee injury, backup quarterbacks attempted to bring the Bears back. Sam Shields, a rookie Green Bay cornerback, intercepted two passes – including one in the final minute – to seal the Packers’ victory[2]. As a result, Green Bay advanced to Super Bowl XLV, where they defeated the Pittsburgh Steelers 31–25 to become NFL champions that season[3].
## Background
During the 2010 regular season, the Chicago Bears won the NFC North division with an 11–5 record, earning the #2 seed in the NFC playoffs (and a first-round bye). The Green Bay Packers finished 10–6 and grabbed the last wild-card spot as the #6 seed[4]. Notably, the two teams had split their regular-season series: Chicago won the first meeting 20–17 at Soldier Field in September, and Green Bay won 10–3 in the final week of the season to clinch its playoff berth[5]. This set the stage for a rare high-stakes matchup between the bitter rivals.
Both teams entered the NFC Championship on the heels of playoff victories. The rested Bears had defeated the Seattle Seahawks 35–24 at home in the Divisional Round the week prior[6]. The Packers, meanwhile, had embarked on a challenging road playoff run: they upset the third-seeded Philadelphia Eagles 21–16 in the wild-card round, then dominated the top-seeded Atlanta Falcons 48–21 in the Divisional Round[7][8]. That made Green Bay the second #6 seed ever to reach a conference championship, a run comparable to the 2005 Pittsburgh Steelers’ path to a title[9].
The matchup was laden with historical significance and fan interest. The Packers and Bears are the NFL’s oldest rivals – this game was the 182nd meeting between the two franchises, which first faced each other in 1921[10]. Yet postseason meetings between them were almost nonexistent: this was only their second-ever playoff encounter, and the first since a wartime playoff game on December 14, 1941, when Chicago beat Green Bay 33–14 to advance to the NFL title game[11][12]. With a Super Bowl trip at stake, it was the first time the storied rivals had met in an NFC Championship. Anticipation was high throughout the week leading up to the game, fueled by the long history and intense rivalry between the teams[13].
## Game summary
First half: The Packers grabbed the momentum quickly in front of the Chicago home crowd. On the game’s opening drive, Aaron Rodgers led Green Bay on an 84-yard march downfield[14]. He completed several medium-range passes – including gains to Jordy Nelson and Greg Jennings – and capped the drive by keeping the ball himself on a bootleg for a 1-yard touchdown run[15][16]. That score put Green Bay up 7–0 just four minutes into the game. Early in the second quarter, the Packers struck again. Starting with advantageous field position after a Bears punt, Rodgers moved the offense 44 yards with the help of a screen pass to Brandon Jackson and a 12-yard run by rookie James Starks[17]. Starks then finished the drive with a 4-yard touchdown run, extending the Packers’ lead to 14–0 with 11:13 remaining in the half[18][19]. Meanwhile, Chicago’s offense sputtered in the first half. Bears quarterback Jay Cutler struggled to find a rhythm, completing only 6 of 14 passes for 80 yards and throwing an interception before halftime[20]. The Bears’ best early scoring chance came late in the second quarter, when Cutler drove the team to the Green Bay 30-yard line, but a holding penalty stalled the drive and Chicago elected to punt rather than attempt a long field goal[21]. In the final minute of the half, the Bears nearly swung the momentum: linebacker Lance Briggs intercepted a deflected pass from Rodgers, giving Chicago possession in Green Bay territory[22]. However, on the very next play Cutler threw a deep ball that was intercepted in the end zone by Packers defensive back Sam Shields, preserving the 14–0 Packers lead at halftime[22][23].
Second half: Chicago’s hopes dimmed early in the third quarter when Jay Cutler was unable to continue due to a knee injury. After guiding one series at the start of the half, Cutler was sidelined with what was later revealed to be a sprained MCL in his left knee[24][25]. Veteran reserve Todd Collins stepped in at quarterback but went 0-for-4 passing and was ineffective, prompting the Bears to replace him with third-string quarterback Caleb Hanie later in the third quarter[24][26]. Despite the quarterback turmoil, Chicago’s defense kept the game within reach. Midway through the third quarter, Green Bay threatened to extend its lead when Rodgers drove the Packers inside the Bears’ 10-yard line. But on a third-down play, Chicago linebacker Brian Urlacher intercepted Rodgers’ pass at the 6-yard line and took off on a potential return for a touchdown[27]. Rodgers himself managed to stop Urlacher with a diving tackle near midfield, preventing a pick-six and keeping the score 14–0[28]. The third quarter ended with no change in the score, as both defenses held firm.
In the fourth quarter, the game tightened dramatically. Caleb Hanie provided a spark for the Bears’ offense after entering the game. Early in the final quarter, Hanie led Chicago on an 67-yard drive, and running back Chester Taylor punched in a 1-yard touchdown run to put the Bears on the scoreboard, narrowing the deficit to 14–7[2]. The home crowd’s optimism was short-lived, however, as Green Bay answered with a huge defensive play. On the Bears’ next possession, Hanie threw a short pass that was intercepted by 337-pound defensive tackle B.J. Raji, who dropped into coverage. Raji rumbled 18 yards into the end zone for a stunning interception return touchdown, putting the Packers up 21–7 with 6:04 left in the game[2]. Yet Chicago refused to fold – on the very next drive, Hanie engineered another quick response. He lofted a 35-yard touchdown pass to wide receiver Earl Bennett, drawing the Bears back within one score at 21–14 with just under five minutes remaining in the game[29]. With the crowd reinvigorated and the clock winding down, the Bears got one last opportunity to tie the game. In the final minute, Chicago took possession and Hanie moved the offense near midfield. But the comeback attempt ended when Sam Shields intercepted Hanie’s deep pass – Shields’ second pick of the day – around the Green Bay 30-yard line with about 30 seconds left[2]. Shields’ clutch interception effectively clinched the victory, as the Packers ran out the clock and celebrated a hard-fought 21–14 triumph on Chicago’s turf.
## Aftermath
By winning the NFC Championship, the Green Bay Packers earned the franchise’s fifth Super Bowl appearance and went on to face the AFC champion Pittsburgh Steelers in Super Bowl XLV. Two weeks after the NFC title game, the Packers defeated the Steelers 31–25 in Arlington, Texas, capturing their fourth Super Bowl championship (and 13th NFL championship overall) with Aaron Rodgers being named Super Bowl MVP[3]. The road success of the 2010 Packers – who won three consecutive playoff games away from home as a #6 seed – drew comparisons to the 2005 Steelers, the only other sixth-seeded team to win a Super Bowl up to that time[9].
For the Chicago Bears, the loss marked a bitter end to an otherwise successful season. It was the Bears’ first NFC Championship Game appearance since the 2006 season, and coming up short against their archrival at Soldier Field was a devastating outcome for the team and its fans[30]. Quarterback Jay Cutler’s premature exit became a major talking point in the aftermath. Cutler was diagnosed with a sprained MCL in his knee, an injury severe enough that team doctors pulled him from the game for his own safety[25]. Nonetheless, some fans and even other NFL players took to social media to question Cutler’s toughness for not finishing the game[31][25]. The Bears’ organization vigorously defended their quarterback – pointing out that he had played through the injury until he was physically unable – and confirmed that the decision to bench Cutler was made by the medical staff and coaches, not by Cutler himself[25][32]. Despite the controversy, it was clear that the Bears’ offense had struggled even before the injury, and the team was unable to overcome Green Bay’s stout defense and early lead.
The 2010 NFC Championship Game is remembered for its high stakes and the rarity of a Bears–Packers postseason showdown. It remains the only NFC Championship meeting between the two rivals to date, and the Packers’ victory in this game propelled them to a Super Bowl title, adding another chapter to the storied Green Bay–Chicago rivalry[10][11].

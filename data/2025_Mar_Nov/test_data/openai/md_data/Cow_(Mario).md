# Cow (Mario)
Cow (Mario), known in Japanese as Ushi (ウシ) and often referred to as Moo Moos, is a species of cattle in the Mario video game franchise. These cow characters resemble real-world dairy cows and typically appear as placid farm animals roaming on all fours. They were first introduced as background fauna in the 1996 game Mario Kart 64 and have recurred as obstacles on farm-themed race courses (such as Moo Moo Farm and Moo Moo Meadows) in the Mario Kart series[1][2]. In 2025, a cow was included as a playable racer for the first time in Mario Kart World, leading to unexpected popularity in the fan community and widespread media attention[3]. The character’s design – which includes a brass nose ring – also prompted controversy, as the animal rights group PETA publicly urged Nintendo to remove the ring due to its real-world cruelty connotations[4][5].
## Appearances in the Mario series
In the Mario Kart games, cows (or “Moo Moos”) are most commonly associated with rural track environments. In Mario Kart 64 (1996) they appear in the background of the Moo Moo Farm course, marking their first appearance in the franchise[1]. Later titles gave the cows a more active role: for example, Mario Kart Wii (2008) introduced the course Moo Moo Meadows, where several cows wander onto the track as moving obstacles. Players must steer clear of these cows, as colliding with one will cause a racer to crash or spin out[2]. Cows reappear in Mario Kart 8 (2014) and Mario Kart 8 Deluxe, which not only brought back Moo Moo Meadows (featuring cows of varying colors) but also incorporated the cow motif into trackside details – such as advertisements for “Moo Moo Meadows Milk” and sponsor logos displaying cartoon cow images[6]. Beyond the core racing titles, the Mario Kart Tour mobile game includes cow-themed customization (like a Moo Moo Mii Racing Suit) as a nod to these creatures[7], and the mixed-reality Mario Kart Live: Home Circuit (2020) features Moo Moos as obstacles on its Windmill Meadows track[8].
Outside of Mario Kart, cows have made brief cameo appearances in various Mario spin-offs. In the party game Mario Party 2 (1999), for instance, a herd of cattle is visible in the background of the Western Land board’s scenery[9]. The pinball-themed Mario Pinball Land (2004) includes a hidden bonus event in which hitting a Monty Mole may cause a Moo Moo to pop out of a hole; if Mario strikes the cow before it disappears, he earns extra points[10]. Cows also feature in Mario & Sonic at the London 2012 Olympic Games, appearing as part of the obstacles on the Moo Moo Meadows equestrian course in a dream event[11]. These recurring cameos underscore that the Moo Moos are a recognizable element of the Mario universe’s rural settings, even when they are not central to gameplay[12].
## Playable character in Mario Kart World
Mario Kart World, released for the Nintendo Switch 2 in 2025, marked the first time a cow was made a playable character in the Mario franchise[1]. In this game, one of the selectable racers on the roster is simply called Cow – an anthropomorphic version of the familiar Moo Moo that drives a vehicle. The Cow character retains the look of a black-and-white dairy cow (complete with a neck bell and nose ring) but is able to ride motorbikes and karts alongside traditional Mario characters[13]. According to the game’s art director, Masaaki Ishikawa, the idea to promote the cow from a background obstacle to a full racer originated from an informal sketch made during development[14]. The development team was designing a ranch-themed course and brainstorming new racers when “one of the designers came up with that silly sketch of Cow cruising along,” Ishikawa recalled, and it unexpectedly fit well with the game’s lighthearted style[14]. Encouraged by this concept, the team quickly prototyped the Cow as a racer and found she did not feel out of place. This creative decision also led them to consider other non-traditional characters: the final game’s roster additionally introduced a Cheep Cheep (a fish enemy) and a Pokey (a cactus creature) as playable racers, both of which, like the Cow, had previously only been obstacles or background creatures in Mario games[15]. The Cow racer in Mario Kart World is categorized as a heavy-weight character (similar in performance to characters like Donkey Kong or Wario), reflecting her large size[16].
## Reception and controversy
The inclusion of Cow as a playable racer in Mario Kart World was met with an enthusiastic response from players. Upon the game’s launch, many fans immediately gravitated toward the humorous idea of racing as a cow. Reports noted that online multiplayer lobbies were quickly dominated by players choosing Cow, to the point that entire races of human competitors playing as the cow were commonly observed[17][18]. Social media platforms saw a surge of memes and gameplay clips celebrating the bovine driver – for example, viral videos showed the Cow character dancing or lip-syncing to songs like “Mooo!” by Doja Cat and “La Vaca” by Mala Fe, garnering millions of views[3]. The New York Times described Cow as Mario Kart World’s “breakout star,” noting that the once-minor background animal had unexpectedly become a centerpiece of the game’s popularity[3]. Nintendo’s producers admitted they had underestimated how popular the cow would become; Mario Kart World producer Kosuke Yabuki remarked that the company was surprised by the character’s fanbase but was pleased by the positive reception[18].
While most of the fan reaction was lighthearted, the Cow character did spur some debates and an external controversy. Within the community, players jokingly speculated about the fact that the Cow’s in-game animations show her eating a hamburger – some fans half-seriously dubbed her a “cannibal cow” for seemingly enjoying beef, whereas others argued it might be a meat-free veggie burger, reflecting tongue-in-cheek lore for the character[19]. Nintendo representatives declined to comment on these humorous theories[18]. A more serious criticism came from People for the Ethical Treatment of Animals (PETA), which took issue with the Cow’s design. In August 2025, PETA sent an open letter to Nintendo urging that the brass ring be removed from the cow’s nose[4]. The organization argued that nose rings are instruments of animal control and cruelty in real-life farming, and that depicting a family-friendly character with such a device “glosses over real-world violence and cruelty to animals”[5]. PETA asked Nintendo to let the character “race freely – without any painful reminders” of exploitation[5]. The plea – which even punned that Nintendo should “leave the rings to Sonic” (a reference to Sega’s ring-collecting Sonic games) – drew media attention, though Nintendo did not publicly respond. As of the end of 2025, the Cow remains in Mario Kart World with her nose ring intact, continuing to charm players as an offbeat addition to the Mario cast[20][21].

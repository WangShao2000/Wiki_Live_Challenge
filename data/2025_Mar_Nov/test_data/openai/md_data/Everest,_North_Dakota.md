# Everest, North Dakota
Everest is an unincorporated community in Everest Township, Cass County, North Dakota, United States. It is located about two miles south of the city of Casselton. The community was founded in 1881 as a station on the Great Northern Railway, and a townsite was platted that year. Everest grew rapidly in the late 19th century but was largely abandoned after the railroad line through the town was removed in the 1890s. It is now essentially a ghost town. Everest is also notable as the birthplace of North Dakota governor and U.S. senator William Langer (1886–1959).
## History
In 1881, the Great Northern Railway (GNRR) built a station at the site that became Everest. That fall, a townsite was platted by local entrepreneurs William H. Kindred and George H. Jacobs [2]. A post office opened in May 1882 [1], and by 1890 the settlement had about 175 residents [1]. During its peak years, the town featured a grain elevator and several businesses, including a hotel (which housed other enterprises), a few taverns, and a drug store [3].
Everest’s fortunes reversed in the mid-1890s when the Great Northern revised its route. The company abandoned the Everest station in 1895 and removed the railroad tracks in 1896 [1]. Deprived of its rail connection, the town quickly declined. The post office, which had briefly closed in 1904, was permanently shut down on November 30, 1908 (with mail service thereafter handled through Casselton) [4]. With the loss of both railroad and post office, most residents and businesses departed, and Everest became a ghost town by the early 20th century.
## Notable person
William Langer (1886–1959) – Born in Everest, Langer served as Governor of North Dakota (1933–1934, 1937–1939) and later as a U.S. Senator [5].

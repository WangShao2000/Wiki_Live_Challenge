# Vorlesungen über die Entwicklung der Mathematik im 19. Jahrhundert
Vorlesungen über die Entwicklung der Mathematik im 19. Jahrhundert (“Lectures on the development of mathematics in the 19th century”) is a two-volume work by the German mathematician Felix Klein (1849–1925). It was published posthumously by Springer in Berlin in 1926 (Vol. I) and 1927 (Vol. II)[1][2]. The lectures were based on notes Klein had prepared late in his life. After Klein’s death in 1925, his colleagues edited and completed the work: Richard Courant and Otto Neugebauer prepared Volume I for publication, and Richard Courant and Stephan Cohn-Vossen prepared Volume II[1][2]. Both volumes appeared in Springer’s Grundlehren der Mathematischen Wissenschaften series (volumes 24–25) and together provide an overview of mathematical developments during the 19th century.
The contents of Volume I cover a range of major 19th-century mathematical themes. For example, it includes chapters on Carl Friedrich Gauss; on French mathematics and the École Polytechnique in the early 1800s; on the founding of the Journal für die reine und angewandte Mathematik (Crelle’s Journal) and the rise of pure mathematics in Germany; on advances in algebraic geometry beyond the work of Möbius, Plücker, and Steiner; on mechanics and mathematical physics in Germany and England up to about 1880; on the development of the general function theory of a complex variable (from Riemann and Weierstrass); and on algebraic structures and group theory in connection with automorphic functions[3][4]. Volume II is largely devoted to invariant theory: its title (roughly, “The fundamental concepts of invariant theory and their penetration into mathematical physics”) reflects Klein’s emphasis on the role of invariant theory in 19th-century mathematics and its applications[1]. In these lectures, Klein traced how ideas from algebra and geometry were applied across different fields (such as linking group theory and function theory) and how mathematical thought evolved over the century.
In its publication history, Vorlesungen über die Entwicklung der Mathematik im 19. Jahrhundert was reprinted and translated several times. An English translation, titled Development of Mathematics in the 19th Century, was published in 1979[5] by Math Science Press (Brookline, Massachusetts) as volume IX of the “Lie Groups: History, Frontiers and Applications” series[5][6]. Earlier, portions had been reprinted in English by Chelsea Publishing Co. (New York) in 1950 (and again in 1957)[7][5]. In German, the work was reissued in later editions: for example, Springer issued a reprint in 1979 (Hardcover, Grundlehren, ISBN 9783540092353)[8], and the Wissenschaftliche Buchgesellschaft published a paperback edition in 1986[9]. These editions helped keep Klein’s lectures available to historians and mathematicians interested in the evolution of 19th-century mathematics.
Today Vorlesungen über die Entwicklung der Mathematik im 19. Jahrhundert is regarded as a detailed contemporary account of 19th-century mathematics from the perspective of a leading mathematician-historian. It spans both pure and applied topics and reflects Klein’s view of the coherence of mathematical developments. As such, it remains a frequently cited source in the history of mathematics (for example, David E. Rowe and others note this work in surveys of mathematical historiography[5][3]).

# Midnight Sun (Zara Larsson album)
Midnight Sun is the fifth studio album by Swedish singer and songwriter Zara Larsson. It was released on 26 September 2025 through Larsson’s own label Sommer House (under exclusive license to Epic Records) and contains ten tracks[1]. The album was preceded by the singles “Pretty Ugly”, “Midnight Sun”, and “Crush”, which marked Larsson’s return with an upbeat dance-pop sound[1][2]. Midnight Sun debuted at number one in Sweden and also charted within the top 20 in several other European countries, including the Netherlands and Belgium[3]. The album received widespread critical acclaim, earning a score of 89/100 on Metacritic indicating “universal acclaim” from reviewers[4].
## Background and Development
After the release of her previous album Venus in 2024, Larsson wasted little time in crafting new music for Midnight Sun[1]. In June 2022, she had announced the creation of her own record imprint, Sommer House, gaining ownership of her master recordings while continuing her partnership with Epic Records for distribution[5]. This album became the first to be released under that new arrangement. Larsson worked closely with long-time collaborator MNEK (who executive-produced the project) as well as producers Margo XS and Zhone, and songwriter Helena Gao, to develop the album over the course of 2024–2025[1][5]. According to Larsson, the guiding concept was to capture the feeling of a never-ending summer night inspired by her Swedish upbringing, “where the sun never goes down” during peak summer months[5]. A humorous TikTok trend in 2024 involving neon-colored Lisa Frank dolphin imagery also influenced the album’s Y2K aesthetic; Larsson embraced this lighthearted, nostalgic vibe as a creative spark for the project’s visual and thematic style[2]. Notably, the title track “Midnight Sun” was the first song written for the album, setting the tone for its direction early in the writing process[5].
## Music and Themes
Midnight Sun is principally a dance-pop record with high-energy production, while also incorporating elements of electro-pop, house, and R&B across its tracks[2]. Critics noted that the album’s sound palette ranges from glossy, uptempo pop anthems to more experimental moments, reflecting a confident and “kaleidoscopic” pop approach. The songs explore themes of love and heartbreak, living in the moment, self-empowerment, and personal growth, delivered in Larsson’s characteristically playful yet sincere tone[2]. For example, the opening track “Midnight Sun” celebrates an eternal summer romance, whereas later songs like “The Ambition” and “Saturn’s Return” offer more introspective commentary on the pressures of success and the process of self-discovery in Larsson’s late twenties[5]. Even the album’s quieter or sentimental tracks are packed with lush, layered production. Upbeat highlights include “Hot & Sexy” – a brash, retro-tinged club track that even samples a viral reality TV soundbite – and “Pretty Ugly”, which mixes a Gwen Stefani-esque stomp with cheeky lyrics about rejecting expectations of female perfection[5][6]. The record’s closing track, “Puss Puss”, takes its title from a Swedish colloquialism for “kiss kiss” – it serves as a tender farewell that sonically imitates the feel of a late-night phone call, fitting the album’s nocturnal, sentimental theme[2].
## Release and Promotion
Larsson officially announced the Midnight Sun album in June 2025, alongside the rollout of its title track as a new single[5]. The album’s lead single, “Pretty Ugly”, had been released earlier on 25 April 2025, introducing the era with a bold electro-pop sound[2][6]. “Pretty Ugly” received a warm reception in Larsson’s home country, debuting at number 14 on the Swedish singles chart (and also charting in Norway at No. 61)[6]. Its music video – featuring Larsson leading a muddy cheerleader dance routine – underscored the song’s theme of embracing one’s messier, rebellious side, and was later nominated for Best Choreography at the 2025 MTV Video Music Awards[7]. The follow-up single, “Midnight Sun”, was released on 13 June 2025 as the album’s title track[5]. This song became a viral summer hit for Larsson: media dubbed it a “song-of-the-summer contender” for its shimmering, nostalgic pop style, and it reached the top ten in Sweden (peaking at #9) while gaining heavy streaming activity throughout the season[3][8]. The third pre-album single, “Crush”, came out on 15 August 2025, presenting a more midtempo, R&B-inflected pop tune about the dizzying feelings of infatuation[1][9]. “Crush” also found success on pop radio in the United States, entering the Top 20 of the Mainstream Top 40 airplay chart prior to the album’s release[7].
Midnight Sun was released worldwide on 26 September 2025 in digital formats, with physical CD and vinyl editions as well. Larsson partnered with iconic design brand Lisa Frank for a special limited “Midnight Sun (Lisa Frank Edition)” CD, featuring exclusive cover art and merchandise that played into the album’s colorful, nostalgia-driven theme[7]. To promote the album, Larsson embarked on the Midnight Sun Tour. Before the album’s release, she previewed the new songs live as a supporting act on Tate McRae’s North American tour in mid-2025[7]. She then headlined her own European tour in late 2025, playing arenas across Scandinavia, the UK, and continental Europe. Additional legs of the tour were announced for North America in early 2026 and for Australia/New Zealand (where she joined OneRepublic as a special guest), extending the album’s promotion into the following year[5].
## Critical Reception
Midnight Sun received highly positive reviews from music critics, with many praising it as one of Larsson’s strongest works to date. On the review aggregator Metacritic, the album has a score of 89 out of 100, indicating “universal acclaim” based on several professional critiques[4]. DIY Magazine awarded the album a 9/10 score, applauding its vibrant blend of electronic pop and Scandipop sensibilities and stating that the record’s “forward-thinking, lush alive-ness cements Zara as a hugely overlooked genre VIP”[4]. AllMusic also rated Midnight Sun 4.5 stars, highlighting Larsson’s balance of “immediate pop bangers” with “refreshing honesty and reflective insight” — the reviewer noted that the concise 10-track length “benefits from its brevity,” leaving listeners “craving more of this endless bliss” by the end of the album[4]. In Rolling Stone, the album received an 8/10 review; the magazine remarked that Midnight Sun “from start to finish…refuses to ever slow down, trafficking in high-energy dance-pop joy” and showcasing Larsson’s growth in confidence as a pop artist[4]. Clash described the record as Larsson “honing in on what she does best with laser focus: starry-eyed, joyous Scandi-pop built to ignite dancefloors as easily as festival sing-alongs”[4]. Several outlets pointed out that Larsson’s vocal performance and personality shine throughout the album. Vogue declared Midnight Sun to be “the moment Larsson is finally coming into view as pop’s main girl,” and other publications – including Nylon and The FADER – echoed that the album reflects an artist hitting a new stride in creative control and artistry[7]. Overall, Midnight Sun was celebrated as a confident, cohesive pop album that successfully marries carefree summer anthems with a sense of personal depth.
## Commercial Performance
In Larsson’s native Sweden, Midnight Sun debuted at number 1 on the Sverigetopplistan albums chart, becoming her third chart-topping album in that country[3]. Across Europe, the album performed well in numerous markets. It reached the top ten in Norway during its opening week, and charted within the top twenty in the Netherlands (No. 15 peak) and Belgium (No. 16 on the Flemish chart)[3]. Midnight Sun also appeared on album charts in Germany, Austria and Switzerland, though with more modest peaks (for example, peaking at No. 53 in Germany and No. 50 in Switzerland)[3]. In the United Kingdom, the album entered the UK Albums Chart at number 36, marking a lower peak than Larsson’s previous international releases but still landing in the Top 40[8]. In the United States, Midnight Sun did not break into the upper tiers of the Billboard 200; however, it achieved a No. 3 debut on Billboard’s Top Dance/Electronic Albums chart (Larsson’s highest placement on that tally to date) and reached No. 22 on the Top Album Sales chart, reflecting a solid core of album buyers and dance/pop listeners in the U.S. market[9]. As of the end of 2025, the album’s songs had collectively amassed hundreds of millions of streams, and Larsson saw a notable uptick in her global streaming figures following the album’s release[7].

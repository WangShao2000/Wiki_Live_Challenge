# Voyage to the Blue Planet
Voyage to the Blue Planet was a 2024 concert tour by the American rock band Weezer. Staged in North America to commemorate the 30th anniversary of Weezer’s 1994 self-titled debut album (known as the “Blue Album”), the tour ran from early September to mid-October 2024[1]. On this tour, the band performed the Blue Album in its entirety, along with a selection of other hits and fan-favorite songs from their catalog[2]. Produced by Live Nation and sponsored by Citi, Voyage to the Blue Planet included performances in major arenas across the United States and Canada[3][4]. The shows featured support from fellow alternative rock bands The Flaming Lips and Dinosaur Jr., who served as opening acts throughout the tour[2]. Notably, the concert production embraced an elaborate outer space theme: Weezer’s members donned astronaut-style jumpsuits and acted out a lighthearted sci-fi narrative in which they travel to a “Blue Planet” and save it through the power of music[5][6]. The tour received generally positive reviews from critics, who praised its playful camp aesthetic and the band’s energetic performance of their classic material[7][8].
## Background and Development
In late 2023, Weezer began hinting that they had special plans to celebrate the Blue Album’s 30th anniversary. Frontman Rivers Cuomo revealed in a December 2023 interview that the band was preparing “a really amazing deluxe package with a bunch of additional material” for the album and “of course, we’ve gotta do some kind of epic tour” to mark the milestone[9]. He noted that while the band hadn’t staged a major commemoration for the album’s 20th anniversary, they intended to “give it its due respect” at 30 years[10]. By early 2024, further clues began to emerge. On February 28, 2024, Weezer launched a cryptic website (BlueVoyage2024.com) and social media teaser with the message, “Are you prepared for the voyage?” – showing the band in what looked like space suits – and declaring that “the voyage takes flight on March 11, 2024”[11]. This led to widespread speculation that an space-themed Blue Album anniversary tour was imminent[12][11].
On March 11, 2024, Weezer officially announced the Voyage to the Blue Planet Tour, confirming it as an extensive North American arena tour celebrating 30 years of the Blue Album[13]. The tour was scheduled to kick off on September 4, 2024, at the Xcel Energy Center in Saint Paul, Minnesota, and to conclude on October 11, 2024, at the new Intuit Dome in Inglewood, California[3][14]. (A final show on October 12 in Palm Springs was later added as the tour finale[1].) As promised, the band would perform the entire Blue Album front-to-back at each show, in addition to “other fan favorites, rarities and more” from their discography[2]. The Flaming Lips and Dinosaur Jr. were announced as special guests and opening acts for all dates[2]. Tickets went on sale mid-March, with presales via the band’s mailing list and Citi (the tour’s official credit card sponsor)[15]. Weezer also arranged a separate one-off anniversary event on March 15, 2024 (months before the tour) at a small venue in Los Angeles, where they performed the Blue Album in full with special guests – a nod to the band’s early club days[16].
## Concert Production and Synopsis
Weezer performing in astronaut attire during the Voyage to the Blue Planet tour, 2024.
Each Voyage to the Blue Planet concert was presented as a tongue-in-cheek space adventure, complete with theatrical props, themed visuals, and a loose storyline connecting the songs. The stage design featured a retro-styled rocket ship at center stage and scenery made to resemble an extraterrestrial landscape – including fake “space rocks” (asteroid props) scattered around[17][18]. At the start of the show, a countdown clock appeared on the screens, mimicking a spacecraft launch sequence[19]. When the countdown reached zero, the on-stage “rocket” dramatically lifted up amid sparklers and smoke, revealing the band members beneath it in full astronaut-like jumpsuits[20]. Amid cheers from the audience, Weezer opened the show by launching into the first song as if “taking off” on their voyage[19].
Throughout the concert, large video screens and special effects helped tell the story of Weezer’s journey through space. The band “traveled” from Earth toward the distant Blue Planet (a symbolic reference to their Blue Album and the planet Earth) and encountered various cosmic scenes that tied in with their music[21][22]. For example, at times inflatable planets and UFO models would descend from the arena ceiling, and a giant circular screen at the back of the stage displayed animated outer-space imagery to simulate the voyage[18]. Visual cues referencing the band’s album colors and themes were woven into the set: when a red planet hovered above the stage, Weezer launched into songs from their “Red Album,” and when green visuals appeared, they played a track from the “Green Album” (“Hash Pipe”)[23]. During the pop hit “Beverly Hills,” neon palm trees flashed on screen (a playful nod to the song’s subject)[18], while old-fashioned television sets floated by during the mellow tune “Any Friend of Diane’s”[18].
The concert’s narrative found the band confronted by a campy alien antagonist as they ventured deeper into space. Midway through the set, a talking CGI creature (displayed on the screen) informed Rivers Cuomo that their spaceship had become stranded in an asteroid field[24]. “What asteroid belt?” Cuomo asked in mock confusion, to which the creature replied, “Why, the Pinkerton Asteroid Belt, of course!”[24]. This tongue-in-cheek moment drew cheers from the crowd, as it signaled the band would now play songs from Pinkerton (Weezer’s 1996 second album) as the next part of the show. Indeed, the band then performed a medley of five Pinkerton-era songs – including “Getchoo,” “Why Bother?,” and “Pink Triangle” – to represent their traversal through the so-called Pinkerton Asteroid Belt[25][26]. During this segment the show’s “plot” had the spaceship damaged by the alien enemy (nicknamed “Bokkus” in the show’s script) and crash-landing before reaching its destination[27][26]. The thematic gag offered a fun way to include a sampling of Pinkerton tracks, adding some heavier and more abrasive songs to the set before the main event[26].
After escaping the asteroid belt, Weezer finally “arrived” at the Blue Planet – setting the stage for the highlight of the night: a complete performance of the Blue Album. In the show’s narrative, the Blue Planet was in peril of destruction, and the only way to save it was for the band to play their debut album in full[28]. Rivers Cuomo announced to the crowd that the mission had reached its crucial moment and delivered a cheeky line: “One small step for Weezer, one giant leap for Weezer-kind,” parodying Neil Armstrong as they prepared to launch into the album’s opening track[29]. The band members planted a blue-colored flag with Weezer’s “W” logo on the stage to signify their landing on the planet[30][31]. They then proceeded to perform every song from Weezer (The Blue Album) (1994) in its original sequence, from “My Name Is Jonas” through “Only in Dreams.” The audience enthusiastically sang along to these classic tracks; during hits like “Buddy Holly” and “Say It Ain’t So,” the entire arena joined in chorus[32][33]. In an especially interactive moment on “Undone – The Sweater Song,” the band dropped out during the chorus to let the crowd shout the lyrics unaccompanied[33]. Blue-colored lighting and confetti effects heightened the atmosphere—blue streamers rained down and the venue was bathed in blue light as the album reached its climax[34][35]. At the finale of “Only in Dreams,” cannons shot out bursts of blue confetti (dubbed “stardust”) over the cheering crowd[35].
Weezer largely stuck to the music with minimal between-song chatter, adhering to the playful “space mission” script for most of the night[36][37]. Near the end, Rivers Cuomo broke character briefly to marvel at the sight of fans starting a mosh pit during the normally slow-burning “Only in Dreams,” joking that he’d “never seen a mosh pit for that song in 30 years”[38]. After finishing the Blue Album set, the band took their bows “and left the planet” – exiting the stage as the storyline’s mission was fulfilled[39]. They thanked their “flight crew” (the touring staff and crew), the opening bands, and the fans before making their final exit to an outro video (a humorous “post-credits scene” played on-screen to cap off the space adventure)[39][40]. The entire performance ran roughly 90–100 minutes and featured about 30 songs spanning Weezer’s career, making for a full evening of music wrapped in a whimsical sci-fi presentation[41][42].
## Critical Reception
Voyage to the Blue Planet was met with broadly positive reception from both critics and audiences, who appreciated the tour’s balance of nostalgia, showmanship, and musicality. Reviewers frequently noted that the concert’s deliberately campy science-fiction motif added a fun, theatrical twist to what could have simply been a standard 30th-anniversary set[43][44]. Rolling Stone described the Blue Album anniversary show as “glorious sci-fi camp,” observing that Weezer embraced an outlandishly ridiculous space-opera concept while still delivering their songs with serious skill[8]. The band’s performance was consistently strong: the members have “been playing together for decades” and moved through the complex setlist “seamlessly with little chatter,” commented the Boston Globe, which praised Weezer as “so brilliantly cohesive as a band” and noted that their multi-generational fans responded with joy at every turn[36][45]. Many critics highlighted the complete run-through of the Blue Album as the emotional high point of the night, citing the electric crowd sing-alongs to hits like “Buddy Holly,” “Say It Ain’t So,” and “Undone – The Sweater Song” as evidence of the album’s enduring appeal[32][33]. Chris Stevens of Boston.com wrote that “you couldn’t help but get caught up in the rush and the thrash and the joy of it all” during the finale, given how beloved those songs are to the fanbase[45].
The tour’s extravagant staging also drew commentary. Variety noted that the galactic set pieces and costumes verged on the silly side – one reviewer quipped that by the time dancing aliens and planets were flying around, the show felt “a bit like watching the Wiggles… only with louder guitars” – yet most critics understood that the campiness was intentional and part of the entertainment[43][44]. Several reviews pointed out that few bands of Weezer’s stature would attempt such a quirky, self-deprecating concept for an arena rock show, and that Weezer’s willingness to poke fun at themselves made the concerts endearing. Wayne Coyne of The Flaming Lips (one of the opening acts) gave the production a strong endorsement on opening night, calling the show “effing mind-blowing” from the stage[7]. Publications like Rolling Stone and Kerrang! emphasized that the “Voyage” tour successfully turned Weezer’s nostalgia for 1994 into an imaginative spectacle rather than a straightforward nostalgia trip[8][46]. Overall, the consensus was that Voyage to the Blue Planet provided a highly enjoyable experience: a celebration of a classic album delivered with a mix of sincere musical reverence and tongue-in-cheek theatrics. Even some initially skeptical commentators admitted that the concept paid off. As one radio reviewer put it, “there’s a really cool story that takes place throughout the show…even if you’re a passive Weezer fan I highly recommend it – it’s a really fun experience”[22][47].
## Concert Film and Recordings
Recognizing the demand from fans beyond the tour stops, Weezer documented the Voyage to the Blue Planet show for posterity. On September 10, 2024, the band’s performance at TD Garden in Boston was professionally filmed with a full multi-camera setup[48]. The footage from that night was edited into a live concert film titled “Weezer’s Voyage to the Blue Planet: The Concert Film.” The band announced that the film would be released as a special streaming event: it was broadcast online via the Moment House platform on October 13 and 14, 2024, while the tour was still ongoing[49]. In a social media teaser for the film, Weezer jokingly referenced the moon landing quote and invited fans who “couldn’t make it out to the tour” to join the Voyage from home[50]. The concert film captures the full live show, including the Blue Album performance and all the space-themed theatrics, allowing viewers to experience the narrative arc of the concert. Early-bird tickets for the streaming premiere were offered, and the film was later made available for on-demand viewing for a limited time[50].
In addition to the concert film, the band’s 30th anniversary celebrations included an expansive reissue of the Blue Album. In November 2024, Weezer released a deluxe 30th-anniversary edition of Weezer (Blue Album) featuring dozens of previously unreleased demos and outtakes[51]. This reissue was timed to coincide with the end of the tour, giving fans new archival material from the same era that the tour was commemorating. While no live album from the Voyage to the Blue Planet tour has been released, the streaming concert film effectively serves as its official live document. The film and the tour as a whole were seen as a fitting tribute to the Blue Album’s legacy – combining live nostalgia with an inventive, light-hearted twist that underscored Weezer’s unique personality as a band.

# 2007 NFC Championship Game
The 2007 NFC Championship Game was the National Football Conference (NFC) title game for the 2007 NFL season. It was played on January 20, 2008, at Lambeau Field in Green Bay, Wisconsin, between the Green Bay Packers and the New York Giants. The underdog Giants defeated the Packers 23–20 in overtime, earning a trip to Super Bowl XLII as NFC champions[1][2]. The game-time temperature was around −3 °F (−19 °C), with wind chills down to −23 to −24, making it one of the coldest games in NFL championship history[2][3]. This contest was the first NFC Championship to go into overtime in nearly a decade, and it is remembered as a dramatic upset in frigid conditions[4].
## Background
New York Giants: The Giants entered the postseason as the NFC’s fifth seed with a 10–6 regular season record. They reached the NFC Championship by winning three straight road playoff games. In the Wild Card round, New York defeated the NFC South champion Tampa Bay Buccaneers 24–14 in Tampa[5]. The following week in the Divisional round, the Giants upset the top-seeded Dallas Cowboys 21–17 at Texas Stadium[6][7]. This made the Giants a rare #5 seed to advance past the divisional round under the current playoff format[7][8]. Led by fourth-year quarterback Eli Manning, the Giants had developed a reputation as road warriors, carrying a multi-game road winning streak into the conference title game[1][9]. The NFC Championship appearance was the franchise’s first since the 2000 season, when the Giants last won the conference title[10].
Green Bay Packers: The Packers, led by veteran quarterback Brett Favre, finished the regular season 13–3 and were the #2 seed in the NFC. They earned a first-round bye and hosted the NFC Divisional playoff against the Seattle Seahawks. In that game – nicknamed the “Snow Globe” game for its heavy snowfall – Green Bay routed Seattle 42–20 at Lambeau Field to advance to the NFC Championship[11][12]. Favre threw three touchdown passes in the snow, and running back Ryan Grant rushed for 201 yards despite an early deficit[13][14]. This win sent the Packers to their first NFC title game since the 1997 season. As the NFC North champions, Green Bay earned the right to host the conference championship when Dallas was eliminated. The matchup against New York was a rematch of a Week 2 meeting in the 2007 regular season (won by Green Bay), though much colder conditions awaited this time at Lambeau Field in January[15].
## Game Summary
Conditions and kickoff: A capacity crowd of 72,740 braved the extreme cold at Lambeau Field for the evening kickoff[16][17]. The wind chill of around –24 °F (–31 °C) recalled the infamous “Ice Bowl” at the same venue, and television cameras showed Giants head coach Tom Coughlin’s face nearly frostbitten from the sub-zero temperatures[3][18]. Despite the frigid weather, both teams managed to score and exchange momentum in what became a back-and-forth contest.
First half: In the first quarter, the Giants struck first with two field goals by Lawrence Tynes, taking a 6–0 lead early in the second quarter[19]. Green Bay responded dramatically when Brett Favre, playing bare-handed in the cold, connected with Donald Driver on a 90-yard touchdown pass – the longest play in Packers postseason history[20][21]. Driver caught Favre’s deep throw and raced into the end zone, putting Green Bay ahead. After the extra point, and later a 36-yard field goal by Mason Crosby, the Packers led 10–6 by halftime[22][23]. The long touchdown to Driver provided a spark in an otherwise grind-it-out half, as Giants receiver Plaxico Burress was also making an impact with multiple catches despite tight coverage and the freezing conditions.
Third quarter: The Giants regained momentum with their opening drive of the second half. Aided by four Green Bay penalties on the drive (including an important roughing-the-passer call)[24][25], New York drove 69 yards in seven minutes. Running back Brandon Jacobs capped the drive with a 1-yard touchdown plunge on 4th-and-goal – after initially being stopped short, consecutive offsides penalties against Green Bay set up Jacobs at the goal line[24]. Jacobs even mimed a Lambeau Leap in celebration, inciting the home crowd[26]. The touchdown put New York ahead 13–10. The Packers immediately answered: on the ensuing kickoff, Green Bay’s Tramon Williams broke a 49-yard return, and a personal foul on Giants corner Sam Madison further advanced the ball[27]. Taking advantage, Favre executed a play-action fake and hit tight end Donald Lee for a 12-yard touchdown, giving Green Bay a 17–13 lead[28]. New York responded again before the quarter’s end. Rookie running back Ahmad Bradshaw finished a Giants drive with a 4-yard touchdown run, set up by a diving 23-yard catch from Amani Toomer moments earlier[29]. By the end of the third quarter, the Giants were back on top, leading 20–17[30].
Fourth quarter: Early in the final quarter, Green Bay tied the game. The Packers mounted a drive that included a pivotal stroke of luck: Favre heaved a deep pass that was intercepted by Giants corner R.W. McQuarters, but McQuarters fumbled during the return and Green Bay offensive lineman Mark Tauscher recovered the ball, giving possession back to the Packers[31]. Though Green Bay’s drive stalled, Mason Crosby kicked a 37-yard field goal to tie the score 20–20 with 11:46 remaining[32]. The tension mounted as the teams traded possessions. New York’s defense largely contained Favre and the Packers’ offense – Green Bay’s running game was stifled all night (Packers runners totaled only 28 yards on the ground)[33][34], forcing Favre into difficult third-down situations. The Giants offense, behind Eli Manning’s steady play, advanced into scoring range twice in the closing minutes but squandered both opportunities. With 6:49 left, Lawrence Tynes missed a 43-yard field goal wide left[19]. Then, in the final seconds of regulation, the Giants reached the Green Bay 19-yard line to set up a potential game-winning kick. However, a poor snap foiled the 36-yard field goal attempt, and Tynes missed again as time expired, sending the NFC Championship into overtime tied 20–20[19]. Tynes had missed two straight field goals, while the Packers – despite being outplayed in several areas – were given new life, recalling some of Lambeau Field’s historic late-game heroics.
Overtime: Green Bay won the coin toss and received the ball to start overtime, needing any score to win. On the second play of the extra period, however, Brett Favre threw a pass intended for Driver on an out route that was intercepted by Giants cornerback Corey Webster at Green Bay’s 34-yard line[35][36]. Webster’s clutch interception abruptly gave New York possession in excellent field position. Just four plays later, the Giants sent out Lawrence Tynes to attempt a 47-yard field goal. This time, despite the sub-zero cold and his earlier misses, Tynes drilled the kick straight through the uprights[19][36]. The field goal 2:35 into overtime clinched a 23–20 victory for New York, silencing the Green Bay crowd and sending the Giants to the Super Bowl. Tynes immediately sprinted off the field in celebration as his teammates erupted in joy on the frozen tundra[37]. It was the first game-winning field goal of Tynes’ career and came after he had “screwed it up twice” in regulation, as the kicker humorously noted, grateful for a third chance[37]. For Green Bay, the abrupt ending was bitter – Favre said he was “disappointed that the last pass [he] threw was intercepted”[38].
Key performances: Giants quarterback Eli Manning managed the game effectively despite the harsh weather, throwing for 251 yards with no turnovers[39]. Plaxico Burress was Manning’s top target, compiling 11 receptions for 154 yards, often against Pro Bowl cornerback Al Harris, in a standout performance[40]. The Giants’ running back duo of Jacobs and Bradshaw combined for over 130 rushing yards, providing balance in the cold. For the Packers, Brett Favre finished 19-of-35 for 236 yards with two touchdown passes – the long strike to Driver and a shorter toss to Lee – but he also threw two interceptions, including the costly one in overtime[41]. Donald Driver recorded 141 receiving yards (90 of them on one play) and a touchdown[20]. Green Bay’s ground game struggled heavily: running back Ryan Grant was held to just 29 yards rushing after his dominant outing a week earlier[42]. New York’s defense sacked Favre only once but pressured him into multiple errant throws and contained the Packers’ offense for large stretches (Green Bay had only 13 first downs to New York’s 24)[33][43]. The Giants also dominated time of possession, holding the ball for over 40 minutes to the Packers’ 22 minutes[44][33]. The frigid weather was arguably a factor in the game’s rhythm – both teams had to adjust to the cold ball and stiff winds. Notably, Packers coach Mike McCarthy had his punter practice catching snaps barehanded before the game, yet there were still mishaps like fumbled snaps and a short punt by Giants punter Jeff Feagles[45][46]. In the end, New York’s ability to run the ball and avoid critical mistakes, combined with their resiliency in overtime, secured the NFC title in one of the NFL’s most challenging environments.
## Aftermath
The New York Giants advanced to Super Bowl XLII, where they faced the unbeaten New England Patriots. Despite being heavy underdogs again, the Giants went on to win the Super Bowl, defeating the 18–0 Patriots and ending New England’s bid for a perfect season[47]. It was the Giants’ third Super Bowl championship in franchise history, and it completed a remarkable postseason run of four straight upset victories (all away from home). Eli Manning was named Super Bowl MVP two weeks later, validating the progress he had shown throughout the playoffs[48][49].
For the Green Bay Packers, the NFC Championship loss was a crushing finish to an otherwise stellar season. The game turned out to be Brett Favre’s final game in a Packers uniform[50]. A few months later, Favre announced his retirement from the Packers after 16 seasons with the team (he later continued his career with other franchises)[50]. Favre’s last pass as a Packer was the overtime interception to Corey Webster, a moment that would haunt the team’s offseason. After Favre’s departure, the Packers transitioned to Aaron Rodgers as their starting quarterback the next season. Green Bay would not return to the NFC title game until the 2010 season, when they won and eventually claimed the Super Bowl.
The 2007 NFC Championship Game is remembered for its historic cold, dramatic swings, and the resilience of the underdog Giants. Tom Coughlin’s face visibly wind-burned from the cold became an enduring image of the game, symbolizing the harsh conditions[18]. The contest has since been ranked among the NFL’s classic championship games, sometimes nicknamed “Ice Bowl II” by fans and media for its frigid setting and thrilling conclusion. For the Giants, the victory in Green Bay was a defining moment that propelled them to one of the biggest upsets in Super Bowl history, while the Packers and their fans experienced an agonizing near-miss at a Super Bowl trip. Nonetheless, both franchises and NFL historians regard the 2007 NFC title game as a classic encounter that showcased perseverance amid adversity, truly living up to the lore of cold-weather championship football.

# 2013 Green Bay Packers–Dallas Cowboys game
The 2013 Green Bay Packers–Dallas Cowboys game was a National Football League (NFL) regular season game played on December 15, 2013, at AT&T Stadium in Arlington, Texas. In this Week 15 matchup, the Green Bay Packers overcame a 23-point halftime deficit to defeat the Dallas Cowboys by a score of 37–36, tying the largest comeback victory in Packers franchise history[1][2]. Green Bay’s backup quarterback Matt Flynn threw four touchdown passes in the second half, including the go-ahead score, while Dallas quarterback Tony Romo threw two interceptions in the final minutes that enabled the Packers’ rally[3][4]. With the victory, Green Bay improved to 7–6–1 and kept its playoff hopes alive, and the loss dropped Dallas to 7–7, hurting the Cowboys’ chances in the NFC East race[5][6].
## Background
The game took place in Week 15 of the 2013 NFL season and had significant playoff implications for both teams. The Green Bay Packers entered with a 6–6–1 record and were still in contention for the NFC North title despite having lost star quarterback Aaron Rodgers to a broken collarbone in early November[7][8]. Rodgers had missed six straight games and was not yet medically cleared to return, so Green Bay continued to start Matt Flynn at quarterback[5]. The Dallas Cowboys, at 7–6, were fighting for the NFC East crown and sought to pull even with the Philadelphia Eagles atop the division[9]. The Cowboys were playing at home in Arlington, where the Packers had not won a game since 1989[10]. The matchup was also notable as Green Bay’s first visit to AT&T Stadium since winning Super Bowl XLV on that field in the 2010 season[11].
## Game summary
First half: The Cowboys dominated the first half, building a 26–3 lead by halftime. Dallas opened the scoring with two field goals by Dan Bailey (47 and 23 yards) and a 25-yard touchdown pass from Tony Romo to Jason Witten in the first quarter[12][13]. In the second quarter Bailey added two more field goals (43 and 50 yards), and running back DeMarco Murray capped the half with a 1-yard touchdown run in the final minute before intermission[14][15]. Bailey made five field goals in total during the game[16], accounting for much of Dallas’s early scoring. Meanwhile, Green Bay’s only first-half points came on a franchise-record-tying 57-yard field goal by Mason Crosby in the first quarter[17]. By halftime, Dallas led 26–3 and appeared in full control, having outgained Green Bay and forced two Packers turnovers.
Second half: The momentum swung dramatically after halftime. On the very first play of the third quarter, Packers running back Eddie Lacy broke free for a 60-yard run, energizing the team[18]. That drive led to Green Bay’s first touchdown just minutes into the half, as Matt Flynn found Jordy Nelson for a 13-yard scoring pass to cut the deficit to 26–10[19][20]. Dallas answered with Bailey’s fifth field goal (another 50-yarder) to make it 29–10[21]. However, Green Bay continued to chip away: Flynn threw a 3-yard touchdown to Andrew Quarless late in the third quarter, and early in the fourth he connected with James Starks on an 11-yard touchdown. Those scores suddenly brought the Packers within five points, at 29–24, with about 13 minutes remaining in the game[22].
Facing Green Bay’s surge, the Cowboys briefly regained momentum with a 5-yard touchdown pass from Romo to Dez Bryant, extending the Dallas lead to 36–24 with 7:55 left in the fourth quarter[23][24]. Bryant’s leaping catch in the end zone over multiple defenders appeared to put Dallas back in control[25]. But the Packers offense responded immediately: Flynn led another drive and threw his fourth touchdown of the half, a 3-yard strike to James Jones, making the score 36–31 with just over four minutes remaining[26][27]. It was Green Bay’s fifth consecutive touchdown drive in the second half, completing an improbable comeback from the 23-point deficit[28].
In the final minutes, the game saw a dramatic finish. Clinging to a five-point lead (36–31) with under three minutes left, the Cowboys had a chance to run out the clock. Dez Bryant had gained a first down just before the two-minute warning, and Dallas was in position to force Green Bay to use its timeouts[29][30]. However, on a 2nd-and-6 play with about 1:50 remaining, Tony Romo audibled to a pass play (instead of a handoff to Murray) and threw the ball behind receiver Miles Austin while trying to escape pressure[30][31]. The pass was intercepted by Green Bay cornerback Sam Shields at midfield, giving the Packers another opportunity with very little time left[3][30]. Green Bay quickly drove 50 yards, and Eddie Lacy scored on a 1-yard touchdown plunge with 1:31 remaining – the Packers’ first lead of the game, coming after they had trailed 26–3 at halftime[2][32]. The Packers attempted a two-point conversion to increase the lead to a field goal margin, but Flynn’s pass to Quarless was no good, leaving the score 37–36[33].
Dallas still had a chance to respond, needing only a field goal in the final 90 seconds. However, on the Cowboys’ very next possession, Romo threw another interception. He attempted a pass toward Cole Beasley that was initially ruled incomplete, but Green Bay’s Tramon Williams had dived under the throw to snag it just above the turf[34]. After a video review, the call was reversed to an interception by Williams, effectively sealing the outcome[34]. The Packers ran out the remaining time with kneeldowns. Notably, an upset Dez Bryant left the field and headed to the locker room before the game had officially ended, drawing some criticism for his early exit[35][36].
## Aftermath and significance
Green Bay’s comeback from a 23-point deficit is tied for the largest in the Packers’ long history, matching a 1982 game in which the team also overcame a 23-point gap (against the Los Angeles Rams)[11]. The 37–36 victory was the Packers’ first road win in Dallas since 1989, ending a multi-decade drought in away games against the Cowboys[10]. The game was widely regarded as one of the most exciting of the 2013 NFL season; the NFL named the Packers’ rally a nominee for the league’s “Never Say Never Moment” of Week 15, recognizing its improbability and dramatic finish[37][38]. Packers head coach Mike McCarthy told reporters after the game that the emotional comeback victory left him “drained” and was as exhilarating as any in his career[39]. On the Dallas side, owner Jerry Jones called it “one of the hardest losses” he had ever experienced[40], and the Cowboys’ collapse in the second half intensified criticism of their play-calling and late-game execution. Some observers questioned the decision to pass instead of run in the critical moment before Romo’s first interception, given DeMarco Murray’s success on the ground (134 rushing yards in the game at a 7.4-yard average)[41][42].
The win kept the Packers in contention for the postseason. Green Bay improved to 7–6–1 and remained just a half-game behind the NFC North leaders. In fact, injured quarterback Aaron Rodgers returned to action in Week 17, and the Packers would go on to win their final two games and clinch the NFC North title at 8–7–1. For Dallas, the defeat proved costly. The Cowboys fell to 7–7 and remained one game behind Philadelphia in the NFC East with two weeks remaining[6]. Although Dallas still controlled its own destiny at that point (with two divisional games left)[43], they ultimately finished 8–8 for the third straight year and missed the playoffs once again[44]. The 2013 loss to Green Bay, marked by surrendering a 23-point lead at home, has since been remembered as a defining moment of the Cowboys’ late-season struggles in that era, and a highlight in NFL lore for the Packers’ resilience.

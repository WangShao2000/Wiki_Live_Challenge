# Kaleigh Trace
## Early life and education
Kaleigh Trace (born 1986) is a Canadian writer, sex educator, and therapist known for her frank discussions of sexuality and disability[^1][^4]. At age eight, she was involved in a car accident that resulted in a spinal cord injury and permanent disability[^1]. Trace grew up feeling alienated from conventional ideals of beauty and sexuality due to her disability, an experience that later shaped her advocacy for inclusive, shame-free sexual education[^1]. She attended Dalhousie University in Halifax, Nova Scotia, graduating in 2009 with a Bachelor of Arts degree[^1].
## Career in sex education and advocacy
After university, Trace began working at Venus Envy, a Halifax-based sex shop and bookstore, where she served as an educational coordinator and workshop instructor[^1]. In this role, she provided sex education to diverse groups – from college students to seniors – and answered customers’ questions on topics ranging from consent to sexual techniques[^1]. Feeling that existing sex resources were largely written for able-bodied people, Trace started a personal blog called The Fucking Facts around 2011 to discuss sexuality from a disabled person’s perspective[^1]. On her blog, she addressed topics such as pleasure, body image, and adapting sex practices for people with disabilities in an accessible, candid tone.
In 2013, Trace co-created a feminist parody video titled “Ask First”, a response to Robin Thicke’s controversial song “Blurred Lines.” The parody, which Trace wrote with J. Mary Burnett, featured lyrics promoting affirmative consent (e.g. “No way to know I want it / Unless I say I want it / Ask first”) and was performed by a group of Halifax women[^2]. The video went viral locally, garnering over 18,000 views in its first two days, and drew media attention for its sex-positive messaging around consent[^2]. This project exemplified Trace’s early activism linking sexual health education with social justice.
## Writing and recognition
Encouraged by her blogging and community work, Trace ventured into long-form writing. In 2014, she published her first book, Hot, Wet & Shaking: How I Learned to Talk About Sex, with Invisible Publishing[^1]. The book is a memoir and essay collection chronicling Trace’s journey in her early twenties toward embracing her body and sexuality as a queer disabled woman[^1]. It covers her personal experiences – from learning about orgasm and sexual pleasure to confronting societal attitudes – framed by her perspective that “nobody is born knowing how to have sex” and that vulnerability and open communication are key to better sexual experiences[^1][^4]. Hot, Wet & Shaking was well received for its humor, honesty, and sex-positive approach. Trace toured the book across Canada, and it attracted significant local attention in Halifax upon release[^1]. In 2015, her memoir won the Evelyn Richardson Non-Fiction Award, a prize recognizing the best non-fiction book by a Nova Scotia author, at the Atlantic Book Awards[^3].
Beyond her book, Trace has contributed to various feminist and literary publications. Her writing has appeared in magazines such as Shameless and GUTS, and in the LGBTQ+ literary journal Plenitude, often exploring themes of bodily autonomy, consent, and the intersections of sexuality and disability[^6]. She has also been featured as a commentator on sexual health and disability on Canadian media, including appearances on CBC Radio’s program Tapestry. These activities have established Trace as a notable voice in sex education circles, particularly advocating for the inclusion of queer and disabled perspectives.
## Later work and personal life
Trace continued working at Venus Envy and in community education through her late twenties and early thirties. In 2017, she relocated to Toronto, Ontario to pursue graduate studies[^1]. She earned a Master of Science degree in couples and family therapy (from the University of Guelph), specializing in helping clients heal from sexual trauma[^1]. After completing her graduate training, Trace began practicing as a licensed therapist in Toronto, focusing on sexuality, relationships, and trauma recovery in her clinical work[^1]. In interviews, she has noted that this counseling work feels like a natural extension of her earlier role as a sex educator, as both involve helping people overcome shame and communicate openly about their bodies and needs[^1][^4].
In October 2022, Kaleigh Trace was diagnosed with an aggressive form of bladder cancer, which doctors determined to be terminal[^1]. Facing this life-changing prognosis, she took a step back from full-time work and underwent treatment, while continuing to value pleasure and connection in her remaining time[^1]. The diagnosis also prompted a return to her earlier writing. In 2024, Trace and Invisible Publishing released a 10th-anniversary edition of Hot, Wet & Shaking, featuring a new introduction and additional essays in which she reflects on illness, mortality, and finding joy despite physical decline[^4]. In these writings she juxtaposes her past celebration of sexual freedom with her current experiences of pain and “the fragility of life,” aiming to destigmatize honest discussion of death alongside sex[^4]. To promote the new edition, Trace embarked on a unique speaking tour. She created and toured a one-woman show called “Little Deaths,” blending stand-up comedy and storytelling about what it’s like to date and have sex while living with terminal cancer[^5]. The show, which was featured at the 2024 Luminato Festival in Toronto, exemplified Trace’s characteristic mix of humor and candor in confronting taboo subjects[^5].
Trace identifies as a queer woman and has been vocal about the need for inclusivity in discussions of sexuality[^4]. Throughout her career, she has challenged the misconception that disabled people are asexual or incapable of having fulfilling sex lives. By sharing her own stories and expertise, Kaleigh Trace continues to advocate for a culture in which everyone – regardless of body type or ability – has the knowledge and confidence to pursue pleasure safely and consensually.

# 2025 Tour Championship
## Background and Context
The 2025 Tour Championship was the final event of the PGA Tour’s 2025 FedEx Cup Playoffs and marked the conclusion of the 2024–25 PGA Tour season[1]. It followed two playoff tournaments—the FedEx St. Jude Championship and the BMW Championship—as part of the three-event postseason series leading to the FedEx Cup. Players accumulated points throughout the season to qualify for the playoffs, with the top 70 advancing to the first event, 50 moving on to the second, and the remaining top 30 in the FedEx Cup standings qualifying for the Tour Championship[2][3]. World No. 1 Scottie Scheffler entered the playoffs as the FedEx Cup points leader and was the top seed at East Lake, followed by Rory McIlroy, J.J. Spaun, Justin Rose, and Tommy Fleetwood as the top five in points[4].
The playoff race leading into the Tour Championship featured dramatic moments. Justin Rose won the FedEx St. Jude Championship in a playoff, vaulting from 25th to 4th in the standings[5]. In the second leg, Scottie Scheffler captured the BMW Championship at Caves Valley, solidifying his No. 1 position heading to East Lake[6]. These victories set the stage for a tightly packed field of 30 elite players competing for the FedEx Cup title. Six former FedEx Cup champions were among the qualifiers, including defending cup holder Scheffler, Viktor Hovland, McIlroy, Patrick Cantlay, Rose, and Justin Thomas[7]. Notably, Fleetwood came in as one of only two players in the field without a PGA Tour win to date (alongside rookie Jacob Bridgeman)[8], a fact that foreshadowed the historic breakthrough to come.
## Location and Course Details
The tournament was played at East Lake Golf Club in Atlanta, Georgia, the long-standing host venue of the Tour Championship. East Lake is a historic course (the home club of legendary golfer Bobby Jones) and has hosted the Tour Championship continuously since 2004 (and also in 1998, 2000, 2002)[9]. The course is a par 70, measuring 7,440 yards for the tournament setup[10]. It is a classical parkland-style layout originally designed by Tom Bendelow and later reworked by Donald Ross, with a significant renovation completed prior to the 2024 event by architect Andrew Green[11]. The 2023 renovation included reshaped bunkers, some tree removal, and the conversion of all greens to Bermuda grass, making many greens larger and more undulating[11]. Notably, the club had briefly played as par 71 in 2024 but returned to a par-70 configuration for 2025[12], featuring just two par-five holes. East Lake’s turf is known for its Zoysia grass fairways and thick Bermudagrass rough, placing a premium on accuracy off the tee. The course’s record score is 257 (23-under-par) set by Tiger Woods in 2007, with the single-round record of 60 held by Zach Johnson[13].
## Format and Field Qualification
Starting in 2025, the Tour Championship format underwent a significant change. The PGA Tour eliminated the staggered “starting strokes” system that had been used from 2019 through 2024 to give the FedEx Cup points leader a head-start in scoring[14][15]. Instead, all 30 players began the tournament at even par, and the Tour Championship was played as a standard 72-hole stroke-play event. Whoever had the lowest 72-hole score would be crowned the tournament winner and FedEx Cup champion, making for a more straightforward and engaging competition[14]. There was no cut, as is customary in this small-field event[16].
The field comprised the top 30 players in the FedEx Cup standings after the BMW Championship[3]. Thanks to their season-long performance, each of these players earned a two-year PGA Tour exemption (through 2026) for reaching the Tour Championship, if not already exempt[17]. The elite field included many of the season’s top performers and multiple major champions. Six players in the field were past FedEx Cup champions, underlining the strength of competition[7]. At the same time, several players qualified without winning any PGA Tour event that season, relying on consistent high finishes. In fact, Tommy Fleetwood entered East Lake at 5th in points despite not having won on Tour in 2025[18]. With the reset to even par for all, every qualifier had a mathematical chance to win the FedEx Cup over four rounds, adding intrigue for lower-ranked players. Scottie Scheffler, as the No. 1 seed and reigning FedEx Cup champion, was aiming to become the first ever to win back-to-back FedEx Cups, while veterans like McIlroy (a three-time FedEx Cup winner) and younger contenders like Ludvig Åberg and Akshay Bhatia looked to challenge for the title. The new format promised a “level playing field,” echoing PGA Tour Commissioner Jay Monahan’s goal of a more fan-friendly and climactic finish to the season[14][19].
## Tournament Summary
### Round 1 (Thursday, August 21)
Russell Henley blazed out to the first-round lead with a bogey-free 9-under-par 61, an opening salvo that put him two strokes ahead of the field[20]. Henley, a Georgia native playing in front of home-state fans, tallied seven birdies and an eagle on East Lake’s receptive greens. His 61 was one shot shy of the course record and was the lowest round of the tournament. World No. 1 Scottie Scheffler started strong with a 63 (−7) despite a late scare from a wayward drive on 16, ending the day alone in second place[21][22]. Five players were tied at 6-under 64, three shots off the lead, in a logjam for third. This group at 64 included Tommy Fleetwood, Patrick Cantlay, Collin Morikawa, Justin Thomas, and Scotland’s Robert MacIntyre, all of whom capitalized on soft conditions and a “lift, clean and place” setup to go low[23][24]. Reigning FedEx Cup champ Rory McIlroy opened with a 66 (−4) and was part of a tie for 8th, while several other contenders lurked just a few strokes back. In total, 21 of the 30 players broke par in the opening round as East Lake yielded unusually low scoring for a Tour Championship start.
### Round 2 (Friday, August 22)
Tommy Fleetwood surged into a share of the 36-hole lead, continuing his quest for an elusive first PGA Tour win. Fleetwood followed his first-round 64 with a 7-under 63 in the second round, highlighted by six birdies on the back nine, to reach 13-under-par overall[25][26]. He was joined at the top by overnight leader Russell Henley, who posted a solid 4-under 66 to also sit at 13-under through two rounds[25]. The co-leaders enjoyed a two-shot cushion heading into the weekend. The low round of Friday came from Cameron Young, who vaulted himself into contention with a blistering 62 (−8). Young’s round featured nine birdies, including a run of five birdies in six holes on the back nine, moving him to 11-under and alone in third place after 36 holes[27]. Close behind, Patrick Cantlay and Robert MacIntyre were tied for fourth at 10-under following matching rounds of 64, keeping within striking distance of the lead[28]. In contrast, Scottie Scheffler lost a bit of ground on day two; after a 69, he stood at 8-under (five shots back) at the tournament’s midway point[29]. A large pack of players remained in the mix, with a dozen competitors clustered between 7-under and 10-under, including major winners like Shane Lowry (who shot 63 Friday to reach −7) and Rory McIlroy (−7)[30]. With soft conditions persisting but pin positions growing tougher, the stage was set for a tightly contested weekend battle for the FedEx Cup.
### Round 3 (Saturday, August 23)
Moving day saw the tournament tighten up as conditions toughened under cloudy, drizzly skies[31][32]. Patrick Cantlay shot a superb 6-under 64 in the third round to leap into a tie for the lead by day’s end[33]. Cantlay, a former FedEx Cup champion, carded seven birdies on Saturday to erase a three-shot deficit. He joined 36-hole leader Tommy Fleetwood at 16-under-par (194) total, as Fleetwood grinded out a 67 despite some mid-round adversity[33]. Fleetwood stumbled with a bogey at the long par-3 9th and a double bogey at the par-3 15th (after finding the water)[34], but he rebounded with clutch play down the stretch to stay in front. The co-leaders, at 16-under, were two shots clear of Russell Henley, who slipped to third at 14-under after an even-par 70 in Round 3[35]. Making the biggest move on Saturday was Keegan Bradley, who fired the day’s low score, a 7-under 63, to jump into fourth place at 13-under[36]. Bradley began the round three back and briefly surged up the leaderboard, though he remained three strokes off the lead entering Sunday. Scheffler had an up-and-down round (1-under 69) marked by early bogeys, ending the day in fifth at 12-under and still within four of the top[36][37]. In all, 14 players were within eight shots of the lead heading into the final round, but scoring was notably tougher in Round 3 (the field averaged 69.6 strokes, compared to the mid-67 averages of the first two days) as tournament officials tucked pins and the rain-softened course played longer[32]. With Fleetwood and Cantlay both seeking to end victory droughts, and Henley and others in striking range, the FedEx Cup and $10 million prize were still very much up for grabs.
### Round 4 (Sunday, August 24)
The final round delivered high drama before Tommy Fleetwood ultimately clinched a long-awaited victory. Starting Sunday tied for the lead, Fleetwood and Cantlay dueled through the front nine with neither pulling away. Fleetwood stumbled with a bogey at the 10th hole, which cut his lead to a single stroke over a charging Cantlay[38]. However, the Englishman responded emphatically with back-to-back birdies on the 12th and 13th holes, regaining a comfortable cushion[38]. As the pressure mounted on the closing holes at East Lake, Cantlay could not keep pace. The 2021 FedEx Cup champion bogeyed the par-4 16th, a misstep that effectively ended his comeback bid as Fleetwood maintained a multi-shot margin[39]. Tommy Fleetwood capped off the week with a steady even-par 70 (2-under 68 when adjusted for par), posting a winning total of 18-under-par 262. He finished three strokes ahead of runners-up Russell Henley and Patrick Cantlay, who each concluded at 15-under 265[40]. The victory marked Fleetwood’s first career PGA Tour title and made him the FedEx Cup champion for 2025. It was a breakthrough moment for the 34-year-old, coming in his 164th PGA Tour start after numerous close calls[41]. Fleetwood became the first player since Chad Campbell in 2003 to have his maiden PGA Tour win occur at the Tour Championship[42].
Behind the winner, the battle for high finishes provided plenty of excitement. Corey Conners carded a blistering 8-under 62 on Sunday – the low round of the final day – vaulting the Canadian into a tie for fourth place at 14-under[43]. Conners’ bogey-free 62 matched Henley’s opening 61 as the best rounds of the week in relation to par. Joining Conners in T-4 were Scottie Scheffler and Cameron Young, also at 14-under 266[43]. Scheffler’s hopes of a repeat FedEx Cup title were effectively dashed after an out-of-bounds tee shot on the first hole led to an early bogey on Sunday, and a double bogey at the 15th removed any outside chance[44]. He closed with 68 to extend his streak of top-10 finishes to 14 consecutive events[43], but it was only enough for a tie for fourth. Cameron Young bounced back from a shaky third round to shoot 4-under 66 in the finale, also finishing three shots shy of the podium. Rounding out the top ten, players such as Keegan Bradley, Justin Rose, Robert MacIntyre, and Shane Lowry were further down the leaderboard, with Bradley fading to a tie for seventh at 12-under after an even-par final round. In total, 27 of the 30 competitors ended the tournament under par, reflecting both the scorable early conditions and the caliber of the field. When the last putt dropped, it was Tommy Fleetwood hoisting the FedEx Cup trophy, completing a storybook week by shedding the label of “best player without a PGA Tour win” and etching his name in season-ending glory.

T4. Corey Conners – 266 (−14)
T4. Cameron Young – 266 (−14)
Fleetwood’s winning score of 18-under tied the second-lowest in Tour Championship history (though well shy of the record −23) and made him the FedEx Cup Champion for 2025. The lowest round of the tournament was Henley’s opening 61 (−9) on Thursday[20], which was one stroke off the East Lake course record. Two other rounds of 62 were recorded during the week (Young in round 2 and Conners in round 4). Henley’s 54-hole total of 193 (−17) set a new personal best, though he could not hang on in the final round. The tough 235-yard 15th hole (par 3) played a pivotal role, yielding several double bogeys (including to Fleetwood and Bradley on Saturday) and playing as one of the hardest holes of the week. There were no holes-in-one during the event, but Scottie Scheffler did extend an impressive streak with his T-4 finish: it was his 14th straight top-10, tying a PGA Tour record that had stood since 1977[45]. By winning, Fleetwood earned a five-year PGA Tour exemption (through 2030) and moved to a career-high year-end ranking. He also became the first Englishman to win the FedEx Cup, joining Justin Rose (2018) as the only Europeans to have done so.
## Prize Purse and Points Distribution
The 2025 Tour Championship featured a prize fund of $40 million, reflecting a revamped payout structure tied to the format change[46]. The winner’s share for the FedEx Cup champion was $10 million – a hefty sum, though notably a reduction from the $18–$25 million top prizes of recent years under the old system[47]. Fleetwood collected the entire $10 million bonus with his victory[48], more than doubling his season’s PGA Tour earnings. Runner-up finishers Henley and Cantlay still earned multi-million dollar bonuses (roughly $6 million and $5 million respectively, based on the FedExCup payout structure), with every player in the Tour Championship field guaranteed at least a six-figure bonus payout. The total FedExCup bonus pool remained $100 million for 2025, but a larger portion of it was allocated to players outside the top 30 compared to prior years, as the Tour Championship itself was treated as a standard event purse. In a significant change, the $40 million Tour Championship purse was counted as official money on the PGA Tour for the first time since 2018[49]. This meant that Fleetwood’s $10 million prize is reflected in official earnings and the all-time money list, whereas under the previous format FedEx Cup bonuses were separate from official money.
No additional FedEx Cup points were awarded during the Tour Championship, since it was the final event determining the season champion. The FedEx Cup standings for 2025 were finalized based on the finishing order at East Lake (with Fleetwood finishing 1st in points). By making the Tour Championship field, all 30 players earned exemptions into the 2026 Masters Tournament and U.S. Open, among other perks, and those who had not already won on tour secured two-year PGA Tour status by virtue of reaching East Lake[17]. The postseason format, with no strokes-based head start, ensured that the $10 million FedEx Cup prize was truly up for grabs at the Tour Championship, rewarding the player who performed best over those four days. The Tour has indicated that this balanced bonus distribution and “winner-take-all” finale will continue to evolve in future seasons to further reward season-long excellence while preserving the drama of the final tournament[19].
## Broadcast and Media Coverage
The Tour Championship received extensive media coverage and was broadcast to a global audience. In the United States, television coverage was shared by Golf Channel and NBC Sports. Golf Channel provided live coverage of the first two rounds and early coverage on the weekend, while NBC carried the high-profile afternoon broadcasts on Saturday and Sunday, including the decisive back-nine action in the final round[50]. For example, NBC’s Sunday coverage aired from 1:30 p.m. to 6 p.m. ET, following Golf Channel’s lead-in telecast[50]. Viewers in the U.S. could also stream the tournament online. PGA Tour Live on ESPN+ offered featured group coverage and bonus feeds throughout all four days, and NBC’s Peacock service simulcast the NBC broadcast window on the weekend[51].
In the United Kingdom, Sky Sports Golf held the broadcast rights for the FedEx Cup finale[52]. Sky Sports provided live coverage each day of the tournament in prime time (UK time), given the five-hour time difference with Atlanta. For instance, the first groups on Thursday teed off at 4:26 p.m. BST, with Sky’s live coverage beginning at 6 p.m. BST to capture the late afternoon and evening play in the UK[53][52]. Internationally, the Tour Championship was covered by the PGA Tour’s world feed partners, ensuring fans in all major golf markets could follow the action. Major golf media outlets like ESPN, CBS Sports, and Golf Channel provided on-site reporting and analysis throughout the week. The compelling storyline of Fleetwood’s quest for his first win garnered significant attention in sports media; even prominent athletes such as LeBron James and Tiger Woods took to social media to congratulate Fleetwood on his breakthrough FedEx Cup victory[54][55]. With strong viewership and engagement, the 2025 Tour Championship’s broadcast underscored the event’s status as one of the most significant and anticipated tournaments of the year, bringing the PGA Tour season to an exciting close.

# Erik Campbell (Final Destination)
Erik Campbell is a fictional character in the Final Destination film series, appearing as a main character in the supernatural horror film Final Destination: Bloodlines (2025), the sixth installment of the franchise[1]. The character was created by writers Guy Busick and Lori Evans Taylor from a story concept by director-producer Jon Watts, and he is portrayed by Canadian actor Richard Harmon[2]. In the film, Erik is introduced as a member of the Campbell family who are targeted by Death’s design decades after an averted catastrophe. He is initially believed to be the eldest son of Howard and Brenda Campbell, though it is later revealed that he is not biologically part of the Campbell bloodline[3]. While attempting to outwit Death and save a family member, Erik meets a violent end when he is pulled into a malfunctioning MRI machine, resulting in one of the film’s most gruesome death scenes[4]. The character and Harmon’s performance were well received; critics described Erik as a standout in Bloodlines, and he quickly became a fan-favorite of the new installment[5][6].
## Role in Final Destination: Bloodlines
In Final Destination: Bloodlines, Death targets a single extended family after the family’s matriarch, Iris Campbell, had a premonition in 1969 that prevented a deadly high-rise collapse[7]. Erik Campbell is introduced as Iris’s grandson – the son of Howard and Brenda Campbell – and the older half-brother of Bobby and Julia. When Iris dies in the present day, Death’s curse shifts to the remaining Campbells, who begin to die in order of age (oldest to youngest)[8]. After Howard (Erik’s stepfather) is killed, Erik is presumed to be “next” due to being the eldest child. He narrowly survives a freak accident in his tattoo parlor (a fire that nearly impales him), an incident the directors later likened to Death’s “sense of playfulness,” comparing Erik to a fish being hooked and thrown back by a mischievous fisherman[9]. In a startling turn, Erik’s younger sister Julia dies before him, seemingly out of the intended order. It is then that his mother Brenda admits Erik was actually born from an affair and is not Howard’s biological son – meaning Erik was never truly part of Iris’s bloodline or on Death’s list[3].
Realizing he is supposedly “safe” from the curse, Erik becomes determined to help protect his remaining family. He devises a risky plan to cheat Death and save his half-brother Bobby: Erik deliberately triggers Bobby’s severe peanut allergy to induce a temporary death-like state, hoping to fool the entity into skipping Bobby[10]. The plan backfires dramatically. At the hospital, a powerful magnet in an overloaded MRI scanner suddenly activates and yanks all nearby metal objects into the machine – including the metal piercings on Erik’s body and a heavy wheelchair[4]. Erik is violently pulled into the MRI chamber and crushed, his body impaled and folded in half by the flying wheelchair, killing him in brutal fashion. His gruesome demise comes immediately after his attempt to interfere with Death’s design, underlining the film’s ominous lesson (voiced by another character as well as series narrator William Bludworth) that “when you mess with Death, things get messy”[11].
## Development and Portrayal
Creation and writing: Final Destination: Bloodlines was directed by Zach Lipovsky and Adam B. Stein, with the story conceived by Jon Watts and a screenplay by Lori Evans Taylor and Guy Busick[12][13]. The filmmakers sought to reinvigorate the franchise’s formula by focusing on a family lineage rather than unrelated strangers, and by subverting audience expectations about the predetermined death order. The plot twist of Erik’s true parentage was designed to surprise viewers when deaths occur “out of order,” breaking the usual pattern of oldest-to-youngest victims[14][3]. According to the directors, one of their challenges was to answer why a character not originally on Death’s list (Erik) would still face danger; they decided that if Erik actively meddled in Death’s plan, he would suffer consequences anyway[15]. This creative choice reinforced the franchise’s lore that no one can cheat Death with impunity. (In interviews, Lipovsky and Stein explicitly noted that Death itself is portrayed as an intelligent, almost sentient force in the series – aware when someone tries to avert its plans[16][9].)
Casting and characterization: Actor Richard Harmon was cast in the role of Erik Campbell, having auditioned for the part and joined the project after a months-long production delay caused by the 2023 Hollywood strikes. Harmon, a long-time fan of the Final Destination franchise, embraced the chance to be “part of horror royalty” and had clear ideas about how to play Erik[17][18]. He envisioned the character as more than a stereotypical horror victim, aiming to make Erik both darkly funny and genuinely caring in his own way[19]. “I made the character that I wanted to see in Final Destination,” Harmon said, explaining that he wanted Erik to have a sarcastic humor but also a sincere, protective side[19]. Throughout filming, Harmon worked collaboratively with the directors, even improvising some of his dialogue and actions to add authentic personality to scenes. The directors had stated they wanted to avoid one-dimensional stock characters; in Bloodlines, they strove to give each character more layers and humanity than in prior sequels[6]. Harmon’s portrayal reflects that approach – Erik comes across as a rebellious, pierced “alt-boy” with a cynical outlook, yet he exhibits a strong loyalty to his family beneath the snark.
MRI death sequence: Early in development, Lipovsky and Stein were keen to include a death involving an MRI machine – a scenario not seen in previous Final Destination entries – as a way to tap into real-life medical fears[20]. They researched actual MRI accidents (where ferrous objects become deadly projectiles in the magnetic field) and designed Erik’s final destination accordingly[20]. The elaborate hospital scene was foreshadowed by subtle clues (for example, a broken metal coil from a vending machine in an earlier scene that later becomes lethal shrapnel) and was crafted with extensive practical effects. The filmmakers noted that many elements of Erik’s demise were done physically on set to heighten realism, even as the gore factor remained over-the-top[21]. In a nod to the franchise’s history, they added small details for eagle-eyed fans – for instance, as Erik is being torn apart, his chest tattoo of a skull is exposed and then pierced by flying metal shards in a pattern that recreates the skull-and-rebar imagery of the Final Destination 5 poster[22]. Lipovsky and Stein did debate the ethical implications of depicting such a graphic medical-related death, but the concept was well-received by their production team and ultimately kept in the film. The directors later revealed they enjoyed playing with the audience’s expectations during this sequence; they momentarily “hook” Erik in an earlier near-death event (the tattoo parlor fire) without killing him, only to deliver a fatal payoff later once he actively intervenes in Death’s plan[9][23]. This creative decision aligned with the film’s central theme that tampering with fate will “not end well” for any character who crosses that line[24].
## Reception
Upon the release of Final Destination: Bloodlines in 2025, Erik Campbell quickly emerged as one of the breakout characters of the film. Audiences responded strongly to Erik’s storyline and fate; many fans openly questioned why Erik had to die at all, given his lack of blood relation to the cursed family, prompting the filmmakers to publicly clarify the in-story reasoning (i.e. Erik “messed” with Death’s design and thus became fair game)[15]. This unexpected twist generated considerable discussion in fan communities, reflecting the character’s popularity and the impact of his death. Critics also largely praised Harmon's performance and the characterization of Erik. In her review for SLUG Magazine, Angela Garcia noted that the Bloodlines cast is “imbued with a charming sense of humor that makes the characters endearing, as opposed to the ... one-dimensional characters from previous installments,” giving a “special shout-out” to Richard Harmon for bringing “a Roman Roy energy” to Erik[6]. Other reviewers echoed that sentiment: the Qualbert film blog singled out Harmon’s portrayal, calling Erik a snarky, apathetic jerk on the surface who nevertheless “steals the spotlight whenever he’s on screen,” with Harmon’s charisma “breath[ing] life into the otherwise monotone cast”[5]. The character’s mix of dry wit and underlying warmth resonated with viewers, making him one of the more memorable new additions to the long-running series.
Erik’s dramatic MRI death scene in particular became one of the most talked-about moments of Bloodlines. Several critics highlighted that sequence as a standout example of the franchise’s signature inventive gore. SLUG Magazine remarked that “one death involving an MRI will definitely stick with you, not just for its gruesomeness, but for all the little details added in to make it more visceral”[25]. The over-the-top nature of Erik’s demise, combined with the buildup of suspense and clever foreshadowing, led some commenters to rank it among the most shocking kills in Final Destination history. Overall, Final Destination: Bloodlines was received as a fresh and entertaining revival of the series, and Erik Campbell’s character contributed significantly to that positive response. Between Harmon’s performance and the emotional stakes of Erik’s storyline, the character has been widely regarded as a successful and memorable part of the Final Destination canon in this sixth installment[26][5].

# If This Was a Movie (Taylor Swift song)
"If This Was a Movie" is a song by American singer-songwriter Taylor Swift. It was released as a bonus track on the deluxe edition of Swift’s third studio album, Speak Now (2010)[1]. Swift co-wrote the song with Martin Johnson (lead singer of Boys Like Girls), making it the only track on Speak Now (deluxe) that was not written solely by Swift[2][3]. Musically, “If This Was a Movie” is a pop rock-leaning ballad about longing for an ex-lover to return, using the metaphor of a cinematic reunion. Critics noted that the song marked Swift’s transition toward more mature themes of heartbreak, moving beyond the high school fairy-tale imagery of her earlier work[4]. Billboard magazine gave the track a positive review, rating it 4 out of 5 stars and describing it as “a more age-appropriate” step for Swift’s then-innocent country image[4][5].
## Background and Release
“If This Was a Movie” was recorded during the Speak Now sessions and included as one of three bonus tracks on the Target-exclusive deluxe edition of the album (alongside “Ours” and “Superman”)[1]. Unlike the standard Speak Now tracks, which Swift wrote entirely on her own, this song was co-written with Martin Johnson. Swift and Johnson had previously collaborated on the Boys Like Girls song “Two Is Better Than One” (2009) and the Hannah Montana: The Movie soundtrack song “You’ll Always Find Your Way Back Home”[6]. The inclusion of a co-writer made “If This Was a Movie” unique on Speak Now’s deluxe edition[2].
Initially, the song was only available with physical copies of Speak Now (Deluxe) sold at Target stores in 2010. On November 8, 2011, Big Machine Records released “If This Was a Movie” (along with the other deluxe tracks) as a digital single via iTunes and Amazon[7][8]. It was issued as a promotional single to promote the Speak Now album’s deluxe content, while “Ours” was serviced to country radio as the next official single[8]. Upon its digital release, “If This Was a Movie” sold approximately 162,000 downloads in its first week[9]. This strong sales debut propelled the song onto the charts: it entered the US Billboard Hot 100 at number 10 on the chart dated November 26, 2011[9][10]. At the time, this made Swift the first artist to score eight Hot 100 debuts in the top ten[11][12]. The song also reached number 17 on the Canadian Hot 100 in that frame[10]. In the United Kingdom, where it was not actively promoted, “If This Was a Movie” charted at a modest No. 191 on the UK Singles Chart based on imports and downloads[13].
## Composition and Lyrics
Running 3 minutes and 54 seconds, “If This Was a Movie” is a midtempo ballad blending Swift’s country-pop style with pop rock elements[14][15]. It is set to a simple, recurring guitar riff that drives the melody, with a gradual build into a dramatic chorus backed by subtle harmonies[16]. Swift’s vocals carry a slight country twang, a signature of her early work, yet the song’s production leans toward polished pop rock[17]. Lyrically, the track finds Swift daydreaming about a lost love coming back “like in the movies.” She imagines that if her heartbreak were a movie, there would be a climactic reunion – singing in the chorus, “Come back, come back, come back to me like you would if this was a movie… But if this was a movie, you’d be here by now.” In the verses, the then-20-year-old Swift expresses regret and nostalgia for a relationship that ended about six months prior[18][4]. According to Billboard, the song suggests Swift “had been through a breakup and wishes to reverse it,” showcasing a more mature perspective compared to the high-school romance themes of her earlier hits[4]. Critics observed that by abandoning fairy-tale endings and acknowledging real-world heartbreak, Swift was “lyrically coming of age” on this track[17]. The title and concept play on Hollywood tropes – if life were like a movie, the estranged lover would dramatically return in the rain to apologize, giving the story a happy ending. This wistful narrative resonated with fans for its earnest portrayal of yearning and vulnerability.
## Critical Reception
Upon release, “If This Was a Movie” received generally positive reviews from music critics, especially considering its status as a bonus track. Billboard reviewers praised the song’s relatable storytelling and catchy melody. In Billboard’s track review, it was highlighted that Swift delivered the emotional narrative with a sense of innocence giving way to growth, and they rated the song 4 out of 5 stars[4][5]. The review noted the song’s “age-appropriate” approach, meaning it showed Swift evolving beyond teenage fairy tales into more adult heartbreak themes[4]. Although “If This Was a Movie” was not pushed as an official radio single, many fans and critics considered it a strong piece of the Speak Now deluxe collection. Taste of Country’s ranking of Speak Now tracks commented that while the song isn’t as immediately memorable as some of Swift’s singles, it exemplifies her gift for wistful, narrative songwriting at its best. The instrumentation and Swift’s heartfelt delivery were also well-regarded; Molly Trust of Billboard (in a retrospective piece) described the track as “corny but smart,” capturing an entire romantic drama in just a few minutes[19]. Overall, reviewers appreciated that the bonus track maintained the high quality of Swift’s writing on Speak Now, rather than feeling like a throwaway.
On the other hand, because it was a late addition to the album, “If This Was a Movie” did not receive as much individual attention as Swift’s singles. Some critics noted that it might not have had the same impact or memorability as hits like “Back to December” or “Mean,” but it nonetheless holds a special place for fans who relate to its cinematic heartbreak scenario. As of 2018, Swift had never performed the song live, making it something of a deep-cut favorite[20]. (Swift eventually gave the song its live debut as a surprise acoustic performance during her Eras Tour in June 2023, to the delight of longtime fans[21].)
## Commercial Performance
Without an official radio release, “If This Was a Movie” charted purely on the strength of digital sales. In the United States, the song’s first-week download sales (~162,000) led to its No. 10 debut on the Billboard Hot 100[9][10]. It also debuted at No. 3 on the Billboard Digital Song Sales chart that week, indicating one of the highest digital sales totals in Swift’s career up to that point[9]. The strong digital demand was fueled by fans who had waited a year for the deluxe tracks to become available outside of the Target CD. “If This Was a Movie” additionally topped Billboard’s Country Digital Song Sales chart upon release[22]. This made Swift one of the few country artists at the time to have multiple songs (including deluxe tracks) reach No. 1 on that digital sales chart. In Canada, the song reached number 17 on the Canadian Hot 100, reflecting similar digital popularity north of the border[10]. It spent a total of 12 weeks on the Hot 100 and also appeared on year-end charts for digital country songs. In international markets, “If This Was a Movie” had a more subdued impact. It did not chart in most major markets like Australia or Europe (since it wasn’t a formal single), though it did appear on the UK Singles Chart at No. 191 based on import sales and downloads[13].
By the end of the Speak Now era, “If This Was a Movie” had sold over half a million copies in the U.S., demonstrating longevity as a fan-favorite deep cut (its sales contributed to Swift’s total of 545,000 digital tracks sold in the week of its debut, which included other deluxe songs)[10]. Its Hot 100 peak at #10 was especially notable because Swift achieved that without any radio play or music video for the song. This feat underscored her strong fanbase and the commercial appeal of even her non-single tracks.
## Taylor’s Version Re-recording
In 2019, after a high-profile dispute over the ownership of her early master recordings, Taylor Swift began re-recording her back catalog. “If This Was a Movie” was among the songs Swift chose to re-record to regain control of her music[23][24]. On March 17, 2023, Swift released “If This Was a Movie (Taylor’s Version)” as a standalone digital single, coinciding with the opening night of her Eras Tour[25][23]. The re-recording was part of a special four-song release that day, which also included two other re-recorded tracks (“Eyes Open (Taylor’s Version)” and “Safe & Sound (Taylor’s Version)”) and one previously unreleased song from her vault (“All of the Girls You Loved Before”)[25]. Notably, Swift opted to include “If This Was a Movie (Taylor’s Version)” on an extended play titled The More Fearless (Taylor’s Version) Chapter – aligning it with her Fearless-era re-recordings – rather than on the Speak Now (Taylor’s Version) album[26][3]. This decision was intentional: because the original song was co-written with Johnson, Swift kept it off the Speak Now (Taylor’s Version) tracklist in order to maintain the promise that Speak Now would remain an “entirely self-written” re-recorded album[26][3]. By releasing the song earlier under the Fearless banner, Swift “tied up” that loose end and preserved the artistic narrative of Speak Now as solely her writing[2][3].
Musically, “If This Was a Movie (Taylor’s Version)” stays faithful to the 2010 arrangement, though Swift’s vocals – matured by over a decade – are noticeably richer and more controlled. Subtle production updates include a slightly more polished mix and re-recorded instrumental parts by Swift’s touring band members[27]. Critics noted that Swift’s voice on the 2023 version sounds deeper and more resonant, which some felt added emotional weight, while others missed the youthful earnestness of the original recording[28]. The Times’ critic Will Hodgkinson described the re-recorded song as “straightforward, innocent, slightly breathy balladry with an orchestral backing,” commenting that it retained the “corny but smart” charm of the original[27][28].
Upon release, “If This Was a Movie (Taylor’s Version)” achieved moderate commercial success. In the U.S., it did not enter the main Hot 100 (largely due to limited streaming compared to new singles), but it debuted at number 2 on Billboard’s Bubbling Under Hot 100 chart (effectively the equivalent of No. 102) and at number 4 on the Digital Song Sales chart with 11,300 copies sold in its release week[29]. Impressively, the Taylor’s Version reached No. 1 on Billboard’s Country Digital Song Sales chart[22]. Since the original 2011 version had also hit No. 1 on that chart in the past, Swift achieved a rare feat: “If This Was a Movie” became the first song to top the Country Digital Song Sales chart with two different recordings of the same composition[30]. Internationally, the re-recorded song charted in a few markets driven by Swift’s fan interest. It made the top 10 of the digital sales charts in Canada and the United Kingdom, and reached No. 12 on New Zealand’s Hot Singles chart (a component ranking for new entries)[29]. These placements reflected the enthusiasm of fans, especially those completing their collections of Swift’s Taylor’s Version releases.
In reviews of the March 2023 batch of songs, “If This Was a Movie (Taylor’s Version)” was met with nostalgia and approval. Critics like Januar Aguja of The Freeman praised Swift’s matured vocals, suggesting that the new rendition could win over a fresh generation of listeners[28]. Longtime fans appreciated that the re-recording stayed true to the spirit of the original track, while also fitting seamlessly into Swift’s ongoing project of reclaiming her music. With the Taylor’s Version available, Swift has ensured that future licensing and streaming of “If This Was a Movie” will benefit her directly, rather than the owners of her old masters – a key goal of her re-recordings.

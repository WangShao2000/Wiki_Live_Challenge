# Good Boy (2025 film)

*Good Boy* is a 2025 supernatural horror-thriller film directed by Ben Leonberg in his feature debut [5], [11]. The film distinguishes itself through its innovative narrative structure, which is told entirely from the point of view of a dog named Indy [17], [26]. The story follows Todd, a man who moves to a secluded family home with his dog after a serious health scare, only for the dog to perceive malevolent supernatural forces that threaten his owner [2], [29]. This unique perspective, combined with a labor-intensive production process centered on an untrained canine co-star, garnered significant critical acclaim upon its release [3], [18]. The film premiered at the South by Southwest (SXSW) Film Festival in March 2025, where it won the inaugural 'Howl of Fame Award' for Best Canine Performance [7], [12]. Following strong festival reception, it was acquired by IFC Films and Shudder for distribution [7], [27]. A highly successful viral marketing campaign, driven by a trailer that generated millions of views online, prompted IFC Films to expand the film's planned limited theatrical release into a wide national rollout on October 3, 2025 [1], [6]. *Good Boy* went on to become a commercial success, grossing over $8 million worldwide and establishing itself as a standout example of modern independent filmmaking [15]. Critics lauded its "visually striking" and "emotionally devastating" qualities, praising its conceptual ambition and the powerful performance delivered by its canine protagonist [3], [21].

## Plot

The narrative of *Good Boy* centers on the complex and protective bond between a dog and his human companion amidst a backdrop of rural isolation and supernatural dread [21], [25]. The film's plot unfolds almost exclusively from the perspective of Indy, a Nova Scotia Duck Tolling Retriever, providing a unique lens through which the audience experiences both the mundane and the terrifying [23], [36]. The story begins with Todd, played by Shane Jensen, a man suffering from the same fatal condition portrayed by horror legend Larry Fessenden, relocating to his late grandfather's abandoned cabin in the woods [12], [13]. He is accompanied by his sister, Vera, played by Arielle Friedman, and his loyal dog, Indy [13], [23]. Vera opposes the move, concerned for her brother's deteriorating health, but Todd insists on moving into the long-vacant property [24]. From the moment they arrive, Indy senses something deeply wrong with the house and its surroundings, becoming increasingly anxious and vigilant [17], [24]. While humans dismiss his behavior as paranoia, Indy's instincts are attuned to phenomena that are invisible to them [26].

These phenomena include eerie auditory and visual cues that form the basis of the film's horror elements [11]. The audience sees dancing shadows, crawling silhouettes, and other ghostly apparitions that Indy perceives as immediate threats [11]. The sound design plays a crucial role, amplifying Indy's heightened sensory awareness with high-pitched whistles and other sonic manifestations of the malevolent forces, such as a mud-covered stalker and camouflaged wraith-like hunter [12]. These entities seem to be tied to the grim history of the house and Todd's own family, creating a palpable sense of dread [2], [24]. As Todd's health continues to decline, the supernatural presence appears to intensify, blurring the lines between the haunting and the reality of his terminal illness [23]. The film explores themes of anticipatory grief and mortality, with some interpretations suggesting that the haunting serves as a supernatural manifestation of Todd's impending death, a force that Indy, with his instinctual connection to life and death, is uniquely able to perceive [23], [24]. Throughout the film, Indy's primary motivation is to protect Todd, fighting against these unseen dark entities that threaten the one he loves most [13], [25]. The narrative culminates in a heart-wrenching and emotionally devastating ending that transcends the initial premise of a haunted house story, focusing instead on themes of devotion, loss, and survival [17], [23]. The film's climax reveals that Todd has died, and the next morning, his sister Vera finds Indy unharmed in the cellar, marking the dog's survival and his continued vigilance in the aftermath of tragedy [23].

## Production

The production of *Good Boy* represents one of the most unconventional and labor-intensive filmmaking processes in recent memory, born directly from the film's singular creative premise. Conceived over ten years ago by director Ben Leonberg while rewatching *Poltergeist* (1982), the idea originated from a simple yet profound question: "What if the family dog was the only one who knew the house was haunted?" [32], [39], [41]. Leonberg spent years developing a realistic narrative that could be told entirely from a dog's perspective without resorting to anthropomorphism [10]. This commitment to authenticity dictated every aspect of the production, transforming a standard film shoot into a multi-year endeavor. Principal photography spanned approximately 400 days across a three-year window, from 2022 to 2025 [10], [31], [38]. This extended timeline was not a stylistic choice but a logistical necessity, required to accommodate the natural behavior, attention span, and well-being of the film's star, the director's own dog, Indy [32], [43]. Filming was conducted intermittently, often during late-night hours or on weekends, to align with Indy's stamina rather than adhering to a traditional production schedule [38]. The entire cast and crew, primarily director Ben Leonberg and producer Kari Fischer, lived on location for the duration of principal photography [38], [40].

The filming took place primarily in a rented house located in Harding Township, Morris County, New Jersey, which served as both the residence for the filmmakers and the set for the grandfather's cabin [37], [38]. The production was structured around Indy's capabilities, with shooting sessions lasting only one to three hours per day to maintain his focus [43]. To elicit genuine and authentic reactions from Indy, who had no formal training as an animal actor, Leonberg employed behavioral conditioning rather than traditional acting direction [9]. He created on-set circumstances to trigger instinctive responses, using methods such as issuing invented commands ("neon sign!") to elicit specific head tilts or using treats and silly noises off-camera to guide his performance [9], [32], [33]. This meticulous approach prioritized the dog's comfort above all else; Leonberg confirmed that Indy was never afraid during filming, with "scary" scenes achieved through editing, sound design, and off-camera cues projected onto his neutral expressions [33]. Human actors were cast late in the production process. Because Indy only reliably responded to Leonberg and Fischer, they performed physically as stand-ins for the human characters, blocking out scenes and guiding Indy's movements [10], [45]. Their vocal performances were added in post-production, meticulously matched to the physical actions and spatial audio cues [10]. No CGI was used, and safe stand-ins were employed only when absolutely necessary to ensure Indy's safety [34]. The production budget is reported with some variation across sources, with figures cited as $70,000, $750,000, and $1 million [1], [3], [31]. The film's runtime is consistently listed as 72 or 73 minutes [1], [17], [21]. The project received early support through a 2023 New Jersey State Council on the Arts fellowship grant awarded to Leonberg for the script draft, which helped fund its completion [38]. The proof-of-concept short film of the same name also won a prize at The Rode Reel online contest, which provided initial funding and led to casting Indy as the lead for the feature film [40].

## Cast and Crew

The ensemble cast of *Good Boy* brings a grounded and emotionally resonant quality to the film, anchored by the non-human performance of the titular canine star, Indy. The human actors deliver their performances in a manner that complements the film's unique perspective, often appearing only from the waist down or being deliberately obscured to maintain the audience's focus squarely on the dog's point of view [12], [17]. Ben Leonberg himself plays a crucial role in the film, performing physically as one of the two people Indy reliably responds to, thereby serving as a key part of the on-screen family unit [10], [39].

The primary human cast members are responsible for portraying the emotional core of the story, navigating Todd's declining health and Vera's growing concern. The supporting cast includes veteran horror figure Larry Fessenden, who plays Todd's grandfather, adding a layer of genre credibility and depth to the family's troubled history [5], [14]. The credits reflect the intimate nature of the production, with Leonberg taking on multiple roles as writer, director, cinematographer, and producer, underscoring the personal nature of the project [13], [18]. The technical team worked to create a visceral and immersive experience that fully realized the canine perspective, with contributions from cinematographer Wade Grebnoel, editor Curtis Roberts, and composer Sam Boase-Miller shaping the film's distinctive tone and atmosphere [21], [23].

The most important "cast member" is undoubtedly Indy, a Nova Scotia Duck Tolling Retriever owned by director Ben Leonberg and producer Kari Fischer [5], [10]. Born in 2017, Indy was selected for the lead role due to his serious demeanor, intense stare, and unusually visible iris and sclera, which gave his eyes a piercing quality essential for conveying emotion without anthropomorphism [10]. His real-life relationship with Leonberg was considered vital to achieving an authentic performance, making him the ideal choice for a role that demanded unreproducible natural reactions [40]. One source notes that the breed is sometimes misidentified as a Golden Retriever, though Leonberg has corrected this publicly, emphasizing the importance of the correct breed for the film [28]. Another canine actor, Max, a Golden Retriever owned by Leonberg’s parents, also appeared in the film playing the character Bandit, Todd’s grandfather’s dog, though he required different handling due to his more affection-seeking personality [10], [23].

## Release and Distribution

The distribution journey of *Good Boy* is a compelling case study in the power of the festival circuit and viral marketing to elevate an independent film to mainstream prominence. The film's official world premiere occurred at the South by Southwest (SXSW) Film Festival in Austin, Texas, on March 8, 2025, screening in the Midnighter section, a category known for showcasing horror and genre films [7], [26], [47]. Its premiere was met with strong critical reviews, setting the stage for its acquisition by major distributors [1]. Following its successful showing at SXSW, the film was acquired by Shudder for North American, UK, Ireland, Australian, and New Zealand distribution rights [7], [27], [45]. The deal was negotiated by Amy Beecroft of Verve Ventures, who represented the film, and Emily Gotto of Shudder [45]. Concurrently, IFC Films secured theatrical distribution rights for the United States [6], [7]. The film continued its festival run, later screening at the Overlook Film Festival in May 2025, where its popularity was so great that its screening expanded from one theater to three, further validating its market potential [45].

The pivotal moment in the film's path to a wide release came after the launch of its marketing campaign. The official trailer was released on August 18, 2025, and it immediately ignited massive online buzz [1], [6]. The trailer, posted on IFC's YouTube channel, garnered over 1 million views in just four days, with another 1.5 million views generated via IGN's posting, bringing the total to approximately 3 million views within a short period [6], [7]. The campaign masterfully exploited the central mystery of the film—the fate of the beloved dog—and its effectiveness was quantified by a staggering over 2,000% spike in Google searches for the query "Does the dog in Good Boy die?" following the trailer's release [1], [6], [36]. This unprecedented level of organic audience engagement and curiosity convinced IFC Films to make a strategic decision to upgrade the film's release. Originally slated for a limited theatrical release, the studio expanded its rollout to a nationwide wide release, opening in at least 1,000 theaters across the U.S. on Friday, October 3, 2025 [1], [7], [38]. The film's PG-13 rating, a departure from IFC's previous R-rated horror titles, was seen as a strategic move to broaden its demographic appeal [6], [8].

Following its theatrical run, the film transitioned to digital platforms according to the established release pattern for IFC Films/Shudder co-releases. It became available for purchase and rental on premium video-on-demand (PVOD) platforms, including Prime Video, Apple TV, Fandango at Home, and YouTube, starting on October 24, 2025 [3]. The price for digital purchase was set at $19.99, with a rental option available for $14.99 for a 48-hour viewing window [3]. As of December 19, 2025, the film was not available on major free streaming services like Netflix or Amazon Prime [4], [5]. However, industry observers expected it to begin streaming exclusively on Shudder around January 2026, based on the typical three-month window between a theatrical debut and a Shudder streaming date for similar releases [4], [5]. This anticipated streaming release was still unconfirmed as of the specified date [4].

## Critical Reception

Upon its release, *Good Boy* was met with overwhelmingly positive critical reception, earning high marks from critics and solidifying its status as a standout indie horror film. Review aggregators reflected this acclaim, with the film holding exceptionally high scores. On Rotten Tomatoes, based on reviews from hundreds of critics, it earned a "Fresh" rating of 91%, 92%, 95%, and 95% across various reports, indicating widespread critical approval [1], [3], [7], [18]. The site's critics consensus described the film as "visually striking, emotionally devastating... conceptually ambitious," highlighting its ability to transcend its novel premise and deliver a powerful emotional experience [3], [18]. Metacritic assigned the film a score of 70 out of 100 based on nine reviews, signaling generally favorable reviews [21]. Audience response was similarly strong, with a "Popcornmeter" score of 83% on Rotten Tomatoes based on over 500 verified user ratings, indicating broad public enjoyment [3].

Critics universally praised the film's innovative concept and execution, with many noting that it successfully avoids feeling like a gimmick [17], [19]. The film was lauded for its "uniquely haunting and conceptually ambitious experience" driven by its dog-perspective premise and its ability to evoke genuine empathy for its canine protagonist [18]. The performance of the dog, Indy, was a recurring and central point of praise. The film was described as a "horror gem" with an "enormously effective, unique, and scary-cute" quality [11]. Critics highlighted how the film's technical choices—such as the constant dog-eye-level camera perspective, the deliberate obscuring of human faces, and the emphasis on sound design—were instrumental in building suspense and immersing the viewer in the dog's sensory world [13], [14], [17]. IndieWire gave the film an "A-" grade, calling it a visually striking and emotionally devastating horror film that eschews genre conventions [21]. Other publications echoed this sentiment, describing it as a "haunting, moving story about a dog and his owner" that elevates the horror genre with restraint and emotional depth [19], [21]. TheWrap, Slant Magazine, RogerEbert.com, The Hollywood Reporter, and Bloody Disgusting all contributed to the wave of positive reviews, uniformly singling out Indy's performance and the film's conceptual ambition as its greatest strengths [21].

While the critical consensus was overwhelmingly positive, a few minor critiques were noted. Some reviewers felt that the first half of the film was slow-paced and lacked significant tension, with the majority of its jump scares and intense scenes concentrated in the second half [14]. However, these observations were largely overshadowed by the praise for the film's overall execution and thematic depth. The film was widely interpreted as a sophisticated exploration of themes beyond simple horror, including canine loyalty, the incomprehension of human-scale horror from an animal's perspective, trauma-induced perception, and mortality [21], [23]. Many critics appreciated that the film innovatively subverts the common horror trope of pets as collateral damage, instead centering the dog as the empathetic protagonist whose survival and protection drive audience investment [14]. The narrative was also praised for its ability to function as both a conventional haunted-house thriller and a poignant allegory about companionship, vulnerability, and the unwavering devotion of animals [13], [19]. Ultimately, the critical response affirmed that *Good Boy* was not merely a novelty but a thoughtfully constructed and deeply affecting cinematic experience.

## Commercial Performance

*Good Boy* achieved remarkable commercial success, far exceeding the expectations for an independent horror film and demonstrating the viability of niche concepts when paired with effective marketing. Its financial performance indicates a significant return on investment and a strong connection with audiences. The film's wide theatrical release on October 3, 2025, marked the beginning of its box office run, opening in 1,650 theaters [16]. On its opening day alone, Friday, October 3, it grossed $975,250 [16]. The film's opening weekend saw it earn $2,343,694 domestically [15]. This strong start propelled its cumulative box office revenue, which reached $6,614,122 by November 6, 2025, after just 35 days in theaters [16]. By December 19, 2025, the film's domestic box office gross had settled at $6,624,062 [15].

The film's international performance contributed significantly to its overall earnings. As of December 19, 2025, its worldwide gross totaled $8,677,663 [15]. This figure breaks down into domestic earnings of $6,624,062 and international earnings of $2,053,601. The international breakdown shows earnings from Europe/Middle East/Africa ($229,827), Latin America ($1,016,780), and the Asia Pacific region ($787,422) [15]. This global reach highlights its appeal beyond the North American market. The film's profitability is starkly illustrated by the reported production budgets, which vary from $70,000 to $750,000 [3], [31]. Even with the higher end of the budget estimate, the film's worldwide gross of over $8 million represents a substantial profit. One source claimed the film fully recouped its budget of $2.3 million by early October 2025, shortly after its opening weekend, underscoring its rapid financial success [31].

Following its theatrical run, the film entered the digital marketplace through premium video-on-demand (PVOD) channels. It began streaming on platforms such as Prime Video, Apple TV, Fandango at Home, and YouTube starting on October 24, 2025 [3]. Digital sales and rentals provided another significant revenue stream, complementing the theatrical earnings. As of December 19, 2025, the film was not yet available on subscription-based streaming services like Shudder, although it was expected to arrive on the platform around January 2026, following the standard release window for IFC Films/Shudder co-distributed titles [4], [5]. The film's DVD and Blu-ray release was scheduled for December 2025, though the exact date was subject to change and market availability [2]. The combination of a successful wide theatrical release, strong international sales, and a robust PVOD strategy contributed to *Good Boy*'s impressive commercial trajectory, cementing its status as one of the most profitable indie films of the year.

## Thematic Analysis

Beyond its innovative narrative structure and successful execution as a horror film, *Good Boy* delves into a rich tapestry of profound themes, using the supernatural premise as a vehicle to explore the complexities of loyalty, mortality, perception, and the human-animal bond. The film's unique perspective allows it to examine these concepts from a fresh angle, challenging the audience to see the world through the eyes of an animal and consider experiences that are often overlooked or misunderstood by humans [33]. At its core, the film is a powerful meditation on unconditional canine devotion [19]. Indy's actions are driven solely by his instinctual desire to protect his owner, Todd, a sentiment that remains steadfast despite the confusion and fear he feels [13]. This unwavering loyalty forms the emotional backbone of the story, investing the audience in Indy's struggle and making the film's climactic moments of sacrifice and survival all the more poignant [17]. The narrative underscores the deep, soulmate-like connection between dogs and their owners, a theme that resonates strongly with pet owners and elevates the horror elements with genuine pathos [19].

A central and more complex theme is the film's nuanced exploration of mortality and anticipatory grief [24]. The supernatural hauntings are intricately woven with Todd's deteriorating health, creating an ambiguity that is central to the film's emotional power. Some analyses interpret the ghostly forces and shadowy figures not as external entities, but as a manifestation of Todd's terminal illness and the looming specter of death, perceived through the dog's limited but acute understanding [23], [24]. This interpretation suggests that the haunting is a metaphor for the process of dying itself—a creeping darkness that Todd is preparing Indy for, perhaps even leading him to the cabin to help the dog comprehend eventual loss [24]. The film leverages the well-known canine ability to smell death, grounding this supernatural reading in a tangible biological reality [13]. This dual-layered interpretation allows the film to function on both a literal horror level and a deeply symbolic one, inviting viewers to contemplate the ways in which animals mediate our experiences of life, death, and the afterlife [33].

Furthermore, *Good Boy* engages with contemporary social anxieties, subtly incorporating post-pandemic themes into its narrative [39]. The film's focus on isolation, the erosion of trust in institutions like medicine, and evolving definitions of family can be seen as reflections of broader societal shifts [39]. The remote, decaying cabin becomes a microcosm of a disconnected world, where the only reliable connection is the one between a man and his dog. The film also explores the incomprehension of human-scale horror from an animal's perspective [21]. The supernatural events are presented in a way that is accessible to the dog's sensory experience—auditory cues, strange smells, fleeting visual impressions—but remain frustratingly opaque to the human characters [33]. This creates a sense of unease for the audience, who are forced to rely on the dog's limited perception, mirroring the feeling of being unable to grasp the full scope of a loved one's internal struggle with illness. Through its blend of visceral horror and quiet, emotional storytelling, *Good Boy* transcends its genre roots to become a thought-provoking and deeply affecting exploration of love, loss, and the profound communication that exists between species.

## Awards and Recognition

Throughout its festival run and subsequent release, *Good Boy* garnered significant critical acclaim and special recognition, most notably for its canine star. The film's unique premise and exceptional execution captured the attention of festival juries and audiences alike, contributing to its reputation as a standout independent feature. The pinnacle of this recognition came at the South by Southwest (SXSW) Film Festival, where *Good Boy* premiered in the Midnighter section on March 8, 2025 [26], [47]. In a testament to the film's central performance, Indy, the Nova Scotia Duck Tolling Retriever, was awarded the inaugural 'Howl of Fame Award' for Best Canine Performance [7], [12]. This special award, created specifically for the 2025 festival, honored an "unforgettable method performance" and recognized the jury's citation of Indy's "uniquely authentic perspective," his "natural and chilling" responses to unseen phenomena, and his crucial role in transforming the film's domestic settings into realms of suspense and terror [46]. The award highlighted the film's achievement in creating a believable and emotionally resonant canine protagonist without resorting to anthropomorphism, a feat that was widely praised by critics throughout the festival circuit [14], [46].

The film's success was not limited to a single award. It received positive notices at several other prominent film festivals, further solidifying its status before its wider release. After its SXSW premiere, *Good Boy* screened at the Overlook Film Festival in May 2025 [7]. There, the film's popularity was so evident that its scheduled screening was expanded from one theater to three to accommodate the demand [45]. At the festival, it received an honorable mention for Scariest Feature Film, further cementing its credentials within the horror genre community [45]. The film also found a receptive audience at other international and niche festivals, including the Melbourne International Film Festival, Film at Lincoln Center, and the Calgary Underground Film Festival, confirming its broad appeal and artistic merit beyond its initial premieres [9]. These festival screenings, coupled with its strong critical reception, were instrumental in securing its distribution deal with IFC Films and Shudder, proving that the film had a compelling story to tell that resonated with discerning viewers [27], [45].

In summary, the accolades and festival success of *Good Boy* underscore its impact as a critically acclaimed and artistically significant work. The 'Howl of Fame Award' stands as a landmark recognition, celebrating not just the film's technical achievements but also the profound and unique performance that lies at its heart. The film's consistent positive reception across multiple festival platforms demonstrates its ability to connect with diverse audiences and critics, ultimately paving the way for its successful theatrical and commercial release. This collection of honors serves as a powerful validation of the director's vision and the extraordinary effort that went into bringing this unconventional story to the screen.

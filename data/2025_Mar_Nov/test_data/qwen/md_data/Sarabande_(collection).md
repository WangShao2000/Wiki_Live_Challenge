# Sarabande (disambiguation)

The term "Sarabande" refers to several distinct and unrelated organizations across different creative industries. This disambiguation page provides detailed information on each entity to clarify the various subjects that may be referred to by this name. The most direct interpretation of the user query, "Sarabande (collection)," points to a specific fashion collection; however, the name is also used by a prominent literary press, a London-based arts foundation, and a long-standing American record label specializing in film scores. Each of these entities operates independently with unique histories, missions, and areas of influence.

## Sarabande Books

Sarabande Books is an award-winning, internationally-distributed, nonprofit independent literary publishing house headquartered in Louisville, Kentucky [13], [19]. Founded in 1994 by Sarah Gorham and Jeffrey Skinner, the press is dedicated to creating "lasting editions that honor exceptional writing" [14], [18]. It specializes in contemporary poetry, short fiction, and essays, often championing innovative, hybrid, and experimental forms that may be marginalized by for-profit publishers [14], [16]. The press has built a catalog of nearly three hundred titles and is distributed nationally and internationally by Consortium Book Sales and Distribution (CBSD) [12], [13], [20]. Its editorial leadership includes Kristen Renee Miller as Editor-in-Chief and Danika Isdahl as Managing Editor and Designer, who succeeded founder Sarah Gorham [4], [17]. The press publishes between eight and twelve books annually and maintains a commitment to producing beautifully designed physical editions that remain in print [15], [20].

Sarabande Books' reputation is built upon its rigorous selection process and its support for emerging writers through prestigious annual competitions [21]. These flagship contests include the Kathryn A. Morton Prize in Poetry, the Mary McCarthy Prize in Short Fiction, and The Sarabande Prize in the Essay [11], [19]. Each prize offers a $2,000 advance and publication, and finalists are considered for publication regardless of winning [19], [34]. The judging panels for these contests feature prominent figures in the literary world; for example, Ocean Vuong judged the 2024 poetry prize and Aimee Bender judged the 2024 fiction prize [11]. The press also administers a chapbook prize and runs open submission periods during designated windows, requiring a $30 submission fee for contest entries [20], [21]. All of its contests adhere to the Community of Literary Magazines and Presses (CLMP) Contest Code of Ethics, ensuring transparency and ethical judging processes [19], [34].

The list of authors published by Sarabande Books is distinguished by numerous accolades from the literary establishment, including Pulitzer Prize winners, National Book Award recipients, Guggenheim Fellows, and National Book Critics Circle Award winners [13], [19], [20]. Notable works from its catalog demonstrate its commitment to diverse and formally innovative voices. For instance, Karisma Price's debut poetry collection, *I'm Always so Serious* (2023), received critical acclaim for its exploration of Black life in the United States and intergenerational trauma [37]. Similarly, Corey Van Landingham’s poetry collection *Reader, I*, published in April 2024, was praised for its engagement with themes of marriage and selfhood [38], [39]. Another critically lauded work is Lauren K. Watel’s *Book of Potions*, winner of the Kathryn A. Morton Prize, which was described by nine prominent poets as a masterpiece of sustained prose rhythm [36]. The press continues to be an active force in contemporary literature, with recent publications including David Lawrence Morse's *The Book of Disbelieving* (2023) and upcoming titles like Kiki Petrosino's memoir *Bright* (2022) [14], [15]. Beyond publishing, Sarabande Books serves as an educational resource through programs like the Sarabande Writing Labs and the Flo Gault Student Poetry Prize, supporting both emerging and established writers [12], [18].

## Sarabande Foundation

The Sarabande Foundation is a UK-based charitable organization founded in 2006 by the late fashion designer Lee Alexander McQueen [2], [23]. Established with the vision of creating a fearless, non-hierarchical environment for artistic collaboration, the foundation continues to operate following McQueen's death in 2011 [23]. It supports over 200 designers and artists through scholarships, studio spaces, and mentoring programs, explicitly distinguishing itself from commercial or editorial "collections" by focusing on nurturing individual creative talent [2], [23]. The foundation fosters interdisciplinary collaboration among artists working in fields such as sculpture, painting, photography, weaving, and digital art [1], [3]. In 2015, Trino Verkade, McQueen's first employee, founded another entity named the Sarabande Foundation in London, suggesting a continuation or evolution of McQueen's original vision, which is now celebrated as having created a model that other brands are beginning to replicate [1].

The foundation's headquarters and primary operational hub is located at 5 Carlos Place in Mayfair, London, where it operates studio spaces that have supported hundreds of artists [1]. These studios host exhibitions showcasing works by supported artists and alumni, covering a wide range of media including jewellery, photography, painting, and sculpture [1], [3]. The foundation actively engages with the public through curated events and residencies. A notable example is the "House of Bandits" residency held at Selfridges in London, which featured live studio spaces for rotating alumni artists, window installations recreating the studios of different creatives, and sales of limited-edition works by alumni and supporters like Thom Browne and Tim Burton [2], [23]. In October 2023, the foundation hosted a pop-up event named 'House of Bandits' during Frieze Week, featuring emerging indie jewellery designers whose work drew inspiration from history, mythology, and biology [23].

Beyond providing resources for practicing artists, the Sarabande Foundation also focuses on community-building and career development. It hosts events like "What Now?", a post-college guide for recent graduates offering one-to-one advice, which attracted over 800 attendees in London and was scheduled to take place in New York in May 2024 [24]. The foundation's community includes multidisciplinary artists such as NEWGEN award winner Arron Esh, who debuted at London Fashion Week, and Dean Hoy, a soft sculpture artist known for his "Bears Who Care" collaborations [22]. While some sources indicate the foundation is headquartered in Haggerston, London, with a secondary site named 'Sarabande High Road', its primary base of operations and public-facing activities are centered in Mayfair [1], [22]. Through its combination of practical support, public programming, and community engagement, the Sarabande Foundation serves as a living testament to Alexander McQueen's ethos of fostering visionary talent without hierarchical constraints.

## Varèse Sarabande

Varèse Sarabande is an American record label that specializes in motion picture and television soundtrack recordings [6], [28]. Formed in 1978, the label was created through the merger of two separate companies: Tom Null's Sarabande Records and Chris Kuchler's Varèse International Records [28], [29], [31]. The name itself is a composite, combining the surname of French-born composer Edgard Varèse with "sarabande," a slow Spanish dance form [28], [29]. Initially established as an avant-garde classical music label, the merged entity pivoted its focus to film scores starting in June 1978, releasing its first soundtrack LP that year [6], [30]. It became one of the world's largest producers of original motion picture score CDs during the 1980s and 1990s, reissuing rare or unavailable albums and releasing new works by artists no longer under contract [27], [29].

The label's historical significance lies in its crucial role as an archivist and curator of cinematic music history [10]. By making classic film scores accessible to enthusiasts and scholars, Varèse Sarabande preserved a vital part of cultural heritage. A prime example is its 2004 restoration and reissue of Elmer Bernstein's iconic 1960 score for *The Magnificent Seven*, a Silver Age masterpiece that had become commercially scarce [10]. Scholarly analysis confirms the label's importance, with academic texts on fantasy cinema citing its releases of scores by composers like Jerry Goldsmith (*The 7th Voyage of Sinbad*) and Tangerine Dream (*Legend*) as essential archival resources [41]. Since its acquisition by Concord Music in February 2018, Varèse Sarabande has operated as a division of Concord's Craft Recordings imprint, continuing its legacy with a modern, collector-focused strategy [6], [33].

Today, Varèse Sarabande's product offerings emphasize high-quality, deluxe, and limited-edition physical media, particularly vinyl records [6]. The label is known for producing special edition LPs, often pressed in exotic colored vinyl, accompanied by extensive liner notes written by noted journalists and artist essays [6]. For example, its mid-to-late 2024 release slate included limited-run colored vinyl editions of scores by Hans Zimmer, Danny Elfman, Michael Giacchino, and Don Davis, each with strict worldwide print limits and a price point around $40 per title [6]. This approach caters to collectors and dedicated fans, positioning the label as a premium purveyor of film music rather than a mass-market distributor. The label's catalog remains vast, encompassing thousands of soundtrack releases and continuing to issue new soundtracks for film and television [30], [33]. The iconic inkblot logo, created by blowing a straw on India ink, is a recognizable symbol of its long history in the industry [30].

## Sarabande (Alexander McQueen collection)

The "Sarabande" collection refers to the Spring/Summer 2007 ready-to-wear fashion collection by the late British designer Alexander McQueen [26]. Presented on a circular stage at the Cirque d’Hiver in Paris, the show was named after and inspired by the Baroque-era court dance of the same name, a slow and stately movement that contrasted with McQueen's typically frenetic energy [25], [26]. The collection was widely acclaimed by critics as one of McQueen’s most beautiful and romantic works, hailed as evidence of his status as a "romantic genius" and elevating his reputation to a peak where commercial compromise was unnecessary [26]. It served as a powerful commentary on the decay of beauty, achieved through deliberate, performative destruction of its central garment [26].

The aesthetic of the collection was Edwardian-inspired, featuring black-and-white ruffled gowns, tight hourglass-structured dresses, chiffon explosion skirts, and dusky pink silk corseted gowns adorned with spilling floral motifs [26]. However, the collection's true centerpiece was a final showpiece dress named 'Sarabande' [25]. Made from nude silk organza, this dress was meticulously embroidered with hundreds of real, frozen flowers [25]. As the model walked down the runway, the flowers began to thaw and disintegrate, transforming the garment into a "living, dying, transient thing" [25], [26]. This act of controlled decay embodied McQueen's recurring thematic preoccupations with transience, fragility, and the inevitable decline of beauty [25]. The collection was so impactful that it directly spurred demand for bespoke bridal wear from brides and contributed foundational pieces to the 2011 landmark exhibition "Alexander McQueen: Savage Beauty" at The Metropolitan Museum of Art [26].

The 'Sarabande' dress was accessioned and documented by The Metropolitan Museum of Art in 2011, cementing its status as a significant piece of cultural and artistic history [25]. Its inclusion in the museum's permanent archive alongside other iconic designs underscores its importance beyond mere haute couture. The collection as a whole solidified McQueen's position as a master of theatrical storytelling through fashion, where clothing becomes a vehicle for powerful narrative and emotion [26]. The 'Sarabande' collection stands as a testament to his ability to blend exquisite craftsmanship with profound, often unsettling, artistic statements, leaving a lasting legacy in the annals of fashion history [26].

## Other Notable Uses of the Name

While the primary interpretations of "Sarabande" relate to the entities discussed previously, the name has been used in other contexts, most notably within the realm of contemporary classical music. One prominent example is the 2017 album *Black Sarabande* by British composer and pianist Robert Haigh, released on the New York-based Unseen Worlds label [7]. This work consists of eleven tracks that blend piano melodies with electronics, thematically rooted in Haigh's childhood memories of a coal-mining village in South Yorkshire [7]. The album is characterized by its hushed, Satie- and Harold Budd–influenced minimalism, use of discordant piano tones, ghostly overtones, and immersive, near-silent passages [7]. Critical reception highlighted its atmospheric ambiguity and affective depth, situating it within the context of ambient and minimalist traditions and praising its evocation of imaginary rural landscapes [7]. Unlike the other entities, which are organizations or a single fashion collection, *Black Sarabande* is a standalone musical composition, representing another distinct use of the name in the arts.

Another significant musical reference is the 1975 solo studio album titled *Sarabande* by English keyboardist and composer Jon Lord, best known as a member of the rock band Deep Purple [8]. This album marked a departure from his rock-oriented work, featuring orchestral rock and progressive rock elements recorded with the Philharmonia Hungaria Orchestra conducted by Eberhard Schoener [8]. The project brought together a stellar lineup of guest musicians, including Andy Summers before he joined The Police, and showcased Lord's compositional talents outside of his rock context [8]. Reissued by Eagle Rock Entertainment, the album preserves its original dynamic range while enhancing audio clarity, demonstrating its continued relevance and appeal to listeners of symphonic and progressive rock [8]. Like Haigh's album, Lord's *Sarabande* is a singular artistic work rather than the name of a collection or organization.

Furthermore, the name "Sarabande" appears in other cultural products, though these are less prominent in the provided sources. An example is the poetry collection *Reader, I* by Corey Van Landingham, published by Sarabande Books in 2024 [4], [39]. While the book is published by the press, its title is a direct literary reference to Charlotte Brontë's *Jane Eyre* ("Reader, I married him"), indicating a thematic engagement with classic literature rather than a connection to the press's name [39]. Similarly, Marianne Chan's 2024 poetry collection, *Leaving Biddle City*, published by Sarabande Books, uses a fictionalized name for her hometown, again showing the press's role as a publisher rather than a creator of titles [39]. These examples illustrate that while "Sarabande" is a common name in the arts, it is often used as a component of a larger title or as the name of a specific work, rather than as a descriptor for a broader collection in the way the user's query implies. The most direct and unambiguous answer to the query remains Alexander McQueen's fashion collection.

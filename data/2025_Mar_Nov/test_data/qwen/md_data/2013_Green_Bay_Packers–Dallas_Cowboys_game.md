# 2013 Green Bay Packers–Dallas Cowboys Game

The December 15, 2013, National Football League (NFL) regular-season matchup between the Green Bay Packers and the Dallas Cowboys stands as one of the most dramatic and improbable games in modern league history [1], [6]. Played at AT&T Stadium in Arlington, Texas, the contest saw the Packers overcome a staggering 23-point deficit at halftime to defeat the Cowboys 37–36 [1], [3], [10]. This result was not only a testament to the Packers' resilience but also marked a historic moment for both franchises. For the Packers, it represented the largest comeback in their storied franchise history, tying a previous record set in a 1982 game against the Los Angeles Rams [1], [6]. Conversely, for the Cowboys, the loss was a catastrophic implosion, as it became the first instance in their entire 57-year history that they had ever lost a game after holding a lead of 20 points or more at the half; their record in such situations prior to this game was a perfect 46–0 [3]. The game was broadcast nationally on Fox, with Joe Buck and Troy Aikman providing commentary for the television audience [1]. The attendance for the Week 15 clash was 91,054 spectators, who witnessed an unforgettable reversal of fortune [1], [8]. The victory kept the Packers' slim playoff hopes alive during a critical stretch of the NFC North race, while the devastating loss dealt a severe blow to the Cowboys' aspirations in the NFC East [10], [14].

## Background and Context

The Week 15 encounter between the Green Bay Packers and the Dallas Cowboys on Sunday, December 15, 2013, held significant weight for both teams as they navigated a competitive and crucial phase of the 2013 NFL season [1]. The Packers entered the game with a challenging 6–6–1 record, placing them in a precarious position within the tightly contested NFC North division [1]. Their slim playoff hopes were on the line, and a victory against a fellow playoff contender like the Cowboys was essential to keep their postseason aspirations from slipping away [10], [14]. The Packers' path to contention would remain difficult, with upcoming games against the Pittsburgh Steelers and on the road against the Chicago Bears [10]. In contrast, the Dallas Cowboys came into the matchup with a respectable 7–6 record, positioning themselves as a formidable force in the NFC East [1]. As defending division champions, the Cowboys were actively trying to solidify their hold on the top spot in the division and secure home-field advantage throughout the playoffs [10]. The winner of this game stood to gain a substantial advantage in the standings, making the matchup a de facto "game of survival" for both franchises.

The stage for this pivotal contest was set at AT&T Stadium in Arlington, Texas, a venue known for its large crowds and electric atmosphere [1], [4]. The game was scheduled for a 3:25 p.m. CST kickoff and was televised nationally on the Fox network, with the broadcasting team of Joe Buck, Troy Aikman, and Pam Oliver calling the action for millions of viewers across the country [1]. The official assigned to oversee the game was referee Walt Coleman [1]. Prior to the game, the Cowboys were favored to win by four points, reflecting the perception that their strong start to the season would give them an edge over the struggling Packers [1]. However, the pre-game narrative failed to account for the seismic shift in momentum that would define the afternoon. The Cowboys' offensive prowess, particularly in the first half, was expected to pose a significant threat to the Packers' defense, while the Packers' offense was seen as vulnerable without its star quarterback, Aaron Rodgers, who was sidelined with an injury [12], [14]. The absence of Rodgers meant that the Packers' starting quarterback duties fell to Matt Flynn, a move that would ultimately become central to the story of this historic comeback [12], [14]. The stage was therefore set for a clash between two determined teams, where the outcome would have profound implications for their respective seasons. The game was not just a regular-season affair; it was a high-stakes battle that would test the mettle of both organizations and deliver one of the most memorable finishes of the year [11].

## First Half Dominance by the Cowboys

The first half of the December 15, 2013, game between the Green Bay Packers and the Dallas Cowboys was a masterclass in offensive execution by the host team, resulting in one of the most dominant performances in franchise history and a crushing 26–3 lead at the end of the second quarter [1], [6], [14]. From the opening drive, the Cowboys' offense, led by quarterback Tony Romo, established a punishing rhythm that overwhelmed the Packers' defense [2]. Dallas began the scoring process early in the first quarter, taking a 3–0 lead when kicker Dan Bailey connected on a 47-yard field goal at the 10:56 mark [2], [8]. The Packers responded quickly with a field goal of their own, Mason Crosby booting a 57-yarder to tie the score at 3–3 with 7:40 remaining in the quarter [2], [8]. However, the tide turned decisively in favor of the Cowboys, who scored 23 unanswered points to seize control of the game. Dallas extended their lead with another Bailey field goal, this one from 23 yards out, followed by a spectacular 25-yard touchdown reception by tight end Jason Witten from Tony Romo, giving the Cowboys a 13–3 advantage at the end of the first quarter [2], [9]. The Cowboys' dominance continued unabated into the second quarter. Bailey added three more field goals, from distances of 43, 50, and another 50 yards, showcasing his leg strength and contributing significantly to the burgeoning lead [2], [9]. The final score of the first half belonged entirely to the Cowboys, culminating in a one-yard touchdown run by running back DeMarco Murray, which capped off a seven-play, 75-yard drive and sent the Dallas Cowboys to the locker room with a commanding 26–3 lead [2], [9].

The statistical disparity in the first half was stark, underscoring the extent of the Cowboys' offensive explosion. Dallas amassed a franchise-record 332 total yards in the first two quarters alone, while Green Bay managed only 33 yards [6]. The Cowboys' average drive started from their own 29.7-yard line and lasted a mere 2:32, highlighting their efficiency and ability to quickly move the ball down the field [4]. By halftime, the Cowboys had already recorded 27 first downs and forced the Packers into multiple three-and-outs [4]. Their success was built on a potent combination of a dynamic passing attack and a powerful ground game. Tony Romo was surgical through the air, completing passes with precision, while DeMarco Murray consistently broke tackles and found daylight, finishing the first half with 93 rushing yards on just 11 carries, averaging an impressive 7.4 yards per attempt [10]. The Packers' defense, which had been a strength for much of the season, appeared completely unprepared for the onslaught. They were unable to contain either the run or the pass, allowing the Cowboys to dictate the pace and tempo of the game from the outset [11]. The sheer scale of the deficit facing the Packers at halftime—23 points—was unprecedented and seemed insurmountable, casting a long shadow over the remainder of the contest [3], [10]. Head coach Mike McCarthy and the Packers' coaching staff had little time to regroup and devise a strategy to counter the overwhelming force they faced, as the second half loomed with a mountain to climb [14].

## The Historic Second-Half Comeback

The second half of the 2013 Green Bay Packers–Dallas Cowboys game was a breathtaking display of football, defined by an unprecedented turnaround that saw the Packers erase a 23-point deficit to win 37–36 [1], [3]. After entering the locker room trailing 26–3, the Packers emerged with a newfound resolve and a new leader under center, Matt Flynn, who stepped in for the injured Aaron Rodgers [12], [14]. The catalyst for the comeback was a 60-yard run by running back Eddie Lacy, which sparked the Packers' offensive surge and ignited the dormant Lambeau Field faithful watching on television [1]. With renewed energy, the Packers' offense took the field and began methodically chipping away at the Dallas lead. The defining characteristic of the second-half rally was the Packers' ability to score on five consecutive possessions, a sequence that transformed the game's momentum and shattered the Cowboys' aura of invincibility [6], [12]. The comeback was not a single play but a sustained period of dominance that showcased the resilience and talent of the Packers' roster. Each touchdown drive was crucial, slowly but surely dismantling the Cowboys' defense and erasing the memory of the disastrous first half.

The scoring summary of the second half perfectly illustrates the magnitude of the comeback. Green Bay opened the third quarter with a touchdown drive of their own, beginning with a 13-yard touchdown pass from Matt Flynn to Jordy Nelson, cutting the Dallas lead to 26–10 [2], [8]. Although the Cowboys added a field goal from 50 yards out to extend their lead to 29–10, the Packers answered immediately with another touchdown, this time a 3-yard pass from Flynn to Andrew Quarless, bringing the score to 29–17 [2], [9]. The Cowboys briefly regained some composure with a 5-yard touchdown catch by Dez Bryant, putting them up 36–17 late in the fourth quarter, but it was too little, too late [2], [9]. The Packers then unleashed a furious final push. James Starks caught an 11-yard touchdown pass from Flynn, followed by a 3-yard touchdown reception from James Jones, tying the game at 36–36 with minutes remaining [2], [9]. The decisive play came with 1:31 left on the clock, when Eddie Lacy powered in from one yard out for a touchdown, giving the Packers their first lead of the second half [2], [12]. Despite a failed two-point conversion attempt, the Packers retained a one-point lead [2], [9]. The Cowboys got one last chance, but a desperate heave from Tony Romo was intercepted by Tramon Williams, sealing the incredible victory for Green Bay [1], [10]. One source noted that this interception was initially ruled an incomplete pass but was overturned on video review, adding a layer of officiating drama to the final moments of the game [11]. This remarkable comeback, where the Packers scored 34 unanswered points, remains a cornerstone of Packers history and a cautionary tale for all sports teams about never counting anyone out until the final whistle [1], [7].

## Individual Performances and Key Plays

The outcome of the December 15, 2013, game was dictated by the extraordinary performances of several key players whose actions on the field directly influenced the dramatic turn of events. For the Green Bay Packers, the entire team contributed to the historic comeback, but the efforts of quarterback Matt Flynn and running back Eddie Lacy were paramount. Starting in place of the injured Aaron Rodgers, Matt Flynn engineered one of the most efficient second-half passing attacks in recent NFL history [12], [14]. On 26-of-39 passing, Flynn threw for an astonishing 299 yards and four touchdowns, orchestrating five consecutive touchdown drives that fueled the Packers' rally [1], [12]. His touchdown passes were spread across the offense, finding five different receivers: Jordy Nelson (twice), Andrew Quarless, James Starks, and James Jones, demonstrating a balanced and effective attack [1], [2]. On the ground, Eddie Lacy was the engine of the Packers' offense, rushing for 141 yards on 21-22 carries and scoring a crucial one-yard touchdown with 1:31 remaining to put the Packers ahead for good [1], [10], [12]. His performance was instrumental in keeping the Packers' offense moving and helped him reach 1,000 yards for the season [10]. Defensively, the victory was sealed by cornerback Tramon Williams, who intercepted a desperation pass from Tony Romo with less than two minutes left, securing the win and cementing his place in Packers lore [1], [10].

For the Dallas Cowboys, the game was a tale of two halves, with their initial dominance collapsing in the face of a ferocious second-half assault. Quarterback Tony Romo was brilliant in the first half, throwing for 358 yards and two touchdowns, but his performance in the fourth quarter was the primary cause of the devastating loss [1], [10], [12]. He threw for a total of 358 yards and two touchdowns in the game, but two critical interceptions thrown in the final minutes directly enabled the Packers' comeback [1], [3], [10]. These turnovers occurred on the Cowboys' final two drives, nullifying a stated plan to run the ball and force Green Bay into timeouts, thereby dooming their chances of closing out the game [3]. Wide receiver Dez Bryant provided a bright spot for Dallas, recording 11 receptions for 153 yards and a 5-yard touchdown catch late in the fourth quarter that gave the Cowboys a brief lead [1], [2], [11]. Running back DeMarco Murray was also a key contributor in the first half, rushing for 134 yards and a touchdown on 18 carries, but received only seven attempts in the second half despite the large lead [10], [12]. Kicker Dan Bailey was reliable, kicking five field goals in the game, including two from 50 yards out, but his success could not compensate for the offensive breakdowns that ultimately cost the Cowboys the game [2], [11]. The contrast between these individual performances perfectly encapsulates the game's narrative: the Packers' collective heroics versus the Cowboys' singular failure to execute when it mattered most.

## Aftermath and Legacy

The aftermath of the 37–36 loss for the Dallas Cowboys was one of profound disappointment and disbelief, with team leadership offering candid assessments of the team's catastrophic second-half performance. Owner Jerry Jones described the defeat as "one of the hardest losses that I’ve experienced," expressing his anguish over squandering a 23-point lead and witnessing a dominant first-half showing evaporate [11]. Head coach Jason Garrett provided a simple yet damning explanation, stating, "We played very well in the first half... and then we didn’t play well in the second half. It’s just as simple as that" [11]. Cornerback Brandon Carr was even more direct, labeling the team's collapse a "complete debacle" [11]. The loss dropped the Cowboys to a 7–7 record, leaving them one game behind the Philadelphia Eagles in the NFC East with two games remaining on the schedule [10], [11]. Despite the poor result, owner Jones publicly reaffirmed his support for head coach Jason Garrett, stating unequivocally that he would remain in his position regardless of future outcomes [11]. This immediate post-game fallout underscored the shock value of the loss and the difficulty of absorbing such a dramatic reversal of fortune. The game served as a painful reminder of the fragility of leads in professional football and the importance of sustained effort throughout all four quarters.

For the Green Bay Packers, the victory was a lifeline that kept their 2013 season alive [14]. The team improved to a 7–7–1 record, preserving their slim playoff hopes during a critical stretch of the NFC North race [8], [10]. The win was particularly sweet as it came against a division rival and featured a historic comeback that would be remembered for years to come. The game solidified the legacy of Matt Flynn, who delivered a career-defining performance in relief of Aaron Rodgers, and elevated Eddie Lacy to stardom with his explosive rushing day [10], [12]. The victory provided a crucial morale boost for a team fighting to stay relevant in a tough conference. The game itself has since been immortalized in Packers history as one of their greatest comebacks, often cited in discussions of the franchise's most memorable victories [1], [6]. It stands as a testament to the unpredictability of the NFL and the power of perseverance. The intense media coverage and widespread discussion surrounding the game ensured its lasting legacy, making it a reference point for debates on the greatest comebacks and collapses in sports history. The image of Tramon Williams intercepting Tony Romo to seal the win is etched in the memories of fans on both sides, symbolizing the dramatic pendulum swing that characterized this unforgettable contest.

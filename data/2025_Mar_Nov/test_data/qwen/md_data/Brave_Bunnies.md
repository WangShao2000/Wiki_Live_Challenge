# Brave Bunnies

"Brave Bunnies" is a Ukrainian animated preschool television series that follows the adventures of two bunny siblings, Bop and Boo, as they travel the world with their family aboard the "Bunny Bus" [2], [8]. Created by Olga Cherepanova, the series is designed for children aged two to five and aims to teach social-emotional learning through playful storytelling [3], [4]. The show promotes themes of diversity, acceptance, curiosity, creativity, and courage [10], [11]. It is a 2D-animated series comprising 104 seven-minute episodes across two seasons [3], [18]. Its development history is uniquely intertwined with geopolitical events, particularly the full-scale Russian invasion of Ukraine in February 2022, which led to a significant restructuring of its production and ownership, with Canadian media company WildBrain acquiring majority control to ensure the franchise's continuity [3], [31]. The series premiered globally on December 5, 2020, and has since been broadcast in over 80 countries worldwide [3], [19].

## Premise and Characters

The central premise of "Brave Bunnies" revolves around a family of curious and courageous bunnies who embark on a "Big Journey" to explore the world and discover new friends across diverse biomes such as jungles, savannahs, forests, plains, and mountains [6], [7]. The primary vehicles for this exploration are the "Bunny Bus," a mobile home for the rabbit family, and the "Bunny Bike," a smaller mode of transport used for more intimate adventures [6], [17]. The narrative structure of each episode is consistent, focusing on the siblings Bop and Boo meeting animal children who live in the various environments they visit [7]. These encounters often involve playing games and engaging in activities that foster friendship and celebrate differences [2], [10]. The overarching goal of the series is to model positive behaviors and provide young viewers with real-life strategies for communicating across differences without fear or stereotypes, thereby promoting values such as respect for global diversity, empathy, and problem-solving [11], [30]. The show is explicitly educational, aiming to teach preschoolers about acceptance of diversity, communication with peers who are different, and essential social interaction skills through its episodic format [4]. This educational mandate is reinforced by the involvement of child psychologists in the script development process to ensure the content effectively fosters empathy, acceptance, and respect for diversity [6].

The main cast of characters forms the heart of the series. The titular protagonists are Bop and Boo, brother and sister bunnies who lead the explorations [6], [12]. They embody the series' core value of "bunny bravery," demonstrating curiosity, creativity, openness, responsibility, and an eagerness to learn and experience new things [11], [22]. Their parents, Ma Bunny and Pa Bunny, accompany them on their journeys, providing guidance and support [19]. The family also includes four younger siblings known as the Bunny Babies, who add to the dynamic and familial feel of the show [6], [7]. Beyond the immediate family, the bunnies encounter a wide array of animal friends throughout their travels. These include a tiger, kangaroo, bear, fox, and llama, among others, representing the diverse wildlife and cultures they discover [7]. The series also features recurring non-bunny characters such as Ricky Racoon and Franny Fox, who appear in the book adaptations and help reinforce the themes of friendship and cooperation [24]. Each character is designed to contribute to the show's mission of promoting inclusivity and positive social interactions. The visual style of the series is bright and kid-friendly, complementing its cheerful and adventurous tone [19]. The combination of social skill-building, educational content, comedy, catchy songs, and a vibrant aesthetic creates an engaging and accessible experience for its target preschool audience [19].

## Development and Production History

The creation of "Brave Bunnies" originated from the creative vision of Olga Cherepanova, a Ukrainian creative producer and author who conceived the original story [6], [13]. She co-founded the Ukrainian studio Glowberry Creative Production, which was established in 2009 as a niche publishing house before evolving into a dynamic international creative and production company focused on developing children's animated content [5], [9]. Glowberry became part of FILM.UA Group in 2016 [5], [17]. The series was developed at Glowberry under the leadership of Olga Cherepanova, who served as the Creative Director and series creator [3], [15]. The production team assembled for the series reflected a deliberate strategy to create a globally appealing property from its inception. The creative team included UK-based director Tim Fehrenbach, Canadian head writer John van Bruggen, and Ukrainian art director Anna Sarvira [6], [13], [20]. This multinational collaboration was instrumental in shaping the show's international appeal and facilitating its co-production and distribution deals. The series received official support from the Ukrainian State Film Agency and the Ministry of Culture of Ukraine, highlighting its significance within the national creative industry [13], [20].

The initial production phase of "Brave Bunnies" was a co-production between the Ukrainian studio Glowberry and the Spanish animation studio Ánima Kitchent [2], [10], [15]. Globally, the series was distributed by the multi-award-winning UK-based studio Aardman Animations, a partnership brokered by the content agency Big Picture Licensing (BPL) [6], [10], [12]. The series was pitched at MIPJunior 2020 and attracted the highest number of viewers in the Screening Library, signaling strong international interest from the outset [6]. Season 1 consisted of 52 episodes, each seven minutes long [3], [15]. The production process for Season 1 was marked by adaptability; voice recording sessions were handled by Fitzrovia Post in London and were adapted for remote work using Covid-safe technology under the direction of Keiran Brown, showcasing the industry's resilience during the pandemic [2].

The most significant turning point in the franchise's history occurred in February 2022 with the full-scale Russian invasion of Ukraine [3]. The conflict caused the production of "Brave Bunnies" to be suspended, placing the future of the intellectual property in jeopardy [3]. In response to this disruption, a pivotal corporate restructuring took place. In December 2022, Canadian media giant WildBrain acquired a majority stake in the "Brave Bunnies" IP through a three-way partnership facilitated by Big Picture Licensing [3], [11], [31]. Under this agreement, WildBrain became the majority stakeholder, while Glowberry and Ánima retained minority stakes [7], [23]. This acquisition allowed WildBrain to assume global co-production, distribution, licensing, and merchandising leadership for all future seasons, effectively stepping in to secure the franchise's continuity [31]. The production of Season 2 resumed with WildBrain's Vancouver animation studio joining Glowberry and Ánima as a co-producer [7], [15]. Season 2, also consisting of 52 seven-minute episodes, was delivered beginning in June 2023 [7], [15], [22]. This strategic intervention by WildBrain not only stabilized the project but also ensured its continued growth on the global stage, transforming the ownership and management structure of the beloved franchise [11].

## Broadcast and Distribution

Since its debut, "Brave Bunnies" has achieved significant international success, securing broadcast rights in over 80 countries worldwide [3], [18]. The series began its journey with a premiere on the PLUSPLUS TV channel in Ukraine on December 5, 2020 [3], [6]. Following its domestic launch, it quickly expanded its global footprint, premiering in the United Kingdom on Milkshake! (Channel 5) on February 6, 2021 [2], [4]. This UK premiere was notably successful, reaching a total audience of 4.4 million and ranking #1 in its commercial channel time slots [17]. Other early international premieres occurred in Germany on Super RTL in March 2021, Israel on HOP! in April 2021, and Australia on ABC in September 2021 [3], [6]. The series has maintained a steady expansion into new territories, with broadcasts launching in Norway, Sweden, Finland, and Latvia in 2021 [19]. By October 2021, the series had already secured broadcast in a dozen countries, including South Korea and Canada [19].

The distribution network for "Brave Bunnies" is extensive and includes both linear television and digital streaming platforms across multiple continents. Key broadcasters have acquired the series for their pre-school programming blocks, including Nick Jr. in the UK, Family Jr. in Canada, Super RTL in Germany, Yle in Finland, and HOP! in Israel [3]. A major milestone in its distribution was the acquisition by HBO Max for Latin America (LATAM), where it premiered in mid-2022 [1], [16]. Subsequently, WarnerMedia secured a licensing deal for the series to air in 20 territories across Southeast Asia, Australia, and New Zealand, premiering on the Cartoonito block on Cartoon Network in Southeast Asia and on HBO GO in Taiwan and Hong Kong [16]. The series continued its European expansion in 2024, launching in Norway, Germany, the UK, and Sweden [14]. In Poland, Season 2 is set to launch on TeleTOON+ [18], [21]. As of late 2025, the series remains widely available on various platforms. It can be streamed ad-free on Sunflower TV's YouTube channel for Ukrainian refugees [3], and is available on general SVOD services like Hoopla in the United States and Canada, Paramount+ in the United Kingdom, and Foxtel Now in Australia [8]. However, there appears to be a discrepancy in the availability of Season 2; while its first episode aired on November 30, 2023, it remained unavailable for streaming or rental on any service as of that date, indicating potential logistical or contractual challenges in its rollout [8].

## Multimedia Expansion

The "Brave Bunnies" franchise has successfully expanded beyond its original television series into a robust multimedia ecosystem, encompassing a book series and a line of licensed consumer products. This diversification strategy has helped to build brand recognition and extend engagement with its preschool audience across multiple platforms. The book series adaptation began with a collection launched in Italy in Q4 2021, published by DeA Planeta Libri, a subsidiary of Grupo Planeta and DeAgostini [13], [32]. This initial Italian release included sticker books titled *Amici Incredibili* (Incredible Friends) and *In Giro per il Mondo* (Around the World), alongside hardcover titles such as *Eroi Controvento* (Brave Heroes), *Strisce da Esploratore* (Hidden Treasure), and *Pulizia Cosmica* (A Great Mess) [13], [32]. The books were distributed nationally through 800 bookstores, online platforms, and over 300 mass-market outlets [13], [32]. The franchise's literary presence continued to grow with the acquisition of publishing rights by Ladybird, an imprint of Penguin Random House, in the United Kingdom [17]. Ladybird released its first two books, *Brave Bunnies Spring to the Rescue* and *Brave Bunnies Make a Friend*, in March 2022 [17]. In Australia, Five Mile Publishing holds the publishing rights, and in the UK, the first book in the series, *Brave Bunnies in the Jungle*, was authored by Olga Cherepanova and illustrated by Anna Sarvira, with development input from child psychologist Natalia Pashko, further emphasizing the franchise's educational focus [5], [7]. The books feature the same core characters, including Bop, Boo, Ma Bunny, Ricky Racoon, and Franny Fox, and continue to promote social-emotional learning themes like bravery, playfulness, and friendship [24].

The expansion into consumer products is centered around a strategic partnership with Spin Master, who serves as the global master toy licensee for the "Brave Bunnies" intellectual property [15], [28]. Spin Master launched the first range of licensed toys in spring 2022 and followed up with a significant product launch in Germany and Italy in the third quarter of 2023 [1], [15]. The toy line includes a variety of products such as figures, plush toys, playsets, games, and puzzles, all designed to align with the series' spirit of exploration and adventure [1], [33]. A key aspect of this product launch was its innovative and environmentally conscious packaging. The packaging is plastic-free and designed for dual use, featuring reusable cardboard punch-out props and coloring sheets that integrate directly into the play experience [1], [33]. This design choice reflects modern sustainability trends and reinforces the brand's positive values. The products were made available at major retailers in both countries, including Toys Center, Prenatal, and IBS in Italy, and Smyths, Mueller, and MyToys in Germany [1], [33]. The timing of this launch in mid-2023 was particularly significant, occurring during the ongoing war in Ukraine [1]. Olga Cherepanova publicly expressed that the launch provided "assurance that together we can bring back a prosperous time for all," framing it as a symbol of hope and resilience [1], [33]. James Martin, Senior Vice President of Marketing at Spin Master, confirmed that the company's management of the "Brave Bunnies" toy line remains active and continues to be a key part of its Entertainment content pipeline, as cited in multiple 2024 financial results presentations [25], [27], [28], [29].

## Business Ecosystem and Ownership

The business ecosystem surrounding "Brave Bunnies" is characterized by a complex co-production and rights management structure that evolved significantly following the Russian invasion of Ukraine. Initially, the intellectual property (IP) was owned and developed by Glowberry, the Ukrainian studio founded by creator Olga Cherepanova [5], [17]. Production for Season 1 was a co-production between Glowberry and the Spanish studio Ánima Kitchent [2], [15]. Global distribution and licensing for Season 1 were managed by the UK-based studio Aardman Animations, a move facilitated by the content agency Big Picture Licensing (BPL) [6], [10]. This initial setup positioned the franchise for immediate international distribution upon its release. However, the full-scale Russian invasion in February 2022 forced the suspension of production and triggered a fundamental shift in the franchise's corporate ownership and operational control [3], [31].

In December 2022, a pivotal transaction saw Canadian media conglomerate WildBrain acquire a majority stake in the "Brave Bunnies" IP [3], [31]. This acquisition was formalized through a three-way partnership between WildBrain, Glowberry, and Ánima, again facilitated by Big Picture Licensing [11], [23]. As part of this agreement, WildBrain assumed the role of majority stakeholder, taking over global co-production, distribution, licensing, and merchandising responsibilities for Season 2 onwards [3], [31]. The production of Season 2, which was delivered beginning in June 2023, was co-produced by WildBrain’s Vancouver animation studio alongside Glowberry and Ánima, the original producers of Season 1 [7], [15], [22]. While WildBrain now leads the global management of the franchise, the original creators and partners retain significant interests and territorial rights. Specifically, Glowberry retains distribution and licensing rights within Ukraine, and Ánima retains these rights in Spain [11], [23]. This territorial rights model allows the original stakeholders to maintain a presence and control in their respective home markets. WildBrain CPLG, the licensing arm of WildBrain, manages global distribution and consumer products, ensuring a cohesive global strategy [7], [21].

The commercialization of the franchise is managed through a network of specialized partners. The global master toy license is held by Spin Master, who has been actively managing the "Brave Bunnies" toy line as part of its Entertainment content pipeline [15], [27]. Spin Master's continued reporting on the franchise in its 2024 financial results confirms its ongoing relevance and commercial activity [25], [28]. For publishing, rights are handled by different partners in various regions: KidsMe serves as the exclusive distributor and licensing agent in Italy, Ladybird (an imprint of Penguin Random House) in the UK, and Five Mile Publishing in Australia [7], [17], [32]. This decentralized yet coordinated approach to rights management allows the franchise to be effectively localized and marketed in key territories while being overseen by a single global entity, WildBrain, for strategic direction and large-scale operations. This structure balances the need for global consistency with the importance of local market expertise.

## Reception and Significance

"Brave Bunnies" has been generally well-received for its positive messaging and educational content tailored to a preschool audience. Parental feedback highlights that the series effectively models desirable social behaviors, such as sharing toys, helping friends, and apologizing when necessary [30]. The show is noted for its gentle handling of emotional moments, such as disappointment or fear, resolving them quickly and constructively [30]. It delivers a clear moral at the end of each episode, reinforcing themes like the value of friendship or the joy of discovery [30]. Critics and reviewers have praised its focus on fostering empathy, acceptance, and respect for diversity, with some noting the intentional involvement of child psychologists in the script development process to achieve these goals [6]. The series is categorized as an entertaining and educational 2D animated program that successfully combines social skill-building with comedy, catchy songs, and a bright visual style to engage its young viewers [4], [19]. The show's emphasis on themes like curiosity, creativity, teamwork, and kindness aligns with contemporary expectations for high-quality preschool programming [21], [30]. Some parental reviews suggest that while the resolution of conflicts is effective, the show may occasionally oversimplify emotional challenges, though this is a common characteristic of the genre [30].

Beyond its critical reception, "Brave Bunnies" holds profound symbolic significance, particularly due to its origins in Ukraine and the circumstances surrounding its production. The franchise was born out of the Ukrainian creative industry and represents a significant achievement for the sector, described by its creator as a "huge breakthrough for the Ukrainian animation world" [10]. This national pride was amplified by the events of the full-scale Russian invasion in February 2022. The subsequent acquisition of the majority IP by WildBrain was framed not just as a commercial decision but as an act of corporate solidarity that ensured the survival of a culturally important Ukrainian asset [11], [31]. The launch of the Spin Master toy line in Germany and Italy in mid-2023, timed with the ongoing war, was imbued with powerful symbolism. Olga Cherepanova publicly stated that the launch provided "assurance that together we can bring back a prosperous time for all," positioning the franchise as a beacon of hope, resilience, and collective optimism [1], [33]. This narrative transforms the product launch from a purely commercial event into a culturally resonant one, connecting the brand directly to the experiences and aspirations of Ukrainians during a time of crisis. The franchise thus serves as a case study in how creative industries can demonstrate resilience and how brands can navigate and leverage geopolitical events to connect with audiences on a deeper, more human level.

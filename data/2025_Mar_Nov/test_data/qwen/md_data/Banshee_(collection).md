# Banshee (Collection)

The term "Banshee (collection)" refers to multiple distinct creative projects, primarily within the fields of literature and visual arts, which share a common mythological inspiration drawn from Irish folklore. A "banshee," in traditional belief, is a supernatural female spirit known for wailing to herald the impending death of a family member. This potent symbol of prophecy, grief, and feminine power has been adopted as a conceptual framework by various publishers, editors, and curators. The most prominent and extensively documented of these is a forthcoming literary anthology titled *Banshee: Mythological Irish Women Retold*. This work is conceived as a major contemporary literary event, edited by journalist and editor Ailbhe Malone and featuring contributions from ten of Ireland's most celebrated living authors [8], [9]. The anthology aims to undertake a "radical reclamation" of ancient Irish myths, shifting the narrative focus from male-centric legends to center the previously marginalized women at the heart of the stories—figures historically relegated to roles as mothers, warriors, or witches [10], [15]. This report will provide a comprehensive analysis of this primary literary anthology, detailing its contributors, publication history, and thematic ambitions. It will also situate this work within the broader landscape of other projects bearing the name "Banshee," including the independent Irish literary journal *Banshee*, founded in 2014 by Laura Cassidy, Claire Hennessy, and Eimear Ryan, which has published numerous award-nominated writers since its inception in 2015 [5], [11]. By examining these distinct but thematically related collections, this report offers a definitive overview of the multifaceted cultural projects encapsulated under the name "Banshee."

## Literary Anthology: *Banshee: Mythological Irish Women Retold*

The literary anthology *Banshee: Mythological Irish Women Retold* stands as the most significant and well-documented collection associated with the term. Conceived as a landmark feminist literary project, the anthology seeks to rewrite the narrative of ancient Irish mythology by placing women at the forefront of the stories [9]. Edited by Ailbhe Malone, an Irish journalist and editor recognized for her curatorial work, the collection brings together ten original short stories from some of Ireland's most prominent contemporary authors [3], [9]. The project originated from a perceived gap in the publishing market for modern retellings of Irish myths, particularly those centered on women's experiences and perspectives [7]. The resulting volume is positioned not merely as a series of retellings but as a deliberate act of reclamation, aiming to restore agency and complexity to female figures who have been historically sidelined or stereotyped in the original lore [10], [13]. The themes explored within the anthology are rich and varied, encompassing treacherous landscapes, salt-crashing seas, generational curses, and mystical islands, all set against a backdrop of desire, danger, and defiance [9], [13]. The overarching goal is to celebrate womanhood and honor the enduring cultural identity shaped by these ancient tales, reframing them for a modern audience [8].

The contributor list for the anthology represents a broad and distinguished cross-section of contemporary Irish literature, lending considerable weight and credibility to the project. The consistent reporting across multiple sources provides a high degree of confidence in the final roster of authors. These writers bring diverse voices and styles to the task of reimagining the myths, ensuring a multifaceted exploration of the theme. Each author contributes a unique perspective, collectively creating a powerful chorus that amplifies the anthology's core message. The selection includes established novelists, prize-winning short story writers, and acclaimed poets, making the collection a significant event in the Irish literary calendar. The inclusion of Megan Nolan, Naoise Dolan, and Jess Kidd, among others, signals a commitment to both literary quality and contemporary relevance, bridging the gap between ancient tradition and modern sensibility. The thematic content of the anthology is explicitly framed around the idea of reclaiming female narratives. The promotional copy describes the book as focusing on "unruly mothers, rule-breaking queens, defiant mermaids and women outrunning their destiny" [3], [9], [13]. This language suggests a departure from traditional portrayals of female figures in Irish myth, who are often depicted as passive objects of fate or vessels for male heroism. Instead, the anthology promises to explore these characters with a new lens of agency, rebellion, and emotional depth. The settings described—treacherous landscapes, salt-crashing seas, generational curses, and mystical islands—provide a dramatic and atmospheric backdrop for these retellings, connecting the personal struggles of the female protagonists to the elemental forces and ancient magic of the Irish landscape [3], [9], [13]. The project's ambition is clear: to create a corrective to existing mythology publishing by centering the stories of women, thereby offering a fresh and empowering perspective on a foundational part of Irish cultural heritage [3]. This approach positions the anthology as more than just a collection of stories; it is presented as a cultural intervention aimed at reshaping how these myths are understood and remembered in the 21st century.

## Publication and Reception

The publication history of *Banshee: Mythological Irish Women Retold* is marked by a competitive acquisition process and association with a major publishing house, underscoring its anticipated significance in the literary world. The project was acquired by Christina Demosthenous, then Publisher at Renegade Books, for UK and Commonwealth rights through a three-way auction [7], [8]. This competitive bidding indicates strong early interest from industry professionals in the anthology's premise and its lineup of contributors. Following Demosthenous's departure from Renegade Books, the publication of the anthology was overseen by editor Abigail Scruby at John Murray, an imprint of Hachette Book Group [7], [8]. However, more specific and consistent sourcing identifies Little, Brown Book Group, an imprint of Hachette UK, as the ultimate publisher [12], [15]. This clarity is crucial for accurately documenting the book's release. The cover design was created by Kildare-based artist Aoife Cawley, whose specialization in folklore and mythology lends a fitting artistic vision to the project [3], [7], [8].

The anthology is scheduled for a wide-release format, appearing in hardback, eBook, and audio editions on February 26, 2026 [3], [7], [8], [14]. This multi-format release strategy ensures accessibility for a broad readership. Detailed physical specifications are also available: the hardback edition contains 320 pages and measures 240 x 156 mm [12], [15], [16]. Pricing information varies by region, with the book listed at £20 in the UK and €24.99 in Ireland [14], [16]. The ISBN for the hardback edition is consistently reported as 9781408749357 across multiple sources, providing a stable identifier for bibliographic purposes [12], [15], [16], [17]. The publication date is a critical piece of information, with every source confirming it for Tuesday, February 26, 2026 [3], [7], [8], [14]. To build anticipation, a formal book launch event was scheduled for the evening of Wednesday, February 25, 2026, at Waterstones (Hodges Figgis) in Dublin, giving readers a chance to engage with the editors and potentially the contributors [13].

The pre-publication reception for the anthology has been overwhelmingly positive, characterized by enthusiastic endorsements that highlight its thematic power and literary merit. One of the most prominent pieces of feedback comes from bestselling author Patricia Scanlan, who described the collection as "A mystical, magical, empowering modern re-imagining of the Irish goddesses" and added, "I LOVED these superb stories" [14]. This testimonial, featured on the official sales page, serves as a strong indicator of the book's intended effect and appeal. While post-publication reviews would be necessary for a complete picture, this early praise reflects a widespread view that the anthology successfully achieves its goal of celebrating womanhood through a compelling and respectful re-engagement with ancient stories [8]. The project's framing as a "celebration of womanhood and an homage to ancient Irish stories that continue to shape cultural identity" resonates with contemporary literary trends focused on diversity, inclusivity, and the re-examination of historical narratives [8]. The combination of a prestigious publisher, a star-studded author lineup, and a powerful feminist thesis positions *Banshee: Mythological Irish Women Retold* as a culturally significant release poised to make a substantial impact upon its publication in late February 2026.

## Thematic Framework and Critical Concept

The core concept animating the anthology *Banshee: Mythological Irish Women Retold* is a deliberate and explicit feminist reclamation of ancient Irish mythology. This is not a simple exercise in nostalgia or a straightforward retelling of old tales; rather, it is presented as a "radical reclamation" designed to fundamentally alter the reader's perception of these foundational stories [13], [14]. The critical premise of the project is that traditional Irish myths, while rich in narrative, have historically centered male figures—the warriors, kings, and heroes—and relegated women to peripheral, often archetypal roles [9]. These roles are typically limited to being mothers, warriors, or witches, serving as either catalysts for male action or obstacles to be overcome [9], [10], [15]. The anthology's mission is to move these women from the shadows of the legend into the bright light of the narrative stage, granting them the complex interior lives, desires, and agency they were denied in the original texts. This act of reclamation is framed as a corrective measure, addressing a recognized omission in the broader field of mythology publishing regarding Irish stories, especially those that center women [3].

This thematic ambition is articulated through a specific and evocative vocabulary. The promotional material repeatedly emphasizes the need to celebrate "unruly mothers, rule-breaking queens, and women outrunning their destiny" [3], [9], [10], [13], [16], [17]. This language directly challenges the passive or purely functional depictions of women in many myths. An "unruly mother" suggests a figure who defies societal expectations, perhaps prioritizing her own needs or choices over maternal duty. A "rule-breaking queen" implies a leader who operates outside conventional power structures, driven by her own moral code or ambition. A woman "outrunning her destiny" signifies a rejection of fatalistic plots where female characters are pawns of fate, instead choosing to actively shape their own futures. By foregrounding these concepts, the anthology promises a version of the myths where female characters are not merely subjects of the plot but its architects. This shift is further contextualized by the setting of the stories, which are placed within powerful natural environments—"treacherous landscapes, salt-crashing seas, generational curses, and mystical islands"—that mirror the internal turmoil and transformative journeys of the protagonists [3], [9], [10], [13].

The thematic pillars of the anthology are desire, danger, and defiance [9], [13], [14], [17]. These elements are presented as the essential components of a new kind of mythological storytelling. **Desire** moves beyond the simple plot mechanics of seduction or romance found in older tales; it becomes a powerful force driving the characters' decisions, representing everything from a thirst for knowledge and love to a burning ambition for power. **Danger** is not just the external threat of monsters or battles, but also the internal and social perils faced by women navigating patriarchal societies, even in a mythical past. **Defiance** is the active response to this danger and constraint, manifesting as rebellion against gods, kings, or societal norms. Together, these themes form a cohesive framework for exploring the complexities of womanhood within a mythological context. The project's creators, led by editor Ailbhe Malone, position this collection as a celebration of womanhood itself, honoring the resilience and strength embodied in these ancient stories while simultaneously giving them a modern, empowering voice [8]. The result is an ambitious literary endeavor that seeks to honor the past not by preserving its limitations, but by illuminating the hidden potential of its female characters and re-centering them as the true agents of myth.

## Comparative Analysis with Other "Banshee" Collections

While the literary anthology *Banshee: Mythological Irish Women Retold* is the most prominent collection bearing the name, it exists within a broader ecosystem of creative works that use "Banshee" as a thematic touchstone. The most significant of these is the independent Irish literary journal also named *Banshee*. Founded in 2014 by Laura Cassidy, Claire Hennessy, and Eimear Ryan, the journal has been a fixture in the Irish literary scene since its first issue launched in September 2015 [5], [11]. Unlike the finite, single-volume nature of the anthology, the journal is an ongoing periodical, publishing twice yearly in spring and autumn [5], [6]. Its scope is broader, featuring a curated mix of short stories, flash fiction, personal essays, and interviews, providing a platform for both emerging and established Irish and international writers [6], [11]. The journal's editorial stance is defined by a commitment to literary quality and accessibility, consciously avoiding academic esoterica and clichéd themes such as substance-abusing young men or farming poems in favor of forward-looking and underrepresented narratives [6]. A notable characteristic of the journal is its editorial team, composed entirely of women in their late twenties and early thirties, which has resulted in a disproportionate number of submissions from female writers—an emergent outcome rather than a stated policy [6]. This gender dynamic creates a distinct and welcoming space for diverse voices.

The relationship between the journal and its parent company, Banshee Press, is symbiotic. In 2019, Banshee Press expanded its operations to include full-length book publishing, beginning with Lucy Sweeney Byrne’s debut novel *Paris Syndrome*, which had originally been featured in *Banshee* #1 [11]. Since then, the press has grown its list to include a range of authors such as Dylan Brennan and Mary Morrissy [5]. A key policy of the book publishing arm is that it only accepts proposals from individuals who have already contributed to the journal, creating a direct pipeline from the periodical to the books it publishes [5]. The journal itself is free to submit to, accepting short stories, poetry, creative non-fiction, and flash fiction from writers worldwide without charge, and it employs a rotating team of guest editors to ensure a diversity of curatorial perspectives [11]. This structure makes the journal a vital institution for nurturing new talent and fostering a vibrant literary community in Ireland.

In contrast to the journal's long-form, biannual format, another distinct "Banshee" collection is the art exhibition *Banshees Remix*. Presented by Asya Geisberg Gallery in New York City from June 27 to August 8, 2025, this exhibition is a curated event focused specifically on sculpture [1]. It features eighteen women sculptors and is described as a "resurrected and expanded" second iteration, combining eight original artists with ten newly added ones [1]. The artistic approach in *Banshees Remix* engages deeply with the mythological banshee as a symbol of protest and refusal. The exhibition frames the banshee as "a re-embodied cry," drawing on Virginia Moore's poem 'The Banshee' (February 1942) to anchor its reinterpretation [1]. The works on display utilize a wide array of materials—including concrete, neon, steel, ceramic, wood, glass, leather, and medicinal plants—and incorporate technologies like 3D printing, photography, and sound, demonstrating a contemporary engagement with the medium [1]. The exhibition culminated in a performance by Julia Elsas's all-female Sonic Mud band on July 10, 2025, which used hand-built ceramic instruments and incorporated crying and wailing to celebrate a "powerful cycle of rebirth" [1]. This ephemeral, event-based nature makes the art exhibition fundamentally different from the two literary collections, which are permanent, text-based publications. While all three projects draw from the same mythological wellspring, their formats, goals, and methods of engagement are distinct, highlighting the versatility of the banshee as a cultural symbol. The literary anthology is a scholarly and narrative re-examination, the literary journal is a continuous forum for contemporary writing, and the art exhibition is a visceral, performative reinterpretation.

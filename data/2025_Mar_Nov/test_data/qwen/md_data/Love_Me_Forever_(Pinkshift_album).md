# Love Me Forever (Pinkshift album)

*Love Me Forever* is the debut studio album by American punk band Pinkshift, released on October 21, 2022, through Hopeless Records [[1]), [2], [4]]. The album established the band as a vital force in the contemporary rock scene, characterized by its fusion of pop-punk, grunge, and post-hardcore influences, emotionally charged vocals, and raw lyrical exploration of personal trauma, mental health, and societal pressures [[1]), [9]]. Formed at Johns Hopkins University in Baltimore in 2018, the band consists of Ashrita Kumar (vocals), Paul Vallejo (guitar), and Myron Houngbedji (drums) [5], [16]. Their emergence during the COVID-19 pandemic shaped an unconventional, digitally-native songwriting process that resulted in a cohesive yet complex debut [14]. Produced by Will Yip at Studio 4 in Conshohocken, Pennsylvania, the album runs for a total of 37 minutes and 53 seconds across 12 tracks [[1]), [7]]. It received widespread critical acclaim, being named one of the best albums of 2022 by publications including *Alternative Press*, *Kerrang!*, and *Paste*, and was recognized for its role in challenging the demographic homogeneity of the alternative rock genre [[1]), [27]]. While most reviews were highly positive, some noted a perceived drop-off in energy in the latter half of the album [24].

## Background and Creation

The creative genesis of *Love Me Forever* is deeply intertwined with the global events of the COVID-19 pandemic and the unique academic and artistic backgrounds of its creators [14], [18]. Pinkshift formed in 2018 while its founding members were students at Johns Hopkins University in Baltimore [16], [45]. The original trio consisted of vocalist Ashrita Kumar, guitarist Paul Vallejo, and drummer Myron Houngbedji [16]. The band's formation began when Vallejo heard Kumar singing a cappella and invited them to record a cover of Britney Spears' "...Baby One More Time," which led to the recruitment of Houngbedji after the duo overheard him practicing the drum part to My Chemical Romance's "Helena" [45]. All three members come from immigrant families—Kumar is Indian American, Vallejo is Peruvian American, and Houngbedji is from Benin—and were raised with strong work ethics emphasizing financial independence [17]. They met while preparing a Britney Spears cover in 2018, showcasing a blend of pop culture and punk sensibilities from the outset [43]. Before forming Pinkshift, Kumar had been working as a lab technician and studying neuroscience, Vallejo was pursuing a master's in chemical engineering, and Houngbedji was also a neuroscience student [17]. This background in hard sciences would later be credited for instilling a hands-on, detail-oriented approach to running their own careers despite signing with a major label like Hopeless Records [17].

The album itself was largely conceived and developed during the pandemic-induced lockdowns that began in March 2020 [14]. This period created an "unintentional creative bubble" for the band, who had been separated and then reunited in late 2020 in Baltimore to write new material [14], [16]. A key aspect of their creation process was an unconventional, digital-first methodology. Due to circumstances like the lack of access to a real drum kit initially, the band adopted a rigid songwriting process where all ideas were recorded directly into a computer and fleshed out digitally before being brought into a live setting [14]. This approach allowed them to develop their sound without external pressures and contributed to the album's cohesive feel [14]. This period also saw the beginning of their first recordings, with 'On Thin Ice' recorded on March 13, 2020, coinciding with the onset of lockdowns [14]. Their early work gained viral traction months later via platforms like Reddit with the song 'I'm Gonna Tell My Therapist On You', which amassed 42,000 YouTube views and 26,000 monthly Spotify listeners by 2020 [14], [43]. This success laid the groundwork for their major-label debut.

Lyrically, the album draws heavily from the band members' lived experiences, grounding its themes in authenticity. The project began as a creative outlet for Kumar during a bout of depression [17]. The resulting music explores visceral anxieties related to adulthood, generational nihilism, and alienation in a world shaped by social media and the pandemic [12], [18]. The band's ethnically diverse lineup—featuring a non-binary lead singer in Kumar—is central to their identity and mission [15], [27]. Pinkshift has been positioned as part of a broader movement within rock music aimed at increasing inclusivity and representation, specifically advocating for people of color (POC) in a genre historically dominated by white artists [42], [51], [55]. This sociocultural framing elevates the album beyond its musical qualities, positioning it as a statement on identity and community within subcultures like punk and goth [55]. The band's commitment to these values is evident in their lyrical content, which addresses systemic racism in America and racial inequity in alternative rock [11]. The development of the album was thus not just a musical endeavor but a foundational act of self-definition and cultural commentary for the band [14].

## Recording and Production

The production of *Love Me Forever* was handled by the acclaimed producer Will Yip, whose work is known for capturing both the raw energy and intricate details of a band's sound [[1]), [13]]. Yip has previously worked with bands such as Turnstile, Mannequin Pussy, The Wonder Years, and Tigers Jaw, making him a fitting choice for Pinkshift's ambitious debut [13], [42], [58]. The album was recorded in March 2022 at Studio 4 Recording in Conshohocken, Pennsylvania, a facility where Yip frequently works [[1]), [7]]. The recording sessions were remarkably efficient, lasting only three weeks, followed by a single day off before the band embarked on a two-month tour with PUP [7]. This swift and focused recording process speaks to the band's preparedness and the clarity of their vision for the album. The production quality is consistently praised for its razor-sharp punk aesthetic, effectively highlighting the technical proficiency of the musicians while preserving the emotional intensity of their performances [13].

The instrumentation on the album showcases a dynamic interplay between the three band members, each contributing distinct elements that define the album's sound. Myron Houngbedji's drumming is described as muscular, breakneck, and punchy, with articulate kick and snare hits and colorful fills reminiscent of artists like Travis Barker [5], [12]. His playing provides the aggressive backbone for many of the album's more intense tracks [12]. Paul Vallejo's guitar work is equally versatile, incorporating steady, building riffs, crunchy bass tones, and emo-inflected textures [5]. His style reflects deep influences from bands like My Chemical Romance and Pierce the Veil, contributing to the album's hybrid sound [44]. However, it is Ashrita Kumar's vocal performance that stands out as a defining feature of the album. Critics have lauded Kumar's ability to navigate a wide emotional spectrum, contrasting "simmering yet soft moments of femininity" with "disinhibited aggression" [31]. This dynamic range creates an exhilarating emotional arc throughout the album, with performances described as "staggering" and a powerful showcase of their "seemingly limitless talent" [9], [31]. The collaboration between the three members resulted in a technically assertive and unyielding performance that forms the core of the album's impact [9].

The songwriting credits for the album are shared among the three band members: Ashrita Kumar, Paul Vallejo, and Myron Houngbedji [[1]), [23]]. This collaborative effort is a cornerstone of their creative process, which was intentionally non-formulaic [44]. Kumar often developed lyrics from personal poems or built upon instrumental ideas brought to the group by Vallejo and Hounggedji [44]. There was no definitive formula for creating the songs, allowing for a fluid and responsive approach where each finished track required unanimous investment and emotional resonance from the entire band [44]. A notable exception to this collaborative process is the track 'In a Breath'. This song was written solely by Ashrita Kumar in 2018, long before the band Pinkshift existed [[1])]. Originally intended as a private piece to be listened to alone in her car on difficult days, the song was about feeling "cut off from yourself, being trapped in your body" [41]. Despite its pre-band origin, it was adapted and included on *Love Me Forever* due to its thematic centrality and its function as an emotional anchor within the album's otherwise aggressive sonic palette [[1]), [41]]. Its inclusion demonstrates the band's willingness to integrate deeply personal material into their collective artistic vision, adding a layer of vulnerability and sincerity to the final product [41].

## Music and Style

*Love Me Forever* is a genre-defying work that synthesizes a wide array of musical influences into a cohesive and energetic whole [44]. The album is primarily classified under the broad umbrella of Rock, with specific stylistic tags including Pop Punk, Post-Punk, Post-Hardcore, Grunge, and Emo [6], [21], [50]. The band deliberately eschews rigid subgenre conventions, instead drawing distinct elements from a diverse list of touchstones [44]. Cited influences include Nirvana and Soundgarden for heavier, grungier elements; Arctic Monkeys for indie-rock flair; Turnstile and Microwave for hardcore and post-hardcore structures; No Doubt for pop-punk energy; and My Chemical Romance and Pierce the Veil for emo-inflected guitar work [[1]), [5], [44], [45]]. This intentional genre fluidity allows the album to shift seamlessly between blistering mosh-pit anthems and more introspective, melodic passages [9]. The band's sound fuses '90s grunge, 2000s pop-punk, riot grrl, and post-hardcore, creating a rich tapestry of sonic textures [43]. This eclectic mix of styles contributes to the album's dynamic nature, preventing it from becoming monotonous and showcasing the individual and collective tastes of its members [44].

Musically, the album is characterized by high-energy instrumentation, jagged guitars, melodic elements, expressive vocals, and hard-hitting drums [[1]), [9]]. The band pushes punk boundaries through innovative techniques, such as the use of metric modulation in 'nothing (in my head)' and the employment of the double harmonic minor scale in 'BURN THE WITCH', which introduces a rare South Asian musical touch to the punk genre [31]. This rhythmic complexity, combined with instrumental interlocking between guitar and drums, demonstrates a sophisticated compositional approach [31]. The album's structure is carefully crafted, featuring a deliberate pivot from aggressive tracks to more subdued moments [41]. The standout track 'in a breath' serves as this pivotal moment, functioning as a piano-led ballad that foregrounds Kumar's vocal power without compromising the album's overall intensity [9], [41]. This strategic placement of a vulnerable ballad amidst a barrage of punk anthems highlights a mature artistic vision that balances emotional extremes [45]. The album also features a variety of tempos and arrangements, from the metal-infused 'GET OUT' to the No Doubt-inspired 'the kids aren’t alright' and the '90s-tinged anthem 'BURN THE WITCH' [45].

The album's runtime is 37 minutes and 53 seconds, spread across 12 tracks [[1])]. The track durations vary, with several songs clocking in under three minutes, though standout tracks like 'Dreamer' extend to four minutes and seventeen seconds, providing a sense of resolution [6], [19]. Some listener critiques have noted the frequent use of short songs as a minor flaw, with 'let me drown' being cited as "criminally short" [25]. However, others describe the album as cohesive and fun, praising the strong synergy between the lyrics and instrumentals [25]. The final track, 'Dreamer', is noted as a closer centered around trauma, providing a thematic conclusion to the album's journey [45]. The album's sonic landscape is further enriched by specific musical choices, such as the unhinged laughter that concludes the track 'cherry (we’re all gonna die)', reflecting generational trauma and dissonant coping mechanisms [46]. This attention to detail and structural sophistication underscores the album's status as a formidable debut that successfully expands upon the band's earlier pop-punk style [[1]), [9]].

## Lyrical Content and Themes

The lyrical content of *Love Me Forever* is a raw and honest exploration of the psychological and social anxieties of modern youth, drawing heavily from the personal experiences of the band members [17], [18]. The album delves into a wide range of themes, including abusive relationships, trauma, alienation, anxiety, depression, and insecurity [[1]), [11]]. These topics are addressed with a vulnerability that resonates deeply, framed by a perspective that rejects cut-throat academic culture in favor of artistic fulfillment [18]. Lead vocalist Ashrita Kumar's lyrics center on authenticity, processing personal betrayal, and resisting subjugation and oppression [15], [18]. The album captures the visceral anxieties of facing adulthood in a world increasingly mediated by social media and shaped by the aftermath of the COVID-19 pandemic [18]. This focus on mental health and emotional candor is a central pillar of the band's identity, reinforced by their origins with the viral single 'i'm gonna tell my therapist on you' [53].

Specific tracks serve as potent case studies for the album's thematic depth. The opening track, 'i’m not crying you’re crying', embodies a "raw and unfiltered emotion" and a panicked denial in response to the question "are you okay?" [10]. The track 'cherry (we’re all gonna die)' uses ironic affirmations, repeating the mantra "Everything’s going to be okay / it’ll never be okay" and ending with anxious laughter to reflect dissonant coping mechanisms [11], [46]. 'Trust Fall' expresses sneering animosity toward a mirror of the past, while 'BURN THE WITCH' reinterprets historical witch prosecutions as a metaphor for societal groupthink targeting women [11], [13]. The song was inspired by Kumar's personal experience of being ousted from a college dance team, lending it a layer of direct, autobiographical pain [15]. 'the kids aren’t alright' is described by Kumar as a "war cry" addressing the collective crisis exposure of their generation, including police brutality, natural disasters, mass shootings, and the pandemic, and the psychological toll of performing normalcy amid perpetual emergencies [46].

Beyond personal struggles, the album also engages with broader sociopolitical issues. It touches upon intergenerational trauma and systemic racism in America, placing Pinkshift's music within a wider conversation about racial inequity in the alternative rock scene [11]. As a band led by a non-binary person of color, their presence and message challenge the historical whiteness of the punk and alternative scenes [42], [55]. This advocacy for POC representation is a core part of their mission, positioning them as part of a movement to make the rock scene more inclusive [51], [57]. The album's lyrical scope is vast, moving from intimate confessions to broader social commentary, all held together by a consistent thread of defiance and vulnerability. Even the title track, 'Love Me Forever', conveys a sense of vulnerability through its themes of anger, alienation, and power dynamics, with an ironic and insistent refrain that destabilizes the listener [22], [23]. Ultimately, the lyrics of *Love Me Forever* provide a powerful and relatable voice for a generation navigating a complex and often hostile world [12].

## Release and Physical Formats

*Love Me Forever* was officially released on Friday, October 21, 2022, marking Pinkshift's debut full-length album and their introduction to a wider audience through Hopeless Records [10], [13]. The album served as the foundation for the band's subsequent North American tour, which began the same day with a hometown show in Baltimore [58]. To build anticipation for the release, the band strategically dropped a series of promotional singles in sequence throughout the summer of 2022 [3]. The promotion began with the lead single 'nothing (in my head)', released on June 1, 2022, which was also their first song for the label [3], [10]. Following this, the second single, 'i’m not crying, you’re crying', was released on July 13, 2022; it serves as the album's opening track [3], [10]. The third single, 'GET OUT', was released on August 24, 2022, followed by the fourth single, 'in a breath', on September 21, 2022 [3]. This rollout culminated with the full album release just over a month later. The band's prior work includes the 2021 EP *Saccharine* and several standalone singles released before their major-label deal, such as 'On Thin Ice' and 'Rainwalk' [3], [43]. The album's release preceded later projects like the 2023 EP *Suraksha* and their sophomore album *Earthkeeper* [27], [30].

The physical release of *Love Me Forever* was comprehensive, catering to fans of different formats. The album was made available on both Compact Disc (CD) and Long Play Vinyl (LP) [6], [50]. The CD was assigned the catalog number HR6658-2, while the LP was given the catalog number HR6658-1 [6], [50]. Multiple barcodes were listed across various retail and distribution channels, including 790692665815 and 790692665822, indicating potential variations in scanning systems or retailers [6], [20], [36]. The manufacturing location for the physical copies was confirmed to be the Czech Republic [21], [36]. The vinyl release was particularly noteworthy for its extensive range of limited-edition variants, designed to appeal to collectors and dedicated fans. These exclusive pressings included:

It is important to note a documented error present in the physical release. The liner notes for the LP incorrectly order tracks 9 and 10, listing 'Love Me Forever' (track 10) before 'BURN THE WITCH' (track 9) [20], [60]. The correct track order, which aligns with the digital versions and the band's setlists, is maintained on the album itself [41], [60]. This discrepancy highlights the complexities of physical media production but does not alter the listening experience for most consumers. The robust physical release strategy, encompassing standard and multiple limited-edition vinyl formats alongside a CD version, indicates a well-planned marketing campaign designed to maximize reach and engagement with the fan base.

## Critical Reception and Legacy

Upon its release, *Love Me Forever* was met with widespread critical acclaim, establishing Pinkshift as a significant new voice in the modern rock landscape [[1]), [58]]. The album was lauded for its infectious energy, skilled musicianship, and emotional honesty [9], [27]. Several prominent publications named it one of the best albums of 2022. *Alternative Press* and *Kerrang!* both recognized it as a top release of the year, with *Kerrang!* describing it as "sharp, skilful [*sic*], and bristling with infectious energy" [[1]), [27]]. Similarly, *BrooklynVegan* and *Paste* included it in their lists of the best punk albums of 2022, while Stereogum hailed it as a "fierce, furious, vulnerable debut" from an "essential act in modern pop-punk" [[1]), [58]]. The band's recognition extended to mainstream music press, with a feature in the October 2022 issue of *NME Australia* discussing the album [52]. Furthermore, *NME*'s 'The NME 100' list for 2022 highlighted the band as pivotal to making the scene more inclusive, alongside acts like Meet Me @ The Altar and Magnolia Park [57]. The album's critical standing was further solidified by its appearance on year-end lists and community polls, such as a #23 spot on a curated 2022 list and its inclusion in the r/indieheads 2022 End-of-Year voting [40], [62].

While the reception was overwhelmingly positive, some analyses offered a more nuanced perspective, pointing to areas where the album could be considered less consistent. One detailed review scored the first half of the album highly, praising its aggressive, rage-fueled sound and catchy choruses, but noted a "noticeable drop-off in quality and energy" in the latter half [24]. Tracks like 'in a breath', 'Cinderella', 'let me drown', and 'Dreamer' received lower scores compared to the earlier songs, suggesting a potential dip in momentum towards the end of the record [24]. Another critique mentioned the frequent use of short songs as a minor flaw, though it was balanced by an overall assessment of the album as "cohesive" and "fun" [25]. These critiques do not detract from the album's overall success but provide a more complete picture of its strengths and weaknesses, avoiding an overly simplistic narrative of universal praise. The album's legacy is therefore that of a near-flawless, albeit not perfect, debut that perfectly captured the zeitgeist of Gen Z's engagement with rock music [56].

The enduring legacy of *Love Me Forever* lies in its role as a defining statement of purpose for Pinkshift and its contribution to a more inclusive and diverse rock scene. The album was a cornerstone of the post-Warped Tour pop-punk revival, characterized by Gen Z artists who embrace nostalgia without defensive posturing [56]. By centering the voice of a non-binary person of color and explicitly addressing themes of mental health, trauma, and systemic racism, the band challenged the traditional demographics of the genre [11], [42]. Hopeless Records founder Louis Posen identified Pinkshift as one of the artists leading the label—and the scene—into a new chapter, noting their freedom to explore any sound, appearance, or message [57]. The album's success paved the way for their sophomore album, *Earthkeeper* (2024), which was created using a different, more jam-based approach developed after years of touring [28]. In summary, *Love Me Forever* is not merely a successful debut; it is a culturally significant album that articulated the feelings and frustrations of a new generation of rock fans while simultaneously expanding the boundaries of what the genre can look and sound like [51], [57].

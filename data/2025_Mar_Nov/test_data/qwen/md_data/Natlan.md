# Natlan

## Lore and Governance

Natlan is a fictional nation within the video game Genshin Impact, developed by HoYoverse [1], [2]. It is not a real-world location but a canonical region situated within the continent of Teyvat, serving as the sixth major nation to be introduced in the game's world [23], [32]. The region's narrative framework is established through its Archon Quest Chapter V, which forms the central storyline for its introduction [1]. Natlan's governance is headed by Mavuika, who holds the title of Pyro Archon and is revered as the God of War [1]. This leadership structure positions Natlan under the dominion of the Pyro element, a thematic pillar that permeates its culture, environment, and gameplay mechanics [4], [15]. The nation's history is marked by a significant struggle against the Abyss, a primordial force seeking to corrupt and consume worlds; the people of Natlan have historically united under the leadership of their Pyro Archon to defend their land against this existential threat [1].

The political and social fabric of Natlan is organized around a unique tribal system comprising six distinct tribes [1], [30]. These tribes represent different facets of Natlanese society and culture, each with its own traditions, governance, and connection to the natural world. While all six tribes are canonical elements of Natlan's lore, three were made explorable at the region's initial launch in Version 5.0: the Children of Echoes, the Scions of the Canopy, and the People of the Springs [7], [15]. Further exploration of the nation, including access to the remaining tribes, was unlocked in subsequent updates, specifically in Version 5.5, 'Day of the Flame's Return', released on March 26, 2025 [6], [30]. This phased release allowed players to gradually uncover the full scope of Natlan's societal structure. To facilitate deeper immersion and progression within the region, the game introduces a Tribe Reputation System [6], [32]. By completing the "Tribal Chronicles," a series of story quests tied to each respective tribe, players can increase their standing and unlock exclusive rewards, including high-level ascension materials [32].

A defining characteristic of Natlan's historical relationship with its environment is its coexistence with Saurians, dragon-like beings that are considered descendants of ancient dragons native to the region [1], [7]. These creatures are not merely background fauna but integral components of Natlanese life, capable of traversing diverse terrains including land, water, and even lava [4]. This harmonious companionship between humans and evolved dragons is depicted as the result of long-term evolution, fostering a deep bond of trust and mutual respect [7]. The relationship is so profound that Saurians serve as both loyal companions and essential tools for traversal and combat, a concept that is directly implemented as a core gameplay mechanic known as "Saurian Indwelling" [7], [42]. Furthermore, Natlan's lore details a period during which leaving the nation was restricted, requiring approval mediated by the Wayob, which are described as obsidian totem deities [1], [6]. This restriction was imposed due to a perceived survival necessity tied to the ongoing conflict with the Abyss. However, after the successful purging of the Abyss from the Night Kingdom, a sacred site within Natlan, this restrictive measure was lifted, symbolizing a newfound freedom and security for the nation [1]. This event marks a pivotal moment in Natlan's history, shifting its narrative focus from one of defense to one of renewed exploration and expression of its vibrant culture.

## Culture and Society

The culture of Natlan is a rich tapestry woven from explicit inspirations drawn from Pre-Columbian American, Sub-Saharan African, and Oceania traditions [2], [9], [25]. This intentional cross-cultural synthesis is evident in nearly every facet of the region, from its mythology and language to its music and artistry. The developers at HoYoverse have publicly confirmed these inspirations, aiming to create a world that reflects a diverse range of global heritage [9]. The official soundtrack for Natlan serves as a prime example of this integration, featuring Swahili lyrics, classical Spanish guitar, castanet rhythms, and chants performed by the Vienna University Philharmonic chorus that emulate Mesoamerican and Polynesian styles [2]. This auditory landscape immerses players in a world that feels simultaneously ancient and globally resonant.

The linguistic and mythological foundations of Natlan are deeply rooted in specific real-world belief systems. Mesoamerican influences are pervasive, with direct references to Aztec and Maya cosmology appearing throughout the region's lore. For instance, the name Xiuhcoatl, an Aztec fire serpent, and Kukulkan, the feathered serpent deity of the Maya, are prominent concepts within Natlan's narrative [3], [29]. Similarly, names like Tonatiuh (Aztec sun god), Xilonen (a maize goddess), Citlali (meaning 'star'), and Xbalanque (one of the Maya Hero Twins) are used for characters and places, grounding the world in tangible mythological archetypes [3], [19]. The name Chichen Uctokah, used for a significant location, is a clear allusion to the famous Mesoamerican archaeological site of Chichén Itzá [3]. West African, and particularly Yoruba, influences are also strongly present. The supreme deity Ọlọrun is referenced, and characters named Iansan (a warrior goddess of storms and rebirth) and Ekwensu (a trickster figure from Igbo culture) draw from these rich pantheons [13], [19], [25]. Oceanic and Polynesian motifs further enrich the cultural palette. The name Mavuika is derived from Mahuika, the Māori fire deity, while Mualani is named after a Hawaiian High Chiefess [16], [19]. Broader Polynesian themes of navigation and stewardship are echoed in the design of Saurian companions and certain in-game events [30].

This commitment to specific cultural borrowing extends to the visual arts and material culture of Natlan. In-character designs incorporate historically grounded elements, such as Citlali’s purple-tipped hair reflecting documented use of indigo dye in 16th-century Mesoamerica as seen in the Florentine Codex [29]. Varesa’s black pleated skirt is designed to resemble Zulu 'isidwaba' cowhide skirts, and her leg warmers modernize traditional 'amashoba' bands [29]. Kinich’s attire draws inspiration from Mesoamerican 'ichcahuīpīlli' armor and 'tlahuiztli' status suits [29]. Even architectural and artistic motifs are carefully selected; Ajaw’s tail curls mirror the 'xicalcoliuhqui' stepped fret motif common in Mesoamerican art, and Xilonen’s design resembles a jaguar man mural found at Cacaxtla, Mexico [29]. This level of detail demonstrates a deliberate effort to create an authentic aesthetic, moving beyond generic "inspiration" to a more direct engagement with source materials. However, despite these efforts in lore and design, this very specificity would later become a focal point for criticism, as the visual execution of its characters would fail to match the stated cultural depth, creating a significant disconnect with the player base [12], [26].

## Geography and Environment

Natlan is characterized by a dramatic and volatile landscape defined by severe volcanic activity, geothermal features, and a harsh, sun-scorched climate [4], [7]. Located in the western portion of the continent of Teyvat, the nation lies directly west of Sumeru's expansive Great Red Sand desert, also known as the Desert of Hadramaveth [1], [4], [7]. This positioning situates Natlan as a land of contrasts, bordering a vast arid expanse yet possessing its own unique ecosystems forged by intense geological forces. The region's geography is dominated by massive volcanic formations, including the Great Volcano of Tollan, which serves as the spiritual and geographical heart of the nation and the home of the Sacred City of Tollan, an ancient city built by the Dragonborn atop a sacred mountain roused by subterranean magma [31], [36]. These volcanic environments are not just scenic backdrops but functional spaces within the game, filled with hazards like liquid Phlogiston and offering unique traversal opportunities [35], [36].

The environment of Natlan is also punctuated by areas of relative fertility and abundance, most notably the seaside hot springs inhabited by the People of the Springs tribe [1], [15]. These geothermal oases provide a stark contrast to the surrounding scorched earth, yielding exceptionally sweet and juicy fruits that are a staple of Natlanese cuisine and economy [31]. Other notable geographical features include the Tequemecan Valley, associated with the Children of Echoes tribe and their tunneling Saurians; Coatepec Mountain, home to the cliff-dwelling Scions of the Canopy; and Atocpan, the fertile lands of the Collective of Plenty tribe [31], [34], [35]. The region's terrain is varied enough to support multiple distinct ecological niches, allowing for a diverse array of flora and fauna adapted to its extreme conditions. This diversity is reflected in the placement of Statues of the Seven across the map, which are strategically located in areas corresponding to the territories of the various tribes, from the valleys and mountains to the coastal cliffs and ancient ruins [35].

The environment itself is a living, breathing entity in Natlan, intrinsically linked to its people and their way of life. The region is imbued with a powerful elemental energy known as Phlogiston, a high-temperature energy native to Natlan that flows through the land and powers many of its unique phenomena [42]. Phlogiston is not only a resource for powering Saurian abilities but also manifests in the environment as radiant glows on objects, wind tunnels, and pools of liquid Phlogiston that characters and Saurians can traverse [32], [42]. This energy source is fundamental to the region's identity, fueling everything from the growth of its unique vegetation to the magical properties of its artifacts. The entire ecosystem is thus predicated on this raw, untamed power, reinforcing Natlan's thematic identity as the "Nation of Pyro" and emphasizing a worldview where humanity exists in a state of constant, dynamic interplay with the primal forces of nature [15], [32].

## Gameplay Mechanics

The introduction of Natlan represents a significant expansion of *Genshin Impact*'s gameplay, introducing several interconnected systems that are thematically consistent with its identity as the "Nation of Pyro" and "Nation of War" [15], [24]. These mechanics are not mere cosmetic additions but are deeply integrated into the region's lore and function as a core part of the player's experience. The cornerstone of Natlan's gameplay is the **Nightsoul's Blessing**, a combat and exploration mechanic exclusive to Natlan characters and the Pyro Traveler [15], [39]. This system functions as a secondary fuel bar, akin to Stamina, that allows characters to enter a heightened state of power [39]. Activating a character's Elemental Skill triggers this state, enhancing their abilities and enabling special actions [41]. The Nightsoul's Blessing is powered by a resource called Nightsoul Points, which deplete over time or when using skills [39]. When these points are exhausted, the system transitions to Phlogiston, a resource that can be replenished by consuming food items [33]. This mechanic visually and mechanically reinforces the idea of Natlan's inhabitants drawing strength from a primal, spiritual wellspring, aligning perfectly with the region's theme of passion and raw emotion [18].

Complementing the Nightsoul system is the innovative **Saurian Indwelling** mechanic, which allows players to merge their spirits with one of three distinct types of Saurians [7], [42]. Each Saurian type is associated with a specific tribe and possesses unique traversal abilities suited to Natlan's diverse terrain. The **Tepetlisaurus** is adept at climbing and tunneling, making it ideal for navigating the canyons of the Children of Echoes [7], [42]. The **Yumkasaurus** provides enhanced height-reach and grappling capabilities, perfect for the cliffside dwellings of the Scions of the Canopy [7], [42]. Finally, the **Koholasaurus** excels at swimming and can traverse liquid Phlogiston, catering to the seaside hot springs of the People of the Springs [7], [42]. This system adds a strategic layer to movement, encouraging players to switch Saurians based on the environment. All Saurians require Phlogiston to activate their special skills while indwelled, tying this traversal mechanic directly to the region's primary energy source [42]. Another crucial resource is **Phlogiston** itself, a high-temperature energy that is ubiquitous throughout Natlan [32], [42]. It appears as a radiant glow on unmarked environmental objects and can be collected by destroying certain items or touching Phlogiston sources [42]. This resource fuels Saurian abilities and restores Phlogiston levels for characters like Varesa, making it a vital component of gameplay [33].

To further deepen player engagement with the world's social structure, Natlan introduces a **Tribe Reputation System** [6], [32]. Players can undertake "Tribal Chronicles," a series of story quests tied to each of the six tribes, to earn reputation points [32]. Increasing one's standing with a tribe unlocks exclusive rewards, including valuable ascension materials for Natlan characters, and provides insight into the unique customs and histories of each group [32]. This system fosters a sense of belonging and investment in the region's communities. Finally, the **Nightsoul Burst** mechanism acts as a catalyst for advanced combat synergies [39], [40]. Triggered whenever any party member deals elemental damage to an opponent, the Nightsoul Burst does not have a standalone effect but instead serves as a universal trigger for numerous character-specific abilities and talent effects [40]. The cooldown for this burst is reduced the more Natlan characters are present in the active party, encouraging team compositions centered around the region's heroes [39], [40]. This intricate web of interconnected systems—Nightsoul, Saurians, Phlogiston, Tribes, and Nightsoul Bursts—collectively creates a cohesive and immersive gameplay experience that is uniquely tailored to the identity of Natlan.

## Character Design and Representation Controversy

While Natlan's lore and gameplay mechanics received praise for their creativity and thematic coherence, the region's character designs sparked a significant and sustained controversy within the gaming community, centered on accusations of "whitewashing" [14], [25]. The controversy arose from a stark discrepancy between HoYoverse's explicit statements citing Pre-Columbian American, Sub-Saharan African, and Oceania cultures as primary inspirations and the predominantly light skin tones of the characters [9], [11]. This was particularly contentious given the stated cultural pillars, which include Latin American and West African populations often associated with melanated skin tones [25], [26]. Critics pointed out that even the Natlan Archon, Mavuika, was depicted with unusually pale skin, undermining the authenticity of the cultural homage [11]. The backlash was amplified by the fact that some characters, like Iansan and Ifa, were among the few Natlan characters with slightly darker skin tones, yet they were still criticized as insufficient or perpetuating stereotypes [26].

The criticism was not limited to anonymous players; it was vocally supported by a number of prominent English-language voice actors for the game, including Khoi Dao (Albedo), Zeno Robinson (Sethos), Valeria Rodríguez (Sucrose), Jenny Yokobori (Yoimiya), Alejandro Saab (Cyno), and Anne Yatco (Raiden Shogun) [12]. These actors publicly took to social media to express their concerns about the disrespectful and inauthentic portrayal of characters whose inspirations were tied to marginalized cultures [12], [13]. Their involvement lent significant weight to the criticism, highlighting a perceived failure in cultural sensitivity and research on the part of the developer [13]. One of the most prominent points of contention was the character Iansan, whose name is derived from Ọya/Iansan, a Yoruba warrior goddess [13], [19]. Voice actor Valeria Rodríguez specifically criticized the design, arguing it failed to honor the cultural significance of the deity she represented [13]. This sentiment was echoed across the community, with many feeling that the game was engaging in performative diversity rather than genuine representation.

This controversy surrounding Natlan was seen by some observers as part of a broader, systemic issue within HoYoverse's development practices. Analysis pointed to similar patterns of backlash against previous regions, most notably Sumeru, which faced identical criticism for its lack of racial diversity despite having Arabic-named characters without appropriate tan or brown skin tones [11]. This suggested a recurring problem in the studio's approach to representing cultures outside of East Asian archetypes. The scale of the community's disapproval was substantial, evidenced by the "Genshin Impact Diversity Petition," which garnered over 126,000 signatures demanding greater ethnic representation in character designs [26]. As of the publication of reports covering the controversy, HoYoverse had not issued a formal public response to these widespread concerns, a silence that further fueled frustration and disappointment among players and critics alike [14]. This failure to address the valid criticisms of its creative team and vocal community members became a defining aspect of Natlan's reception, overshadowing many of its positive contributions to the game's world.

## Legacy and Community Reception

In summary, the introduction of Natlan into *Genshin Impact* stands as a landmark event, marking the sixth major addition to the world of Teyvat and significantly expanding the game's narrative and mechanical scope [23], [32]. Its release, beginning with Version 5.0 on August 28, 2024, and continuing with Version 5.5 on March 26, 2025, brought a new level of thematic depth to the game, solidifying its identity as the "Nation of Pyro" and "Nation of War" [5], [6]. The region's lore, inspired by a confluence of Mesoamerican, West African, and Oceanic cultures, presented a rich and imaginative world populated by compelling characters and unique concepts like Saurians and the Nightsoul [2], [3]. The innovative gameplay systems, including the Nightsoul's Blessing, Saurian Indwelling, and the Tribe Reputation System, were widely praised for their seamless integration with the region's core themes, offering players a fresh and engaging set of tools and challenges [15], [32], [42]. From a purely technical and design standpoint, Natlan is a testament to the developers' ambition and their capacity to construct a complex and memorable world within the established framework of Teyvat.

However, the legacy of Natlan is profoundly complicated by the intense and well-documented controversy surrounding its character designs. The perception of "whitewashing" created a significant schism in the community, with many praising the region's creative world-building while others expressed deep disappointment and frustration over what they viewed as a failure of cultural respect [14], [26]. The public criticism from voice actors lent considerable credibility to these concerns, framing the issue not just as a matter of personal preference but as a serious critique of the developer's cultural competency and commitment to inclusive representation [12], [13]. This controversy highlighted a critical tension between the goals of global appeal and the need for authentic representation, becoming a case study in the challenges of cross-cultural game design [25]. The developer's non-response to these widespread concerns further exacerbated the situation, leaving a lasting impression of neglect in the eyes of many players [14].

Ultimately, Natlan's place in the history of *Genshin Impact* is dualistic. On one hand, it is celebrated for its bold thematic direction and the introduction of groundbreaking gameplay mechanics that have enriched the player experience. On the other, it is remembered for a significant misstep in its execution, where the gap between stated intent and final product generated a substantial and damaging backlash. This duality ensures that Natlan will continue to be a subject of discussion and analysis, representing both the creative potential and the inherent risks of venturing into unfamiliar cultural territories in digital media. Its legacy serves as a powerful reminder that world-building in games extends beyond lore and mechanics to encompass the visual representation of its inhabitants, a responsibility that carries immense weight in a globalized audience.

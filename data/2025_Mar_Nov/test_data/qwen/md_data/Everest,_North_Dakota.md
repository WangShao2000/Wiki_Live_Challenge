# Everest, North Dakota

Everest is an unincorporated populated place in Cass County, North Dakota [5], [9]. It is a historically significant but sparsely populated settlement whose primary notability stems from its origins as a Great Northern Railroad station in the late 19th century [5]. Located approximately two miles south of the city of Casselton, its history is tied to the expansion of railroads across the American Midwest [5], [15]. The community's name has a unique origin, derived from a misspelling of the name of Charles A. Everitt, editor of *The Lisbon Star*, rather than being named for the famous mountain [5]. While no longer a thriving community, its official designation in federal geographic databases confirms its existence as a distinct place name [9], [11]. The area is served by the ZIP code 58723 and falls within the broader Fargo, ND-MN Metropolitan Statistical Area [19].

## Geography and Administration

Everest is situated within the administrative boundaries of Cass County, North Dakota [2], [9]. Its precise geographic coordinates are 46°51'35"N latitude and 97°13'26"W longitude, which can also be expressed in decimal degrees as 46.8596957° N and -97.22120059999999° W [4], [5], [12]. This specific location places it in the central part of the state, within the civil township that shares its name, Everest Township [6], [23]. As an unincorporated community, Everest does not possess its own municipal government; instead, local governance and administration fall under the jurisdiction of both Cass County and Everest Township [23]. The U.S. Census Bureau recognizes Everest Township as a county subdivision (cousub) for statistical purposes, confirming its legal and administrative structure within Cass County [6]. This relationship is further supported by the official roster of townships maintained by Cass County government, which includes Everest Township among its listed subdivisions [23].

A common point of confusion regarding its location arises from some sources incorrectly placing Everest in Divide County, North Dakota [8]. However, definitive geographic data from authoritative federal and state sources resolves this discrepancy. The Geographic Names Information System (GNIS), the official repository of domestic geographic names maintained by the U.S. Geological Survey (USGS), classifies Everest as a "Populated Place" in Cass County [9], [12]. This classification is corroborated by multiple independent datasets, including the North Dakota GIS Hub, which serves as the state's infrastructure for geospatial data, and Randymajors.org, an interactive mapping tool that verifies the placement of civil townships within Cass County [3], [14]. Therefore, Cass County is the undisputed and correct administrative county for Everest. The community lies along the corridor of Interstate 94 (I-94), a major north-south highway in the United States [15]. Specifically, I-94 passes through the western edge of Everest Township at mile marker 529.007, providing direct road access to the nearby city of Casselton, which is located just four miles to the east [15]. The only named exit on I-94 explicitly associated with Everest Township is Exit 529, which serves the community of Lynchburg [15].

The postal identity of the area is defined by the ZIP Code Tabulation Area (ZCTA) 58723 [19]. ZCTAs are generalized spatial representations of five-digit ZIP Codes created by the U.S. Census Bureau for the purpose of tabulating and displaying demographic data [17], [18]. It is important to distinguish ZCTAs from actual USPS mail delivery routes, as they are statistical constructs designed to approximate service areas while protecting individual privacy [17]. Cass County as a whole encompasses 39 different ZIP codes, making 58723 one of several postal districts serving the region [19]. Geographically, Cass County itself is the 10th largest county in North Dakota by total area, covering 1,767.811 square miles, of which 1,764.935 square miles are land [16]. Everest's location within this expansive county provides context for its rural character. The entire area operates under Central Standard Time (CST) [4]. ## History

The history of Everest is intrinsically linked to the westward expansion of the railroad system in the late 19th century. The community was established in 1881 as a station on the Great Northern Railroad [5]. Its founding marked a period of rapid settlement and development in the newly organized territories of the Dakotas. The site was specifically chosen in the northeast quarter of section 15-139-52, located within the boundaries of what would become Everest Township [5]. The establishment of the railroad station likely spurred the creation of a small village, which was officially platted in the same year, 1881 [5]. This development reflects a common pattern of growth where transportation corridors dictated the location of new communities.

A significant milestone in the community's early history was the opening of its post office on May 24, 1882 [5]. The appointment of Carl Knuppel as the first Postmaster signifies the formal recognition of Everest as a settlement with regular communication needs. The post office became a vital hub for the community, facilitating correspondence and commerce during its peak years. However, the reliance on the railroad as the primary driver of economic activity also made the community vulnerable to changes in the industry. The Great Northern Railroad made the decision to remove the station from its operations in 1895, and the physical tracks were subsequently lifted from the ground in 1896 [5]. This event effectively severed the community's main link to the regional economy and likely precipitated its decline. Following the removal of the station, the post office struggled to remain operational. It experienced a temporary closure on August 13, 1904, and another on September 17, 1904, before being permanently closed on November 30, 1908 [5]. With the post office shut down, mail was rerouted to the nearby and more prominent city of Casselton, marking the end of Everest's brief period of active community life [5].

The historical records provide insight into the community's population trends during its active years. The 1890 United States census reported a population of 175 people living in Everest [5]. This figure indicates a modest but viable settlement during the height of its development. However, subsequent decades saw a dramatic decrease in population. By the 1960 census, the reported population had dwindled to just 10 individuals [5]. This sharp decline suggests that most residents had moved away, leaving behind the structures of the former village. The closure of the post office in 1908 appears to have been the critical turning point that initiated this depopulation. Since 1960, no further census data is available in the provided sources to determine if any inhabitants remained or if the area became completely uninhabited. Despite its sparse population, the community retained its official designation in geographic nomenclature systems, indicating its continued existence as a named place on maps and in official records [9]. The narrative of Everest's history is therefore one of boom followed by bust, a story common to many small railroad-dependent towns that failed to adapt after the industrial shift that the decline of the rails represented.

## Etymology

The name "Everest" holds a unique and noteworthy distinction, as it is not named after the world-famous mountain in Asia. Instead, the community was named in honor of a man, Charles A. Everitt, due to a common historical error: a misspelling [5]. This detail provides a compelling backstory that adds a layer of human history to the place's identity. The name was chosen to commemorate Charles A. Everitt, who was identified as the editor of *The Lisbon Star*, a newspaper presumably located in Wisconsin [5]. The act of naming a new community after a respected individual was a common practice during the settlement of the American West, intended to create a sense of stability, progress, and connection to the broader culture of the nation. By honoring Mr. Everitt, the founders of the community likely sought to imbue their new settlement with a similar sense of importance and forward-looking ambition.

The misspelling that led to the name "Everest" instead of "Everitt" is a classic example of how place names can evolve through usage and documentation. Such errors are not uncommon in the chaotic process of frontier settlement, where names were often transcribed from spoken word or handwritten notes, leading to variations that eventually became standardized. The fact that the spelling "Everest" has persisted in official records, such as the Geographic Names Information System (GNIS), means that the original misspelling has become the definitive name for the community [9], [11]. This etymological quirk makes the name itself a piece of history, a tangible remnant of the community's founding era. It separates the North Dakota community from the globally recognized Mount Everest, avoiding any potential confusion and giving it a unique identity rooted in local history rather than international geography. The story of its name underscores the importance of personal connections and the role of media figures like newspaper editors in shaping the cultural landscape of the 19th-century American frontier. The legacy of Charles A. Everitt lives on, not in the pages of *The Lisbon Star*, but in the very name of a small, quiet community in the heart of North Dakota.

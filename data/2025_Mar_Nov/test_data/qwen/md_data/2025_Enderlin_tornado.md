# 2025 Enderlin Tornado

The 2025 Enderlin tornado was a violent and exceptionally rare atmospheric phenomenon that struck southeastern North Dakota on June 20, 2025 [5], [6]. Officially classified as an EF-5 on the Enhanced Fujita scale, it marked a significant event in American meteorological history due to its intensity and its status as the first such tornado confirmed in the United States since the 2013 Moore, Oklahoma tornado [1], [2], [12]. The storm originated from a powerful mesoscale convective system that also produced widespread straight-line winds through a derecho, impacting a broad swath of northern states and southern Canada between June 19 and June 22, 2025 [6]. The tornado carved a destructive path approximately 12 miles long and over a mile wide, causing catastrophic damage to farmsteads and infrastructure along its trajectory [1], [5]. Its confirmation as an EF-5 following extensive forensic analysis by the National Weather Service (NWS) Grand Forks office highlighted the extreme forces at play, with evidence including the derailing and displacement of dozens of railroad cars weighing hundreds of thousands of pounds [5], [6]. The event claimed the lives of three individuals in rural areas east of the town of Enderlin, while the city itself sustained only power-related disruptions [4], [5]. The tornado's occurrence in North Dakota represents a historical anomaly, being the first EF-5 to be confirmed in the state since the devastating 1957 Fargo tornado, which occurred exactly 68 years earlier [5].

## Meteorological History and Official Rating

The 2025 Enderlin tornado was the product of a highly unstable weather regime that developed across the northern Great Plains [6]. It formed within a larger, powerful severe thunderstorm complex that evolved into a radar-defined derecho—a widespread, long-lived wind storm associated with a band of rapidly moving showers or thunderstorms—which affected central and eastern North Dakota, as well as adjacent parts of Minnesota [8]. The tornado specifically touched down just before midnight local time on June 20, 2025 [5]. Different official records provide slightly varying but consistent timestamps for this critical moment. One source indicates the tornado began its ground contact at 11:02 p.m. Central Daylight Time (CDT), which corresponds to 04:02 UTC [5]. Another official database entry places the time of the event near Enderlin Airport at 22:04 CST (04:04 UTC) [8], [11]. Regardless of the exact minute, the event occurred late in the evening, catching many residents off guard. The tornado's duration was remarkably brief but intensely destructive, lasting for approximately 19 minutes before it lifted off the ground [5]. It dissipated around 11:21 p.m. CDT (04:21 UTC) [5].

Initially, the National Weather Service (NWS) conducted a preliminary assessment of the damage and assigned the tornado an Enhanced Fujita (EF) rating of at least EF-3 [4], [6]. This initial rating reflected the significant destruction observed, particularly the leveling of homes and farmsteads. However, subsequent, more detailed post-storm forensic analysis, conducted by NWS meteorologists in Grand Forks in collaboration with engineers and wind damage experts, revealed far greater levels of destruction than initially apparent [2], [12]. This extended investigation led to the official upgrade of the tornado's rating to EF-5, the highest possible classification on the scale, signifying catastrophic damage resulting from unimaginable wind forces [1], [5]. The official confirmation of this upgraded rating occurred on or before December 17, 2025, with one report specifying the date of the upgrade announcement as October 6, 2025 [1], [5], [6]. The decision to elevate the rating was not arbitrary; it was based on a rigorous examination of specific, high-end damage indicators that could only be explained by winds exceeding 200 mph [12].

The primary evidence supporting the EF-5 classification came from the analysis of damage to industrial and transportation infrastructure. A key indicator was the derailment of 33 train cars on CPKC's Elbow Lake Subdivision southeast of Enderlin [5], [6]. The force required to dislodge and hurl these massive railcars is a benchmark for assessing the most intense tornadoes. Specifically, the analysis noted that fully loaded grain hopper cars, weighing up to 286,000 pounds, were tipped over completely [5]. Even more dramatically, empty railroad tanker cars, some weighing as much as 72,000 pounds, were lofted and thrown considerable distances [5]. One such empty tanker car was found tossed 475.5 feet (approximately 144.9 meters) south of County Road 46 [1], [5]. These specific, quantifiable examples of vehicle displacement provided concrete physical proof of the wind speeds. Other corroborating damage indicators included the complete destruction of farmsteads, which were swept clean to their bare foundations or even basement levels, the debarking of trees, the bending of steel electrical transmission towers, and the uprooting of large tree root balls near the Maple River [2], [5], [12]. The combination of these extreme damage signatures allowed forecasters to confidently assign the EF-5 rating, confirming it as the first such tornado in the United States since the 2013 Moore, Oklahoma event and the 11th overall since the Enhanced Fujita scale was adopted in 2007 [2], [6].

## Path of Destruction and Damage Analysis

The Enderlin tornado's path of destruction was both long and exceptionally wide, carving a scar across the landscape of southeastern North Dakota [1], [5]. Officially documented in the NOAA Storm Events Database, the tornado's track began approximately three miles east of the town of Enderlin, near the border of Cass and Ransom Counties, and was geolocated specifically to the area of "ENDERLIN ARPT" (Airport) in RANSOM CO. [4], [11]. The tornado moved generally northeastward, traversing a distance of 12.1 miles (19.47 km) before lifting [5]. At its widest point, the funnel cloud spanned 1.05 miles (1.69 km or 1.7 km), creating a vast zone of devastation [2], [5], [12]. The intensity of the tornado varied along its path, transitioning from a lower-rated EF-2 strength when it first impacted the ADM Edible Bean Specialties facility at 136th Avenue SE and County Road 136, to its full EF-5 fury as it crossed the CPKC railway line southeast of Enderlin [5].

The damage inflicted by the EF-5 winds was catastrophic and indiscriminate. The most compelling evidence of its power came from the obliteration of industrial-scale assets. As previously noted, the derailment of 33 train cars was a primary factor in the rating upgrade [6]. The force of the wind was sufficient to completely topple fully loaded grain hopper cars, some of which weighed as much as 286,000 pounds, and to throw empty, 72,000-pound railroad tanker cars great distances [5]. This level of structural disruption is indicative of winds that can exceed 200 mph. Beyond the railways, the tornado systematically destroyed every structure in its direct path. Farmsteads were completely leveled, leaving behind only bare foundations or the concrete slabs of basements, a classic signature of EF-5 intensity [2], [12]. Two homes were entirely destroyed, killing two people inside one residence [5]. A third person was killed when the tornado passed directly over another home northwest of the highway [5]. The sheer power of the vortex stripped trees of their bark, snapped them like twigs, and uprooted entire trees, sometimes displacing their massive root balls [2], [5]. Steel electrical transmission towers, designed to withstand significant stress, were visibly bent and twisted by the wind, further testifying to the storm's ferocity [2], [12].

In stark contrast to the devastation along its main path, the city of Enderlin itself experienced a relatively minor impact. While the surrounding rural areas were subjected to EF-5 winds, the urban core of Enderlin sustained primarily power-related issues, with approximately 24,000 customers losing electricity across the state due to the broader storm system [4], [12]. The tornado's immense energy was focused in a narrow corridor east of the town, sparing the populated center. However, the psychological impact on the community was undoubtedly profound, given the proximity of such a catastrophic event. The storm also caused significant collateral damage, including the blowing over of a cell tower, which would have disrupted communication services in the area [5]. The overall damage profile, characterized by the complete removal of buildings from their foundations and the violent displacement of heavy objects, left no doubt in the minds of the forensic survey teams that they were dealing with a truly exceptional and deadly storm.

## Human Toll and Casualty Reports

The human cost of the 2025 Enderlin tornado was tragically clear, with three lives lost in the rural communities east of the town of Enderlin [1], [4]. Local law enforcement and emergency responders worked diligently to account for all residents in the heavily damaged area. The Cass County Sheriff’s Office played a crucial role in confirming the fatalities. During a press conference held on June 22, 2025, Sheriff Jesse Jahner announced the grim discovery of two individuals who had been found deceased at a residence located east of Highway 46 near 140th Avenue SE [9]. He later specified that a third fatality was discovered at a second, nearby residence north of the first location [9]. Following a comprehensive grid search of the entire damage area, officials confirmed that no additional fatalities or major injuries were found [9].

The identities of the victims were released by the Cass County Sheriff’s Office on June 22, 2025 [9]. The three individuals who lost their lives were:
*   Michael Dalton Dehn, 73 years old.
*   Katherine Ann Pfaff-Dehn, 73 years old.
*   Marcario Machuca Lucio, 89 years old.
All three were residents of Enderlin, North Dakota [9]. Their deaths underscore the peril faced by those caught outside in mobile homes or poorly constructed buildings during such a powerful tornado. The news of the fatalities was widely reported by local media outlets, including KVRR Local News, which covered the aftermath and the recovery efforts [10]. Some reports indicated that storm chasers may have been among the first to locate the bodies of two of the victims, highlighting the chaotic and tragic nature of the scene immediately following the tornado's passage [10]. One storm chaser, John Farley, described the experience of finding the deceased as "the worst chase ever," emphasizing the somber reality of witnessing the human toll of these events firsthand [10].

A significant point of discrepancy exists regarding the official death toll. While multiple credible news sources and statements from the Cass County Sheriff’s Office confirm three fatalities, the NOAA Storm Events Database, which serves as the authoritative national repository for verified storm data, lists the number of direct and indirect deaths as zero [8], [11]. This conflict requires careful consideration. The news reports and official statements from local law enforcement likely reflect immediate investigations and public announcements made shortly after the disaster. In contrast, the NOAA database entry, last updated on December 17, 2025, represents the consolidated, officially vetted data from the National Weather Service's storm survey [11]. The reason for this discrepancy is not detailed in the provided sources, but it could stem from various factors, such as the official NWS survey classifying the deaths as indirect rather than direct tornado causes, or potentially a lack of definitive attribution in the formal data entry process. For the purposes of this report, the more detailed and corroborated accounts from law enforcement and media—confirming three fatalities—are presented as the factual narrative, while the conflicting data from the NOAA database is noted as an official record. No injuries were officially reported as a direct result of the tornado [5].

## Regional Context and Broader Weather System

The 2025 Enderlin tornado was not an isolated event but rather the most violent manifestation of a large and prolonged severe weather episode that gripped the northern plains from June 19 through June 22, 2025 [6]. This multi-day period saw a succession of powerful storms affecting a vast area that included northern U.S. states like Montana, North Dakota, Minnesota, and South Dakota, as well as southern Canada, particularly Saskatchewan [6]. The overarching system responsible for the Enderlin tornado was a potent derecho, a widespread, long-lived wind storm associated with a band of strong thunderstorms [8]. This derecho complex produced widespread straight-line wind damage, with hurricane-force winds reaching up to 120 mph recorded across several states [6]. The Enderlin tornado was embedded within this larger system, meaning it formed from a supercell thunderstorm that was part of the broader mesoscale convective system that defined the derecho [8].

The severity of the storm system was evident in the sheer number of severe weather events it spawned. Across North Dakota alone, the NOAA Storm Events Database recorded a total of 39 separate severe weather events between June 20 and June 21, 2025 [11]. This included 15 distinct tornadoes, ranging from EF0 to the violent EF5, as well as 22 thunderstorm wind events, one hail event, and one flash flood [11]. The Enderlin EF-5 was one of several violent tornadoes to strike the region during this outbreak. Other significant tornadoes included an EF-4 that touched down in Elizabeth, Cass County, and an EF-3 in Urbana [8]. This concentration of intense tornado activity underscores the extreme instability and wind shear present in the atmosphere during this period. The presence of multiple violent tornadoes within a single storm complex is a testament to the exceptional meteorological conditions that prevailed.

Beyond the direct threat of tornadoes, the derecho component of the storm system caused widespread and significant secondary impacts. The most notable of these was a catastrophic blow to the region's agricultural sector. According to ND Agriculture Commissioner Doug Goehring, the prolonged, damaging winds associated with the derecho complex resulted in an estimated loss of 50 to 80 million bushels of grain storage capacity [8]. This figure highlights the immense economic toll the storm took on the farming industry, which is a cornerstone of the North Dakota economy. Additionally, the storm system triggered widespread power outages, affecting approximately 24,000 customers in North Dakota [4]. These cascading effects—from the violent tornadoes to the widespread wind damage and agricultural losses—paint a picture of a truly multi-faceted regional disaster that tested the resilience of communities and infrastructure across a large geographic area.

## Aftermath, Legacy, and Official Documentation

The aftermath of the 2025 Enderlin tornado was characterized by a massive recovery effort focused on clearing debris, restoring power, and providing support to affected families [4]. While details on specific government aid programs or long-term rebuilding initiatives are not available in the provided sources, the immediate response involved local emergency services, state resources, and likely federal assistance through agencies like FEMA. The destruction was concentrated in rural areas, requiring specialized equipment and personnel to navigate the devastated landscapes and assist survivors. The psychological impact on the community of Enderlin and the surrounding counties was profound, as residents grappled with the loss of life, property, and the traumatic memory of the event [10].

The legacy of the Enderlin tornado is firmly rooted in its rarity and historical significance. For meteorologists and climatologists, its confirmation as an EF-5 is a landmark event. It ended a long drought of such violent tornadoes in the United States, marking the first EF-5 to be officially recorded since the devastating May 20, 2013, tornado that struck Moore, Oklahoma [1], [2], [12]. This 12-year gap without a U.S. EF-5 tornado made the Enderlin event a subject of intense scientific interest and discussion. Furthermore, its occurrence in North Dakota was historically unprecedented. The tornado was the first EF-5 to be confirmed in the state since the infamous April 9, 1957, tornado that ravaged the Fargo-Moorhead area, an event that occurred exactly 68 years prior to the Enderlin tornado [1], [5]. This historical parallel underscores the extraordinary nature of the event and its place in the annals of North Dakota's weather history.

The official documentation of the event is maintained by several authoritative bodies, ensuring a standardized record for future reference. The primary source for verified meteorological data is the NOAA Storm Events Database, which compiles information from official NWS storm surveys [7]. This database contains the official statistics for the Enderlin tornado, including its path length, width, timing, and final EF-5 rating [7], [11]. As noted, however, this official record contains discrepancies, such as the reported zero fatalities, which differ from accounts from local law enforcement and news media [8], [11]. Despite these inconsistencies, the database remains the definitive source for the storm's physical parameters. The detailed forensic analysis conducted by the NWS Grand Forks office, which led to the EF-5 upgrade, will serve as a case study for future storm surveys, providing a rich dataset of damage indicators used to classify extreme tornado intensity [1], [12]. In summary, the 2025 Enderlin tornado stands as a powerful reminder of nature's destructive potential and a significant chapter in the ongoing study of severe weather in the United States.

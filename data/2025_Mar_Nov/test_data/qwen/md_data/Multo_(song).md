# Multo (song)

"Multo," a 2024 single by the Filipino band Cup of Joe, stands as a landmark achievement in Philippine popular music. Performed by the six-piece ensemble from Baguio, the song masterfully blends melancholic synth-pop balladry with profound lyrical introspection [2], [5]. Written by band member Raphaell 'Rapha' Ridao and his brother Redentor Immanuel Ridao, and produced by Shadiel Chan, "Multo" serves as the lead single for their debut studio album, *Silakbo* [[12]), [13]]. Officially released on September 14, 2024, the track immediately signaled a deliberate artistic evolution for the group, departing from their previous upbeat pop roots toward a more emotionally vulnerable and sonically ambitious style [2], [11]. The title, which translates to "ghost" in Filipino, functions as a powerful and recurring metaphor throughout the song, representing not literal hauntings but the inescapable presence of past selves, lingering regrets, and unresolved memories related to career, friendship, and love [3], [6]. This nuanced exploration of the human condition propelled the song to unprecedented commercial success, making it the first work by any Filipino act to achieve multiple chart milestones on Billboard's global charts [1], [4]. Its sustained dominance on both local and international charts, coupled with widespread cultural resonance and critical acclaim, solidifies "Multo" as a defining anthem of the modern P-Pop era and a watershed moment for the genre's global recognition [6], [8].

## Release and Production

The release of "Multo" marked a pivotal moment for the Filipino band Cup of Joe, signaling a strategic and successful pivot in their musical direction [2]. The song, performed by the six-piece ensemble consisting of vocalist Raphael Ridao, guitarists Gabriel Fernandez and CJ Fernandez, keyboardist Gian Bernardino, bassist Xen Gareza, and drummer Ralph Gavieres, was officially released as a single on Friday, September 14, 2024 [1], [5]. However, the band executed a surprise drop, announcing the track just three hours before its scheduled midnight release, creating immediate buzz and anticipation among their fanbase [11]. It served as the lead single for their highly anticipated debut studio album, *Silakbo*, which was subsequently released on January 17, 2025 [[5], [12])]. The song was positioned as the seventh track on the album, anchoring a specific and crucial emotional section of the record's overarching narrative structure [[7], [12])]. The production of "Multo" was handled by Shadiel Chan, whose role has been instrumental in shaping the band's signature sound [[12]), [14])]. Producer Shadiel Chan had already established this sonic aesthetic with the band's preceding single, "Misteryoso," which he described as fusing classic OPM with modern pop and featuring a "fairytale-like atmosphere," thus laying the groundwork for the experimental and emotionally textured approach taken with "Multo" [[14])].

The composition of "Multo" was a deeply personal endeavor for the band members. It was co-written by Raphaell 'Rapha' Ridao and his older brother, Redentor Immanuel Ridao [[12])]. The creative process was catalyzed during the pandemic, a period when both brothers experienced the loss of someone important to them [13]. This shared experience of grief informed the song's poignant examination of "the ghosts of our past" and the emotional aftermath of failed relationships [11], [13]. The song's duration is 3 minutes and 57 seconds [[12])]. The band's overall sound is defined as experimental pop, characterized by a fusion of acoustic pop, retro-wave, alternative pop ballads, and neo-folk [[14])]. "Multo" exemplifies this blend, delivering a haunting yet catchy tune driven by swelling synths and mesmerizing harmonies, transforming raw emotions into unforgettable melodies [[6], [14])]. The song's genre classification has been described variably as a synth-pop ballad, aligning with its melodic and atmospheric qualities, while other sources have tagged it with "Pop Rock" and "Dream Pop," reflecting its dynamic build and ethereal textures [2], [5], [10]. This stylistic ambition represents a significant departure from Cup of Joe's earlier, more traditional pop-oriented performances, showcasing their artistic maturity and willingness to explore new sonic territories [2]. The decision to release "Multo" as the lead single was a calculated move to introduce fans to the mature and introspective direction of the *Silakbo* album, setting the stage for its eventual monumental success [5].

## Composition and Thematic Content

The thematic core of "Multo" revolves around the Filipino word "multo," which translates to "ghost" [2]. The song utilizes this term as a rich and evocative metaphor to explore complex internal states rather than external supernatural phenomena [6]. The "ghost" in question is not a spirit of a deceased person but an internal manifestation—a persistent echo of one's own feelings, unchosen life paths, and unresolved regrets [3], [6]. This introspective theme delves into the psychological landscape of being haunted by one's past self, the weight of pivotal decisions, and the lingering "what-ifs" that stem from career aspirations, friendships, and romantic entanglements [3], [6]. The lyrics poignantly capture a state of emotional paralysis and the difficulty of moving forward, encapsulating the idea of learning to sit with grief and confront the pain of unresolved feelings [6], [13]. The song's emotional depth is amplified by a haunting yet memorable melody, creating a powerful juxtaposition between its melancholic tone and its infectious, dramatic build [6].

Several key lyrical excerpts powerfully illustrate these themes of memory, regret, and the inescapable nature of the past. One of the most frequently cited lines is "Pasindi na ng ilaw / Minumulto na ‘ko ng damdamin ko," which translates to "The light is off / My feelings have become a ghost that haunts me" [2], [6]. This verse directly establishes the central metaphor, framing the singer's own emotions as the source of their torment [6]. Another verse further explores this inability to escape one's inner demons: "Hindi na makalaya dinadalaw mo ‘ko bawat gabi / wala mang nakikita haplos mo’y ramdam pa rin sa dilim" ("You haunt me every night, I can't be free / Even though I can't see you, your touch is still felt in the dark") [3]. These lines emphasize the intangible yet profoundly felt presence of these internal "ghosts." The chorus continues this exploration with lines such as "Kala ko’y payapa boses mo’y tumatawag pa / binaon naman na ang lahat tinakpan naman na ‘king sugat" ("I thought everything would be peaceful, your voice still calls out / But everything has been buried, the wound has been sealed again"), illustrating the cyclical nature of painful memories that resurface despite efforts to suppress them [3]. Through these vivid and poetic lyrics, the song captures the universal experience of carrying the emotional residue of the past, transforming abstract feelings of nostalgia and regret into tangible, haunting imagery [[14])].

The song's thematic resonance has struck a deep chord with audiences, particularly on social media platforms like TikTok [2]. Listeners have widely reinterpreted the song's metaphor, applying the concept of a "multo" to various personal experiences. For many, the "ghost" represents lost loved ones, faded friendships, or past versions of themselves that they can no longer reclaim [2]. This broad interpretive flexibility has contributed significantly to the song's organic virality and cultural impact, allowing a wide range of individuals to find personal meaning in its lyrics [2]. The song's placement within the band's debut album, *Silakbo*, provides a crucial narrative framework for understanding its thematic weight. *Silakbo* is structured as a concept album based on the five stages of grief—denial, anger, bargaining, depression, and acceptance—as defined by Elisabeth Kübler-Ross [[7], [12])]. "Multo" is strategically positioned as the centerpiece of the 'DEPRESSION' stage, functioning as the seventh track on the album [[12]), [13]]. This placement frames the song's intense emotional landscape not as a standalone piece but as the climactic expression of despair and emotional haunting within a larger, cohesive narrative about processing loss [7]. Guitarist Gab Fernandez explained at a press conference that the album's structure moves through these stages, with "Multo" serving as the opening track of the 'Depression' stage, reinforcing its role as the emotional core of the album's journey through grief [13]. This intentional context elevates the song from a simple ballad to a pivotal moment of catharsis and vulnerability within a grander artistic vision.

## Music Video and Visual Narrative

The official music video for "Multo" serves as a compelling visual extension of the song's central metaphor, translating its abstract themes of haunting memories and emotional duality into a tangible narrative [9]. Directed to complement the song's melancholic tone, the video features prominent Filipino actors Elijah Canlas and Miles Ocampo in leading roles [9]. The plot centers on the struggle to move on after a breakup, depicting the protagonist's (played by Ocampo) difficulty in letting go of bittersweet memories and confronting the painful fragments of her past [9]. According to Billboard Philippines’ regional music writer Rome Saenz, the video effectively captures the "heart-wrenching themes behind the chart-topping anthem," providing a visual story for the internal conflict explored in the lyrics [9]. The performance of the Cup of Joe band itself is also featured, intercut with the main narrative, grounding the abstract emotions in a live musical expression [9].

A key element of the video's visual language is its sophisticated use of color grading to symbolize the theme of longing for the past and emotional duality [9]. The narrative alternates between warm and cold color palettes, creating a visual dichotomy that mirrors the protagonist's internal state of grappling with painful memories (cold scenes) and moments of cherished nostalgia (warm scenes) [9]. This technique, combined with a mastery of light and editing, creates a sense of "flickers and whirlwinds of emotion," visually conveying the fragmented and overwhelming nature of reliving past pain [9]. The director's skillful execution allows the audience to viscerally experience the character's emotional turmoil, enhancing the viewer's connection to the song's message [9]. The video's stripped-down Cozy Cove live performance version, which showcases a more intimate and raw rendition of the song, has amassed 3.3 million views, further contributing to the song's status as a slow-burning chart phenomenon and demonstrating the versatility of its presentation across different formats [2].

The combination of a strong cast, a relatable narrative, and advanced visual techniques makes the "Multo" music video a significant artistic component of the song's overall impact. By illustrating the struggle to move on amidst painful memories, the video provides a concrete representation of the song's abstract concept of a "multo"—an emotional echo from the past that refuses to be silenced [9]. This visual storytelling complements the lyrical depth and amplifies the song's emotional resonance, helping to explain its profound connection with audiences. The critical praise for the video's ability to capture the song's heart-wrenching themes underscores its effectiveness in communicating the song's core message without relying solely on the lyrics [9]. Ultimately, the music video is not merely promotional material but an integral part of the artistic statement, enriching the listener's experience and solidifying "Multo" as a multi-faceted cultural artifact that resonates on both auditory and visual levels.

## Critical Reception and Accolades

Upon its release, "Multo" garnered overwhelmingly positive critical reception, with reviewers praising its emotional depth, innovative sound, and the band's successful artistic evolution [[2], [14])]. The song was recognized as a standout work of artistry, earning significant accolades that affirmed its quality and importance within the Philippine music scene. At the inaugural Filipino Music Awards held in October 2024, "Multo" won the prestigious award for Song of the Year, while the debut album *Silakbo*, which features the track, was honored with Album of the Year [6]. This dual victory highlighted the song's excellence both as a standalone single and as a vital component of a cohesive and powerful artistic statement. Such high-level recognition from a formal awards body serves as a strong validation of the song's craftsmanship and its impact on listeners and critics alike.

Beyond industry awards, "Multo" has been celebrated by music critics and publications, who have acknowledged its place in the contemporary P-Pop canon. The song was listed as an honourable mention in a curated list of notable 21st-century Philippine pop songs, a distinction that places it alongside other significant releases from the 2020s, including works by globally recognized acts like SB19 and BINI [8]. This inclusion reinforces its cultural relevance and positions it as a representative and influential work of current Filipino alternative pop [8]. The song's inclusion in such a list signifies that its success was not merely commercial but also critically acclaimed, cementing its legacy as a significant contribution to Philippine music history. The song's release year of 2024 was explicitly noted in this context, further establishing its timeline within the modern P-Pop landscape [8].

The critical acclaim for "Multo" extends to its production and composition. Producer Shadiel Chan's role in shaping the band's unique sound has been consistently highlighted, with his work on "Multo" being seen as a culmination of the artistic vision developed through previous singles like "Misteryoso" [[14])]. Critics have praised the song's blend of "haunting, synth-driven" melodies with relatable and introspective lyrics, describing it as a poignant examination of the "ghosts of our past" [5], [11]. The band's definition of their sound as "experimental pop," incorporating elements of retro-wave and neo-folk, was seen as a successful and authentic artistic statement, with "Multo" serving as its definitive example [[14])]. This positive reception from music journalists and industry professionals helped build momentum for the song even before its massive chart success, establishing it as a critically respected work long before it became a mainstream hit. The combination of critical praise, industry awards, and inclusion in expert-curated lists demonstrates that "Multo" is not just a commercially successful song but a critically lauded piece of art that has made a lasting impression on the Philippine music landscape.

## Chart Performance and Historic Milestones

"Multo" achieved a series of unprecedented chart successes that not only broke numerous records but also marked historic firsts for the Philippine music industry. The song's performance on local charts was nothing short of dominant, establishing it as a cultural phenomenon within the Philippines. It holds the record for the longest-running No. 1 song on the *Billboard Philippines* Hot 100 chart, having ruled the top spot for over four months, or at least 17+ weeks, as of August 2025 [5]. This sustained reign shattered previous records, surpassing the previous benchmark set by Zack Tabudlo's "Pano" [4]. Furthermore, "Multo" achieved a remarkable milestone on the Spotify Philippines Daily Chart, where it accumulated over 1.18 million daily streams and set a single-day record of 2.3 million streams, ultimately securing the longest-running #1 position with 125 consecutive days at the top as of the article's publication [4]. This "slow-burning phenomenon" demonstrated its immense and enduring popularity among Filipino listeners [2].

On the international stage, "Multo" made history by becoming the first song by any Filipino act to enter the Billboard Global 200 chart [1], [6]. The song debuted at No. 181 on the chart dated April 15, 2025, and peaked at No. 80, marking a watershed moment for P-Pop's global visibility [1], [4]. Its success extended beyond the global chart, as it also entered the Billboard Global Excl. US chart at No. 74—the highest debut and the first Top 100 entry for any Filipino song on that specific list [1]. This achievement highlighted the song's strong regional engagement and growing international appeal. The song's streaming prowess was equally impressive. As of April 19, 2025, "Multo" set the record for the most weekly streams ever recorded by a Filipino song on Spotify's weekly Global Top 200, amassing 17.582 million streams [1]. This figure underscores the song's universal appeal and its ability to connect with a global audience.

"Multo" proved that a song rooted in Filipino culture and language could achieve unparalleled success on the world's most prominent music charts, paving the way for future artists and validating the global relevance of P-Pop. The song's sustained dominance on local charts, combined with its groundbreaking international success, cements its legacy as one of the most significant and successful songs in the history of Philippine popular music.

## Legacy and Cultural Impact

The legacy of "Multo" extends far beyond its chart-topping success; it represents a pivotal moment in the evolution of Philippine Pop (P-Pop) and has left an indelible mark on the cultural landscape. The song's primary impact was the significant artistic transformation it spurred within its creators, the band Cup of Joe. By successfully transitioning from their previous "upbeat pop roots" to a more mature, melancholic, and synth-driven sound, they demonstrated artistic growth and expanded the possibilities for their own future work [2]. This shift was not merely superficial but a deep dive into emotionally vulnerable territory, a departure that resonated powerfully with their audience and validated their artistic ambitions [2], [11]. The song's success provided the band with a platform to explore deeper and more complex themes, solidifying their reputation as serious and innovative musicians. This newfound artistic freedom has been reflected in their subsequent career, including a sold-out 3-day concert tour titled "Stardust" at the Smart Araneta Coliseum, a direct consequence of the song's immense popularity and the momentum it generated [4].

On a broader scale, "Multo" has been credited with breaking new ground for the entire P-Pop industry. Its historic achievements on Billboard's global charts were not just personal victories for Cup of Joe but a collective triumph for Filipino artists everywhere [1], [4]. By becoming the first Filipino act to chart on the Billboard Global 200 and achieve a Top 100 entry on the Billboard Global Excl. US chart, the song shattered barriers and proved that Filipino music could compete and thrive on a global stage [1], [6]. This accomplishment has likely inspired a new generation of artists and opened doors for increased international collaboration and exposure for the genre. The song's critical acclaim and inclusion in expert-curated lists of notable P-Pop songs further solidify its place in the modern canon, ensuring its historical significance will be remembered and studied by future generations [8]. It serves as a case study in how a song rooted in cultural specificity (using the Tagalog word "multo") can achieve universal emotional resonance and global commercial success.

Ultimately, the cultural impact of "Multo" lies in its ability to foster a deep and personal connection with a vast audience. The song's exploration of universal themes like regret, memory, and the search for identity allowed listeners to project their own experiences onto its lyrics, leading to widespread reinterpretation and sharing on social media [2]. This organic spread of the song's message contributed to its slow-burning rise to fame and created a shared cultural touchstone. The band's willingness to confront difficult emotions in their music encouraged a dialogue about mental health and the process of grieving, using their platform to provide comfort and catharsis to those experiencing similar feelings [13]. In conclusion, "Multo" is more than just a hit song; it is a testament to the power of authentic artistic expression. Its legacy is multifaceted: it transformed the career of Cup of Joe, elevated the profile of P-Pop on the world stage, and provided a soundtrack for a generation navigating the complexities of memory and identity.

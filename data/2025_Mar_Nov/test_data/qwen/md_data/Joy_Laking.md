# Joy Laking

Joy Snihur Wyatt Laking is a Canadian visual artist renowned for her paintings of Nova Scotia's natural landscapes and towns [4], [5]. For over five decades, she has worked primarily with watercolour, oil, and acrylics, developing a distinctive style characterized by strong composition and an expressive use of color and form [4], [7]. Her professional practice began in Nova Scotia in 1972, and her work has been exhibited nationally and internationally, earning her significant recognition within the Canadian art community [2], [6], [8]. Laking is also recognized for her role as an advocate for realism in art, co-founding the organization PLANS (Professional Living Artists of Nova Scotia) to support contemporary realist painters in the province [3], [6]. Her body of work includes numerous solo exhibitions at prominent galleries, public commissions, and the publication of a book chronicling her artistic journey [1], [8]. Through her art and community leadership, Laking has made a substantial contribution to the cultural identity of Nova Scotia.

## Biographical Background

Joy Snihur Wyatt Laking was born in 1950 in Owen Sound, Ontario, Canada [1], [2]. She pursued higher education at the University of Guelph, where she graduated in 1972 with a Bachelor of Arts degree, majoring in Fine Art [1], [2], [6]. Following her graduation, she moved to Nova Scotia, where she has lived and worked as a professional artist since that year [2], [6], [8]. She currently resides in Portapique, near Great Village, Colchester County, or Bass River, in Nova Scotia, where she maintains her home and studio gallery [1], [3], [9]. The studio is located on a Bay of Fundy salt marsh, providing a constant source of inspiration for her work [4].

Laking’s artistic vocation was deeply influenced by her family background. Her mother was a professional painter, and her father crafted marionettes and toy trains, creating a household saturated with creative energy [1]. Her childhood home reportedly had art drawers in every room, reflecting a pervasive environment of artistic exploration from an early age [6]. This nurturing atmosphere likely played a crucial role in fostering her passion for art. A pivotal and profoundly influential event in her life was the death of her mother from breast cancer around 1972, shortly after she completed her university studies [1], [6]. This personal tragedy is described as having a significant impact on her career path, shaping the emotional depth and dedication evident in her subsequent work. In 1972, the same year she graduated and lost her mother, she married Jim Wyatt; together they have a son and a daughter [1], [9]. Her husband has been involved in the operation of the Joy Laking Gallery, which she runs near Bass River, Nova Scotia [6], [9]. Despite living with asthma and muscular dystrophy, she continues to paint professionally [9].

## Artistic Practice and Philosophy

Joy Laking’s artistic practice is defined by a dual methodology that combines spontaneous outdoor creation with meticulous studio refinement, resulting in a unique and cohesive body of work [5], [7]. A central element of her process is plein-air painting, where she completes entire watercolour works directly on location [5]. This technique allows her to capture the immediacy of a moment, focusing intensely on the transient effects of tides and lighting over a period of four to seven hours [5]. These on-location studies serve as the foundation for her larger studio paintings [7]. While working outdoors, she often prioritizes capturing the essence of the scene through direct observation, later translating these impressions into more developed compositions in her studio.

Her studio process is one of contemplation and development, often evolving over several weeks [7]. Paintings may be derived from on-location studies, photographs, or even physical objects like flowers brought indoors [7]. This extended period of reflection allows her to refine her initial sketches into complex works that reflect a deep engagement with their subject matter. Laking herself describes her artistic philosophy as one where all her paintings are 'singing'—a phrase that encapsulates her pursuit of a distinct artistic voice and aesthetic harmony [7]. This concept is rooted in her technical priorities, which include a strong compositional structure, a rich tonal range, and an expressive application of materials [7]. In her watercolours, she emphasizes the expressive potential of the paper white, while in her oils and acrylics, she focuses on creating rich color palettes [7]. This commitment to both structural integrity and expressive quality is what defines her mature style and distinguishes her work within the contemporary realist movement.

Beyond her individual artistic output, Laking engages with her community through her art in meaningful ways. One notable example is the installation of her watercolour painting *Chairs at High Tide* in the healing garden at North Cumberland Health Care Centre in Pugwash, Nova Scotia [5]. The artwork became the twentieth piece in the Pugwash Open Air Gallery, an initiative that displays large-scale reproductions of local art in outdoor public spaces to promote both artistic excellence and community wellness [5]. This act demonstrates her belief in the power of art to contribute positively to public life and well-being, extending the reach and purpose of her work beyond traditional gallery settings.

## Professional Career and Major Works

Joy Laking’s professional career, spanning over fifty years, is marked by a consistent focus on Nova Scotia as her primary subject and a series of significant exhibitions that have solidified her reputation as a leading regional artist [2], [5]. Her career highlights include numerous solo exhibitions at prominent institutions across Canada, national tours of her work, and public commissions that demonstrate widespread recognition of her talent. A chronological overview of her major professional milestones illustrates the trajectory of her success and the breadth of her artistic output.

The opening of her own gallery in Portapique in 1988 provided her with a dedicated space to showcase her work and connect directly with the public [1]. The gallery remains open by appointment as of 2025, continuing to serve as a hub for her art [1]. Her residency at Port Bickerton Lighthouse in 2016 and her trip to paint in Claude Monet’s gardens in 2017 highlight her continued engagement with both iconic Canadian locations and international artistic heritage [1], [2]. The tour of her 1989 exhibition at the Art Gallery of Nova Scotia is particularly noteworthy, as it indicates that her work was considered significant enough for a wider audience across the province, showcasing her ability to produce a body of work with broad appeal and critical resonance [1], [2], [4]. Furthermore, her work has transcended the gallery circuit, appearing on official provincial materials and charitable commemorative stamps, underscoring her status as a celebrated figure in Nova Scotia culture [1].

## Advocacy and Community Engagement

Joy Laking's influence extends beyond her individual artistic production to encompass active advocacy and leadership within the broader art community, particularly concerning the promotion of realism as a vital and legitimate artistic practice in Nova Scotia [3]. Recognizing the challenges faced by contemporary realist painters, including geographic isolation and a perceived lack of institutional support, she took a proactive role in shaping the regional art scene [3]. In 2009, she co-founded PLANS (Professional Living Artists of Nova Scotia), a collective of realist painters dedicated to supporting its members and raising the profile of realism in a province with a long-standing tradition of representational art [3], [6]. By creating this organization, Laking and her colleagues sought to build a stronger network for collaboration, mentorship, and mutual promotion, thereby addressing some of the systemic barriers facing artists working outside of major urban centers.

Her commitment to advancing the cause of realism was not limited to organizational founding but also included curatorial and public advocacy. In 2014, she served as a key organizer for the 'Realism Revisited' exhibition at the Dalhousie Art Gallery [3]. The exhibition, which ran from January 17 to March 9, was curated by Tom Smart and co-piloted by Peter Dykhuis, director-curator of the gallery, and was held to mark the gallery’s 60th anniversary [3]. This high-profile show provided a significant platform to re-examine and celebrate the relevance of realism in contemporary art. Beyond organizing exhibitions, Laking has publicly advocated for greater institutional recognition of realism, citing its historical dismissal in many public galleries as a challenge that needed to be addressed [3]. This vocal stance positions her as a thought leader who uses her standing within the art community to fight for the legitimacy and visibility of her chosen artistic style.

This dedication to community and advocacy is further reflected in her institutional affiliations and service. She is an elected member of the Society of Canadian Artists, a peer-selected honor that signifies recognition by her fellow professionals [2], [6]. Additionally, she served two terms on the Board of Governors for the Art Gallery of Nova Scotia, demonstrating a deep commitment to the stewardship and development of the province's premier art institution [2]. Her involvement in bringing exhibitions like 'Across the Surface' to Truro also highlights her role in enriching the cultural offerings of various communities within Nova Scotia [6]. Through these multifaceted efforts—as a founder, curator, advocate, and board member—Joy Laking has consistently worked to strengthen the artistic ecosystem in which she operates, ensuring greater opportunities and recognition for realist artists in the region.

## Broader Contributions and Personal Resilience

Joy Laking's contributions to public discourse and her response to profound community trauma reveal a multifaceted artist engaged with social issues and capable of expressing deep human emotion through her work. Beyond her celebrated landscape paintings, she authored and published the educational play *Invisible Prisons*, a monologue-based work that addresses the serious issue of domestic abuse [7]. Released in two versions in 2013 and 2014, the play was made freely available to schools, community groups, and police departments through the website www.invisibleprisons.com [7]. This initiative demonstrates her desire to use her creative talents for social good, aiming to raise awareness and provide resources to combat a pervasive social problem. Her authorship adds a significant dimension to her profile, establishing her not only as a visual artist but also as a writer and advocate for vulnerable populations.

This capacity for empathy and social commentary is poignantly illustrated by her personal experience during the April 18–19, 2020, mass shooting in Nova Scotia, one of Canada's deadliest acts of violence [9]. The tragedy struck close to home for Laking, as three of her friends were killed. Lisa McCully, a schoolteacher, was killed in her home, while Joanne Thomas and John Zahl were killed at their home, which was subsequently burned to the ground as part of one of 16 crime scenes investigated [9]. The loss of these friends left a deep and lasting impact on Laking. She publicly described experiencing severe depression and a profound sense of loss of artistic inspiration immediately following the event [9]. This candid sharing of her grief marks a significant shift in her emotional and creative life, highlighting the vulnerability of artists and the profound effect that community trauma can have on their work. It underscores the intimate connection between her life experiences and her artistic expression, showing that her art is not created in a vacuum but is deeply intertwined with the joys, sorrows, and resilience of her community.

## Awards, Honors, and Legacy

Joy Laking's contributions to the arts and her community have been formally recognized through several prestigious awards and honors, cementing her legacy as a respected and influential figure in Nova Scotia's cultural landscape. Among her most notable accolades are the Halifax Progress Club Woman of Excellence Award, which she received in 2009, and the Queen's Diamond Jubilee Medal, awarded to her in 2012 [2], [8], [11]. The Queen's Diamond Jubilee Medal was a special Canadian award issued to commemorate the 60th anniversary of Queen Elizabeth II's accession to the throne, recognizing individuals for exceptional achievement and service to their community and country [11]. Recipients are selected through a rigorous process involving federal, provincial, and territorial governments, indicating that Laking's contributions were deemed outstanding on a significant scale [11]. In addition to these honors, she was also awarded the Cobequid Arts Council Lifetime Achievement Award, further testifying to her sustained impact on the arts in her region [8].

It is important to note that while Joy Laking has received numerous honors, she has not yet been appointed to the Order of Nova Scotia, the province's highest civilian honour [12], [13], [14], [16]. The Order of Nova Scotia, established in June 2001, recognizes individuals who have made an outstanding contribution to the prosperity, growth, or general good of the province [12], [13]. Official announcements of recipients for 2021, 2022, 2023, and 2024 do not include her name among the honorees, a fact that has been explicitly confirmed by multiple sources [12], [13], [14], [16]. As of December 2024, a total of 126 individuals had been invested into the order [12]. The absence of her name from these lists provides a clear and accurate record of her honors, distinguishing between the high level of recognition she has already achieved and the distinction of being a member of the province's highest order.

To conclude, Joy Laking's legacy is multifaceted. She is celebrated as a masterful painter whose depictions of Nova Scotia's landscapes have become iconic representations of the province's character [4], [5]. Her long and prolific career, supported by a robust exhibition history and public commissions, has established her as a cornerstone of the regional art scene [1], [8]. Equally significant is her role as a community builder and advocate who worked to elevate the profile of realism and support her fellow artists through the founding of PLANS and her service on the Art Gallery of Nova Scotia's board [2], [3]. Her foray into writing with the socially conscious play *Invisible Prisons* demonstrates a commitment to using her voice for broader societal benefit [7]. Finally, her public articulation of grief following the 2020 mass shooting reveals a deep connection to her community and a capacity for profound emotional expression, adding a layer of human resilience to her story [9]. Collectively, these contributions affirm her status as a vital and enduring cultural figure in Nova Scotia and Canada.

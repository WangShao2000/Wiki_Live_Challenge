# Closures of the University of Wisconsin Branch Campuses

The shutdown of multiple University of Wisconsin (UW) System branch campuses in the 21st century has marked a significant transition in the state's public higher education landscape. These closures, affecting several two-year and satellite campuses, resulted from a combination of persistent enrollment declines, financial pressures, and changing demographic patterns, along with broader political and administrative decisions. This article provides a detailed overview of the campus closures, associated factors, stakeholder responses, and the fate of each affected facility, backed by reliable sources.

## Overview of the University of Wisconsin Branch Campus Network

The University of Wisconsin System has for decades operated a network of branch campuses, many formerly known as UW Colleges. These sites primarily offered two-year associate degrees and sometimes served as access points for regional baccalaureate programs. They were located in small and mid-sized communities across Wisconsin, including Baraboo, Manitowoc, Marinette, Richland Center, and Fond du Lac. The branch campuses were restructured in 2018, linking most with nearby four-year UW institutions to address statewide enrollment and administrative challenges[1].

## List of Closed Campuses and Locations

In the past several years, several UW System branch campuses have closed or announced closure. Verified by official announcements, the primary branch campus closures include:

- **UW–Richland** (Richland Center, Richland County)
- **UW–Manitowoc** (Manitowoc, Manitowoc County)
- **UW–Marinette** (Marinette, Marinette County)
- **UW–Fond du Lac** (Fond du Lac, Fond du Lac County)
- **UW–Baraboo/Sauk County** (Baraboo, Sauk County)[2][3][4]

Some closures involved the suspension of in-person instruction and conversion to remote-only or transferred programs, while others resulted in full campus shutdowns.

## Timeline of Closures

- **UW–Richland:** Announced closure of in-person instruction in November 2022, final closure effective after Spring 2023 semester[2].
- **UW–Manitowoc:** Transitioned academic programs to UW–Green Bay; operations ceased effectively by mid-2024[3].
- **UW–Marinette:** Similarly transferred instruction to UW–Green Bay, with closure finalizing in 2024[3].
- **UW–Fond du Lac:** Announced closure of academic programming in 2024 after years of declining enrollment[4].
- **UW–Baraboo/Sauk County:** Ceased accepting new students in 2023; full closure announced in 2024[4].

These closures followed several years of declining enrollments and ongoing fiscal review, culminating in official announcements and phased wind-downs aligned with the end of academic years.

## Factors Leading to Closure

### Historical Factors

- The reorganization of the UW Colleges in 2018, driven by statewide trends of declining enrollment in two-year programs and efforts to improve efficiency, prompted many campuses to merge administratively with nearby four-year institutions. Despite restructuring, several campuses saw continued declines in student numbers and operating viability[1].

### Financial Factors

- The branch campuses faced budget shortfalls due to reduced state appropriations and tuition revenue tied to shrinking enrollments. The UW System cited multi-million dollar deficits at several sites, noting that maintaining facilities and staff without sustainable student populations was no longer feasible[2].

### Enrollment Factors

- Enrollment at campuses such as UW–Richland and UW–Fond du Lac fell by more than 50% in less than a decade, sometimes with fewer than 100 students registered in recent years[2][4]. This reflected broader national trends of reduced interest in two-year programs and changing demographic patterns in Wisconsin’s rural counties.

### Political and Administrative Factors

- The closures were shaped by statewide policy decisions, budget limitations imposed by the legislature, and UW System Board of Regents oversight. Some local lawmakers criticized the process, arguing that closures were announced with limited consultation and reflected insufficient effort to invest in and reinvent regional higher education. System officials maintained that closures were necessary given "unsustainable" conditions[2][4].

## Responses and Controversies

### University Administration

- UW System executives and campus leadership repeatedly identified the closures as "difficult but unavoidable," citing transparency in communications with staff and students regarding financial and enrollment realities[2].

### State and Local Government

- Local officials and some state legislators objected to the closures, expressing concern about educational access and economic impacts in affected communities. There were calls for additional funding, new missions for branch campuses (such as workforce retraining), and greater collaboration with local stakeholders[4].

### Students, Staff, and Faculty

- Students expressed disappointment and frustration over disruptions to their academic plans, sometimes facing transfers or abrupt relocations to other campuses. Staff and faculty raised issues about job losses, uncertainty, and the erosion of UW System’s mission to serve all regions, especially rural communities[3][4].

### Local Community

- Community members worried about long-term impacts on regional economies, access to higher education, and the repurposing of significant public assets. At several sites, local economic development leaders actively sought alternative uses for the campus properties, including proposals for technical education or business incubation spaces[3].

## Aftereffects and Current Status of Closed Campuses

### Property Disposition

- Many closed campuses remain in limbo, with local governments and UW partners engaged in negotiations over property transfer, sale, or alternative use. UW–Richland’s main site, for example, was offered to Richland County for reuse discussions[2].

### Repurposing and Demolition

- Some facilities have been proposed as local workforce centers or community spaces. Others face uncertain futures, with potential for demolition or sale to private buyers if no public use emerges[3][4].

### Relocation of Programs

- Where possible, academic programming has shifted to nearby four-year UW campuses. Students from closed branches were offered transfers to related programs at institutions such as UW–Platteville, UW–Green Bay, or UW–Oshkosh, sometimes with dedicated advising support[1].

# 1982 Los Angeles Rams–Green Bay Packers Game

## Overview

The 1982 Los Angeles Rams–Green Bay Packers game was contested on December 19, 1982, at Lambeau Field in Green Bay, Wisconsin, during an NFL season that was dramatically impacted by a players’ strike. This regular season matchup stands out because it occurred in a year when labor unrest reduced the NFL schedule to nine games, magnifying the importance of each contest. The Packers’ victory over the Rams in this game had ramifications for postseason aspirations and illustrates both the competitive climate of the truncated 1982 season and the circumstances faced by the competing franchises that year. The game is noted for its significance in the league's strike-shortened context, the performances of key players, and the role it played in determining playoff positions under extraordinary circumstances[1][2][3].

## Background and Context

### The 1982 NFL Season and Players’ Strike

The 1982 NFL season was highly atypical due to a 57-day players’ strike, which interrupted the schedule and resulted in the cancellation of seven weeks of play. This left each team with only nine games in the regular season and forced the league to introduce a unique 16-team playoff tournament, colloquially referred to as the “Super Bowl Tournament,” rather than the standard divisional structure[1]. The climate around the league was one of uncertainty and significance, as each remaining game became critical for playoff qualification.

### Team Situations

#### Los Angeles Rams

The Rams entered the 1982 season amid roster turnover and declining performance relative to their late 1970s successes. Plagued by instability at the quarterback position, the Los Angeles franchise struggled to find form. At the time of the Green Bay game (their final contest of the regular season), the Rams had minimal opportunity to reach the playoffs, having already endured several defeats[3].

#### Green Bay Packers

The Packers, led by head coach Bart Starr, were engaged in a competitive chase for a playoff position. They boasted a balanced offense with quarterback Lynn Dickey and a defense featuring stars such as linebacker John Anderson. The Packers’ standing entering the contest meant that a win would be pivotal for their postseason hopes, especially given the expanded but competitive playoff field[2].

## Game Summary

### Date, Location, and Conditions

The matchup took place at Lambeau Field, which is known for its challenging December conditions. The 1982 game was played on December 19 in the penultimate week of the abbreviated season[5].

### First Half

The Rams opened the scoring in the first quarter with a 34-yard touchdown pass from quarterback Bert Jones to running back Wendell Tyler. The Packers responded quickly, as Lynn Dickey led Green Bay’s offense downfield, culminating in a 1-yard touchdown run by Eddie Lee Ivery. The Packers built momentum and finished the first half with a 13-7 lead after two field goals by Jan Stenerud[5].

### Second Half

Green Bay extended its advantage with a 17-yard touchdown run by Gerry Ellis in the third quarter. The Rams failed to generate sustained offense as the Packers’ defense, sparked by turnovers and key plays, stalled several drives. Early in the fourth quarter, Ivery scored his second rushing touchdown, putting the game effectively out of reach. The Rams added a late touchdown, but the final outcome was a 35-23 Packers victory[5].

## Significant Events and Notable Performances

### Key Players

- **Lynn Dickey (Packers, QB):** Completed 19 of 29 passes for 273 yards and a touchdown, efficiently directing the offense and distributing the ball to multiple receivers.
- **Eddie Lee Ivery (Packers, RB):** Rushed 17 times for 109 yards and two touchdowns, providing balance to the Green Bay attack.
- **James Lofton (Packers, WR):** Caught 5 passes for 125 yards, making several explosive plays.
- **Bert Jones (Rams, QB):** Passed for 234 yards and two touchdowns but was intercepted twice, underlining the Rams’ offensive inconsistency[5].

### Turning Points and Highlights

- The Packers forced four turnovers, which stifled Los Angeles’s scoring chances.
- The game featured several long pass completions by Dickey, including multiple receptions over 25 yards by Lofton.
- There were no major injuries or controversial officiating incidents reported in the available accounts[5][6].

## Impact on the Season and Aftermath

### Playoff Picture and Team Implications

The Packers’ victory improved their record to 5-3-1, ensuring participation in the 16-team playoff, where they subsequently defeated the St. Louis Cardinals in the first round before falling to the Dallas Cowboys. The game affirmed the effectiveness of Green Bay’s offense in a high-stakes environment. For the Rams, the loss concluded their strike-shortened campaign with a 2-7 record, one of the poorest in franchise history, and marked the end of Bert Jones’s NFL career as he retired after the season due to recurring injuries[1][2][3][5][6].

### Broader Significance

The 1982 Packers–Rams game is illustrative of a season characterized by labor unrest, improvisational scheduling, and the implementation of a playoff format never used before or since. It served as a critical result for the Packers franchise, as it was their first playoff appearance since the NFL-AFL merger, and one of their few postseason berths between the Lombardi era and the 1990s. For the Rams, meanwhile, the result was confirmation of a team in transition and initiated significant changes in subsequent years, including coaching turnover and roster rebuilds[1][2][3][6][7].

## Legacy

The December 19 contest is remembered within the context of the 1982 season’s uniqueness; it is often cited as symbolic of the competitive pressures that arose from the strike-shortened schedule. The game’s non-traditional playoff implications and the performances of players such as Dickey, Lofton, and Ivery are regular fixtures in retrospectives on the era. No major controversies, milestone records, or disputes occurred during the contest; its chief significance is as a representative case of NFL adaptation during labor adversity[1][2][3][5][6][7].

# 2013 Green Bay Packers–Dallas Cowboys Game

## Lead

The 2013 Green Bay Packers–Dallas Cowboys game took place on December 15, 2013, at AT&T Stadium in Arlington, Texas, during Week 15 of the 2013 NFL season. The game is notable for the Packers' historic comeback, where they overcame a 23-point halftime deficit to defeat the Cowboys 37–36. This marked the largest comeback in Green Bay's storied history and had significant playoff implications for both teams. The game showcased pivotal quarterback play, critical coaching decisions, and dramatic shifts in momentum, subsequently drawing widespread media attention for its remarkable turnaround and impact on the NFC playoff picture[1][2][3][4][5][6].

## Background

### Team Context

**Green Bay Packers**  
Entering the game, the Packers (6–6–1) were without their star quarterback, Aaron Rodgers, who was sidelined due to a collarbone injury. Backup Matt Flynn was set to make the start, with Green Bay seeking to remain alive in the NFC North division race after a series of inconsistent performances late in the season[1][2][5].

**Dallas Cowboys**  
The Cowboys (7–6) were contending for the NFC East crown, tied with the Philadelphia Eagles, and sought to capitalize on home-field advantage to solidify their playoff positioning. Quarterback Tony Romo had led a potent offense but faced scrutiny over late-season performances in previous years[3][4][5].

### Pre-game Narratives and Implications

The game drew media attention due to the return of former Packers head coach Mike McCarthy to Dallas and the matchup between two of the league’s most recognized franchises. Both teams were under pressure, with playoff hopes hanging in the balance as the regular season approached its final weeks[4][5]. Key injuries, particularly Rodgers’s absence and Dallas’s defensive struggles, were focal points in pre-game analysis[2][5].

## Game Summary

### First Half

The Cowboys dominated early, capitalizing on Packers turnovers and strong passing from Tony Romo. Dallas built a commanding 26–3 lead by halftime, with running back DeMarco Murray and receivers Dez Bryant and Jason Witten contributing key plays. The Packers offense struggled, turning the ball over twice and failing to convert drives into touchdowns[1][2][3][4].

### Second Half: Comeback

Green Bay began the second half with renewed urgency. Matt Flynn led the Packers to five consecutive touchdown drives. Eddie Lacy, James Starks, and Jordy Nelson all found the end zone in the third and fourth quarters. The defense contributed with critical stops and two interceptions in the fourth quarter, both by cornerback Sam Shields and linebacker Tramon Williams[1][2][3][4][5].

A pivotal moment came late in the fourth quarter with Dallas leading 36–31. Romo threw an interception that led to a go-ahead Packers touchdown by Lacy. Dallas regained possession, but another Romo interception on the next series allowed Green Bay to run out the clock and complete the comeback[1][4][5][6].

## Key Plays and Turning Points

- **Packers’ Five Touchdown Drives (Second Half):** Green Bay scored on all five of their second-half drives, a feat attributed to precise execution and adjustment in play-calling.
- **Romo’s Fourth-Quarter Interceptions:** Two crucial interceptions thrown by Tony Romo in the final minutes, both while Dallas had the lead, dramatically shifted momentum.
- **Eddie Lacy’s Go-Ahead Touchdown:** With 1:31 left, Lacy ran in for a 1-yard touchdown to give Green Bay its first lead since the opening quarter[4][5].
- **Defensive Adjustments:** The Packers’ defense tightened in the second half, limiting Dallas to 10 points after halftime and forcing key turnovers[1][2][6].

## Records and Milestones

- The Packers’ comeback from a 23-point deficit is the largest in franchise history, surpassing a 20-point comeback against the Los Angeles Rams in 1982[4][5].
- It was the first time Green Bay won a regular-season game after trailing by 20 or more points[4].
- The collapse marked one of the most significant in Cowboys’ history, leading to intense scrutiny of coaching decisions and late-game play-calling[5][6].

## Aftermath

### Playoff Implications

- The Packers’ victory kept their playoff hopes alive, and they eventually won the NFC North with the return of Aaron Rodgers in Week 17.
- The Cowboys, with the loss, fell behind the Eagles and ultimately failed to reach the postseason, sparking controversy and criticism around late-game execution and coaching strategy[1][4][6].

### Media and Public Reaction

- National coverage highlighted the dramatic reversal, with commentary focusing on the Packers’ resiliency and Cowboys’ struggles with late-season leads.
- The game was cited as further evidence of Dallas's difficulties in critical moments, and the outcome fueled debate around Tony Romo’s and head coach Jason Garrett’s decision-making, especially the continued use of the passing game instead of running to protect the lead[5][6].

### Notable Quotes and Perspectives

- Packers head coach Mike McCarthy described the win as “a very special memory for our football team.”[5]
- Cowboys owner Jerry Jones and head coach Jason Garrett defended play-calling choices, but many media analysts argued for a more conservative approach late in the game[6].

## Notable Reactions

- The comeback is frequently referenced in lists of top regular-season comebacks in NFL history.
- The result influenced discussion in the following off-season about coaching changes and team direction for the Cowboys, though no immediate coaching firings occurred.

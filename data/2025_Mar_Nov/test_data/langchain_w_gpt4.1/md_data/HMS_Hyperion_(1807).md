# HMS Hyperion (1807): A Comprehensive Overview

## Lead Section

HMS Hyperion was a 32-gun fifth-rate frigate of the Royal Navy, launched in 1807 at Dover and constructed by shipbuilder John King. Designed during the height of the Napoleonic Wars, Hyperion represented the Royal Navy’s strategic need for swift, versatile warships capable of patrolling, commerce protection, and fleet support. As a member of a widely used class of frigates, she embodied contemporary advances in ship design and naval technology, and participated in the operational demands of the era. While specifics of her service record are primarily gleaned from naval biographies, official lists, and ship logs, available sources confirm her role in typical deployments rather than headline-grabbing battles. The fate of Hyperion, common among warships after their operational lifespan, is indicated by archival records, though precise details may remain incomplete. The ship’s career demonstrates the broader significance of frigates in shaping British maritime power during the early nineteenth century.

## Design and Construction

### Origins and Shipyard

HMS Hyperion was ordered and built amid an accelerated British naval build-up to meet wartime requirements. Shipbuilding records attribute her construction to John King, at the Dover shipyard, with her launch completed in 1807[1][2]. This period saw the rapid expansion of fifth-rate frigate production, driven by the Royal Navy’s strategic emphasis on agility and firepower.

### Specifications

- **Class and Type:** 32-gun fifth-rate frigate[1][2]
- **Designer:** Ships of her class generally followed plans by Sir John Henslow and William Rule, Surveyors of the Navy, though specific attribution for Hyperion varies in later compendia[1]
- **Dimensions:**
  - Length on gun deck: ~129 feet
  - Beam: ~34 feet
  - Depth of hold: ~11 feet
  - Tonnage: ~700 tons burthen (BM)[1][2]
- **Armament:**
  - 32 guns, including primarily 12-pounder long guns on the main deck, supplemented by lighter carronades or smaller guns on quarterdeck and forecastle[1][2]

Construction innovations typical of her class included copper sheathing for the hull, which minimized marine growth and enhanced hull longevity, and refined hull designs that improved speed, handling, and crew habitability[2].

### Context and Purpose

Hyperion was designed and constructed within the strategic context of the Napoleonic Wars. Fifth rates were critical to Britain’s overseas patrol, trade escorting, and blockade efforts, able to operate independently or as part of fleet squadrons. Such vessels matched tactical needs for multi-role efficiency during global conflict[1][2].

# Joy Laking

## Overview

Joy Laking is a Canadian visual artist recognized for her evocative paintings that capture the scenery and character of Atlantic Canada. Her artistic career, spanning several decades, is noted for its dedication to the landscapes, rural communities, and people of Nova Scotia. Laking’s work has been featured in numerous exhibitions, and she has contributed to the cultural landscape of Canadian art through her distinctive approach, community involvement, and public art projects. This article provides a comprehensive overview of her biography, artistic style, notable works, exhibitions, recognition, contributions, and public reception based on available and reliable sources.

## Biography

Joy Laking was born in England and emigrated to Canada as a child [1]. She settled in Nova Scotia and began developing her artistic practice there, focusing especially on painting scenes from around the province. Details regarding her education and early artistic training are less documented in primary sources. Based in Parrsboro, Nova Scotia, Laking has established both a studio and a gallery, engaging actively with the local community [2]. Her career trajectory is shaped by both her personal migration and her immersion in the Nova Scotia landscape, which became central to her artistic output.

## Artistic Style and Methods

Laking works chiefly in watercolour, acrylic, and oil. Her paintings are celebrated for their vibrant use of colour, precise draftsmanship, and ability to capture light and atmosphere. She is particularly known for plein air (outdoors, on-location) painting, a practice she maintains year-round, often documenting changing seasons, coastal vistas, rural interiors, and local events [2].

Thematically, her work exhibits a deep appreciation for rural life's simplicity and complexity, the natural environment, and cultural traditions of Nova Scotia. Laking tends to paint from observation, prioritizing authenticity in depicting tidal marshes, fishing villages, farmlands, and portraits of community members. Her style is representational, rooted in realism but often infused with expressive colour and compositional drama.

## Major Works and Projects

While Laking’s career includes an extensive range of paintings, several projects and works stand out:

- **Public Murals:** Notably, Laking has completed several public art commissions, including a prominent mural in Truro, Nova Scotia, which reflects local heritage and history [3].
- **People of the Maritimes:** An ongoing series depicts individuals from around the Maritime provinces, focusing on their stories and daily life [2].
- **Book Illustrations:** Laking’s artwork features in children’s literature and local history books, as well as exhibition catalogues highlighting Nova Scotia’s environment [1].

## Exhibitions

Joy Laking’s work has been widely exhibited across Nova Scotia and Canada. She has held solo shows in institutions such as the Art Gallery of Nova Scotia and the Craig Gallery [4], and her work is regularly displayed in her own studio-gallery space in Portapique. Laking has participated in group exhibitions celebrating Maritime art and landscape painters alongside national touring shows [1], drawing both critical and popular attention.

She is also an advocate for community engagement in the arts—organizing local exhibitions, workshops, and open studio events that invite the public to interact directly with her work and process.

## Recognition and Honors

Laking’s artistic contributions have been acknowledged with several awards and honors. She is the recipient of the Queen Elizabeth II Diamond Jubilee Medal for her service to Canadian arts [5]. Laking has been profiled in regional and national media outlets, signifying her importance within realisms of Atlantic Canadian art. Her work has featured in local tourism campaigns and she has served as an artistic mentor to emerging Nova Scotian artists [2].

## Contributions to Canadian Art and Legacy

Joy Laking is regarded as a significant contributor to Nova Scotia’s—and by extension, Canada’s—visual arts scene through:

- Persistent documentation of Atlantic Canada’s culture and landscape;
- Community leadership in promoting the arts, including education, advocacy, and collaborative projects;
- Artistic mentorship and participation in regional artist networks;
- Public commissions that celebrate local history and foster civic engagement;
- Preservation and celebration of Maritime heritage, contributing to the broader narrative of Canadian identity in art.

Her paintings are held in public and private collections, and her commitment to everyday subjects has resonated with audiences seeking authentic representations of the Canadian east coast. Laking’s practice demonstrates the enduring relevance of representational and landscape painting in Canadian art.

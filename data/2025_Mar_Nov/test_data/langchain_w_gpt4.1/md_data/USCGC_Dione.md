# US Coast Guard Cutter Dione

## Lead Section

The United States Coast Guard Cutter Dione (USCGC Dione) was a patrol vessel commissioned into the United States Coast Guard in the early 20th century, serving through multiple eras including World War II. Built for coastal patrol, law enforcement, search and rescue, and wartime convoy duties, Dione was notable for its participation in key maritime operations and patrols, exemplifying the transition of Coast Guard cutters from peacetime to wartime service. Dione's history reflects the evolution of US maritime agencies and naval technology in the interwar and wartime years. ## Construction and Commissioning

USCGC Dione was one of the 125-foot patrol boats built for the US Coast Guard as part of a fleet expansion during the Prohibition era. According to official US Coast Guard historical records, Dione was constructed at the shipyards of the American Brown Boveri Electric Corporation in Camden, New Jersey, and launched in 1927, with commissioning following shortly thereafter. The vessel was part of the "Active"-class cutters, built primarily for law enforcement and patrol duties along the US coastline. These ships were intended to interdict illegal smuggling and enforce federal regulations, especially in regards to Prohibition enforcement in the late 1920s and early 1930s[1].

## Technical Specifications

Dione belonged to the Active-class patrol boats, typically measuring 125 feet in length. The technical specifications commonly recorded for cutters of this class are as follows:

- **Length:** 125 feet (38.1 meters)
- **Beam:** Approximately 23 feet (7 meters)
- **Draft:** Approximately 8 feet (2.4 meters)
- **Displacement:** Roughly 230 tons
- **Engines:** Originally powered by diesel engines, capable of speeds around 13 knots
- **Armament:** Initially armed with a 3"/23 caliber deck gun; during World War II, anti-submarine warfare armaments (such as depth charge racks) were sometimes added
- **Crew Complement:** Varied with the era; usually between 30 and 40 personnel[2]

Records indicate that the design choice for the Active-class cutters reflected the need for reliability, extended range, and operational flexibility, intended for lengthy patrols and the ability to operate in varying sea states.

## Operational History

### Peacetime Service

Dione operated primarily along the US East Coast during its early service years, focusing on interdiction of smuggling and general law enforcement. The vessel was homeported at locations including Boston and New London, engaging in routine patrols, search and rescue assignments, and regulatory enforcement of fisheries and immigration rules[1].

### World War II Service

With the onset of World War II and the transfer of the Coast Guard to the US Navy, Dione's role shifted to include defense-related missions. The cutter was mobilized for anti-submarine patrol and convoy escort work along the Atlantic coast and offshore maritime lanes. Like many cutters of its class, Dione's armament was upgraded to meet threat assessments, and its patrol patterns adjusted to respond to German U-boat activity in American waters[3]. Specific action reports from official Coast Guard archives denote Dione's participation in convoy escort duties, submarine detection patrols, and protection of vital shipping corridors.

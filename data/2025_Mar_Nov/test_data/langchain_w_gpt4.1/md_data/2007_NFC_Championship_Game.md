# 2007 NFC Championship Game

## Overview

The 2007 NFC Championship Game was contested between the New York Giants and the Green Bay Packers on January 20, 2008, at Lambeau Field in Green Bay, Wisconsin. As the culminating match of the NFC postseason, its outcome determined the conference's representative in Super Bowl XLII. The Giants won, 23–20, in overtime, securing their spot in the Super Bowl and concluding a dramatic contest defined by extreme cold, pivotal moments, and historic ramifications. The game is especially notable for its impact on NFL history, its memorable performances, challenging conditions, and its role as the final game of Brett Favre’s tenure with the Packers[1][2].

## Background and Team Context

### New York Giants

- **Regular Season Performance**: Finished with a 10–6 record, earning the fifth seed in the NFC. The team, coached by Tom Coughlin, overcame inconsistent play early in the season, improved defensive performance, and demonstrated resilience, especially in road games[1][3].
- **Key Players**: Eli Manning (quarterback), Plaxico Burress (wide receiver), Brandon Jacobs (running back), and a defensive front led by Michael Strahan and Osi Umenyiora.
- **Path to Championship Game**:
  - Wild Card Round: Defeated Tampa Bay Buccaneers 24–14.
  - Divisional Round: Upset the top-seeded Dallas Cowboys 21–17, showcasing a strong defensive effort and clutch play from Manning[1][3].
- **Strategic Themes**: The Giants’ postseason run was marked by turnover-free performances from Manning and strong road play, becoming the third team ever to reach a Super Bowl via three road playoff victories[1].

### Green Bay Packers

- **Regular Season Performance**: Posted a 13–3 record to win the NFC North, led by head coach Mike McCarthy and veteran quarterback Brett Favre. The team featured a balanced offense and an opportunistic defense[2][4].
- **Key Players**: Brett Favre (quarterback), Donald Driver (wide receiver), Ryan Grant (running back), Charles Woodson (cornerback), Nick Barnett (linebacker).
- **Path to Championship Game**:
  - Divisional Round: Overcame a 14–0 deficit to defeat the Seattle Seahawks 42–20, highlighted by Grant’s 201 rushing yards, a franchise postseason record[2][4].
- **Team Strengths**: The Packers combined Favre’s passing acumen with a late-season running game surge, complemented by solid defensive play.

## Chronological Game Summary

### First Half

- **Conditions**: Play took place in −1°F temperatures at kickoff, with wind chills dropping to −23°F, the third-coldest in NFL history[2]. The harsh weather, dubbed "Ice Bowl II," influenced strategies and execution.
- **Opening Drives**: Both teams traded field goals. The Giants took an early lead, with Manning effectively managing the offense and Burress making key receptions.
- **Second Quarter**: Giants led at halftime, 13–10. Plaxico Burress accumulated over 100 receiving yards by halftime, repeatedly besting Packers coverages.

### Second Half

- **Momentum Swings**: Favre connected with Donald Driver for a 90-yard touchdown in the third quarter, one of the longest in NFL postseason history[3].
- **Critical Errors and Pressure**: Giants kicker Lawrence Tynes missed two field goals in the second half, including a potential game-winner as regulation expired, sending the game to overtime[2].

### Overtime

- **Decisive Play**: Packers won the coin toss, but on the second play from scrimmage, Giants cornerback Corey Webster intercepted Favre. The Giants advanced to Green Bay’s 33-yard line.
- **Game-Winning Kick**: Lawrence Tynes successfully converted a 47-yard field goal, securing the Giants’ 23–20 victory and their berth in Super Bowl XLII[2].

## Key Player Contributions and Notable Plays

- **Eli Manning**: Completed 21 of 40 pass attempts for 251 yards, with zero turnovers under intense pressure and conditions[1].
- **Plaxico Burress**: Registered 11 receptions for 151 yards, setting an NFC Championship Game record and consistently providing critical third-down conversions[3].
- **Lawrence Tynes**: Overcame two missed field goals, ultimately delivering the winning kick in overtime[2].
- **Brett Favre**: Threw for 236 yards and 2 touchdowns but also recorded 2 interceptions, including the pivotal overtime turnover[1].
- **Donald Driver**: Recorded 5 catches for 141 yards, including the 90-yard touchdown[3].
- **Corey Webster**: His overtime interception proved decisive, shifting momentum and field position in the Giants’ favor[2].

## Statistical Highlights

- **Turnovers**: Giants forced Packers into two critical turnovers, both Favre interceptions[1].
- **Rushing**: Brandon Jacobs contributed 67 rushing yards, while Ryan Grant was limited to 29 yards after a stellar previous performance[4].
- **Temperature**: The −1°F kickoff temperature was the coldest for any championship game since the original "Ice Bowl" in 1967[2].

## Post-Game Impact and Legacy

- **Giants**: The win propelled the Giants to Super Bowl XLII, where they defeated the undefeated New England Patriots, completing one of the most unexpected playoff runs in NFL history. This established Eli Manning and Tom Coughlin among the notable figures in NFL postseason lore[1][3].
- **Packers**: Brett Favre’s interception marked his final play as a Green Bay Packer, as he announced retirement after the season. The Packers subsequently underwent significant transitions at quarterback and adjusted organizational leadership[4].
- **Historical Recognition**: The game is frequently cited among the most memorable NFC Championship contests for its conditions, stakes, and dramatic finish.

## Controversies and Significant Narratives

- **Favre’s Final Pass**: The overtime interception became an emblematic moment in the Hall of Fame quarterback’s career, leading to extensive post-game discussion and analysis[4].
- **Weather Safety**: Intense cold conditions prompted debate regarding athlete safety and NFL procedures for extreme weather.
- **Coaching Decisions**: Critical evaluations of play calling in overtime, especially the Packers’ sudden-death strategy, were discussed in post-game media, but no major officiating disputes or other controversies were prominent[2][4].
- **Tynes’ Redemption**: Tynes’s missed opportunities and ultimate success created a narrative of perseverance and resilience, widely discussed in subsequent reporting[2].

# Brave Bunnies

## Overview

**Brave Bunnies** is an animated children's television series created by Olga Cherepanova and produced by the Ukrainian animation studio Glowberry, in collaboration with De Agostini Editore and Anima. The show is designed for preschool audiences and is notable for its focus on promoting curiosity, acceptance of diversity, and problem-solving through the everyday adventures of two young rabbit siblings. Since its debut in 2020, the series has garnered international distribution across more than 50 countries, multiple broadcast platforms, and has been adapted into several languages. Its unique approach to preschool programming, blending entertainment with educational and social values, has contributed to its recognition within the children’s media landscape.

## Creation and Development

Brave Bunnies was conceptualized by Olga Cherepanova, drawing inspiration from her observations as a mother and educator on the importance of social inclusion and adventure in early childhood development. The series was developed and initially produced by Glowberry, an animation studio based in Kyiv, Ukraine. From the outset, the project secured international partners, including Italy’s De Agostini Editore and Spain’s Anima, helping to create a series with universal appeal and high production values. Cherepanova’s goal was to address common childhood experiences — such as meeting new friends from different backgrounds and working together to solve simple problems — using anthropomorphic animal characters and gentle narratives. Industry support and co-production arrangements allowed the series to reach a broad audience through major international distributors [1].

## Production

Glowberry, a subsidiary of FILM.UA Group, led the creative development and animation production of Brave Bunnies. Pre-production and scripts were handled by a team led by Cherepanova, with input from educational consultants to align storylines with preschool learning needs. Animation was carried out in partnership with Anima Kitchent in Spain, using vibrant colors and simple designs intended to appeal to and be easily understood by young children. De Agostini Editore managed aspects of European funding and distribution strategy. Production began in 2019, with Season 1 comprising 52 episodes of seven minutes each. Later seasons and additional content were developed following positive reception and international sales. The series’ design emphasizes clarity, warmth, and a gentle tone, intentionally avoiding peril and conflict to suit its intended audience [2].

## Broadcast History and Distribution

Brave Bunnies premiered in Ukraine and Italy in early 2020, followed by rollouts across Europe, Latin America, Asia, and other territories. The series has been licensed to broadcasters and streaming services including:

- Milkshake! (UK, Channel 5/ViacomCBS)
- Super RTL (Germany)
- ABC Kids (Australia)
- YLE (Finland)
- HOP! (Israel)
- Nick Jr. (multiple global regions)
- Clan (Spain)
- Discovery Kids (Latin America)

It is also available on streaming platforms such as YouTube and Amazon Prime Video in some regions. The show has been dubbed into more than 20 languages including English, Spanish, Italian, German, Finnish, and Hebrew. By 2023, Brave Bunnies was reportedly broadcast in over 50 countries, reflecting its adaptability and success within the globally competitive preschool market [3].

## Main Characters and Themes

### Main Characters

- **Boo**: The older, adventurous sister who often leads the duo’s explorations.
- **Bop**: Boo’s younger brother, who is curious and learning about the world.
- **Brave Bunny Family**: Parents and siblings who provide a loving, supportive background.
- **Animal Friends**: Each episode introduces new animal friends (e.g., Fox, Hedgehog, Piglet), each with unique traits.

### Themes

Recurring themes of Brave Bunnies include:

- **Curiosity and Adventure**: Each episode revolves around the bunnies setting out to discover new friends and environments, reinforcing positive risk-taking and curiosity.
- **Inclusion and Diversity**: The Brave Bunnies encounter animal friends with different abilities, backgrounds, and cultures, modeling open-mindedness and empathy.
- **Problem-Solving and Teamwork**: Stories focus on cooperative play, sharing, and creative solutions to common preschooler dilemmas.
- **Emotional Intelligence**: Emphasis is placed on recognizing and appropriately expressing feelings, as well as understanding others’ emotions [1][2].

## Reception

Brave Bunnies has been positively received by industry critics and educational consultants, who have highlighted its focus on social skills, gentle humor, and sensitive approach to diversity as strengths. The show was recognized as a finalist for several international awards, including Best Animated Preschool Series at the Cartoon Forum and finalist in the 2021 Licensing International Awards. It has been praised by outlets such as Kidscreen and Animation Magazine for combining entertaining adventures with meaningful life lessons. Audience response has also been favorable, reflected in strong broadcast sales and the expansion into multiple language versions and markets. The show’s approach to inclusion and gentle teaching moments has been noted as a distinguishing feature within its genre [2][3][4].

## Merchandising

Following the show’s initial success, Glowberry entered into licensing partnerships to produce a range of Brave Bunnies merchandise. This includes books, plush toys, board games, clothing, and digital apps targeting preschoolers and their parents. Leading toy companies in the UK and Europe have secured representation rights, expanding the property beyond television screens. Book tie-ins, including storybooks, coloring books, and educational activity books, have been released in several languages. The show’s distinctive visual style and appeal to parents seeking positive content for children have contributed to its performance in the children’s consumer product sector [5].

## Controversies and Disputes

As of 2025, there are no widely reported controversies or disputes associated with Brave Bunnies. The series’ content and marketing have generally aligned with industry standards for preschool programming and have not attracted notable criticism from educators, parent groups, or media watchdogs. The distribution and success of the series have not been significantly affected by political or cultural issues, including those related to its Ukrainian origins. Some isolated scheduling changes or regional variations in content dubbing have been reported, but these are typical for internationally adapted children’s programming [2][4].

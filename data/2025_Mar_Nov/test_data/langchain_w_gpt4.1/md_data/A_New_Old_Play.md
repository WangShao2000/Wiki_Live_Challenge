# A New Old Play

## Overview

*A New Old Play* is a Chinese feature film released in 2021. Directed and written by Qiu Jiongjiong, the film is a blend of theatrical storytelling and cinematic technique, focusing on the life and afterlife journey of a veteran Sichuan opera clown named Qiu Fu. The film stands out for its inventive use of visual style and narrative structure, drawing inspiration from 20th-century Chinese history and the director's own family background. Notably, *A New Old Play* was screened in the International Competition section of the 74th Locarno Film Festival, where it was awarded the Special Jury Prize[1].

## Background and Production

*A New Old Play* emerged from the cinematic vision of Qiu Jiongjiong, a filmmaker known for his creative engagement with Chinese theatrical traditions and history. The film was produced by Thomas Y. Siu and Jia Kai, with Lin Weiqi as the director of photography and Wang Yirui as the editor. The primary language of the film is Mandarin, and it was produced in China in 2021.

The screenplay, also written by Qiu Jiongjiong, weaves together elements of personal memory, the cultural legacy of Sichuan opera, and reflections on the political turmoil experienced in China during the 20th century. The narrative structure deliberately incorporates theatrical conventions and stage design, creating a metatheatrical experience that blurs the boundaries between performance and lived reality[1].

## Plot

The narrative follows Qiu Fu, a well-known clown in Sichuan opera, as he embarks on an afterlife journey following his death. Guided by supernatural agents, Qiu Fu relives pivotal moments from his own life, as well as the collective history of his troupe and community, encountering historical figures and events from modern Chinese history. By juxtaposing personal memory with the country's political and cultural shifts, the film explores cycles of loss, memory, and artistic survival within the framework of traditional Chinese performance art.

Through a mix of dark humor and drama, the story examines how artists and their communities endure significant upheavals, relying on cultural and artistic traditions to navigate their experiences. The film adopts highly stylized visuals and shifts between the realms of life and death, emphasizing themes of legacy, remembrance, and creative resilience[1].

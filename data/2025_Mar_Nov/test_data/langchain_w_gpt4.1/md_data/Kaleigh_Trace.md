# Kaleigh Trace

## Overview

Kaleigh Trace is a Canadian writer, sex educator, and disability advocate known primarily for her work addressing sexuality, disability, and feminism. Trace’s published works and public lectures have been recognized for their candor and contribution to the discourse on sexual health and the experiences of people living with disabilities. Her first book, *Hot, Wet, & Shaking: How I Learned to Talk About Sex*, won the 2015 Lambda Literary Award for Lesbian Memoir/Biography. Trace is notable for centering marginalized voices in conversations about sexuality and offering both practical and personal perspectives informed by her own experiences[1].

Trace identifies as queer and disabled, themes that are integral to her writings and public engagements[1][2].

## Professional Career

### Writing

Trace began her career writing as a blogger under the pseudonym “The Fucking Facts,” a platform where she explored issues relating to sexuality, disability, and feminist identity in accessible language. Her writing attracted attention for its combination of humor, honesty, and critical awareness[3].

Her debut book *Hot, Wet, & Shaking: How I Learned to Talk About Sex* (Arsenal Pulp Press, 2014) is a memoir blending personal narrative with sex education and analysis. The book explores Trace's journey toward candidly discussing sex despite social taboos and internalized shame, particularly as related to growing up disabled. In interviews, Trace has explicitly linked her writing to a desire to address the limited and often ableist nature of sexual education in mainstream discourse, particularly for people who identify as queer or disabled[1][2].

### Sex Education and Advocacy

In addition to her writing, Trace has worked as a sex educator, leading workshops and giving lectures at universities, sexual health conferences, and community organizations across Canada. She has been engaged in efforts to make sex education more inclusive and representative, addressing not only the general public but also marginalized groups whose experiences have traditionally been neglected in sexual health and rights education[2][4].

## Notable Achievements

### Lambda Literary Award

Trace’s book *Hot, Wet, & Shaking* received the 2015 Lambda Literary Award in the category of Lesbian Memoir/Biography. This award is recognized as a significant honor in LGBTQ+ literature, conferred by the Lambda Literary Foundation to writers whose work advances queer themes and representation[1].

### Public Recognition

Trace’s work has been noted in Canadian literary and academic circles for challenging prevailing narratives around sex and disability. She has been featured in national media such as CBC Radio, and her essays have appeared in respected venues, contributing to wider discussions on sexuality, gender, and ability. She has been recognized not only for her writing but for her public speaking and commitment to education[4].

## Cultural Impact

Kaleigh Trace is considered an important voice in multiple intersecting fields: queer literature, disability studies, and feminist sex education. Her advocacy has contributed to shifting conversations about sex in Canada and beyond, emphasizing the need for inclusive, shame-free spaces in sexual health discourse. Scholars and community organizations have cited her work as instrumental in improving public understanding of sexuality among disabled and queer populations[2][4].

Her presence as both an educator and a memoirist has helped reduce stigma around sexual expression for people with disabilities. By sharing candid personal stories alongside researched arguments, Trace invites a broader re-imagining of what sexual autonomy and pleasure can look like, challenging ableist, homophobic, and sexist barriers[1][4].

## Critical Reception

*Hot, Wet, & Shaking* was well-received by reviewers and communities invested in LGBTQ+ and disability representation. Literary critics have noted Trace’s mix of humor and analytic insight, and her work has been used by educators as a resource for developing more accessible sex education curricula[1][2].

Academic reviewers and activists alike have credited Trace with making complex and often overlooked topics more approachable for broad audiences. The Lambda Literary Award jury commended the book for its candidness, intersectionality, and ability to speak directly to readers often underserved by mainstream sexual health media[1].
